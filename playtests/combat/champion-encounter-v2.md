## 5. Relevant Traits

### Alpha Trait 1 — Pack Resonance

Same as v1. Passive damage bonus. Drops at substantial throat damage.

---

### Alpha Trait 2 — Coordinated Drive

**What it does:**
Both flankers simultaneously rush and attack a single designated target on the exchange the howl resolves.

**How it activates:**
Alpha throat contraction → howl onset → flankers move. The contraction precedes the howl by one observable beat.

**Prevention — mechanical anchor (new in v2):**
A character who has observed the throat contraction and declares a targeted interrupt action against the Alpha before their normal action resolves may attempt to suppress it. Mechanics:
- The interrupt is declared when the Narrator describes the throat contraction beginning
- It counts as the character's action for that exchange
- It is attempted as a targeted strike or condition application against the Alpha (at no additional difficulty modifier — it is a normal action, just declared out of sequence)
- If the action lands and produces meaningful impact (hit that would normally stagger or disrupt), the howl is cancelled and the Coordinated Drive does not fire that exchange
- If the action misses or produces insufficient impact, the howl resolves and the drive fires normally

This makes the prevention window a real mechanical opportunity, not a narrative possibility. Any character — not just analytical ones — can exploit it once they know the throat contraction is the signal.

---

### Alpha Trait 3 — Icy Breath (Alpha-grade)

Same structure as common Ice Wolf. Larger cone, Cold II direct hit. Same wind-up signal. Familiar to any character with prior Ice Wolf experience.

**Throat damage dual-function signal (new in v2):**
The first time the Alpha's throat takes any damage, the Narrator must describe both effects simultaneously in a single narration:
- The vapor from the Alpha's breath thins visibly
- The next howl attempt produces a rougher, shorter sound than before
These two effects must appear in the same Narrator description at the same moment — not separately. The visual (vapor) and acoustic (howl quality) degradation arriving together is the signal that links both functions to one source.

## 7. Enemy Reading Paths

### 7.1 Friction-Based Reading

**Proactive Narrator signal — flanker glancing (new in v2):**
In exchanges 1 and 2, before any deliberate player observation, the Narrator should describe the flanker glancing behavior without prompting. This signal exists independently of whether the Coordinated Drive has fired. It is the earliest available structural clue and must reach the group before the drive executes.

**Clues from the Coordinated Drive (primary friction path for martial groups):**
- The drive fires once. A martial player under pressure will attribute the simultaneous attack to the howl — instinct connects cause and effect without formal analysis.
- The pack hesitation signal after Alpha is hit provides confirmation through consequence.
- Together: howl triggers drive → hit the Alpha → flankers hesitate. This is a three-step friction chain readable without any analytical framing.

**Clues from impact — Alpha:**
Same as v1. Pack hesitation on Alpha hit must be narrated visibly.

**Clues from throat damage:**
When the Alpha's throat first takes damage: vapor thins AND howl quality degrades in the same description. Both effects, simultaneously. A martial player who landed the hit registers both without needing to analyze them separately.

**Negative signals:**
Flanker hits produce individual response and no structural change. After a martial group focuses flankers for 2–3 exchanges and notices no change in the Alpha's behavior or the other flanker's coordination, instinct drives target switching — especially if the Coordinated Drive has already landed on them.

## 9. Attrition Pressure

**Key difference from v1 (mixed group):**
A martial-skewed group absorbs the first Coordinated Drive fully before understanding the trigger. This is the primary attrition spike. The calibration question is whether the group survives this spike with enough capacity to still respond to the structural discovery.

**What should feel costly:**
- The first Coordinated Drive landing before anyone attempts prevention
- Sustained flanker pressure while the Alpha is ignored
- Pack Resonance compounding flanker hits if the Alpha is not prioritized early

**What should not feel too costly:**
- Instinctive switching to Alpha after the drive — this should feel like the right move even without analytical framing
- Wide positioning by the Conservador — should consistently provide partial protection from the drive geometry

**What should trigger Fatigue 1:**
After the Alpha has been hit and the pack hesitation has been observed — but before the Alpha's throat is substantially damaged. Marcial A (highest attrition character) should reach Fatigue 1 no earlier than exchange 5, after the structural dependency is established through consequence.

## 10. Expected Combat Phases

### Phase 1 — Contact and Instinctive Engagement
**What happens:**
The flankers are the first visible threat. Martial instinct targets the nearest active attacker. The Alpha holds position. Flanker glancing signal delivered proactively by Narrator. No maneuver has fired.

**What players should perceive:**
The big one is not attacking. The flankers are moving oddly before repositioning. The Cold I baseline is already present.

---

### Phase 2 — First Coordinated Drive
**What happens:**
The Alpha howls. The Coordinated Drive fires for the first time. A Marcial character takes double-flank damage. The instinctive connection between howl and drive is immediate.

**What changes:**
The encounter now has a clear attributable cause for the spike in damage. The Alpha becomes a target not through analysis but through consequence.

---

### Phase 3 — Structural Targeting and Drive Prevention Attempt
**What happens:**
One or both Marcial characters redirect attention to the Alpha. Alpha is hit; pack hesitation observed. Alpha attempts a second howl. Whether the prevention window is exploited depends on whether a character calls the throat contraction in time.

**What should become clear:**
Hitting the Alpha changes the flankers' behavior. The group is now operating with the structural dependency understood — even if not formally named.

---

### Phase 4 — Coordination Collapse and Resolution
**What happens:**
Alpha throat substantially damaged. Partial or failed howl. Pack Resonance dropped. Flankers act independently. The encounter becomes a double-Common-enemy cleanup.

## 11. Narrative Signals for the Narrator

> **Independence note:** Each signal must function independently of other abilities shown. The flanker glancing signal must be narrated even if the Coordinated Drive has not yet fired. The dual-function throat signal must appear even if the breath has not yet been used. Signals are parallel discovery paths.

### Proactive flanker signal — exchanges 1 and 2 (new in v2)
- Narrate without being prompted: *"The smaller wolf takes the hit — and just before it resets position, you notice it glance toward the larger one. Not in fear. In expectation."*
- This signal is delivered regardless of whether any player announced they were watching.
- It does not require the Coordinated Drive to have fired.

### If the group attacks only flankers (Alpha ignored)
Same as v1.

### Alpha throat contraction (prevention window onset)
- *"The large wolf's throat tightens — a deep vibration, not yet a sound — something is forming"*
- This description is the declaration trigger. A player who hears it and declares an interrupt toward the Alpha before the howl resolves may attempt prevention.

### If the Coordinated Drive fires
Same as v1. Both flankers move simultaneously. Double-hit on the targeted character.
- After the drive: *"That howl — and then they both moved. At the same time."* Narrate the causal connection explicitly.

### If the Alpha is hit and staggers
- Flankers hesitate — must be narrated with enough specificity to register even for a player in direct combat:
- *"The two smaller ones go quiet — not injured, not retreating. Just quiet for a beat. As if they lost something they were listening for."*

### Dual-function throat signal — first throat damage (new in v2)
- The first time any character lands a meaningful hit on the Alpha's throat:
- *"Something changed — the vapor coming off its breath thinned, and the sound it was building in its chest came out shorter than it should have. One hit. Both things at once."*
- This is a single narration, not two separate descriptions. Both effects in the same sentence.

### If the Alpha's throat is substantially damaged
- Partial howl: one flanker responds, the other doesn't — *"The sound it made was wrong. The right one moved; the left one waited."*
- Pack Resonance dropped: subsequent flanker hits land lighter — narrate if the Marcial player would register the difference.

### If the Alpha's throat is silenced
- Failed howl: *"Nothing — a dry sound, then silence. Both flankers looked toward it. Then looked away."*
- Pack Resonance gone. Flankers fully independent.

### If the Alpha is killed (new in v2)
- *"The large one drops. Immediately — before you can register it fully — the other two change. They stop moving together. One turns toward [nearest character]; the other pulls back. They're not a pack anymore. They're just wolves."*
- This must happen in the same exchange as the Alpha's death. No transition. Immediate collapse narrated explicitly.

### If a character spreads wide (Conservador positioning)
*(deliver when the Coordinated Drive cannot reach the spread character)*
- *"The smaller ones seemed to try to converge on two targets — but the spacing was wrong. There was no angle on the one standing wide."*
- This makes Conservador's positional choice legible as a decision that worked, not as an accident.

### On ambient cold (in any exchange where no direct damage lands)
- Numb extremities. Shortened breath. Ground pulling warmth upward.
- Must be present every time no direct hit lands — confirms the environment is an active cost, not decoration.

## 16. Playtest Log

**Test group:**
Simulated panel — martial-skewed composition: Marcial A (aggressive frontline, no analytical tendency), Marcial B (aggressive, follows Marcial A's lead), Conservador (defensive positioning, spatial awareness but no reading specialization). No Táctico. No Especialista.

**Number of players:**
3 simulated roles.

**Group state:**
No prior knowledge of the Frost Pack Alpha. No Bestiary Lore. No Combat Observation specialization. Cold I ambient at encounter start. Terrain: treeline edge, icy patches in open ground, restricted sightlines. Two flankers visible on approach; Alpha visible a moment later, elevated and behind.

---

### Exchange 1 — Contact and Instinctive Engagement

**What happened:**

Two flankers entered visual range first. The Alpha became visible a beat later — larger, elevated on a rocky outcropping, positioned behind the flanker line. The size disparity was immediately apparent.

Marcial A advanced on the nearer flanker and attacked. Landed a solid hit. The flanker yelped, absorbed the damage, and reset its position — shifting sideways rather than retreating or pressing forward. Instinctive read: *"This one's not running."*

Marcial B followed Marcial A's lead and pressed the same flanker. Second hit. The flanker was visibly impaired but still functional.

Conservador positioned wide — instinctive spacing rather than formal formation awareness. Avoided the direct axis between both flankers.

**Proactive Narrator narration delivered here:**
Before any player declared they were watching, the Narrator described: *"The smaller wolf takes the second hit — and just before it resets, you notice it glance toward the larger one. Not in fear. In expectation."*

Marcial A caught this: *"Did it just look at the big one?"* Filed away without analysis.
Marcial B did not register it — was focused on the next attack.
Conservador from wider spacing: also caught it, with the additional observation that the Alpha's gaze was tracking the group, not the wolves being attacked.

Alpha did not attack offensively. It shifted position slightly — maintaining diagonal sight lines to both flankers.

---

**End of Exchange 1:**
One flanker impaired. Flanker glancing signal delivered proactively — registered by Marcial A and Conservador. Alpha undamaged. No maneuver fired.

---

### Exchange 2 — Continued Pressure and Pre-Maneuver Signal

**What happened:**

Marcial A and B continued pressure on the impaired flanker — martial instinct: press the wounded target.

Conservador, positioned wider, watched the Alpha. It still hadn't attacked. Something about a large predator that isn't engaging felt wrong. No analytical framework — just: *"the big one is waiting for something."*

The Narrator delivered the second proactive flanker glancing signal. The other flanker (undamaged, not under attack) repositioned sideways, and just before doing so, glanced toward the Alpha. *"The uninjured one moves — sideways, not toward you — and you catch the same thing: a glance toward the large one before it shifts."* Conservador registered this clearly: *"They keep looking at it before they move."*

Marcial A: now aware of the pattern, though still not acting on it. Focused on finishing the impaired flanker.

The Alpha's throat tightened. Conservador saw it from wider spacing: *"The big one's doing something with its throat — a vibration, not yet a sound."* Called it to the group: *"Something's happening with the big one."*

But neither Marcial responded with a declared interrupt — they were engaged with the flanker and the warning did not translate to action in time.

The howl resolved.

---

### Exchange 3 — First Coordinated Drive

**What happened:**

Both flankers rushed Marcial A simultaneously from two angles. The impaired flanker that had absorbed two hits still had enough function to participate — Pack Resonance was active, and the coordination signal overrode individual injury.

Marcial A took hits from both sides in the same exchange. Significant damage. Cold I from one bite. Cold compounding with the ambient baseline — approaching Cold II threshold.

Marcial B reacted and tried to intercept one flanker, arriving a beat late.

Conservador, positioned wide, was geometrically inaccessible — the drive could not reach them from both flanker positions simultaneously. The Narrator delivered the spread signal: *"The smaller ones seemed to try to converge on two targets — but the spacing was wrong. There was no angle on the one standing wide."* Conservador registered this as confirmation of their positioning choice.

Marcial A's immediate reaction, unprompted and instinctive: *"That sound it made — and then they both came at once. That's connected."* The attribution happened in the moment of impact, not through analysis. The Alpha's howl was the last thing audible before the double hit. Causal inference through consequence: the howl caused this.

Marcial B, arriving late to intercept: *"What was that sound it made?"*

---

**End of Exchange 3:**
Marcial A has taken the full Coordinated Drive. Significant damage. Approaching Cold II from bite + ambient. Attribution formed instinctively: howl → drive. Alpha is now a target in Marcial A's mind — not because of structural reading, but because of consequence. Conservador's spacing confirmed effective.

---

### Exchange 4 — Instinctive Target Switch to Alpha

**What happened:**

Marcial A, injured and attributing the damage to the Alpha's howl, switched targets without deliberation: *"Hit the big one."*

Marcial B: *"What about the wolves?"* — looked toward the flankers, then toward the Alpha, then toward Marcial A. Followed the switch. Marcial instinct: if your most aggressive ally targets something, that's probably the right call.

Both Marcial attacked the Alpha. Marcial A landed a meaningful hit. The Alpha staggered.

The pack hesitation signal fired. The Narrator, knowing a player in direct combat might miss a subtle description, narrated with specificity: *"The two smaller ones go quiet — not injured, not retreating. Just quiet for a beat. As if they lost something they were listening for."*

Marcial A, mid-combat with the Alpha, still caught this: *"...it got quiet."* Looked sideways. Both flankers had paused. Not hurt — paused. The connection solidified: Alpha state → flanker behavior.

Marcial B registered it clearly from a less-engaged position: *"When we hit it, the others stopped."*

Conservador, from wide spacing, confirmed: *"The three of them are connected. The big one is the signal."*

The Alpha had not yet used its ice breath. The throat had not yet been damaged. But the structural dependency was now established for all three players — through pure consequence, not observation or analysis.

---

**End of Exchange 4:**
Alpha hit. Pack hesitation observed by all three characters. Structural dependency established through consequence: hitting the Alpha changes the flankers. No analytical reading was required. The flanker glancing signal (exchanges 1–2) and the pack hesitation signal (exchange 4) converged on the same conclusion through two different friction routes.

---

### Exchange 5 — Throat Damage and Dual-Function Signal

**What happened:**

Both Marcial characters continued on the Alpha. Conservador moved to reduce flanking geometry further.

The Alpha attempted a second howl. The throat contraction was visible. Conservador called it: *"It's doing the thing again — the throat."*

Marcial A, already focused on the Alpha and now primed by the exchange 3 experience, declared an interrupt: *"I hit it before it finishes."* This was the first use of the prevention window as a declared action — not because Marcial A understood the mechanic analytically, but because the instinct to stop the howl before it produced another double-hit drove the declaration.

The interrupt landed. The Alpha staggered before the howl resolved. The maneuver was cancelled.

The Narrator delivered the dual-function throat signal — because the hit that cancelled the howl landed on the throat:

*"Something changed — the vapor coming off its breath thinned, and the sound it was building in its chest came out shorter than it should have. One hit. Both things at once."*

Marcial A, who landed the hit: registered both in the same moment. *"The breath and the sound — same place."* Not a formal conclusion, but a visceral recognition: one point, two effects.

Marcial B noticed the lighter texture of the Alpha's exhale without being prompted: *"Its breath looks different."*

The Alpha used its ice breath for the first time — a partial application, weaker than its undamaged state, because throat damage had already begun accumulating. The familiar wind-up (same as common Ice Wolf) preceded it. Conservador: *"That's the same thing the other ones do."* Positioned out of the cone.

Pack Resonance had dropped with the first throat hit. The flankers' next attacks, directed at Marcial B, landed lighter than the pre-damage hits. Marcial B noticed the difference — not formally, but: *"That hit less hard."*

---

**End of Exchange 5:**
Alpha's throat substantially damaged. Partial howl cancelled by declared interrupt. Dual-function signal delivered and received by Marcial A. Pack Resonance dropped — noticed by Marcial B through comparative damage. Marcial A approaching Fatigue 1. Conservador unaffected. Marcial B slightly below Fatigue 1 threshold.

---

### Exchange 6 — Partial Howl and Structural Collapse

**What happened:**

Marcial A: Fatigue 1 reached. Still functional, but the cost of the Coordinated Drive hit in exchange 3 plus sustained combat was now a real constraint.

Alpha attempted a third howl. The throat damage was now substantial. The sound it produced was wrong — a strangled vibration, short and rough. One flanker began moving; the other did not respond. The drive fired as a partial execution: single flanker only.

Marcial B intercepted the single flanker easily — a single attacker rather than a simultaneous pair was a completely different threat level.

Conservador: *"It's not working anymore. The sound it made — the other one didn't go."*

Marcial A continued Alpha pressure.

---

### Exchange 7 — Alpha Elimination and Immediate Collapse

**What happened:**

The Alpha's remaining HP was depleted. It went down.

The Narrator delivered the Alpha death signal immediately: *"The large one drops. Before you can register it fully — the other two change. They stop moving together. One turns toward Marcial B; the other pulls back toward the treeline. They're not a pack anymore. They're just wolves."*

Marcial A, Marcial B, and Conservador all registered the shift in the same exchange. No transition state. No intermediate coordination. The flankers were immediately independent.

The remaining two flankers — one impaired from exchange 1 and 2, one undamaged — were now Common enemies. The group handled them across the final exchange without structural complexity.

---

**Final State:**
- Alpha: eliminated (exchange 7)
- Flanker 1: impaired, now independent, eliminated in cleanup
- Flanker 2: undamaged, now independent, retreating — encounter effectively resolved
- Marcial A: Fatigue 1 reached (exchange 6), carried through cleanup
- Marcial B: near Fatigue 1 threshold, functional
- Conservador: below Fatigue 1, positional advantage maintained throughout

### Section 16 continued — Summary Findings

**Actual combat duration:**
7 significant exchanges across 4 phases.

- Phase 1 (exchanges 1–2): contact and instinctive engagement — flanker glancing signal delivered proactively, Alpha non-aggression registered
- Phase 2 (exchange 3): Coordinated Drive fires — instinctive attribution by Marcial A, Conservador positioning confirmed effective
- Phase 3 (exchanges 4–5): structural targeting — pack hesitation observed by consequence (exchange 4), dual-function throat signal delivered (exchange 5), prevention window exploited by instinct (exchange 5)
- Phase 4 (exchanges 6–7): structural collapse — partial howl (exchange 6), Alpha elimination and immediate coordination collapse (exchange 7)

**Moment of first Fatigue:**
Marcial A reached Fatigue 1 at exchange 6 — after the structural dependency was established (exchange 4), the prevention window was first exploited (exchange 5), and Pack Resonance had dropped. The Alpha was still a threat when Fatigue arrived. This is within the acceptable window.

**How the group learned through friction:**

- The flanker glancing signal reached all three characters through proactive narration in exchanges 1–2, before the Coordinated Drive fired. Without this proactive delivery, Marcial B would not have received the signal at all.
- Instinctive howl-attribution: Marcial A connected the howl to the drive in exchange 3 without deliberate analysis — consequence was immediate and unambiguous.
- Pack hesitation: Alpha hit in exchange 4 → flankers paused → structural dependency established for all three characters through narration that was specific enough for a player mid-combat to catch.
- Dual-function throat signal: delivered as a single unified narration in exchange 5. Both effects (vapor thinning, howl shortening) arrived simultaneously. Marcial A registered both without analytical framing.
- Prevention window: exploited by Marcial A in exchange 5 through instinct — the desire to prevent another double-hit drove the declaration without any specialization prompting it.
- Pack Resonance drop: Marcial B noticed lighter hits after throat damage in exchange 5 — registered as a comparative sensation, not a formal observation.

**What the group did not learn:**
The full structural architecture — that the throat is dual-function by design, that Pack Resonance and the howl share the same source — was not formally named by any player. They operated with the correct tactical response (suppress the Alpha, exploit the prevention window, position to reduce drive geometry) without fully understanding why it worked. This is appropriate for a martial composition.

**Which structural element was identified first:**
The Alpha as the howl-trigger for the Coordinated Drive — identified by Marcial A through consequence in exchange 3. The pack hesitation signal in exchange 4 extended this to: hitting the Alpha changes the flankers' behavior (structural dependency).

**Which structural element was neutralized first:**
Pack Resonance — dropped when throat was first hit in exchange 5. The Coordinated Drive trigger was partially neutralized by exchange 6 (degraded howl), and eliminated with the Alpha in exchange 7.

**Was the Coordinated Drive prevention window exploited:**
Yes — once, in exchange 5. Exploited through instinct (prevent another double-hit), not through analytical understanding of the prevention mechanic. The mechanical anchor (declared interrupt against the Alpha) functioned as intended: it allowed a martial player to use the window without requiring formal knowledge of the system.

**Comparison to v1 (mixed group):**

- Discovery path: same structural conclusion, different route. Mixed group: Táctico identified the Alpha by exchange 1 through behavioral analysis. Martial group: all three characters reached the same conclusion by exchange 4, through consequence, the proactive flanker signal, and the pack hesitation narration.
- Fatigue timing: Marcial A reached Fatigue 1 one exchange later (exchange 6 vs. exchange 5 in v1) — because the prevention window was exploited in exchange 5, reducing the second Coordinated Drive to a partial execution. The attrition cost was higher than in the mixed group (full drive landed in exchange 3) but not prohibitively higher.
- Prevention window: exploited once in the martial run (instinctive), twice in the mixed run (once instinctive after Táctico named the signal, once deliberate). The mechanical anchor enabled martial exploitation — without it, the window would likely not have been used.
- Structural collapse signal: Alpha death in exchange 7 produced an immediate, legible collapse. Functioned as designed.

**Designer notes:**

**1. The prevention window is accessible to martial players — but requires the Coordinated Drive to fire first.** The mechanic was successfully exploited in exchange 5, but only because Marcial A had already experienced the drive in exchange 3. A martial player's use of the window is reactive, not predictive: they prevent the second execution, not the first. This is the correct calibration for a martial group — the drive has to teach the window. For live play, the Narrator should ensure the first drive landing does not put a character below functional threshold. If the drive lands and incapacitates the targeted character before exchange 4, the group loses the structural discovery phase.

**2. The pack hesitation signal must be delivered with enough specificity for a combatant to catch mid-action.** The key test was whether Marcial A, actively fighting the Alpha, could register the flanker hesitation. The narration used — *"The two smaller ones go quiet — not injured, not retreating. Just quiet for a beat"* — passed the test. Narrators should be given this specific phrasing or its equivalent, not just instructed to "describe pack hesitation." Vague hesitation narration will be filtered out by players in combat.

**3. Dual-function throat signal works without specialization — confirmed.** The unification of vapor thinning and howl degradation into a single narration produced recognition in Marcial A without any analytical scaffolding. The one-sentence dual-effect description is the right format. Two separate descriptions in two turns would have lost the connection. Keep this as a hard Narrator instruction: both effects, same sentence, first throat damage.

**4. The encounter took one exchange longer than the mixed group run.** Exchange 7 vs. exchange 6 for structural collapse. The additional exchange came from the Alpha not being identified until exchange 3 (after the drive fired), versus exchange 1–2 for the mixed group. This is expected and acceptable — a martial group pays an extra exchange of attrition in exchange for reaching the same structural conclusion. The total attrition cost for Marcial A was higher, but the encounter was resolved within sustainable limits.

**5. No analytical scaffolding was needed for the core discovery path.** The encounter validated: proactive signal delivery (flanker glancing) + consequence attribution (howl → drive) + impact feedback (pack hesitation) is a sufficient discovery chain for a group with no reading specializations. This is the strongest confirmation of the friction path's validity.
