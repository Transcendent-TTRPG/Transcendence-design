## 4. Enemy Summary
**Name:** Ice Wolf

**Brief description:**
A large predatory wolf with pale grey-white fur, exhaling cold vapor with each breath. Its eyes are pale and fixed. It moves in short explosive bursts rather than a sustained lope.

**Primary threat:**
- Bite: high damage, close range, can inflict a status effect (frozen jaw, bleeding — TBD). **Before each bite, a brief movement is visible in the wolf's throat — as if drawing cold upward through the neck before the jaws close.** This gesture is the primary friction signal linking the bite to the throat as its source, independent of the breath weapon.
- Aggressive movement: closes distance fast, can reposition after attacking, hard to keep at range
- Icy breath: cold area emanating from the mouth/throat, slows or applies a cold condition — links directly to the bite's range

## 5. Relevant Enemy Traits

### Trait 1 — Aggressive Rush
**What it does:**
The wolf closes distance rapidly and attacks in the same action. Can move through or past a character's position.

**How it activates or is maintained:**
Activates when a target is outside immediate reach. Likely every turn unless the wolf is already in bite range.

**How it can be detected:**
The wolf lowers its head and shifts weight to its haunches before charging. Observable before the action resolves.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
The group can brace, reposition, or time a reaction. Removes the wolf's positioning advantage.

### Trait 2 — Icy Breath
**What it does:**
Exhales a short cone of freezing air from the mouth/throat. Applies a cold condition (slow, reduced action, or stacking attrition — TBD) to targets in range.

**How it activates or is maintained:**
Linked to the mouth/throat. Cannot be used if the throat is significantly damaged. **The Narrator deploys this with narrative intent — to establish fear, to punish clustering, to demonstrate the threat before the group has spread. The friction path to the throat does not require the breath to have been used first.**

**How it can be detected:**
Visible vapor increases before the wolf opens its jaws wide. Players who have seen the breath once can anticipate it.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
Neutralizing the throat (vital point 1) disables this trait entirely. Severely reduces the wolf's threat ceiling.

## 6. Vital Points or Significant Parts

### Vital Point 1 — Mouth / Throat
**Related to:**
Both bite and icy breath. This is the wolf's primary offensive source.

**Primary friction signal (independent of breath):**
Before each bite, there is a brief visible movement in the wolf's throat — a muscular contraction, a pulse of cold vapor concentrating in the neck just before the jaws close. This gesture is observable at close range and makes the throat's role in the bite perceptible without requiring the breath weapon to have been demonstrated. A character who has only seen the bite can still form the hypothesis "something in the throat is the source."

**What happens if damaged superficially:**
Bite becomes less precise — the wolf still uses it but with reduced effectiveness. Breath is weaker or shorter range. **The wolf reacts with an immediate involuntary behavioral response: head recoil, a short dry cough, visible tremor in the neck.** This response is unambiguous even for a player in direct melee combat who is not observing from distance.

**What happens if damaged effectively:**
Bite applies no secondary effect. Breath is heavily reduced or requires a recovery before reuse.

**What happens if broken or neutralized:**
Bite becomes a basic strike with no special properties. Icy breath is disabled entirely. The wolf shifts to pure physical aggression (rush + body slam TBD).

**What clues does this zone give:**
- [ ] Throat movement visible before each bite — observable at close range, independent of breath weapon
- [ ] Breath visible from mouth — players notice the cold vapor concentrates there when the breath is used
- [ ] Bite leaves cold residue on wounds — indicates the mouth is the source of the cold element
- [ ] First superficial hit produces immediate involuntary behavioral response — unambiguous even in direct combat

### Vital Point 2 — Legs / Hindquarters
**Related to:**
Aggressive rush and repositioning. Removing mobility changes the wolf's tactical role.

**What happens if damaged superficially:**
Rush covers less distance. Wolf cannot pass through a character's position.

**What happens if damaged effectively:**
Wolf loses ability to reposition after attacking. Becomes stationary for that turn.

**What happens if broken or neutralized:**
Rush is disabled. Wolf can only attack targets adjacent to it. Becomes significantly more readable and containable.

**What clues does this zone give:**
- [ ] Limping visible in movement — telegraphs that leg damage is accumulating
- [ ] Rush becomes shorter range — players can observe the degradation over turns

## 7. Enemy Reading Paths

### 7.1 Friction-Based Reading
What can any character learn by interacting with the creature, even without highly developed specializations?

**Clues from the bite (primary independent path):**
- [ ] Before each bite, a visible movement in the throat — muscular contraction or concentrated cold vapor — suggests the cold is drawn from the throat before delivery through the jaws
- [ ] Bite leaves cold residue in the wound — the cold entered through the jaws, not from the wolf's body generally
- [ ] These two signals together are sufficient to form the hypothesis "the throat is the source" without the breath weapon having been used

**Clues from impact:**
- [ ] First superficial hit to the throat produces an involuntary behavioral response (head recoil, dry cough, neck tremor) — clearly different from body hits, readable by a player in direct combat
- [ ] Attacks on the legs produce visible limp after sufficient damage
- [ ] Hitting the body without targeting a vital point produces no behavioral change — the negative signal that something specific matters

**Clues from observing behavior:**
- [ ] The wolf always telegraphs the rush with a posture shift
- [ ] It orients its head toward targets before using breath — players can learn to read this
- [ ] After breath is used, there is a visible recovery pause — observable cooldown

**Clues from receiving or witnessing enemy abilities:**
- [ ] First time icy breath hits — players learn it comes from the mouth, reinforcing the throat as source
- [ ] Seeing the breath used on another character establishes range and source
- [ ] Cold residue on bite wounds connects to the breath cold — same origin

### 7.2 Deliberate Reading

#### Specialization — [Nature / Bestiary Lore — TBD]
**What it can reveal:**
First reading (partial): Cold predators of this type concentrate thermal energy in the head/neck region. The source is not the body generally — it is somewhere in the throat or upper chest.

**What it cannot reveal on first reading:**
The full functional confirmation — that the throat is the unified point for both bite and breath simultaneously — requires either prior observational data (having seen or felt both abilities) or a deeper investment in the reading. A first reading gives direction, not confirmation.

**Advantage over friction-based reading:**
Skips 1–2 turns of trial and error. Group can focus the throat area intentionally from the start rather than discovering it through damage and comparison.

#### Specialization — [Combat Observation / Perception — TBD]
**What it can reveal:**
The wolf's posture shift before rush and head orientation before breath become readable as distinct telegraphed actions — not just "the wolf is moving."

**Advantage over friction-based reading:**
Allows reaction-window exploitation. Characters can time defenses or interrupts to the telegraph rather than reacting after the fact.

## 9. Attrition Pressure

**Actions that should feel costly here:**
- [ ] Taking the icy breath without repositioning — cold condition should accumulate
- [ ] Staying clustered in breath range across multiple turns
- [ ] Ignoring the rush telegraph and taking full impact repeatedly

**Actions that should not feel too costly here:**
- [ ] Attempting to read the wolf — observation should not feel like a wasted action
- [ ] Repositioning — movement should feel like a real option, not a trap

**Dominant attrition type in this encounter:**
- [x] physical
- [ ] cognitive
- [ ] emotional / social
- [x] mixed — cold environment adds a secondary layer that must be felt narratively even when no direct damage lands

**What should trigger the first Fatigue in this combat:**
After the group has identified the throat as the vital point and has begun acting on that knowledge — not simultaneously with the discovery, and not before it. Fatigue 1 should arrive while the wolf is still dangerous but after the group has at least one exchange of operating with the throat as their explicit target.

## 10. Expected Combat Phases

### Phase 1 — Approach and Assessment
**What happens here:**
The wolf appears and closes distance. First rush. Players encounter the bite and may observe the throat gesture for the first time. The ambient cold is described even if no wolf ability lands.

**What players should perceive:**
The wolf's bite involves something in the throat before the jaws close. The cold element is localized — not from the whole body.

**What decisions should they begin making:**
Do we spread out? Do we try to keep distance? Where do we hit it? What was that movement in its neck?

### Phase 2 — Engagement and Pattern Recognition
**What happens here:**
The wolf has been engaged in close combat. Bite residue and/or throat gesture have been observed by at least one player. The Narrator may or may not have deployed the breath — the throat hypothesis is formable either way.

**What changes from the previous phase:**
Players now have friction data sufficient to form a hypothesis about the vital point without requiring the breath to have been demonstrated. First Fatigue likely appears here or at the opening of Phase 3.

**What should become clear to the group:**
Hitting the throat changes the wolf's behavior immediately and unambiguously. The connection between the throat gesture before the bite and the throat as the vital point becomes explicit.

### Phase 3 — Exploitation or Attrition
**What happens here:**
The throat is significantly damaged or the group is managing cold attrition. The wolf enters its erratic phase — movement becomes shorter and more direct, less telegraph. **The Narrator should connect this shift explicitly to the throat damage in their description, so it reads as consequence rather than arbitrary difficulty.**

**What should be at stake now:**
Can the group finish the wolf before cold attrition forces a harder choice? The erratic phase should feel like the wolf responding to being hurt, not like a new enemy.

## 11. Narrative Signals for the Narrator

> **Independence note:** Each signal below should function independently of which other abilities have been shown. A Narrator who has not yet used the breath can still give meaningful clues through the bite gesture and throat-hit response. A Narrator who leads with the breath early can let the icy breath establish the source and let the bite reinforce it. The signals are not a required chain — they are parallel paths that converge on the same answer. Deploy abilities with narrative intent without fear of breaking the friction path.

### On the bite (every use)
- [ ] Just before the jaws close, there is a visible movement in the wolf's throat — a brief muscular contraction, a pulse of cold vapor concentrating in the neck. Observable at close range.
- [ ] After the bite, cold residue is visible in the wound — the cold entered through the jaws, not from the body.

### If the group attacks poorly (body, no vital points)
- [ ] The wolf shakes off hits without changing behavior
- [ ] "It keeps moving exactly as it did before — nothing seems to slow it"
- [ ] Cold vapor from its breath remains dense and consistent

### If the group hits the throat area but insufficiently
- [ ] The wolf recoils its head involuntarily — a brief, sharp movement it did not choose
- [ ] A short dry sound in the throat, different from anything it has made before
- [ ] The vapor of its next exhale is noticeably thinner for a moment
- [ ] "Something in that hit landed differently — the wolf felt it"

### If the group finds the throat effectively
- [ ] The wolf opens its jaw but the breath produces only a weak cold gust
- [ ] Its bite loses the freezing residue — just teeth now
- [ ] "It's still dangerous — but that cold is gone"

### If the enemy changes behavior (injured, desperate)
- [ ] The wolf's movement becomes shorter and more direct — the wide repositioning arcs disappear
- [ ] **The Narrator should name the cause:** "Something in that hit changed it — it stopped circling, it just comes straight now"
- [ ] It focuses on the character who damaged its throat
- [ ] The telegraph for rush becomes less clean — harder to read than before

### On the ambient cold (use in any exchange where no direct damage lands)
- [ ] Extremities grow numb — fingers, toes, the tips of ears
- [ ] Breath shortens and becomes visible even between combat exchanges
- [ ] The ground pulls warmth upward through boots and soles
- [ ] "The cold here is not passive — it works on you whether the wolf touches you or not"

## 12. What Should Happen if the Group Plays "Without Analyzing"
- [ ] The throat gesture before each bite is visible at close range — after 1–2 bites a player in melee range can form the hypothesis without deliberate analysis
- [ ] The involuntary behavioral response at first throat contact is immediate — they learn through result of impact, not through intent
- [ ] Body hits producing no change push trial-and-error toward different zones
- [ ] Cold attrition builds naturally — even without understanding the system, the pressure is felt

---

## 13. What Should Happen if the Group Does Analyze
- [ ] They identify the throat gesture before the first bite and can form the hypothesis in Phase 1
- [ ] They can use the rush telegraph to time a reaction or interrupt
- [ ] Cold attrition is managed more efficiently — they spread, minimize breath exposure, reduce total damage taken
- [ ] The Specialist's partial reading in Phase 1 accelerates focus on the neck/throat region without confirming the full mechanism

---

## 14. Playtest Success Criteria
The encounter works well if:

- [ ] At least one character targets the throat before the end of Phase 2, without being told to — through the bite gesture, impact result, or partial specialization reading
- [ ] A martial-heavy group still converges on the throat at or before Fatigue 1 — not after
- [ ] Icy breath creates a tactical positioning problem, not just a damage number — whether or not it was deployed early
- [ ] First Fatigue arrives after the wolf's logic is understood, not before
- [ ] The group feels the wolf was readable in retrospect, even if they struggled during
- [ ] The Narrator could have withheld the breath for the first 3 exchanges and the throat would still have been findable

## 15. Poor Calibration Signals

### Too opaque
- [ ] Group reaches Phase 3 without anyone targeting the throat
- [ ] The bite gesture is too subtle — players see the wolf bite but don't register the throat movement
- [ ] The throat-hit response is not clearly different from a body-hit response

### Too obvious
- [ ] Group targets throat on turn 1 without any friction
- [ ] Vital point is neutralized before Phase 2

### Too costly
- [ ] First Fatigue arrives before the group has any friction data from the bite gesture
- [ ] Cold condition stacks faster than the group can respond even with correct positioning

### Too flat
- [ ] Wolf is killed by raw damage before any phase transition
- [ ] Throat damage produces no visible behavioral change players can act on
- [ ] The erratic phase shift feels like a difficulty spike rather than a consequence

### Narrator dependency (new in v2)
- [ ] The throat was only found because the Narrator happened to use the breath early — if the breath had been withheld, the friction path would have stalled
- [ ] The Narrator felt forced to deploy abilities in a specific order to keep the encounter learnable
