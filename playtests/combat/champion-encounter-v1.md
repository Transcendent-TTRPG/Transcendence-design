## 1. Enemy Role

**Category:** Champion

**Function within the system:**
First calibration reference for a Champion-category encounter. The Frost Pack Alpha leads two Ice Wolf Pack Members as a coordinated combat unit. The encounter is designed to test whether a group structure — not just a single enemy with vital points — can be read through friction and deliberate observation.

The Alpha's danger does not come primarily from its individual capability (which, in isolation, resembles an enhanced Common enemy). It comes from the coordination structure it enables: a team maneuver that outpaces individual response, a shared trait that amplifies the whole group, and a behavioral dependency that makes each pack member more dangerous in proximity to the Alpha than away from it.

**What this encounter should teach or put to the test:**

- [ ] Whether the Alpha can be identified as the structural center through friction alone (no specialization)
- [ ] Whether the team maneuver (Coordinated Drive) is readable before or after it first fires — and whether understanding it allows the group to prevent future executions
- [ ] Whether suppressing the Alpha produces a visible, unambiguous change in pack coordination
- [ ] Whether specializations accelerate the structural reading without eliminating the discovery phase
- [ ] Whether distributed attrition (multiple attackers) produces correctly paced Fatigue relative to discovery
- [ ] Whether the encounter resolves through structural disruption rather than raw damage accumulation

---

## 2. Playtest Objective

This encounter exists to verify whether:

- [ ] the Alpha's coordinating role is identifiable through behavioral observation before the team maneuver fires a second time
- [ ] the Coordinated Drive telegraphs are independently readable (Alpha throat contraction → howl onset) even before the maneuver executes
- [ ] suppressing the Alpha mid-howl prevents the maneuver — and whether this prevention is discoverable by the group
- [ ] pack behavior visibly degrades when the Alpha is significantly damaged
- [ ] Fatigue 1 arrives after the group understands the coordination structure but before they have fully exploited it
- [ ] the encounter has a recognizable structural collapse phase (flankers become independent Common enemies) that feels distinct from the coherent phase
- [ ] a group without a Tactical Specialist can still identify the formation as the threat vector, not individual wolves

## 3. Encounter Context

**Location:**
Dense treeline at the edge of frozen tundra. Irregular ground: rocky outcroppings, scattered low cover, limited sightlines. The terrain favors the pack — wolves can use the irregular ground to approach from unexpected angles. Some trees provide partial blocking but not reliable cover.

**Environmental conditions:**

- **Ambient Cold (Cold I):** Same as common Ice Wolf encounter — all characters begin at Cold I from the environment. The attrition baseline is present before the first attack.
- **Ice underfoot (select areas):** The open ground near the treeline edge has ice patches. AGI C.R. difficulty 10 when moving quickly across them. Failure: movement halved or character knocked prone.
- **Restricted sightlines:** Characters cannot see all three wolves simultaneously from most positions. The Narrator should describe flankers entering view separately. This makes the coordinated threat less legible at first contact.

**What players know before the combat:**

- [ ] There is a wolf-creature in the area — reports describe something that "coordinates" the attacks
- [ ] Supply routes have been hit multiple times, always from at least two directions simultaneously
- [ ] Previous attacks left frozen bite marks consistent with the known Ice Wolf pattern

**What they do not yet know:**

- [ ] The Alpha is structurally central — without it, the flankers revert to individual predator behavior
- [ ] The Coordinated Drive maneuver — simultaneous flank from both pack members — is triggered by the Alpha's howl
- [ ] The Alpha's throat is both the source of its own ice breath and the mechanism of the pack howl
- [ ] Pack members attack faster and deal slightly more damage while the Alpha is actively directing (Pack Resonance trait)
- [ ] The Alpha will not retreat easily, even when damaged — it will sacrifice positioning to maintain coordinating function

## 4. Enemy Summary

### The Frost Pack Alpha

**Brief description:**
Significantly larger than a standard Ice Wolf — almost half again the mass. Its fur is darker gray-white with visible scarring across the shoulder and neck, suggesting prior territorial combat. Its movements are deliberate rather than explosive: it positions itself, watches, and acts with visible intent. The eyes have a quality that distinguishes it from a common predator — attention, not just hunger.

**Primary individual threat:**

- **Bite:** Same structure as Ice Wolf — throat contraction gesture, physical + Cold I application, recovers cold vapor when throat is undamaged
- **Icy Breath (stronger):** Larger cone, applies Cold II to direct hits (vs. Cold I for common wolf). Short recovery pause after use.
- **Repositioning:** The Alpha moves to maintain coordination position — it does not rush characters directly unless threatened. Its movement choices are tactical (maintain line-of-sight to both flankers, stay out of immediate retaliation range).

**Coordination threat:**

- **Pack Resonance (shared trait):** While the Alpha is active and unsilenced, pack members deal +1 damage on successful hits and their rush covers slightly more distance. Discoverable through: noticing that flanker hits feel harder than equivalent hits from the known common Ice Wolf.
- **Coordinated Drive (team maneuver):** Alpha howl triggers simultaneous rush from both flankers toward a single designated target. The target is flanked from two sides in the same exchange. Prevention: suppress or stagger the Alpha before the howl resolves; or position the group so no single character can be reached simultaneously from two angles.

**Combat style:**

- [ ] aggressive
- [x] territorial
- [ ] ambusher
- [x] controller
- [ ] adaptive
- [x] coordinator
- [ ] other: [ ]

**Pressure it exerts on the group:**

- [ ] Forces the group to divide attention between three targets
- [ ] Pack Resonance means damage output is suppressed if the Alpha is not prioritized — but attacking the Alpha directly exposes the attacker to the flankers
- [ ] Coordinated Drive can incapacitate one character per maneuver use if not prevented
- [ ] Restricted sightlines mean the full formation may not be legible immediately

---

### Ice Wolf Pack Members (×2)

**Brief description:**
Standard Ice Wolf profile — same as the Common-tier case study. Functionally familiar to any group that has encountered a common Ice Wolf. The difference is behavioral: under the Alpha's direction, their movements are coordinated, not individually motivated.

**Individual threat:**
Identical to common Ice Wolf (v3 values). Bite, Aggressive Rush, Cold I on hit.

**Coordinated behavior:**

- Flankers glance toward the Alpha before repositioning — a visible behavioral signal
- When the Alpha designates a target, flankers shift orientation toward it within the same exchange
- After the Coordinated Drive fires, flankers return to flanking positions relative to the Alpha's position, not relative to their last attack position

**When coordination collapses:**
After the Alpha is significantly damaged (throat substantially damaged or Alpha reaches its pressure threshold), flankers revert to independent predator behavior — each attacks the nearest available target without coordination. This makes them functionally equivalent to Common enemies.

## 5. Relevant Traits

### Alpha Trait 1 — Pack Resonance
**What it does:**
While the Alpha is active (unsilenced, unbroken), all pack members within proximity deal +1 damage on successful hits and their Aggressive Rush covers increased distance.

**How it activates or is maintained:**
Passive while the Alpha is functional. Does not require an action. Drops when the Alpha's throat is substantially damaged (equivalent to throat box 2 of 3 filled — same scale as common Ice Wolf).

**How it can be detected:**
Comparative damage observation. A character who has encountered a common Ice Wolf and taken hits from a pack member in this encounter may notice the pack member hits harder than expected. A Tactical character tracking hit-by-hit results may catch the discrepancy. Alternately: if the Alpha is staggered mid-fight and a flanker hits in the same exchange, a perceptive player can compare the result to prior hits and note the difference.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
The group can reduce pack damage output by suppressing the Alpha specifically. This also gives the Alpha neutralization additional tactical meaning: not just disabling the maneuver trigger, but also removing the passive damage bonus.

---

### Alpha Trait 2 — Coordinated Drive
**What it does:**
Both flankers simultaneously rush and attack a single designated target. The target is struck from two directions in the same exchange. The defending character must respond to two simultaneous incoming attacks — harder to avoid, harder to block fully.

**How it activates:**
Alpha performs a partial howl — a visible throat contraction, similar to the common Ice Wolf's bite gesture, but deeper and held longer. The sound carries approximately one beat before the flankers move. This observable window is the prevention opportunity.

**How it can be detected:**
The throat contraction is visible before the sound resolves. A character watching the Alpha (not necessarily in combat with it) can observe the gesture. The first time the maneuver fires, it may not be anticipated — but after one execution, the throat contraction is a learnable signal. The maneuver is reactive-readable, not just retrospectively visible.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
The group can interrupt the Alpha before the howl resolves. Suppressing the Alpha — staggering, applying a significant hit, or applying a condition that disrupts the action — cancels the maneuver. If the group also positions to prevent simultaneous flanking (no single player accessible from two angles), the maneuver fails structurally even if the howl fires.

---

### Alpha Trait 3 — Icy Breath (Alpha-grade)
**What it does:**
Same structure as common Ice Wolf breath, but larger cone and Cold II application on direct hit (Cold I on edge). Stronger individual threat than the pack members.

**How it activates:**
Same visible wind-up as common Ice Wolf. This should feel familiar to any group that has encountered a common wolf — same signal, stronger effect.

**What happens if the throat is damaged:**
Same degradation path as common Ice Wolf. Superficial damage weakens the breath. Substantial damage prevents it. Full neutralization silences the howl — and with it, the Coordinated Drive trigger — simultaneously with disabling the breath.

## 6. Structural Reading Units

### The Alpha
**What it is:** The tactical center. Coordinates pack behavior, enables the Coordinated Drive, maintains Pack Resonance.

**What makes it readable through friction:**
- It does not rush immediately — it positions while flankers advance. This contrasts with common wolf behavior.
- Its movements are oriented toward the group as a whole, not toward a single target.
- After the Coordinated Drive fires, flankers return to positions relative to the Alpha, not relative to the target — observable if anyone tracks the flankers' resetting.

**Negative signal:** Attacking a flanker produces damage and behavioral reaction in that flanker, but no change in the Alpha's behavior or the other flanker's coordination. The group's action had tactical cost on one unit, not on the structure.

---

### The Formation
**What it is:** The spatial arrangement — Alpha positioned behind or elevated, flankers spread at angles that allow simultaneous approach to any central target.

**What makes it readable through friction:**
- Flankers maintain the formation even when one is attacked — they reposition to the formation angle, not to a random attack position.
- If the group spreads out significantly, the flankers shift to mirror the spread — maintaining flanking angles rather than clustering.

**Negative signal:** When a flanker is pushed out of formation position (blocked by terrain, repositioned by a character's action), the Coordinated Drive cannot execute correctly — observable as a failed or degraded maneuver.

---

### Pack Resonance (Shared Trait)
**What it is:** The passive damage amplification the Alpha provides to all pack members.

**What makes it readable through friction:**
- Damage comparison across exchanges — hits that feel harder than a known common wolf should produce.
- If the Alpha is staggered and a flanker hits in the same exchange, the hit is noticeably softer — the difference is the trait dropping.

---

### Coordinated Drive Trigger
**What it is:** The specific Alpha action (howl onset) that activates the team maneuver.

**What makes it readable through friction:**
- The throat contraction precedes the howl by one observable beat — same type of visible gesture as the common wolf's bite contraction.
- After the maneuver fires once, the sequence is learnable: throat tightens → sound forms → flankers move.
- The prevention window is the beat between throat contraction and the howl resolving.

---

## 7. Enemy Reading Paths

### 7.1 Friction-Based Reading

**Clues from observing formation and behavior:**

- [ ] The Alpha does not advance directly — it positions at range and watches. A standard predator closes distance. This one does not.
- [ ] Flankers glance toward the Alpha before repositioning. Observable to any character who watches the flankers between their own actions.
- [ ] After the Coordinated Drive fires, flankers do not pursue or scatter — they reset to flanking positions relative to the Alpha. This reveals that the formation is the reference point, not the target.
- [ ] When the Alpha is hit and staggers, flankers momentarily hesitate before acting. This is the key negative signal: the Alpha's state affects the pack's action timing.

**Clues from impact — Alpha:**

- [ ] Hitting the Alpha produces behavioral consequences in the flankers (hesitation, slower response), not just individual damage to the Alpha. A character who hits the Alpha and watches the flankers' next action can observe the connection.
- [ ] Throat hits on the Alpha produce degraded howl attempts — same sensory signals as the common wolf's throat degradation (rougher sound, less vapor).

**Clues from impact — flankers:**

- [ ] Hitting a flanker produces standard damage and individual behavioral response, with no observable effect on the Alpha or the other flanker. The group's action affected one unit, not the structure.
- [ ] When Pack Resonance is active, flanker hits feel slightly harder than equivalent common wolf hits. If anyone has prior common wolf experience, this discrepancy is noticeable.

**Clues from the Coordinated Drive:**

- [ ] First execution: may be unexpected. But the throat contraction preceding the howl is visible to any character watching the Alpha at that moment.
- [ ] Second execution attempt: a character who watched the first knows what throat contraction means. The prevention window is now identifiable.

### 7.2 Deliberate Reading

#### Specialization — Bestiary Lore (applied to pack structure, not individual anatomy)

**L1 — Structural Direction**
*(S.R. difficulty 18, no observational prerequisites)*

Result: *"Pack predators of this size operate under a dominant coordination structure. The large one is not just the strongest — it is regulating the group's behavior. Disrupting it should change how the others act."*

No mechanism identified. Direction only: target the Alpha to change group behavior.

**L2 — Functional Role Identification**
*(S.R. difficulty 14, requires: having observed the flankers' coordinated repositioning or one Coordinated Drive execution)*

Result: *"The flankers are not acting independently — they're reading the Alpha's signals. The howl is a coordination command, not just a sound. That gesture in the throat before the howl is the signal onset — there's a window before it resolves."*

Mechanism identified for the maneuver trigger. Prevention window now explicit. Pack Resonance not yet confirmed.

**L3 — Full Structural Confirmation**
*(S.R. difficulty 10, requires: Coordinated Drive observed at least once AND Alpha throat damage observed at least once — unavailable otherwise regardless of roll result)*

Result: *"The Alpha's throat is dual-function: it carries both the individual breath weapon and the pack howl. Damaging it degrades both simultaneously. When the throat goes quiet, the flankers lose the coordination signal — they revert to individual behavior. The damage bonus the pack has been generating is the same mechanism: the howl keeps them synchronized."*

Full architecture confirmed. Available only after both relevant systems have been observable in play.

---

#### Specialization — Combat Tactics / Formation Reading

**L1 — Pattern Recognition**
*(Difficulty equivalent to Bestiary L1, no observational prerequisites)*

Result: *"This group is using a formation — the large one is holding a command position. The flankers are anchored to its location, not to their own targets."*

**L2 — Maneuver Identification**
*(Requires: having observed one Coordinated Drive or having watched the Alpha reposition and the flankers adjust accordingly)*

Result: *"They use the formation to set up simultaneous flank attacks. The trigger is the Alpha. If we deny any single character from being reachable from two angles, the maneuver has nowhere to fire — even if the howl goes through."*

Structural prevention strategy identified without requiring Alpha elimination.

## 9. Attrition Pressure

**Actions that should feel costly:**

- [ ] Ignoring the Alpha while flankers accumulate attrition — Pack Resonance means this compounds
- [ ] Being caught by the Coordinated Drive without positioning to reduce the double-flank exposure
- [ ] Clustering in the Alpha's breath range
- [ ] Remaining in formation range without disrupting the Alpha's coordinating function

**Actions that should not feel too costly:**

- [ ] Repositioning to deny flanking angles — movement should feel rewarded
- [ ] Targeting the Alpha instead of a flanker — should feel like an effective choice once the structure is understood
- [ ] Attempting to watch and read the formation — observation should not be punished

**Dominant attrition type:**

- [x] physical — distributed across three attackers
- [ ] cognitive
- [ ] mixed

**Specific pressure from this encounter:**
The distributed action economy means the group is absorbing hits from multiple sources even when managing correctly. The Coordinated Drive produces a spike — one character takes concentrated damage in a single exchange. The attrition floor is higher than in a single Common enemy encounter, but the ceiling per exchange should not be higher unless the Coordinated Drive lands successfully and repeatedly.

**What should trigger the first Fatigue:**
After the group has observed the Coordinated Drive at least once and has formed a hypothesis about the Alpha's coordinating role — but before they have successfully suppressed the Alpha and prevented a second drive. Fatigue should arrive when the structure is understood but not yet disrupted.

## 10. Expected Combat Phases

### Phase 1 — Contact and Formation Assessment
**What happens:**
The pack enters. Flankers advance; the Alpha holds position. The group encounters what looks like an asymmetric common encounter — three wolves, one large. First attacks happen. The Coordinated Drive has not fired yet.

**What players should perceive:**
The large wolf is not attacking. The flankers are moving in a way that feels different from independent predator behavior — they reposition rather than pursue freely.

**What decisions they begin making:**
Do we split attention? Do we focus one wolf? Do we try to keep the large one in view?

---

### Phase 2 — First Maneuver and Structural Discovery
**What happens:**
The Coordinated Drive fires for the first time. One character takes significant double-flank damage. The throat contraction preceding the howl was visible to anyone watching the Alpha. The group now has enough friction data to form a structural hypothesis.

**What changes from the previous phase:**
The encounter is not three wolves — it is a coordinated system with a trigger. The Alpha is the trigger source. First Fatigue is approaching for the most-engaged characters.

**What should become clear:**
Targeting the Alpha changes the group's threat calculus. Preventing the maneuver requires either suppressing the Alpha before the howl or denying the flanking geometry.

---

### Phase 3 — Exploitation and Structural Degradation
**What happens:**
The group has targeted the Alpha. Throat damage is accumulating. Howl attempts produce degraded results — partial drives, single-flanker responses, or no flanker response. Pack Resonance has dropped. Flankers begin acting as independent Common enemies.

**What should be at stake:**
Can the group finish the Alpha (or neutralize its coordination entirely) before attrition from three attackers forces the hardest choices? The flankers are now manageable individually — but they are still active and still accumulating damage.

---

### Phase 4 — Structural Collapse and Resolution
**What happens:**
The Alpha is neutralized (silenced or eliminated). Flankers are operating fully independently. The threat profile has returned to a double-common-enemy encounter.

**Design intent:**
This phase should feel like a recognizable shift — not a sudden trivialization, but a visible reduction in threat architecture. The group should be able to name what changed: "the coordinated attacks stopped." This is the payoff of the structural reading.

## 11. Narrative Signals for the Narrator

The flanker repositioning signal must work even if the Coordinated Drive has never fired. The Alpha throat degradation signal must work even if Pack Resonance has never been explicitly felt. Signals are parallel discovery paths, not a chain.

- [ ] If Pack Resonance is active: flanker hits are landing slightly heavier than the group might expect from what looks like a common wolf

- [ ] "The large wolf's throat tightens — a deep vibration, not yet a sound — something is forming"
- [ ] Any character watching the Alpha at this moment can register this
- [ ] This is the prevention window — the howl has not resolved

- [ ] Both flankers move simultaneously toward the same character — from two different angles
- [ ] "They move at the same instant, from opposite sides — this was not coincidence"
- [ ] The targeted character faces two incoming attacks in the same exchange

- [ ] The flankers noticeably hesitate — their action timing slips
- [ ] "The two smaller ones seem to wait — just for a beat — as if listening for something that did not come"

## 12. What Should Happen if the Group Plays "Without Analyzing"

- [ ] The Coordinated Drive fires in Phase 2 and damages someone significantly — the group feels the threat even without understanding its architecture
- [ ] Continued flanker focus produces ongoing attrition without structural progress — eventually someone shifts to the Alpha by trial and error or intuition
- [ ] Hitting the Alpha produces observable pack hesitation — the group learns through result, not intent
- [ ] Pack Resonance may go unnoticed explicitly, but it contributes to the higher-than-expected attrition that pushes the group toward Fatigue

## 13. What Should Happen if the Group Does Analyze

- [ ] The formation is identified as coordinated before the Coordinated Drive fires, through the flanker glancing behavior and Alpha positioning
- [ ] The prevention window for the drive is recognized at first execution and exploited at the second attempt
- [ ] Suppressing the Alpha shifts the encounter into Phase 3 efficiently — Pack Resonance drops, pack hesitation becomes consistent
- [ ] Attrition is meaningfully lower because the Coordinated Drive lands once instead of three times

## 14. Playtest Success Criteria

The encounter works well if:

- [ ] The Alpha is identified as the structural center by at least one player before the Coordinated Drive fires a second time — without being told
- [ ] The prevention of the Coordinated Drive is attempted at least once after the first execution, using the visible throat contraction as the signal
- [ ] Suppressing the Alpha produces a visible, narratable change in pack behavior that the group can act on
- [ ] Fatigue 1 arrives before the coordination structure has been fully disrupted
- [ ] Phase 4 (structural collapse) feels distinctly different from Phase 2 (intact coordination) — the group can name what changed

## 15. Poor Calibration Signals

### Too opaque

- [ ] Group reaches Phase 3 without anyone identifying the Alpha as the structural center
- [ ] Coordinated Drive fires three or more times without the group recognizing the trigger
- [ ] Flanker glancing behavior goes unnoticed across all exchanges — signal is too subtle

### Too obvious

- [ ] Group correctly identifies the maneuver trigger before it fires once (pure anticipation, no friction)
- [ ] Alpha is targeted and suppressed before the Coordinated Drive fires — pack coordination never demonstrated
- [ ] Structural collapse happens too early for Phase 2 to have established meaningful threat

### Too costly

- [ ] Coordinated Drive lands three or more times before the group understands it — character incapacitated before structure is readable
- [ ] Fatigue 1 arrives before the maneuver has fired once

### Too flat

- [ ] Alpha is eliminated but pack behavior does not visibly change — structural collapse signal fails
- [ ] Pack Resonance drop produces no perceptible difference in flanker damage output — trait is invisible
- [ ] Group defeats all three wolves through raw damage without any structural discovery or coordination disruption

## 16. Playtest Log

**Test group:**
Simulated panel — 4 player archetypes (Marcial, Táctico, Especialista, Conservador). Árbitro role operated as referee throughout. Synthetic diagnostic run designed to evaluate structural readability across different player orientations before running with a live group.

**Number of players:**
4 simulated roles.

**Group state:**
Characters with no prior knowledge of the Frost Pack Alpha. Standard specializations. No informational advantage. One character (Especialista) has Bestiary Lore applicable to predator anatomy and pack behavior. One character (Táctico) has Combat Observation applicable to movement pattern reading. Encounter terrain: dense treeline edge, icy patches in the open ground, restricted sightlines from tree cover.

**Enemy state at encounter start:**
Alpha positioned on a low outcropping behind the flanker line. Flankers advancing from 8 and 10 o'clock relative to the group's entry point. Alpha not yet visible to all characters at the start.

---

### Exchange 1 — Contact and Initial Assessment

**What happened:**

The two flankers became visible first as the group entered the treeline edge. The Alpha came into view a moment later, positioned behind the flankers on elevated ground. The disparity in size and position was immediately apparent.

Marcial advanced on the closer flanker and struck. The flanker yelped, absorbed the hit, and adjusted its position — sliding sideways rather than retreating or pressing forward. Instinctive observation: *"This one didn't try to retaliate, it moved sideways."*

Táctico held position and observed. Registered: the Alpha had not moved despite the attack on the flanker. In a common predator encounter, the dominant animal either joins the fight or retreats. This one continued watching. First structural hypothesis forming: *"It's not attacking because it's directing."*

Especialista attempted a first Bestiary Lore reading at L1 (no observational prerequisites). Result: *"Pack predators of this scale operate with a hierarchical coordination structure. The large one is not just dominant — it's actively regulating the group. Targeting it should change how the others behave."* This is direction only — no mechanism. Valuable as an early filter: don't ignore the large one.

Conservador positioned with lateral distance, avoiding the direct line between both flankers. Already structurally correct — without knowing the maneuver, spacing instinctively reduces flanking vulnerability.

Alpha: did not act offensively. It repositioned slightly — moving to maintain diagonal sight lines to both flankers and to the advancing Marcial. A character watching the Alpha would see its gaze shifting between the flankers and the group, not fixing on a single target.

The flanker that was hit glanced toward the Alpha before resetting its position. Táctico noticed this. *"It looked at the big one before it moved. Not toward me — toward it."*

---

**End of Exchange 1:**
Formation readable by Táctico (behavioral observation). Estructural direction available to Especialista (L1). Marcial has engaged one flanker; Conservador is correctly positioned. No maneuver has fired. No attrition beyond Cold I ambient baseline.

---

### Exchange 2 — Formation Behavior and Second Observation Pass

**What happened:**

Marcial continued pressure on the same flanker, landing a second hit. The flanker was visibly impaired — moving with slightly reduced range. Still functional, but degraded.

Critical observation moment: the other flanker, which Marcial had not touched, repositioned sideways simultaneously. Not toward Marcial — sideways, adjusting angular spread. Táctico tracked this. *"I hit the right one, the left one moved. They're not reacting to me — they're adjusting to each other, or to the big one."*

Especialista observed the Alpha's positioning relative to the flankers and applied the L1 Bestiary reading further. Added inference: the Alpha had shifted its own position in response to Marcial's advance — a small movement that kept it equidistant from both flankers. This is not a common predator's reaction to a threat. It is a coordinator's response to a formation disruption. Especialista communicated this to the group: *"The large one moved to rebalance — it's maintaining position relative to the others, not to us."*

Conservador watched the group geometry carefully. The flankers were now spread at roughly symmetrical angles relative to the group. Táctico and Marcial were in the center zone — reachable from two angles simultaneously if the flankers rushed.

The Alpha's throat tightened. A deep vibration, not yet a sound. The gesture was brief — a muscular pulse in the neck, visible to Táctico who was actively watching the Alpha.

Táctico: *"Something just happened with its throat."* Did not yet understand the mechanism — but the signal was registered.

Both flankers began a synchronized advance.

---

**End of Exchange 2 / Beginning of Exchange 3 — Coordinated Drive fires for the first time.**

---

### Exchange 3 — First Coordinated Drive

**What happened:**

The howl resolved. Both flankers rushed Táctico simultaneously from two angles.

Táctico, who had been watching the Alpha and caught the throat contraction but not yet understood it, was not fully prepared. Took hits from both flankers in the same exchange — first significant damage event. Cold I from the ambient environment was compounding; Cold condition from the bite would escalate to Cold II. Táctico's attrition clock started.

Marcial reacted — moved to intercept one flanker — but the movement was reactive, not preventive. Arrived a beat late.

Conservador, positioned at wider spacing, was not within simultaneous reach. The Coordinated Drive found no viable second target in flanking range — its full geometric effect applied only to Táctico.

Especialista observed the full sequence as it unfolded: Alpha throat contraction → howl → simultaneous flanker movement toward a single target. Connected the Bestiary L1 with what just happened. This is the team maneuver. *"The howl is the trigger. The throat gesture precedes the howl. There was a beat between the gesture and the sound — that's the window."*

Táctico, mid-hit, was still registering what had happened. On recovery: *"They both came at once. That was the big one's signal — the thing it did with its throat."*

The Coordinated Drive had fired. Its architecture was now observable.

---

**End of Exchange 3:**
Táctico has taken the Coordinated Drive hit. First significant damage event. The maneuver has been observed. Especialista has identified the trigger and the prevention window (L2 candidate). Marcial is still engaged with a flanker. Conservador is positioned correctly. Alpha remains undamaged.

---

### Exchange 4 — Hypothesis Formation and Target Shift

**What happened:**

This is the key structural friction signal. The group observed it clearly: Alpha damage → pack hesitation. The dependency is confirmed through consequence, not just through theory.

### Exchange 5 — Alpha Pressure and Partial Howl

## 17. Post-Playtest Adjustments

After the playtest, what should be modified before v2?

- [ ] no adjustments — proceed to mechanical calibration
- [x] narrative signal clarity — flanker glancing signal (exchange 1) is subtle; needs explicit Narrator instruction
- [x] Alpha's own vital point (throat) may be confused with the pack coordination mechanism — both use the throat; the dual-function relationship needs to be clearer in the signal structure

- [ ] Pack Resonance — drop signal worked; no adjustment needed

- [ ] Fatigue timing — arrived at correct window for high-attrition players; no adjustment
- [ ] specialization depth — L1/L2/L3 tiers functioned correctly; no adjustment

**Designer notes:**

**1. Flanker glancing behavior is the best early signal — and the most likely to be missed.**
The signal (flankers look toward the Alpha before repositioning) appeared in exchange 1 and was caught by Conservador. But it requires a character to be watching the flankers *between* their own actions — not during them. In a real session with players managing their own characters, this signal may consistently be missed in the early exchanges when everyone is establishing their positions. Recommendation: give the Narrator an explicit instruction to narrate the glancing behavior as an unprompted description during exchange 1 or 2, not only as a response to a player looking for it. It should be offered before anyone asks.

**2. The dual-function of the Alpha's throat creates a reading ambiguity that needs resolution.**
In the simulation, Especialista correctly understood both functions through L2 and L3 readings. But from a friction perspective, a player who notices throat damage degrading the howl might not immediately connect it to the individual breath weapon — and vice versa. The Narrator needs a clear signal at the moment throat damage first appears that links both: *"the vapor from its breath thins — and the sound it was forming died with it."* One observation, two effects visible simultaneously. This prevents the throat from feeling like it has two separate vital points instead of one dual-function center.

**3. The Coordinated Drive prevention window needs a mechanical anchor, not just a narrative description.**
Currently the prevention window is defined as "the beat between throat contraction and the howl resolving." This is clear in simulation but will be ambiguous in real play: how long is a beat? Can any action interrupt, or only specific ones? Recommendation for v2: define the prevention window as a specific action type — a character who has observed the throat contraction and declares an interrupt against the Alpha before their turn would normally occur may attempt a targeted action (at increased difficulty) to stagger the Alpha. This makes the prevention window a real mechanical opportunity, not just a narrative possibility.

**4. Alpha death = immediate total coordination collapse. Flankers revert to independent predator behavior with no transition state.** *(resolved)*
If the Alpha is killed outright, surviving pack members enter chaos immediately: all coordination bonuses drop, all maneuver triggers become unavailable, and each flanker acts as an independent Common enemy targeting the nearest threat. There is no degraded-but-coherent intermediate state after death — that intermediate state only exists when the Alpha is silenced but still alive (throat substantially damaged). Death skips straight to full collapse. In narrative terms: the pack loses its signal source entirely. Without the Alpha to read, they fall back on individual predator instinct. This must be narrated clearly — the behavioral shift should be immediate and legible, not a gradual fade.

**5. The Conservador's positioning advantage was decisive but invisible.**
The Conservador's wide spacing meant the Coordinated Drive could only reach Táctico in exchange 3 — Conservador was geometrically inaccessible. This was structurally correct behavior, but a player doing this intuitively would not know why it worked. In a real session, this might produce an outcome where one player feels "lucky" rather than feeling like their positioning decision was meaningful. Recommendation: give the Narrator a narration option when the Coordinated Drive can't fully reach a spread character: *"the smaller ones seemed to try to converge on two targets — but the spacing was wrong, there was no angle on the one standing wide."* This makes the correct behavior legible as a decision that mattered, not as an accident of position.
