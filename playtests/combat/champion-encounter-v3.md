First calibration reference for a Champion-category encounter. Validated across two simulated group compositions: mixed and martial-skewed. The structural readability system is confirmed — the Alpha's coordinating role is discoverable through friction alone across both compositions. This version tests whether the proposed mechanical values support the validated structure under real play conditions.

- [ ] Whether the Coordinated Drive damage output is threatening enough to force structural discovery without being high enough to incapacitate the targeted character on first contact

## 3. Encounter Context

**Location:**
Dense treeline at the edge of frozen tundra. Irregular ground, rocky outcroppings, restricted sightlines from tree cover.

**Environmental conditions:**

- **Ambient Cold (Cold I):** Every character begins the encounter at Cold I. The attrition baseline is present before the first attack — same as the common Ice Wolf encounter. This is the environmental floor.
- **Ice underfoot (select areas):** Movement at speed across icy patches requires a Characteristic Roll (AGI, difficulty 10). On failure: movement is halved for that exchange or character is knocked prone (Narrator's choice based on context).
- **Restricted sightlines:** Characters cannot see all three wolves simultaneously from most positions. Flankers enter view separately — the formation's full geometry is not legible at first contact.

**What players know before the combat:**
- [ ] Reports of coordinated attacks on supply routes, always from at least two directions simultaneously
- [ ] Frozen bite marks consistent with known Ice Wolf pattern
- [ ] Something is organizing the attacks — previous encounters suggest more than one wolf

**What they do not yet know:**
- [ ] The Alpha is the structural center — without it, flankers revert to independent behavior
- [ ] The Coordinated Drive is triggered by the Alpha's howl — and the howl onset is a visible, interruptible signal
- [ ] The Alpha's throat is dual-function: carries both the individual ice breath and the pack coordination howl
- [ ] Pack members deal more damage while the Alpha is actively directing (Pack Resonance)
- [ ] Alpha death = immediate coordination collapse, no transition

### The Frost Pack Alpha

**Brief description:**
Significantly larger than a standard Ice Wolf — almost half again the mass. Darker gray-white fur, visible scarring across the shoulder and neck. Movements are deliberate rather than explosive. The eyes have a quality that distinguishes it from a common predator: attention, not just hunger.

**Health pool:** 3d20 *(roll at session start; re-roll each iteration to check if pool size allows sufficient exchanges for structure to be demonstrated)*

**Trait — Bite:**
Attack: 1d10 + 5
Damage on hit: 1d8 physical + Cold condition Level I applied to target
Special: before each bite, a **throat contraction is visible** — a deep muscular pulse in the neck, cold vapor concentrating just below the jaws. Observable at close range. This is a Narrator signal, not a mechanical trigger. It links the bite to the throat as its source, independent of the breath weapon.

**Trait — Icy Breath (Alpha-grade):**
No attack roll — area effect. Larger cone than common Ice Wolf (approximately 1.5× the length of the Alpha, 90-degree arc).
Direct hit: Cold condition Level II applied to each target.
Edge of cone: Cold condition Level I applied.
Recovery: 1 exchange cooldown after use. During recovery, vapor from the Alpha's exhale is visibly thinner.
Telegraph: same wind-up as common Ice Wolf — head orients toward targets, vapor increases, jaws part. Familiar to any character who has encountered a common Ice Wolf.
Narrator note: deploy with narrative intent. The friction path to the throat does not depend on this ability being used at any specific point.

**Trait — Coordinated Drive:**
See dedicated section below.

**Trait — Pack Resonance:**
See dedicated section below.

**Combat style:**
- [ ] aggressive
- [x] territorial
- [ ] ambusher
- [x] controller
- [ ] adaptive *(becomes more direct and less repositioning-focused after throat box 2)*
- [x] coordinator

### Ice Wolf Pack Members (×2)

**Brief description:**
Standard Ice Wolf profile. Functionally identical to the common-tier case study. The behavioral difference is coordination: under the Alpha's direction, their movements are synchronized. After coordination collapse, they revert to independent predator behavior.

**Health pool:** 2d20 each *(same as common Ice Wolf)*

**Trait — Bite:**
Attack: 1d10 + 4
Damage on hit: 1d8 physical + Cold condition Level I applied to target
Same throat contraction gesture as common Ice Wolf — observable at close range.

**Trait — Aggressive Rush:**
Attack: same as Bite.
Covers full distance to target in the same action as a bite. Can pass through or past a character's position. If it passes through: target makes a C.R. (AGI, difficulty 10) or takes the bite as a partial hit (half damage, no Cold application).
Telegraph: head lowered, weight shifts to hindquarters. One beat before resolution — observable to any character watching.

## 5. Pack Resonance

**What it is:**
A passive coordination bonus the Alpha grants to proximate pack members while its coordination structure is intact. Not a trait that needs to be activated — it is the natural result of the group operating under the Alpha's direction.

**Mechanical effect:**
While the Alpha's throat is at 0 or 1 boxes of damage, all flankers deal +1 damage on successful hits.

**Drop threshold:**
Pack Resonance drops when the Alpha's throat reaches box 2 (substantially damaged). It does not return if the Alpha recovers.

**How it degrades:**
At box 1 (superficial throat damage), Pack Resonance is already affected — the howl becomes degraded. At box 2, the howl fails and Pack Resonance drops entirely.

**How it can be detected:**
Comparative damage observation. A character who has taken flanker hits before and after throat damage may notice the hits feel lighter. This may not be analytically registered without a prompt — the Narrator should narrate the lighter hit quality when Pack Resonance drops ("that one landed softer — something about the way the pack moves has changed").

**When it is lost permanently:**
On Alpha death, Pack Resonance disappears in the same exchange. It is a world-state change — it does not require the characters to know it happened. The flankers simply deal standard damage from that point forward.

## 6. Coordinated Drive

**What it is:**
A team maneuver in which both flankers simultaneously rush and attack a single designated target. The simultaneity is what makes it threatening — the targeted character must respond to two incoming attacks in the same exchange.

**Trigger:**
Alpha howl — initiated by throat contraction (visible one beat before the howl resolves).

**Prevention window — mechanical anchor:**
A character who observes the throat contraction and declares a targeted interrupt action against the Alpha before the howl resolves may attempt to cancel the maneuver.

Mechanics:
- Declaring the interrupt counts as the character's action for the exchange
- The action is resolved as a standard targeted strike against the Alpha (no additional difficulty modifier)
- If the action results in a meaningful impact on the Alpha (a hit that would normally produce stagger, pain response, or behavioral disruption): the howl is cancelled and the drive does not fire
- If the action misses or produces insufficient impact: the howl resolves and the drive fires normally

Any character may attempt this — no specialization required. The prerequisite is having observed the throat contraction signal, which is available to any character watching the Alpha when the contraction begins.

**Simultaneous attack resolution:**
When the drive fires, both flankers execute their attack roll (1d10 + 4 each) in the same exchange against the same target. The targeted character makes two defensive responses in the same exchange. If they have already used their primary defensive action earlier in the exchange, at least one of the incoming attacks is at reduced defensive effectiveness.

> *Note: the specific defensive resolution for simultaneous attacks in the same exchange should be confirmed against core combat rules before running. The key structural intent is: two attacks landing simultaneously is harder to fully defend than two attacks on separate exchanges. The exact mechanic follows from how the core system handles stacked incoming actions.*

**Partial drive:**
When the Alpha's throat is at box 1, the howl fires but with reduced reach. Only one flanker responds to the degraded signal — the other hesitates or acts independently. The Coordinated Drive becomes a single-flanker attack in this state.

**Drive unavailability:**
At throat box 2, the howl fails entirely. The Coordinated Drive is unavailable for the remainder of the encounter.
At Alpha death, the Coordinated Drive is permanently unavailable — the trigger source is gone.

## 7. Cold Condition

Same system as common Ice Wolf encounter. Reproduced here for reference.

> *Cold is an elemental alteration with three levels of severity. Multiple applications from different sources escalate the level. Strongest Condition rule applies within the same level.*

### Levels

**Cold I — Leve:**
+1 difficulty to all physical precision actions.
Narrative: *"Your fingers are going numb. The cold is already inside you."*

**Cold II — Moderado:**
+1 difficulty to all physical actions. Cannot sprint or dash.
Narrative: *"The cold has settled into your joints. Moving fast feels like pushing through mud."*

**Cold III — Severo:**
+2 difficulty to all physical actions. Movement reduced to half. Every significant action under Cold III counts as 1.5 actions toward Fatigue threshold.
Narrative: *"Your body is turning on you. Each movement requires a decision."*

### Sources and application

| Source | Cold level applied |
| --- | --- |
| Ambient terrain (at encounter start) | Cold I |
| Flanker or Alpha bite | Cold I |
| Alpha Icy Breath (direct hit) | Cold II |
| Alpha Icy Breath (edge of cone) | Cold I |
| Coordinated Drive (both flankers hit) | Cold I × 2 → escalates to Cold II if character already at Cold I |
| Remaining in breath-affected area 2+ exchanges | Escalate current level by 1 |

### Resistance and recovery

**Resisting Cold application:**
`R.R. = 1d10 + TEN + Elemental Resistance competency level`

| Incoming Cold level | Difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| Cold I | 8 |
| Cold II | 12 |
| Cold III | 16 |

On success: Cold level reduced by one. On failure: full level applies.
Ambient Cold I at encounter start does not allow a resistance roll — it is environmental and unavoidable.

**Recovery:**
One full exchange out of direct combat and away from wolf or breath effects: reduce current Cold level by 1. Recovery from Cold III to Cold II requires two exchanges of rest.

## 8. Alpha Vital Point — Throat (Dual-Function)

**What it is:**
The Alpha's throat is the source of both its individual ice breath and the pack coordination howl. Damage to it degrades both functions simultaneously.

**Damage track:** □ □ □ *(3 boxes)*

**Targeting:**
Declaring a targeted strike at the Alpha's throat costs +2 to the attack difficulty. The throat is a narrow target on a large, intentional animal that positions to minimize exposure.
On success: normal damage applies AND mark one box.

**Dual-function first-damage signal:**
The first time the Alpha's throat takes any damage, the Narrator must describe both effects simultaneously in a single narration — the vapor from the Alpha's breath thinning AND the howl quality degrading in the same beat. These two effects must appear in the same description, not separately across turns. This is the signal that links both functions to one source and must be legible without analytical framing.

**Thresholds:**

*1 box filled — Superficial:*
- Pack Resonance weakens — the coordination bonus is still present but less reliable
- Coordinated Drive becomes a partial drive: only one flanker responds to the howl (the other hesitates)
- Alpha Icy Breath: range reduced to the same as a common Ice Wolf breath
- Alpha Bite: no change yet

Narrator cue (dual-function signal): *"Something changed — the vapor from its breath thinned, and the sound it was building in its chest came out shorter than it should have. One hit. Both things at once."*

*2 boxes filled — Substantial:*
- Pack Resonance drops entirely — flankers return to standard Ice Wolf damage (no bonus)
- Coordinated Drive unavailable — howl fails entirely
- Alpha Icy Breath: weakened — Cold I on direct hit (down from Cold II), no edge application
- Alpha Bite: loses Cold application — physical damage only

Narrator cue: *"The sound it tried to make — nothing came out right. The two wolves looked toward it. Then looked away."*

*3 boxes filled — Neutralized:*
- All coordination functions disabled permanently
- Icy Breath disabled entirely
- Alpha Bite is a basic physical strike only (1d6 physical, no Cold)
- Alpha behavior shifts: pure physical aggression, less repositioning, more direct pressure

Narrator cue: *"It's still dangerous — but that cold is gone. And so is whatever was holding the others together."*

## 9. Structural Reading Units — Mechanical Anchors

### The Alpha

**Mechanical consequence of suppressing:**
Any hit on the Alpha that produces stagger or behavioral disruption (Narrator's judgment — a meaningful hit, not a graze) creates a one-beat pack hesitation. The flankers pause in the same exchange. This is the core friction signal linking Alpha state to pack behavior. It does not require a roll to produce — it is a consequence of any meaningful Alpha hit.

**Behavioral change at throat box 2:**
The Alpha shifts from a coordinating positioning style to more direct aggression — less lateral repositioning, more direct pressure. This is the structural equivalent of the common Ice Wolf's erratic phase shift. The Narrator should name the cause: *"Something about that hit changed how it moves — it's not holding back anymore."*

---

### Pack Resonance — Detection Mechanic

Any character who has taken flanker hits before and after throat box 2 is eligible to notice the lighter impact. This does not require a roll — the Narrator describes the quality difference when the first flanker hits after Pack Resonance drops. The character may act on this information in the same exchange.

---

### Coordinated Drive — Trigger Detection

The throat contraction signal is available to any character watching the Alpha. No roll required to notice it — it is a Narrator description delivered at the moment the Alpha begins the howl onset. The prevention window opens when the contraction is described and closes when the howl resolves (one beat later).

A character who has never seen the drive fire may not know what the contraction means. A character who has seen it fire once knows that the contraction precedes the drive — the connection is learnable through a single observation.

## 10. Enemy Reading Paths

### 10.1 Friction-Based Reading

**Proactive flanker glancing signal (exchanges 1–2):**
The Narrator delivers this without being prompted: *"The smaller wolf takes the hit — and just before it resets, you notice it glance toward the larger one. Not in fear. In expectation."* This is the earliest friction signal and must be delivered before the Coordinated Drive fires.

**Clues from the Coordinated Drive:**
- The drive fires and both flankers move simultaneously toward one target — the simultaneity is the first structural signal
- After the drive: causal attribution to the Alpha's howl is available to any character who was watching
- The throat contraction preceding the howl is the prevention signal — available after one execution

**Clues from Alpha impact:**
- Any meaningful hit on the Alpha produces a one-beat pack hesitation in the same exchange
- This is the primary friction signal for structural dependency: Alpha state → flanker behavior
- Available to any player, no specialization required

**Dual-function throat signal:**
First throat damage → vapor thins + howl quality degrades in the same Narrator description. Two effects, one hit, one sentence.

**Negative signal:**
Flanker hits produce individual damage response and no structural change. After 2–3 exchanges of flanker-only targeting, a player tracking behavioral consequences will notice: hitting the flankers has not changed the Alpha's behavior or the other flanker's coordination.

---

### 10.2 Deliberate Reading — Bestiary Lore (Structural)

> **Reading tier rule:** The same constraint as common enemy reading applies. A specialization may confirm the role or mechanism of an observed structural element, but not the mechanism of an element that has not yet been demonstrated in play. L3 is only available if both the Coordinated Drive has fired and Alpha throat damage affecting pack behavior has been observed.

**Specialization Roll:** `S.R. = 1d10 + Specialization Level + Competency Rank + INT`

**L1 — No observational data (Alpha seen, formation seen, no maneuver fired):**
Difficulty: 18
Result: *"Pack predators of this scale operate with hierarchical coordination. The large one is not just the strongest — it is regulating the group's behavior. Disrupting it should change how the others act."*
What it gives: structural direction. No mechanism. Group knows the Alpha is the target before testing it through friction.

**L2 — One structural element observed (flanker glancing seen, OR Coordinated Drive fired once, OR Alpha hit → pack hesitation observed):**
Difficulty: 14
Result: *"The howl is a coordination command — the flankers are reading that signal, not acting independently. The throat contraction you saw is the onset of that command. There is a window between the contraction and the howl resolving — suppressing the Alpha in that window cancels the maneuver."*
What it gives: maneuver mechanism confirmed. Prevention window made explicit. Does not confirm Pack Resonance or the dual-function throat.

**L3 — Full structural architecture (Coordinated Drive observed AND throat damage effect on pack observed):**
Difficulty: 10
Result: *"The throat is dual-function — it carries both the Alpha's own breath weapon and the pack howl. Damaging it degrades both simultaneously. The passive damage the flankers have been generating while the Alpha is active is also tied to the howl frequency — when the throat goes quiet, that bonus collapses too."*
What it gives: full confirmation including Pack Resonance. Only available after both the drive and the throat-damage effect have been demonstrated in play. Unavailable otherwise, regardless of roll value.

---

### 10.3 Deliberate Reading — Combat Tactics / Formation Reading

**Specialization Roll:** `S.R. = 1d10 + Specialization Level + Competency Rank + CUN`

**L1 — Formation visible, no maneuver fired:**
Difficulty: 16
Result: *"The large one is holding a command position — the flankers are anchored to it, not to their own targets. This is a formation, not a pack."*
What it gives: the Alpha as tactical center. Lower difficulty than Bestiary because this is observable formation reading, not specialist knowledge.

**L2 — One maneuver or repositioning pattern observed:**
Difficulty: 12
Result: *"The howl triggers simultaneous flank attacks. Two prevention strategies are available: suppress the Alpha before the howl resolves, or position so no single character can be reached simultaneously from two angles."*
What it gives: maneuver mechanism + geometric prevention strategy. The positional prevention is unique to Formation Reading — Bestiary Lore only confirms the Alpha interrupt path.

**Partial success (any tier, roll within 3 of difficulty):**
A vaguer result. At L2: *"Something in their positioning is deliberate — the drive that hit [character] was not coincidence, but I'm not certain whether the signal is the sound or something I'm missing."*

## 11. Attrition Pressure

**Actions that should feel costly:**
- [ ] Absorbing the Coordinated Drive without spreading — two Cold I applications escalating to Cold II for a character already at ambient Cold I
- [ ] Focusing flankers exclusively while the Alpha's Pack Resonance compounds hits across exchanges
- [ ] Being caught in the Alpha's ice breath without repositioning — Cold II escalating toward Cold III

**Actions that should not feel too costly:**
- [ ] Redirecting to the Alpha after the first Coordinated Drive — this should feel like the correct instinctive response
- [ ] Declaring a prevention interrupt against the Alpha — should feel viable for any character, not just specialists
- [ ] Wide positioning by a defensive character — should consistently reduce drive geometry

**Fatigue structural guideline:**
Fatigue 1 should arrive no earlier than the point at which the Coordinated Drive has been observed at least once and the Alpha's coordinating role is established through either observation or consequence. The intended window: after the maneuver is understood but before the coordination has been fully disrupted.

Informed groups earn a delay — earlier structural discovery compresses the high-pressure phase. But even a fully informed group pays minimum attrition from three attackers plus the environment; this floor cannot be reasoned away.

## 12. Expected Combat Phases

### Phase 1 — Contact and Formation Assessment
**What happens:**
Flankers visible first. Alpha comes into view behind them. Flanker glancing signal delivered proactively. Alpha does not advance. No maneuver has fired.

**What players should perceive:**
The large one is not attacking. The flankers are moving oddly before repositioning. Something about the group's behavior is structured.

**Phase 1 ends when:** at least one player has registered the Alpha as non-aggressive and potentially coordinating — whether through direct observation, the flanker glancing signal, or a formation reading.

---

### Phase 2 — First Coordinated Drive and Structural Discovery
**What happens:**
The Coordinated Drive fires for the first time. One character takes double-flank damage. The throat contraction preceding the howl was visible. Causal attribution — howl → drive — is now available through consequence or observation.

**What changes:**
The encounter is now readable as a coordination structure. The Alpha is the trigger source. Prevention is possible.

**Phase 2 ends when:** the Alpha is targeted by at least one character — whether through structural reasoning or instinctive attribution.

---

### Phase 3 — Structural Targeting and Degradation
**What happens:**
The Alpha is under pressure. Throat damage accumulates. Pack Resonance drops at box 2. Coordinated Drive fires as partial (box 1) and then fails (box 2). The dual-function throat signal is delivered at box 1.

**What changes:**
The flankers are no longer a coordinated system — they act with reduced effectiveness and no maneuver. Pack Resonance is gone. The encounter's threat profile is shifting.

**Phase 3 ends when:** the Alpha's throat is at box 3 or the Alpha is eliminated.

---

### Phase 4 — Structural Collapse and Resolution
**What happens:**
Alpha neutralized (throat box 3) or dead. Flankers are independent Common enemies.

**If Alpha is silenced (throat box 3, still alive):**
No more howling. No more Pack Resonance. Flankers act individually. Alpha shifts to pure physical aggression — faster, more direct, less repositioning.

**If Alpha is dead:**
Immediate total coordination collapse in the same exchange. Narrator delivers the collapse signal immediately: *"The large one drops. Before you can register it fully — the other two change. They stop moving together. One turns toward [nearest character]; the other pulls back. They're not a pack anymore. They're just wolves."*

The flankers are now a double-Common-enemy encounter. Threat level is significantly reduced. This shift should be legible — the group can name what changed.

## 13. Narrative Signals for the Narrator

> **Independence note:** Each signal must function independently of other abilities shown. Flanker glancing must be narrated even if the Coordinated Drive has not fired. The dual-function throat signal must appear even if the breath has not been used. Signals are parallel discovery paths.

### Proactive flanker signal — exchanges 1 and 2
Deliver without prompting: *"The smaller wolf takes the hit — and just before it resets position, you notice it glance toward the larger one. Not in fear. In expectation."*

### If the Alpha begins its howl onset (throat contraction visible)
*"The large wolf's throat tightens — a deep vibration, not yet a sound — something is forming."*
This description is the prevention window trigger. A player who hears it and declares an interrupt toward the Alpha before the howl resolves may attempt prevention.

### If the Coordinated Drive fires
*"They move at the same instant, from opposite sides — this was not coincidence."*
After the drive, name the cause explicitly: *"That howl — and then they both moved."*

### If the Alpha is hit and staggers
*"The two smaller ones go quiet — not injured, not retreating. Just quiet for a beat. As if they lost something they were listening for."*
This must be specific enough to register for a player in direct combat with the Alpha.

### Dual-function throat signal — first throat damage
*"Something changed — the vapor from its breath thinned, and the sound it was building in its chest came out shorter than it should have. One hit. Both things at once."*
Single sentence. Both effects. First throat hit only — this is the signal moment.

### If Pack Resonance drops (throat box 2)
*"That one landed softer — something about the way the pack is moving has changed."*
Deliver when the first flanker hits after Pack Resonance drops, so the comparative difference is immediately named.

### If the Coordinated Drive is partially cancelled (throat box 1)
*"The sound it made was wrong — shorter. The right one moved; the left one waited."*

### If the Coordinated Drive fails entirely (throat box 2)
*"Nothing came out right. Both wolves looked toward it. Then looked away."*

### If a character spreads wide (Conservador or equivalent positioning)
*(Deliver when the drive cannot geometrically reach the spread character)*
*"The smaller ones seemed to try to converge on two targets — but the spacing was wrong. There was no angle on the one standing wide."*

### If the Alpha is killed — immediate collapse narration
*"The large one drops. Before you can register it fully — the other two change. They stop moving together. One turns toward [nearest character]; the other pulls back. They're not a pack anymore. They're just wolves."*
Deliver in the same exchange as the Alpha's death. No delay. No transition.

### On ambient cold (in any exchange where no direct damage lands)
Numb extremities. Shortened breath. Ground pulling warmth upward.
*"The cold here doesn't wait for the wolves to touch you."*

## 14. What Should Happen if the Group Plays "Without Analyzing"

- [ ] Flanker glancing signal reaches the group through proactive narration before the drive fires
- [ ] Coordinated Drive fires once — howl-to-drive attribution is instinctive for any player who was watching the Alpha
- [ ] Alpha targeted after the drive lands, driven by consequence ("that sound, then they both came")
- [ ] Pack hesitation (Alpha hit → flankers pause) is registered through specific narration
- [ ] Dual-function throat signal arrives as a single perceptible moment — both effects, one sentence
- [ ] Prevention window may or may not be exploited, but it is available to any character who hears the throat contraction description and declares an interrupt

## 15. What Should Happen if the Group Does Analyze

- [ ] The Alpha's coordinating role is identifiable through formation observation in Phase 1, before the drive fires
- [ ] L1 reading (Bestiary or Formation) gives direction without mechanism — accelerates Phase 1 without trivializing Phase 2
- [ ] L2 reading (after one structural element observed) confirms the mechanism and prevention strategy explicitly
- [ ] L3 reading (after drive observed AND throat damage effect observed) confirms full architecture including Pack Resonance dual-function
- [ ] Prevention window is exploited through declared interrupt rather than positional workaround alone
- [ ] Attrition is meaningfully lower — Coordinated Drive lands once instead of multiple times

## 17. Poor Calibration Signals

### Too opaque
- [ ] Alpha not targeted until after Fatigue 1 — group exhausted before structural discovery
- [ ] Coordinated Drive fires three or more times without howl-to-drive attribution developing
- [ ] Pack hesitation narration too subtle — players in direct combat do not register it
- [ ] Dual-function throat signal not noticed despite being delivered

### Too obvious
- [ ] Alpha targeted and suppressed before the Coordinated Drive fires — coordination never demonstrated
- [ ] L1 reading already gives full mechanism — adjust wording or raise difficulty
- [ ] Formation collapse happens before the group has experienced the threat

### Too costly
- [ ] Coordinated Drive damage so high that the targeted character is incapacitated before structural discovery
- [ ] Cold III reached before the Alpha is identified as the structural target
- [ ] Fatigue 1 arrives before the drive has fired once

### Too flat
- [ ] Alpha eliminated but flankers show no behavioral change — collapse signal fails
- [ ] Pack Resonance drop produces no perceptible hit difference — trait is invisible
- [ ] Drive fires multiple times with no instinctive attribution developing

### Structural miscalibration
- [ ] L3 reading reached without both Coordinated Drive and throat-damage effect being observed — reading trivializes discovery
- [ ] Prevention window declared but mechanically unclear — resolution indeterminate
- [ ] Alpha health too low — Alpha eliminated before coordination is demonstrated; too high — Phase 3 becomes attrition slog

### Narrator dependency
- [ ] Alpha identified only because the Narrator happened to use the howl early — if held, the group had no structural signal
- [ ] Flanker glancing signal only delivered on player request, not proactively
