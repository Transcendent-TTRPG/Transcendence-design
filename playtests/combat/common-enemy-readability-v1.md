## 1. Enemy Role
**Category:** Common

**Function within the system:**
First calibration reference for a common but genuinely dangerous creature. The Ice Wolf is designed to be readable through friction — players should be able to figure out its logic without needing specialized knowledge, but specializations should accelerate and deepen that understanding.

**What this encounter should teach or put to the test:**
- [ ] Whether a common enemy with a linked vital point (mouth/throat → bite + breath) feels threatening but fair
- [ ] Whether attrition pressure is felt before the group solves the encounter
- [ ] Whether the cold environment adds meaningful pressure or feels irrelevant
- [ ] Whether icy breath creates a real tactical problem or is just a damage source
- [ ] Whether the wolf's aggressive movement forces positioning decisions

## 4. Enemy Summary
**Name:** Ice Wolf

**Brief description:**
A large predatory wolf with pale grey-white fur, exhaling cold vapor with each breath. Its eyes are pale and fixed. It moves in short explosive bursts rather than a sustained lope.

## 5. Relevant Enemy Traits

### Trait 1 — Aggressive Rush
**What it does:**
The wolf closes distance rapidly and attacks in the same action. Can move through or past a character's position.

**How it activates or is maintained:**
Activates when a target is outside immediate reach. Likely every turn unless the wolf is already in bite range.

**How it can be detected:**
The wolf lowers its head and shifts weight to its haunches before charging. Observable before the action resolves.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
The group can brace, reposition, or time a reaction. Removes the wolf's positioning advantage.

### Trait 2 — Icy Breath
**What it does:**
Exhales a short cone of freezing air from the mouth/throat. Applies a cold condition (slow, reduced action, or stacking attrition — TBD) to targets in range.

**How it activates or is maintained:**
Linked to the mouth/throat. Cannot be used if the throat is significantly damaged.

**How it can be detected:**
Visible vapor increases before the wolf opens its jaws wide. Players who have seen the breath once can anticipate it.

**What happens if understood or neutralized:**
Neutralizing the throat (vital point 1) disables this trait entirely. Severely reduces the wolf's threat ceiling.

## 6. Vital Points or Significant Parts

### Vital Point 1 — Mouth / Throat
**Related to:**
Both bite and icy breath. This is the wolf's primary offensive source.

**What happens if damaged superficially:**
Bite becomes less precise — the wolf still uses it but with reduced effectiveness. Breath is weaker or shorter range.

**What happens if damaged effectively:**
Bite applies no secondary effect. Breath is heavily reduced or requires a recovery before reuse.

**What happens if broken or neutralized:**
Bite becomes a basic strike with no special properties. Icy breath is disabled entirely. The wolf shifts to pure physical aggression (rush + body slam TBD).

**What clues does this zone give:**
- [ ] Breath visible from mouth — players notice the cold vapor concentrates there
- [ ] Bite leaves cold residue on wounds — indicates the mouth is the source of the cold element

### Vital Point 2 — Legs / Hindquarters
**Related to:**
Aggressive rush and repositioning. Removing mobility changes the wolf's tactical role.

**What happens if damaged superficially:**
Rush covers less distance. Wolf cannot pass through a character's position.

**What happens if damaged effectively:**
Wolf loses ability to reposition after attacking. Becomes stationary for that turn.

**What happens if broken or neutralized:**
Rush is disabled. Wolf can only attack targets adjacent to it. Becomes significantly more readable and containable.

**What clues does this zone give:**
- [ ] Limping visible in movement — telegraphs that leg damage is accumulating
- [ ] Rush becomes shorter range — players can observe the degradation over turns

## 9. Attrition Pressure

**Actions that should feel costly here:**
- [ ] Taking the icy breath without repositioning — cold condition should accumulate
- [ ] Staying clustered in breath range across multiple turns
- [ ] Ignoring the rush telegraph and taking full impact repeatedly

**Actions that should not feel too costly here:**
- [ ] Attempting to read the wolf — observation should not feel like a wasted action
- [ ] Repositioning — movement should feel like a real option, not a trap

**Dominant attrition type in this encounter:**
- [x] physical
- [ ] cognitive
- [ ] emotional / social
- [x] mixed — cold environment adds a secondary layer

**What should trigger the first Fatigue in this combat:**
After the group understands the breath weapon is linked to the throat, but before they have managed to significantly damage that vital point. Fatigue should arrive when the wolf is still dangerous, not after the threat is already neutralized.

## 10. Expected Combat Phases

### Phase 1 — Approach and Assessment
**What happens here:**
The wolf appears and closes distance. First rush. Players encounter the bite and observe the breath for the first time.

**What players should perceive:**
The wolf is fast and its cold element comes from the mouth — not the whole body.

**What decisions should they begin making:**
Do we spread out? Do we try to keep distance? Where do we hit it?

### Phase 2 — Engagement and Pattern Recognition
**What happens here:**
The wolf has used breath once. The group has taken some damage. The breath cooldown or the throat-damage reduction may have been observed.

**What changes from the previous phase:**
Players now have enough friction data to form a hypothesis about the vital point. First Fatigue likely appears here.

**What should become clear to the group:**
Hitting the throat changes the wolf's behavior. Targeting it intentionally is now a viable tactical decision.

### Phase 3 — Exploitation or Attrition
**What happens here:**
The throat is significantly damaged or the group is managing cold attrition. The wolf is either degraded (breath disabled, bite weakened) or the group is under real pressure.

**What should be at stake now:**
Can the group finish the wolf before cold attrition forces a harder choice? If the throat was neutralized — does the fight feel clearly winnable now?

## 16. Playtest Log
**Test group:**
Simulated panel — 4 player archetypes (Martial, Tactical, Specialist, Conservative). Synthetic diagnostic run; no live group. Conducted to identify calibration issues before first real session.

**Number of players:**
4 simulated roles. No Narrator player — Narrator role operated as referee throughout.

**Group level / state:**
Characters with no prior knowledge of the Ice Wolf. Standard starting specializations including Vigor. No informational advantage before the encounter. Frozen terrain, open ground, difficult footing.

**Actual combat duration:**
6 significant exchanges across 3 distinct phases.

- Phase 1 (exchanges 1–2): approach and assessment — first rush, first bite, body-hit feedback, head-orientation tell observed.
- Phase 2 (exchanges 3–4): pattern recognition — first breath use, breath source identified, throat hypothesis formed and confirmed.
- Phase 3 (exchanges 5–6): exploitation and attrition — deliberate throat targeting, throat neutralized, wolf entered desperate phase, legs superficially damaged.

**Approximate moment of first Fatigue:**
Between exchanges 5 and 6 — after the throat was identified and superficially damaged, but before the breath was fully disabled. The wolf was still a real threat when Fatigue 1 became reasonable. This is the correct window per design intent.

**How many significant actions did an average character perform before Fatigue 1:**

- Martial: ~4–5 actions of high or moderate exigence (initial bite received, sustained direct combat, wolf's focused aggression in phase 3).
- Specialist: ~3 actions at significant cost, compounded by cold condition from breath edge in exchange 3.
- Tactical and Conservative: did not reach Fatigue 1 during the encounter. Their low-risk profile and correct early positioning kept attrition contained.

**What the group learned through friction:**

- The cold comes from the mouth, not the body — observed when witnessing the breath weapon directly (exchange 3). The cold emanated forward from the jaws, not from the wolf's fur or skin.
- The bite and the breath are the same cold from the same origin — connected by comparing the residue on the Martial's wound with the cold received from the breath edge (exchange 3–4).
- Hitting the body without a specific target produces no behavioral change — implicit feedback in exchange 2: the wolf shook off a clean body hit without any shift in movement, vapor, or behavior.
- The rush is telegraphed by a posture shift (head low, weight to hindquarters) — read by the Tactical in exchange 1 and used to avoid the charge. Taught to the rest of the group in exchange 4.
- There is an exploitable recovery pause after the breath is used — observed in exchange 3. The wolf's head dropped, vapor diminished briefly.
- Hitting the throat produces a different response than hitting the body — validated by the Martial in exchange 4 by instinct: the wolf reacted differently, the vapor thinned, a rougher sound was heard.

**What the group learned through specializations:**

- The throat contains a thermal concentration structure (gland) that drives both bite and breath simultaneously. This was confirmed through Bestiary Lore in exchange 4, after the observational data had already accumulated.
- Damaging that zone interrupts both threats at once — not two separate vital points, one unified source.
- The specialization arrived at the same conclusion as friction-based observation and Tactical reading, but without the 1–2 rounds of validation by impact that the Martial needed. It compressed Phase 2 without trivializing Phase 1, which is the correct behavior.
- The specialization also provided the internal mechanism ("thermal gland") — not just the what, but the why — which gave the Specialist greater decision depth in subsequent exchanges.

**Which vital point did they discover first:**
Mouth / Throat — identified through friction in exchange 3 (breath visibly from the mouth), refined by wound analysis in exchange 4, formally confirmed by specialization also in exchange 4. Three independent paths converged on the same point: observational (Tactical), impact result (Martial), deliberate reading (Specialist).

**Which enemy ability did they neutralize first:**
Icy Breath — disabled in exchange 6 through accumulated throat damage. The bite lost its cold property before the breath was fully disabled (superficial throat damage in exchange 5 removed the bite's secondary effect first). Legs received superficial damage in exchange 6 but were not broken.

**How the combat was ultimately resolved:**
The group converged on the throat as the unified vital point through three independent paths. Coordinated throat targeting in exchanges 5–6 accumulated sufficient damage to disable the icy breath. The bite lost its cold residue effect at superficial throat damage. The Conservative damaged the hindquarters in the final exchange, reducing rush range. The wolf remained physically dangerous but without its defining threats. Martial and Specialist reached the Fatigue 1 threshold; Tactical and Conservative maintained margin. Attrition cost was real but contained — the encounter resolved with meaningful cost paid rather than clean victory.

## 17. Post-Playtest Adjustments
After the playtest, what should be modified?

- [x] narrative signals — first throat hit feedback needs to be more immediate and unambiguous
- [x] vital point legibility — throat feedback at first contact is too subtle for a player in direct combat
- [ ] enemy pressure — rush frequency and breath range were correctly calibrated; no adjustment needed this cycle
- [ ] attrition cost — cold condition timing was appropriate; no adjustment needed this cycle
- [ ] Fatigue timing — arrived at the correct window; no adjustment needed
- [ ] phase clarity — all three phases were distinct and readable; no adjustment needed
- [ ] specialization utility — worked correctly; compressed Phase 2 without trivializing Phase 1
- [ ] overall difficulty — appropriate for a Common enemy
- [x] other: friction signals must be independent of Narrator ability deployment order; throat must be discoverable through bite alone; environmental cold pressure needs narrative presence

**Designer notes:**

**1. First throat-hit feedback must be unambiguous for direct combatants.** *(unchanged)*
The current signals — vapor slightly thinner, rougher sound in the exhale — are sensory and subtle. A player in melee range under pressure may not register them. Recommendation: add a clear behavioral response at the first superficial throat hit — an involuntary head recoil, a short dry cough, a visible tremor in the neck. The sensory layer (vapor change, sound difference) can remain as a secondary signal for observing players, but it cannot be the only signal.

**2. The bite alone must be sufficient to suggest the throat as the focal point — the breath cannot be a required step in the discovery chain.** *(elevated to critical)*
In a mixed group, the discovery chain worked because the Specialist, the Tactical, and the breath weapon converged to confirm the throat. In a martial group, the breath was what finally established "the mouth is the source of the cold" — but that only happened because the Narrator used it early. This is a design dependency that must be removed: the Narrator is not a neutral algorithm. They will use the wolf's abilities with narrative intent — to establish fear, to reward aggression, to create pressure when the scene demands it. If discovering the throat requires the breath to have been used first, then a Narrator who withholds the breath for narrative reasons is inadvertently blocking the friction path.

The bite must carry enough signal on its own. Recommendation: add a brief visible gesture before each bite — a subtle movement in the wolf's throat, as if drawing cold upward through the neck just before the jaws close. This makes the physical chain (throat → teeth → wound) perceptible through the bite alone, without requiring the breath to have been demonstrated. A player who has only seen the bite and nothing else should still be able to form the hypothesis "something in the throat is the source."

**3. Limit the depth of the Specialist's first deliberate reading.** *(unchanged)*
The simulation worked because the first reading was correctly partial ("cold is concentrated, not diffuse — point of origin unknown") and full confirmation arrived only after observational data had accumulated. The risk is that a first read giving "throat is the unified vital point" trivializes Phase 1 completely. The system must enforce that the first reading gives directional information (head/neck region, concentrated source) but not functional confirmation. Full confirmation should require either prior observational data or deeper investment.

**4. The transition to erratic behavior in Phase 3 must read as consequence, not as arbitrary escalation.** *(unchanged)*
When the wolf stopped telegraphing and became more direct, the Tactical correctly identified this as a response to throat damage. A player not tracking the pattern may read it as "the enemy became harder for no reason." Recommendation: the Narrator should explicitly connect the behavioral shift to the damage — "something in that hit changed it — the wide arcs are gone, it just comes straight now." The shift must feel earned by the group's actions.

**5. Environmental cold pressure was invisible throughout both simulations.** *(unchanged)*
The frozen terrain and ambient cold were described but had no narrative presence during either run. Even the Conservative who avoided all breath damage felt no environmental cost. This makes the environment purely decorative. Recommendation: in any exchange where no character took direct wolf damage, the Narrator should describe the ambient cold as a passive presence — numb extremities, shortened breath, the ground leeching warmth upward. It does not need to be a mechanical cost in v1, but it must be perceptible so the "mixed attrition" design intent is actually felt.

**6. Section 11 (Narrator signals) needs an explicit independence note.** *(new)*
The current Narrator signals in section 11 describe what to say based on what the group does — useful, but they implicitly assume the Narrator has been deploying abilities in a certain order. Section 11 should include a short note making explicit that each friction signal (bite residue, throat-hit response, behavior change, leg degradation) must function independently of which other abilities have been shown. A Narrator who has not yet used the icy breath should still be able to give the group a meaningful clue through a bite alone. A Narrator who has not yet shown the rush telegraph can still let the throat feedback speak for itself. The signals are not a chain — they are independent paths that converge on the same answer.
