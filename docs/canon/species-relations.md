# Species Relations

Seven axes frame how species interact. Each axis is derived from the world's actual operating conditions — not from ideal relationships, but from what the setting's physics (survival pressure, Tauma, bio-organic technology limits) actually produces.

## Axis 1: Ecological niche and territorial competition

Species cluster by where they operate, not by which region of the world. The same ecological zone creates both natural allies (complementary coverage) and natural competitors (same resource base).

All twenty species have architectural traditions. Being nomadic does not mean not building — it means not staying. What distinguishes species in this axis is what kind of relationship they have to fixed space: whether they anchor to it, operate through it, or organize their movement around something other than space itself.

### The deep underground

**Talpan** own the true deep — the lethal, pressurized zones no other species can survive without their biological adaptations. They surface only to trade. Nothing competes with them at depth.

**Arakhel** occupy a different layer: the between-space, the connective underground tissue between settlements. Their silk networks run through shallow cave systems, passage-ways under hills, tunnels that cross beneath rivers — not the crushing depth of Talpan territory, but the underground routes that link the inhabited world. Crucially, an Arakhel who maintains a passage node does not need to be physically present to sense it — the silk transmits across distance. This is what sustains the myth: travelers guided through underground passes by something they never see. The physical Arakhel may be nowhere near the crossing point when someone uses it. **Talpan go down. Arakhel go through.**

### Settled species with fixed architectural traditions

Most species are fundamentally settled, each with architectural needs shaped by their biology and cosmology.

**Naghii** build upward — ziggurats and observation towers positioned over ruins, designed to give unobstructed sightlines to the sky. Vertical access is not aesthetic; it is the mechanism of their prophecy system.

**Sauri** build around water management — canals, cisterns, controlled delta architecture. Their settlements are engineering solutions to the problem of containing la Corriente in built form.

**Loxod** build for resonance dissipation. Their cities are massive because scale is required to absorb their own infrasonido output without the residents destroying each other.

**Chelicer** build around the Ordalía chamber as the center of civic structure. Settlement design radiates outward from the judgment space. But like Luphran, they have a mobile wing: the Inquisición travels. Inquisidores operate far from the home settlement, projecting institutional authority into communities that have not adopted the Ordalía system — and sometimes into ones that have no intention of doing so. The home city anchors the faith; the Inquisición extends its reach.

**Ursari** build around the forge-and-druid cycle — settlements positioned between the master forge and whatever extreme natural zone the druids use for vigils.

**Vesper** build inward — sealed vaults, pressure chambers, passage networks with controlled access. Their architecture is about enclosure and pressure, not outward projection.

**Soricin** build on fault lines and volcanic zones, in the geological places most species refuse to inhabit. Their cities are not built despite instability but because of it — the Latido breathes in unstable ground.

**Luphran** have stable home settlements with full governance — pack hierarchy, Portavoces, oral archive — while a significant portion of the population is perpetually at sea on raiding and trade runs. This is a Viking structure: the home exists and is maintained; the maritime wing travels but has a place to return to. **Luphran are not nomadic.** The raiding wing uses sea routes, but the culture is anchored.

**Sauri vs. Loxod competition:** Both build large, water-adjacent cities with engineering requirements. Their cosmological purposes differ (containment vs. archive), but their physical demands overlap enough in river-delta environments to produce jurisdictional friction over the best sites.

### Fixed-closure territories

**Bufoni** domains are fixed and almost autarkic by design. The domain is not a defended perimeter — it is a space the Bufoni know biologically, at every point, better than any intruder ever could. The principle is closure in both directions: neither should other species enter freely, nor should Bufoni leave without cause. A passing caravan that uses what it perceives as empty wilderness has, from the Bufoni perspective, introduced a variable into their domain that was noted before the first step. **Bufoni do not maintain routes. They maintain exclusion.**

### Route operators

Three species relate to the spaces between settlements as something to be operated rather than claimed.

**Ceratox** commit to specific terrestrial territories long enough to establish a calibration baseline — what is normal at this node, at this route, over decades of observation. Without baseline, the signal is uninterpretable. Their territorial commitment is instrumental, not proprietary; they're measuring the space, not defending it.

**Luphran maritime wing** uses sea routes without maintaining them. The routes exist in collective oral memory — in the record of every run made by prior generations. No infrastructure is installed, no markers left. The route is knowledge, not construction.

**Arakhel passage network** is actively maintained — silk is installed, repaired, kept in tension. Travelers crossing an Arakhel-maintained underground passage use Arakhel infrastructure without necessarily knowing it. Whether passage fees are collected depends on what the Arakhel find useful about the traveler.

Ceratox and Arakhel serve different intelligence needs and rarely compete: Ceratox calibrate anomalies in real time via horn on surface routes; Arakhel know the deep structure of their underground network through accumulated silk-memory. One reads the present; the other knows the history of the passage.

### Belief-driven movement

These species move not because they lack a home but because movement is required by what they believe. **None of them dispute territory** — territory is not the point.

**Lapinni** travel toward liminal events: eclipses, auroras, solstices, seismic moments, any occurrence where physical thresholds become permeable. Their camps at these sites can be architecturally extensive — Lapinni do build — but what they build is meant to be left behind or burned when the event passes and the stability begins to reassert itself.

**Yacani** operate in two simultaneous modes. Their pilgrimage routes cross the most hostile terrain available — this is the movement most visible to other species. But they also maintain permanent settlements in disaster epicenters: volcanic aftermath zones, flood basins, seismic collapse sites. These settlements are not ruins; they are functional, continuously inhabited by whoever is too saturated with accumulated suffering to travel. Yacani have permanent homes. The homes are in places where no one else would live.

**Kesh** erase their passage as they go. Their Espejismo caravans move without leaving tracks, without camps others could find or use. Whether they have a home in the sense of a fixed place depends on how devout the specific Kesh is; the most devout have erased even their internal sense of origin point. To an outside observer, a Kesh caravan passed through and left nothing. The dark irony they haven't perceived: the Espejismo's collective commitment to not-existing is itself a dense, coherent faith, and the Tauma is doing with it exactly what it does with every other dense collective faith.

**Drak'kai** wander alone for decades. Their social home is the conclave — a periodic gathering of isolated individuals who share authentication intelligence. Between conclaves, they are everywhere no one expects them, carrying the weight of whatever they've collected.

**Panin** converge on problems in bands and disperse when the inspiration fades. Their camps have architectural ambition — they build for the duration of the problem — but the community dissolves when the encendido does. A Panin settlement is defined by its eventual abandonment.

## Axis 2: Economic interdependence and power asymmetry

### Structural monopolies

**Talpan** hold the most significant structural power in the setting. No other species produces anything close to their level of engineering and materials science. Armies, cities, and expeditions that want the best equipment must deal with Talpan — and Talpan will sell to anyone, without ethical constraint, which makes them simultaneously indispensable and dangerous to every civilization that has ever fought against someone equipped with Talpan technology.

**Yacani** hold a monopoly on trans-hostile-terrain routing. No military campaign, trade convoy, or exploration party can cross certain terrain categories without them. This is not a negotiable position — the biology to survive those crossings doesn't exist elsewhere.

**Zarnag** hold crisis-moment monopoly. When plague comes, when a battlefield has been festering for too long, when a ruin has accumulated enough active Restos to poison the water supply — the same communities that despise Zarnag in peacetime will offer them anything to return. They know this. They price accordingly.

### Accumulated knowledge monopolies

**Naghii** hold the oldest astronomical records in the world. Other species rely on Naghii readings to understand patterns in the sky — but Naghii records contain critical interpretive gaps, internal disputes, and possibly a fundamental translation error in their central doctrine. Communities that build strategies on Naghii astronomical guidance are building on a foundation the Naghii themselves suspect may be cracked.

**Drak'kai** hold the most comprehensive relic authentication service in the world. If a community finds a relic and wants to know whether it's dangerous, whether it's what it appears to be, or whether another community has encountered something similar — Drak'kai are the only reliable source.

### Biological truth monopolies

**Ceratox** hold a different kind of power: the horn does not interpret — it detects. In a world where afflictions, vestigos, and Primordial territory are invisible to everyone else, the Ceratox body provides the only innate, real-time, certain reading of "something here is anomalous." Not what it is. Not whether it's dangerous. Just that something IS wrong, with a certainty no other species can produce. No Rokhart methodology arrives at that reading faster; no Naghii astronomical record covers it at the local ground level.

What individual Ceratox do with this is a separate matter entirely. Some operate as honest calibrators — the value of the signal is its integrity and they protect it. Others embed themselves in governance as court vizirs or settlement advisors, providing readings as a political service. Others use the horn's authority to extort: claiming a signal that wasn't there, suppressing one that was, or reading it so vaguely that the community keeps paying to ask again. The horn doesn't lie. The Ceratox can. The difference between the two is individual character, not species guarantee.

**Loxod** carry the same structural parallel at a different register. Their chemical and emotional archive is biological truth — they hold the actual signature of what happened at a place, not a reconstruction of it. This makes their testimony trusted in ways that written records can't match. It also makes the choice of what to recall, what to emphasize, what to withhold, and how to translate the chemical record into language a resource that can be used or exploited. Their biology speaks truth. They are still individuals.

### Labor dependency

**Formix** are the most exploited species economically. Their traits — tireless, reliable, coordinated, willing to work collectively — make them targets for structured re-enslavement through "legitimate" contracts. Communities that have learned to depend on Formix labor often don't notice (or pretend not to notice) that the structure they've created is a Reina by another name. This is one of the recurring horrors of multicultural settlements.

### Dangerous dependencies

**Chelicer** institutional power does not require adoption. The Inquisición is mobile, and unlike a legal system that applies only to those who accepted it, Inquisidores operate in communities that have not invited them, communities that actively resist them, and communities that have no framework for contesting their verdicts from within the Chelicer structure. Dependency isn't exclusive to settlements that have formally integrated the Ordalía — it extends to any community within reach of where the Inquisición is currently operating. Reversing it requires either political force or the kind of internal Chelicer doubt that produces exiles — neither of which is easy.

Communities that trade with **Talpan** for weapons and later find those weapons pointed at them by an enemy that bought from the same source have no recourse. Talpan will not arbitrate the conflicts their products enable.

## Axis 3: Civilizational value conflicts

### Truth and method

The deepest civilizational incompatibility in the setting runs between how species determine what is true.

**Chelicer** believe truth is a pre-existing divine verdict, extracted through proper ritual. It doesn't depend on evidence — it depends on correct procedure. Once the Ordalía produces a verdict, that verdict IS the truth. Evidence that contradicts it is either fraudulent or an indication the procedure was contaminated.

**Rokhart** believe truth is approached through systematic observation, accumulated evidence, and disciplined removal of the observer's interpretive bias. A pattern that emerges from five hundred years of correlated observations is more reliable than any revelation. Verdicts based on ritual procedure are, to Rokhart, quantifiably self-serving. The Chelicer explicitly call out Rokhart for "hiding behind procedure" — meaning Rokhart refuse to commit to moral certainty. Rokhart would respond that "moral certainty" is exactly what the data says you shouldn't have.

**Naghii** believe truth is written in the sky, and that reading it correctly requires both rigor and a specific kind of receptivity — the igi-an practice of opening oneself to the stars as signal rather than distance. This puts them closer to Chelicer (revelation) on one axis and closer to Rokhart (archive and corroboration) on another. Rokhart respect Naghii discipline but are deeply suspicious of the igi-an's role in producing the "readings" — they've documented that prolonged igi-an practice correlates with susceptibility to extranormal influence. Naghii would interpret that observation as proof of the practice's depth, not its danger.

**Drak'kai** are skeptics of every faith system they've ever encountered. They've watched too many deities shift their demands century by century to trust any claim of immutable divine truth. They would be patient but firm critics of Chelicer revelations, polite but skeptical peers of Naghii astronomical readings, and reluctant admirers of Rokhart methodology — while competing directly with Rokhart over whose archival approach produces better predictions.

**The between-space and its interpreters**: Almost every species has myths about something that guides travelers through underground passages, crossroads, and liminal routes — an entity, a spirit, a divine presence that responds to proper offerings and steers those who approach correctly. The actual mechanism is Arakhel: subtle silk-adjustments to what a traveler encounters, dangerous passages gently closed, navigable routes left open, the faintest sense of direction where the passage branches. The Arakhel don't deny the myth, because the myth sustains the relationship between their network and the travelers who use it. But the interpretive layer placed over the same phenomenon varies completely by species: Naghii see a star-aligned guide; Chelicer see divine will that rewards correct ritual approach; Bufoni see a domain spirit asserting territorial logic; Drak'kai file it as a documented repeated phenomenon and offer no explanation. Formix — who can detect organized chemical intelligence — recognize what it actually is, which disturbs them more than any other species' interpretation would.

**Queens as civilizational truth**: The Formix have, in spreading across the world as refugees and laborers, distributed one specific truth so effectively that it has restructured how many species think about collective organization. Queens are horror. Not Reinas specifically — any structure with too much hierarchical coordination, too much lock-step behavior, too much consistency where individual variation should appear, triggers the Formix-queen pattern recognition in observers who've spent time with free Formix. The political consequence: any community that operates through tight collective action is suspect. The ironic consequence: the more coordinated and effective free Formix communities become, the more other species watch to see if there's a Reina directing from somewhere outside view.

**Panin as social unpredictability**: For species that organize around reliable long-term commitments — Sauri with multi-generational containment projects, Loxod with centuries of archive construction, Luphran with seasonal raiding schedules — Panin represent a specific truth-conflict about what constitutes a society. Panin would say: a community that needs everyone to be predictable has stopped responding to what's real. Other species would respond: a community that can't count on anyone is not a community. The practical problem is also territorial: Panin reproduce quickly when inspired, and a settlement near Panin territory may experience sudden large influxes of Panin energy — problems solved, things broken, structures reconfigured — followed by complete departure when the encendido fades. The environment is changed; the Panin are gone; no agreements were signed.

**Lapinni from the outside**: Most species experience Lapinni as disaster-cultists — species that show up before or during catastrophes, that deliberately use vestigos as detonators of stable things, that celebrate when what others built collapses. The perceived truth: they're deranged and dangerous. The Lapinni's truth: they're accelerating what would happen anyway, and stability that prevents necessary change is the actual pathology. These two truths cannot be negotiated because they don't share a premise about what disaster IS. There is no argument that bridges "the survival infrastructure we built is worth protecting" and "this settlement has been stable long enough that protecting it is now the problem."

### Mobility vs. permanence

**Lapinni** treat permanence as cosmological death. They would find Sauri containment philosophy not just wrong but actively dangerous — a civilization that has made peace with staying still is a civilization inviting catastrophe. Sauri would find Lapinni not just destructive but baffling — the whole point is to hold the form.

**Kesh** erase everything they touch. **Loxod** remember everything. These are not merely different priorities; they're fundamentally opposed models of what the world requires. A Loxod would feel Kesh's erasure as violence against the archive of existence. A Kesh would find Loxod memory to be the most visible target in the world — carrying everything openly invites the predator. There is an additional layer of horror specific to a Loxod who has spent time near a Kesh community: the Loxod would begin detecting the signature of entities accumulating in the Kesh's wake — entities the Kesh themselves cannot perceive, because what the Kesh doctrine generates is as invisible and silent as the Kesh themselves.

**Ursari** need objects to accumulate significance before they're worth working. **Lapinni** destroy accumulated significance as a spiritual act. These two species in the same settlement would produce a specific repeating conflict: the Ursari object that has gathered forty years of community devotion, detonated by the Lapinni who decided it needed to be freed from stagnation.

**Luphran** have a home — but the maritime wing that goes out and comes back does not return identical. Warriors who complete extended raiding campaigns carry marks (accumulated Tauma damage from multiple cruces) and a worldview shaped by sustained violence that does not fully re-integrate into home-settlement life. The home community values them — the Portavoces record their deeds, the marks are honored — but the gap between what the home settlement knows and what the maritime wing has been living creates a chronic tension that every Luphran settlement manages in its own way. The most marked warriors, the ones the meritocracy elevates as most proven, are also the ones who may have already started fighting because the furia demands it rather than because the community needs them. From outside, this is invisible. All that's visible is that the Luphran return.

The external tension is different: species near Luphran maritime routes cannot read whether the approaching ship intends trade or raid. The Luphran themselves may not have decided. This ambiguity is not deception — it is structural. The maritime wing operates in a mode where either outcome is legitimate, and the final choice depends on what the receiving community offers in the first exchange, what the captain reads in that moment, and how much the crew's current drive has built during the approach. Settled coastal communities develop elaborate protocols for initial Luphran contact precisely because the window before the decision is made is the only moment they can influence the outcome.

### Ethics and means

**Talpan** have no ethical constraints on what their technology does after it leaves their hands. This is not hypocrisy — they genuinely don't consider it their concern. Every species that has been on the receiving end of a Talpan-equipped army that bought from the same forge as its own army understands the structural position Talpan occupies. There's no malice. There's also no accountability.

**Vesper** biological practices — pushing lineages past biological limits, creating things that can't die, calling the results gods — are horrifying by any other species' standards. Vesper don't consider what they do cruelty because the theological framework metabolizes cruelty into rigor. A Vesper would hear the word "cruelty" and ask which standard you're applying, and why you're treating your standard as universal.

**Zarnag** are despised for doing what others cannot. The accusation — that they're profaners, corpse-defilers — comes from species that cannot do what Zarnag do and know it. This is a specific social dynamic: the revulsion that attends an indispensable function that the revulsion-feelers are unable to perform themselves.

### Memory and the past

**Loxod** believe the dead's memory must be preserved and not absorbed. They approach death as an archival obligation.

**Zarnag** believe the dead's intent poisons environments if not sated and released. They approach death as a sanitation obligation.

These are not incompatible at the object-level — a Loxod archivist and a Zarnag cleaner can both work a site — but they differ on what the past is FOR. Loxod keep it. Zarnag clean it up so it doesn't kill the living.

## Axis 4: Natural convergence and alliances

Natural alliances are not based on liking each other. They're based on complementary capabilities, shared enemies, or operational needs that can only be met together.

### Ceratox + Drak'kai

The closest thing to a natural pair in the setting. Ceratox detect anomalies; Drak'kai identify what they are. Ceratox explicitly refuse to name what the horn detects — naming is the beginning of self-deception. Drak'kai have the catalog to match a description to a previously documented object. Together they produce something neither can alone: detection + identification without premature interpretation. They would not be friends (Ceratox operate in isolation; Drak'kai wander alone) but they would seek each other out when the signal requires a name.

### Rokhart + Naghii

Both are obsessed with accumulated knowledge and its correct interpretation. They conflict on methodology (Rokhart rejects sacred revelation; Naghii requires it) but respect each other's rigor. They would collaborate on astronomical record-keeping, corroboration of site observations, and multi-site pattern correlation — while disagreeing, sometimes bitterly, about what the patterns mean. The Naghii text explicitly mentions that Naghii advise Drak'kai on false relics; the Rokhart text identifies Naghii as species that "respect their rigor but despise that they reduce the sacred to a replicable pattern."

### Luphran + Yacani

Both are mobile species that operate in hostile environments. Yacani's endurance and environmental tolerance (they can cross terrain that kills others) combined with Luphran's maritime network and sustained military capability creates a combined land-sea operational range that neither achieves alone. Their authority structures are different (Yacani hierarchical by accumulated suffering; Luphran meritocratic by proven performance) but not incompatible — both are fundamentally merit-based, just measuring different things.

### Arakhel + Formix

Arakhel have the wilderness network. Formix can detect chemical signatures through any surface they contact. Together they form a complete environmental intelligence system — Arakhel for spatial and vibrational, Formix for chemical and metabolic. There's also a cultural resonance: Arakhel are afraid that the accumulation in their nodo is something that has learned to use them; Formix are afraid of returning to a structure that tells them what to do. Both have direct experience with the horror of a collective intelligence that has inverted its relationship with the individual. They would recognize each other's fear.

### Zarnag + Sauri

Both work on the boundary between the living and the dead. Both treat death as something that must be correctly managed rather than mourned. Their cosmologies overlap: Sauri believe the dead soberano's body is a vessel that still holds charge; Zarnag believe the dying intent of any person stays attached to their objects. These are not the same idea, but they're adjacent enough that a Zarnag and a Sauri priest would understand each other's work more quickly than either would understand a Lapinni's theology.

### Ursari + Talpan (competitive alliance)

Both produce the best physical objects in the setting. Ursari through the druidic process (community devotion + extreme nature exposure + master forge); Talpan through engineering + Umbral materials. They would be the same buyer's two options for the best weapon, and they know it. This creates competition — but also creates the conditions for trade in different domains. Ursari produce objects that carry genuine Tauma imprints (they just call it "recognition by nature"). Talpan can detect and measure Tauma properties without being able to replicate the druidic process. A Talpan studying an Ursari blade would learn things. Whether the Ursari would consent to the study is a different question.

### Panin + Kesh (short-term operational)

Neither builds legacy, neither stays. Panin through inspiration-cycle mobility; Kesh through deliberate erasure. In a specific operation — entering somewhere fast, assessing what's there, and leaving without trace — Panin's rapid evaluation combined with Kesh's covert capability is extremely effective. They would not form lasting alliances; a Panin's attention will have moved on before any long-term structure could form, and a Kesh's commitment to no-legacy means any formal agreement is already half-erased.

## Axis 5: Threat perception and reputation

### Most feared

**Chelicer** — Feared for institutional power that outlasts individuals. A Chelicer verdict against a family can run for generations. In settlements where their Ordalía system has been adopted, the fear is structural: there's no external appeal. In settlements that haven't adopted it, the fear is more diffuse — Chelicer traveling through make the local authority nervous.

**Talpan** — Feared for ethical indifference. They sell to everyone. No military campaign can assume the other side doesn't have Talpan equipment. Additionally, their practice of "rescuing" the wounded and converting them to cyborg workers has produced specific dread: being helped by Talpan is not necessarily better than dying.

**Lapinni** — Feared as catalysts of catastrophe. Their practice of using vestigos as detonators, combined with their theological hostility to stable settlements, makes them dangerous to any community that has built something worth protecting. You can't negotiate with someone who believes your stability is the problem they're here to solve.

**Soricin** — Feared at civilizational scale. Their mass rituals don't just affect their own settlements; they produce Tauma events that bleed into surrounding regions. An empire that borders Soricin territory has to factor "what catastrophe are they going to catalyze this year" into their planning, and they can't stop it because Soricin will celebrate the attempt to stop them as proof the world is resisting their divine work.

**Vesper** — Feared for their practices. No one who has heard what happens in a Vesper lineage chamber wants to end up as a research subject. Their clinical affect makes it worse — they're not sadistic; they're methodical.

**Formix (as a collective)** — Feared due to unresolved questions about the Reina. Even free Formix are viewed with suspicion: are they truly independent? Is this a new Reina by another name? The fact that Formix can be re-subordinated through "legitimate" contract structures is observed by other species and generates specific anxiety.

### Most despised (unjustly)

**Zarnag** — The universal target of contempt, despite being indispensable. They've integrated this into their worldview: they read other species' revulsion as the luxury of those who've never had to dig someone out before the Hambre takes the village. They don't argue. They charge more when called back after being exiled.

### Most trusted

**Ceratox** — Deeply trusted as intelligence sources. Their refusal to interpret makes them more trustworthy, not less. If a Ceratox says there's something there and it's escalating, that's actionable information without the inflation that comes from their opinion about what it is.

**Yacani** — Essential and reliable as guides. Their survival capability is unquestioned. Their service is dangerous to them and they know it, which makes their commitment credible.

**Loxod** — Trusted as witnesses. If a Loxod was in a place when something happened, their account of it carries weight that written records can't match.

**Rokhart** — Trusted as neutral observers, specifically because they refuse to intervene. The Concordia's non-intervention policy means that Rokhart observations are not shaded by what they wanted to happen. Their information is reliable. The risk is only in the cases where they chose to intervene — then the sample is contaminated.

## Axis 6: Relationship to vestigos and human ruins

This axis documents how each species relates to the Tauma-imprinted objects (vestigos) and to the ruins of the human era — the oldest features of the world, present throughout every civilization's development, built from materials no current species can understand or replicate.

### The empirical handlers (know the effect, not the mechanism)

**Zarnag** work with what they call *Restos* — objects that carry the dying intent of the dead, poisoning their environment if not sated. This is an empirical folk-understanding of vestigo mechanics developed over generations of practical necessity. Their protocols (burning, burial under rite, physical seizure) actually work because they're engaging with the real Tauma dynamic, not because their cosmological explanation is accurate. They are the most practically effective vestigo-handlers in the world by accident of necessity.

Their protocols were built for organic death: bodies, blood-soaked battlefield material, objects held by someone at the moment of dying. Their framework maps cleanly onto those cases because the dying-intent model is a functionally accurate description of what collective belief imprints through baseline Tauma — the person dies, the intent stays attached, the Tauma sustains the imprint until it's sated or the imprint dissolves on its own. For fresh death, this is reliable.

Ruins are a harder problem. Human ruins predate every Zarnag community by millions of years. If Restos from human dying still persist in those sites, they would be among the oldest active imprints in the world. Whether they're still active — whether intent can persist that long, whether the unknown-mechanism materials of ruins sustain or dissolve old imprints — is a question the Zarnag framework doesn't answer because it wasn't built for geological timescales. A Zarnag working a ruin operates by analogy: they test for familiar signals (environmental sickness, fauna disturbance, the particular pressure a charged object puts on proximity) and apply their protocols if those signals appear. But they're extending a tool past its calibrated range. When the protocols don't resolve a ruin site, the Zarnag interpretation is "the Resto hasn't sated yet" — which may be accurate, or may be incorrectly naming something whose mechanism is entirely different.

Their deepest structural vulnerability here is the taboo against records. A Zarnag community does not archive what it finds in pits and ruins. Knowledge passes as oral transmission and inherited practice, not as written empirical log. Each encounter with an unusual site is fresh discovery. This makes them maximally adaptive in the field (they're not committed to a record that might be wrong) and maximally unable to accumulate cross-generational knowledge about sites their predecessors already cleared. A ruin that a Zarnag community worked three generations ago may be re-encountered as if for the first time.

The Umbral del Hambre is the species-level irony of their work: sustained Resto-handling at depth makes them more Affliction-vulnerable, not less. Their most experienced cleaners carry the most accumulated exposure. The elder who has cleared the most contaminated sites is also the one closest to the Gula threshold, permanently.

**Ceratox** detect vestigo activity through their horn's response to Tauma concentration and fluctuation. They register the signal; they refuse to name it. Their calibration logs are the best empirical record of vestigo activity patterns over time in any territory they've monitored long enough to establish a baseline.

Ruins invert their entire methodology. Every calibration practice they've built is designed for known territory — years of baseline accumulation so that a signal variation has context. In a ruin they've never entered before, there's no prior baseline. The horn is receiving, but receiving against nothing. They can detect that something is there; they cannot say whether the current reading is high, normal, or receding for this specific site, because they have no record of this site under any other condition. Their Integridad de la Señal (Stable / Escalating / Receding) requires comparison to establish direction of change — with no prior reading, the technique produces data that can't be oriented.

The unknown-mechanism materials of human ruins compound this. The horn reads Tauma concentration, biological emissions, chemical structure, density. Human materials carry physical properties that produce responses in the horn that don't categorize cleanly against the horn's existing vocabulary — it's not the signature of a vestigo, not the signature of a Primordial-range entity, not the signature of elevated Tauma concentration from geological sources. Something in the material is producing a reading the horn hasn't been trained (over years in stable territory) to recognize. A Ceratox in a ruin cannot tell the client whether what the horn is reading is vestigo activity, an unknown-mechanism material effect, or something their instrument has never encountered before. "Signal present, status unclear, baseline unavailable" is the honest calibration. Many clients don't pay for that.

The Ceratox who return to the same ruin over years — building a site-baseline across repeated visits, under different seasonal conditions, in different states of population activity — produce the world's most reliable ruin-specific anomaly records. These multi-year ruin calibrators are rare, because most Ceratox work living territories, and ruins require a different kind of commitment: returning to a place that was never home, building a baseline for a site that was built for purposes no one understands, reading signals that may not map to any existing category in the calibration log.

La deriva accelerates in ruins. The horn is reading continuously at intensities the territory-baseline never conditioned it for, with no context to orient against, and with the charge-to-respond (the biological impulse to move toward detected danger) firing constantly under conditions where moving toward the signal may be exactly wrong. The partner — *la empatía*, the other Ceratox with the right to say "your body is lying to you" — is the only stabilizer in that environment. A Ceratox entering a high-signal ruin alone is working without the single mechanism that protects the instrument from its own drift.

**Sauri** interpret every form of Tauma phenomenon through the lens of la Corriente — the primordial flow that bodies and architecture must contain. What another species would call a vestigo, a Sauri calls corriente trapped in an inadequate vessel. What a Naghii would call a site with unusual sky-correlation, a Sauri calls a location where la Corriente is pressing without proper form.

Their relationship to vestigos and ruins operates through this frame, which makes it practically effective in some cases and systematically blind in others. The sealed tomb network distributed beneath their cities is, in functional terms, an architecture of preserved bodies that each carried Tauma charge during a soberano's lifetime of containment. Whether those bodies are vestigos with active imprints, stabilized containers that hold what they accumulated, or pressure points that will eventually interact with the living population above is the question their doctrine cannot answer — because the doctrine assumes correctly sealed means permanently contained.

The soberano's living body is the city's primary detection instrument. The skin registers Tauma pressure changes before they become visible through other means — a darkening of scales, a shift in mineral banding — that the court reads as early warning of a failing seal elsewhere in the city. This is biological Tauma-sensitivity without a Ceratox horn's precision. The Sauri interpretation ("the vessel is reading the Corriente") is functionally correct: something in their biology responds to organized Tauma pressure. What they cannot measure is whether what they're detecting is a vestigo-level effect or the edge of a Primordial's influence at the local level.

Their city engineering — canal systems, cistern architecture, designed flow-paths — is an attempt to give la Corriente correct form so it passes through rather than accumulates destructively. Whether this achieves anything in terms of actual Tauma physics is uncertain; what it does is organize the settlement around the belief that correct form is containment. The doctrine's effectiveness cannot be tested against a control, because any failure is attributed to incorrect form rather than incorrect doctrine.

The Riesgo de Contención (their herencia) is the species-level design irony: the Sauri who has spent the most time in sustained containment practice is the most vulnerable to extranormal effects, not the most resistant. The most experienced practitioner carries the most accumulated exposure. The soberano who has been the city's living vessel the longest is the vessel most at risk of catastrophic failure.

### The collectors and catalogers

**Drak'kai** build their civilization around collecting what they call relics — which are, functionally, vestigos. Their greatest skill is knowing which ones are safe to carry and which will start directing the carrier. What they call "interference" is the onset of Aflicción from accumulated vestigo exposure. Their fear of carrying a collection they don't fully understand is completely accurate and they know it. The conclave system exists partly to identify objects that no individual should be carrying alone.

Their deepest civilizational knowledge is a catalog of which objects from which origin points carry which properties — the most accurate vestigo intelligence system in the world, accumulated over centuries of fieldwork at mortal cost to the individual collectors.

Ruins are their richest and most dangerous source material. Every ruin is a site where something old was made by hands no current species can fully understand, for purposes that can only be inferred, and left behind without explanation. A Drak'kai entering a ruin is working at the frontier of the catalog — carrying descriptions, comparison objects, and prior documentation of similar sites, but knowing that novel encounters outnumber confirmed matches. Their magnetorrecepción reads routes within ruins (the same biological perception that reads invisible paths in open terrain responds to the magnetic signature of ruins' materials), but the readings from ruins don't always resolve into known patterns. What the route is routing toward, or away from, is not always interpretable.

The catalog is distributed, not centralized. After a collector works a site, what they found goes to the conclave — passed person to person through encounters and gatherings — not to any single archive. This makes it resilient (no single point of loss) and brittle (the catalog only exists in the memories and notes of living Drak'kai). The species' deepest fear on this point is not their own death, but a dead collector found alone, collection scattered: each object now an unattributed active imprint in the world with no documentation, no context, no record of what it was doing to the person who carried it. Every other Drak'kai who subsequently picks one up is starting from nothing.

The "interference" deepens over time in a specific way: a Drak'kai whose collection carries too many strong imprints begins having trouble distinguishing their own perceptual read of the world from what the collection is feeding them. Their Percepción Interferida (the herencia) is this at the species level — they read objects more accurately than they read living creatures, because the objects have become the primary input. The biological cost of a lifetime of relic-handling is that people become harder to parse than things.

**Loxod** archive cities are designed to dissipate resonance — their massive, specifically engineered urban infrastructure is functionally vestigo-dampening. This isn't intentional on the vestigo level (they don't call it that) but emerges from their need to survive their own infrasonido output in proximity to everything they've accumulated. Paradoxically, their infrasonido also activates nearby vestigos — they're perpetually triggering what they're also trying to contain.

The dampening architecture and the activation are in permanent dynamic equilibrium, not stable containment. Every Loxod body in the city is broadcasting at all times (Firma Sísmica — involuntary, constant, no suppression available), triggering whatever is in range. The city's dampening infrastructure redirects and absorbs that broadcast before it reaches sustained resonance with nearby imprints. If the population grows faster than architects can extend the dampening footprint, or if the city expands into a zone with vestigo density the survey didn't capture, the equilibrium breaks. Archive cities fail when they outgrow their own infrastructure — which is a specific kind of civilizational disaster that other species looking at the ruins afterward would have no framework to understand.

In ruins specifically, the Loxod encounter a problem their city-infrastructure cannot pre-solve. The unknown-mechanism materials of human construction resonate differently than natural stone or organic material — their Trompa Versátil (resonance perception through solid surfaces) picks up something in those materials that their internal vocabulary doesn't have a name for. Not biological presence, not familiar vestigo activation, not the infrasonido echo they're used to reading. The materials respond to their broadcast in unexpected ways. Some ruins seem to dampen the Loxod's own infrasonido selectively, which is alarming: anything that absorbs their broadcast is operating at the same frequency they operate at, and absorbing rather than reflecting means it has some capacity to receive.

Their Archivo Viviente (perfect-fidelity retention) creates a specific hazard in ruin-work. Whatever a Loxod absorbs while inside a ruin — chemical, resonant, emotional residue in the materials, trace imprints from the last beings who occupied the space — stays. With perfect fidelity. Indefinitely. This makes Loxod archivists uniquely valuable for deep ruin survey (they can carry the full sensory record of a site out of it, including details no other species could retain or perceive), and uniquely at risk (they cannot choose not to retain something once perceived). A Loxod who spent a week inside a ruin with heavy residue comes out carrying that residue in their internal archive for the rest of their life.

The Colmillos are, in this context, the Loxod's own vestigo — the autobiographical register grown on their body across their entire life. A Loxod who has worked ruins extensively and lived for a century carries a Colmillo-archive that would be, after their death, one of the most potent vestigos in the world: a physical object imprinted by 200 years of perfect-fidelity absorption, including everything they absorbed in every ruin they ever entered. The archive cities have protocols for handling the Colmillos of dead archivists. Those protocols are closely guarded, and the Colmillos themselves are not displayed — they're kept under management, in the dampening infrastructure, treated as loaded objects that must be contained rather than exhibited.

**Rokhart** are building a theory of how collective belief generates and modifies Tauma events. They observe that communities produce extranormal phenomena shaped by their myths. They've documented enough cases to know this is systematic, not coincidental. They haven't yet formalized that vestigos are the mechanism. When they do, the implication — that the Concordia's own collective belief is generating Tauma events through their observational practice — will be a reckoning.

Ruins are their most methodologically contested sites. The core question they're trying to answer: is anomalous activity in a ruin the result of vestigos (collective belief operating through baseline Tauma, imprinted by whoever lived there after the humans), or is it something else — a property of the materials themselves, a physical resonance effect with no belief component, something pre-vestigo? Distinguishing these requires enough documentation across enough sites to establish whether the anomaly correlates with the building material or with the belief history of subsequent occupants. The Concordia has been building this dataset for centuries. They don't have an answer yet, but they have narrowing hypotheses.

What they want most in a ruin is to reach it before a community has already interpreted it. A ruin that has been inhabited, named, and incorporated into local mythology has been contaminated at the observational level — the community's belief is actively operating on whatever Tauma dynamics are present, making it impossible to separate baseline signal from community modification. They want the ruin in its uninterpreted state. They almost never get it; ruins that anyone knows about have already been named.

Their practical problem in ruins is that they observe but do not handle. A Drak'kai documents a vestigo and carries it. A Zarnag documents a Resto and eliminates it. A Rokhart documents an active imprint and leaves it active, because interfering with it would corrupt the observation. This is methodologically sound and practically the opposite of safe — for the Rokhart team at the site and for the community that continues to live there.

The contamination doctrine extends to ruin work specifically: an observer who has spent enough time in a high-activity ruin begins to organize experience through the categories of the communities they've been documenting there. The Custodios rotate teams out on schedule for this reason. The data in an observer's notes after reassignment is often more useful than their verbal report, because the notes were taken before the contamination fully set, while the verbal report reflects the categories they're now thinking in.

### The intentional creators

**Ursari** create vestigos through their druidic process without understanding that's what they're doing. Their cycle — community devotion building significance in an object, druids exposing that object to extreme natural environments, master forges giving it final form — is functional vestigo creation using the Tauma mechanism their cosmology calls "nature recognizing devotion." The neurological damage their druids suffer from prolonged wilderness vigils is Aflicción from Tauma exposure. The properties their best tools carry are Tauma imprints. They have the most sophisticated vestigo creation process in the world, institutionalized as craft tradition.

The extreme environments their druids seek are not metaphorical extremity — they are geological instability zones: volcanic rifts, fault lines, primordial forests over deep-pressure zones. These are precisely the areas with the highest ambient Tauma concentration in the world. The druid's vigil isn't "nature in its most hostile state" as aesthetic experience; it's sustained immersion of an object carrying dense collective belief inside a maximum-concentration Tauma environment. The mechanism is: collective belief (charged by community devotion) + maximum Tauma concentration + extended duration = the strongest possible imprint. Ursari produce the best tools in the world because they have, by cultural accident, optimized every parameter of vestigo creation. They don't know any of this. They know that nature recognized what they loved.

Their relationship to ruins is secondhand and incidental. Ruins are not their extreme environment — they seek geological and biological extremity, not architectural remnant. A druid who, in the absence of accessible volcanic or deep-forest zones, attempted the vigil in a deep ruin would be extending the process into unknown territory. The unknown-mechanism materials of ruins may respond to the Tauma mechanism in ways high-concentration geological sites don't — amplifying the collective belief imprint differently, interacting with the druid's sustained contact in patterns no prior vigil prepared them for. An object completed through a ruin vigil would arrive at the master smith with properties outside the Ursari reference library. Whether the smith would recognize what they're working with is uncertain. Whether the druid would survive the vigil is less certain than in a known geological site.

The druids who don't return from vigil are a specific category of vestigo source the Ursari tradition doesn't track. An object abandoned in a high-concentration zone when the druid who carried it didn't come back is an object that carries community devotion (already imprinted before the vigil) but was never completed — transformed by extreme Tauma contact without a master smith to give it final form. These objects exist in the world's most hostile environments, partially charged, unattributed, in conditions that continue acting on them after the druid's departure. Nobody knows what they become over time. Nobody is looking for them.

**Vesper** cultivate vestigos through ritual repetition — objects used in the same cut for generations, bowls that have received the same blood for centuries. They understand that repetition accumulates something in objects; they call it "correct gesture." Their most sacred spaces are filled with objects carrying strong imprints. They also create entities through biological pressure and belief — creatures that can no longer die. They call these consagrados; they call the belief that animated the process their god. Both the consagrados and the entities are products of the Tauma mechanism, correctly applied (from the Tauma's perspective) if not from any other.

Of all species in the setting, Vesper are the most metacognitively aware that repetition changes objects. They haven't named the mechanism, but they've observed the output: a blade used for the same cut across enough generations carries something their Método Anatómico can detect — a quality the object wasn't born with. Their term ("correct gesture") is phenomenologically accurate: the same gesture, the same purpose, the same object, the same duration. What they don't have is the causal layer. They know the practice produces the result. They don't know the Tauma is the medium.

Ruins are their preferred operating architecture. The Cámara Dependiente means closed spaces are where they function at full capacity — their echolocation reads geometry, density, structural voids. Ruins provide contained acoustics, thick walls, predictable bounce patterns, and spatial geometry their Zona de Eco can fully map. Their technique explicitly applies to "ruinas interiores." A Vesper working inside a ruin has maximum spatial awareness, maximum anatomical read of whatever is present, and the kind of acoustic isolation that their lines require for sensitive work.

The consagrados create a specific ruin-use problem. An entity that can no longer die eventually requires containment that purpose-built chambers may not sustain indefinitely. Some Vesper lines have moved their oldest and most problematic consagrados into ruins — sealed chambers in structures whose construction methods are unknown, walls whose material properties exceed anything the Vesper can fabricate. Whether this is intentional exploitation of the ruins' properties or practical adaptation to the fact that ruins simply outlast everything else, the result is the same: some of the oldest consagrados in the setting are contained inside human ruins, in chambers that nobody built for this purpose, in structures whose material interaction with Tauma-formed entities at close proximity has never been studied.

A Vesper ritual object carried into a ruin enters a space that may carry its own imprint from prior occupation. The practice of "correct gesture" running inside a ruin's existing charge produces outputs that may differ from those in a purpose-built Vesper chamber. These variations are noticed by experienced practitioners and attributed to the ruin's character — "the space favors this line" or "this architecture is hostile to that gesture." The mechanism they're observing (two accumulated Tauma fields interacting) is real. The explanation they're using is the closest they've reached without the causal vocabulary.

**Soricin** create vestigos at mass scale without any individual intention. Their collective ritos generate the densest concentration of collective belief in the setting, focused through shared fervor toward catastrophic transformation. They attract primordial entities, produce mutations, generate extranormal events — all of which they celebrate as divine confirmation. Their cities, built on fault lines and volcanic zones, sit in some of the highest Tauma-concentration areas available — geological instability drives ambient concentration. The vestigos produced by Soricin mass mourning and mass celebration would likely be some of the most powerful Establecido or Profundo category objects in the world.

The production rate is a function of their biology before it's a function of their theology. An 800 bpm heart generates ritual moments, emotional responses, and collective experiences at a rate other species can't match. A Soricin city produces concentrated collective belief faster per unit of time than any other civilization in the setting — not because their faith is more intense but because their metabolic and experiential rate is. The vestigos accumulating in a Soricin city that has stood for a decade represent more total collective-belief cycles than a human-equivalent city might accumulate in a century. The short lifespan (35-45 years) and rapid construction-destruction cycles mean these objects are also being broken and replaced constantly. Soricin don't have the Drak'kai relationship with preserving charged objects across generations. Their vestigo production is prolific; their vestigo retention is negligible. The Tauma field above a Soricin city is saturated with ongoing production and just as constantly releasing it through destruction.

The rebuilding cycle is the most distinctive vestigo-relationship they have. When the Latido destroys a city block — earthquake, fire, a Primordial incursion — the charged architecture comes down. The vestigos embedded in that infrastructure are destroyed or buried. Within weeks the city is rebuilt faster and denser. The new construction begins accumulating imprints from zero. The Soricin doctrine of "build faster as an act of faith" is, from the Tauma mechanism's perspective, a continuous resetting and re-intensification of the production cycle. The highest-charged vestigos in a Soricin city are never the oldest ones; they're the ones from the most recent catastrophe-and-rebuilding, still warm with the specific fervor of that cycle's particular grief and exaltation.

Ruins under or adjacent to Soricin settlements exist in the highest-intensity incidental Tauma environments in the setting. Any ruin that a Soricin city has built over or beside has been receiving concentrated collective belief output for as long as the city has stood — not directed at the ruin, but radiating through it, into the substrate it occupies. What accumulates in those underground chambers and walls over centuries of Soricin habitation above is not documented, because Soricin don't excavate and don't study what's below them. When a Primordial manifests in a Soricin plaza and the community celebrates and builds around it, the city continues operating with a Primordial-class entity as part of its urban geography. The ruin beneath such a city — with the entity above, the collective belief saturating the entire soil column, and the geological instability of the fault line below — is operating in conditions that no researcher from any other species has studied at length, partly because reaching it requires the cooperation of a civilization that doesn't prioritize staying still long enough to explain what they're standing on.

**Kesh** are the structural mirror of Soricin and the setting's most ironic case of unintended creation. Where Soricin generate Tauma phenomena through mass fervor directed outward toward catastrophe, Kesh generate them through mass fervor directed inward — the collective, disciplined, devotional commitment to not-existing. The Tauma mechanism is identical in both: dense collective belief produces imprints and entities. The difference is what comes out. Soricin entities are loud, catastrophic, and visible. What the Kesh doctrine generates mirrors the Kesh themselves exactly: invisible, silent, formidable in precisely the mode Kesh operatives are formidable. The Kesh are the only species that has generated a class of entity they cannot detect, because those entities are indistinguishable from the absence the Kesh are trying to maintain. They don't know any of this. Every Espejismo caravan that passes through the world leaves something behind.

Their doctrine requires erasing all physical traces of passage: olor, mark, disturbance. They're disciplined at this. What the doctrine does not address — because it has no vocabulary for the Tauma — is the imprint that every biological presence leaves on the Tauma field through which it moves. A Kesh operative who spends days in a space has added to that space's accumulated field regardless of what they did to the physical surface. Erasing footprints doesn't erase the imprint. The Espejismo caravans believe they are moving through the world without leaving a mark. In every physical sense they're nearly right. In the only sense that matters to the setting's physics, they are wrong every time.

Ruins are useful operational terrain. Their Anatomía Fluida makes vertical movement free of penalty and the structural complexity of ruins provides cover geometries that flat terrain doesn't. Their Silencio Inerte bonus applies inside ruins' contained acoustics. A Kesh operative working a ruin is at peak operational capability: maximum spatial flexibility, maximum auditory concealment, minimum noise generation. The doctrine calls for leaving no trace. The ruin's materials have been accumulating imprints from every creature that has touched them for millions of years. The Kesh add their own layer. The ruin cannot be instructed to erase it.

The entities the Espejismo generates follow the caravan's route through whatever terrain the caravan passes. An entity that mirrors the Kesh operational signature — invisible, silent, producing no signal a Ceratox horn would distinguish from absence — operating in proximity to a ruin's accumulated Tauma field is the setting's most unexamined convergence. No one can detect the entity. No one is studying what happens when a Kesh-generated absence-signature operates inside a structure already dense with millions of years of contact imprint. The Kesh don't know the entity is there. The ruin doesn't differentiate it from any other imprint source. Whatever is happening in those ruins after the Espejismo passes through is not in any species' documentation because no one with the instruments to notice has been there to look.

### The users and exploiters

**Talpan** use vestigos as calibration instruments for observing the Falla Primordial. A vestigo, for them, is a lens through which physics they can't yet understand becomes briefly legible. They know the cost — their bodies and minds deteriorate with use — and accept it as a reasonable experimental expense. Their use is deliberately high-frequency and instrumentalized, which means they deplete vestigos faster than any other species.

The mechanism they're exploiting is real but incomplete. A vestigo's accumulated belief creates a dense Tauma imprint — a localized concentration of the mechanism that also drives Primordial formation and the Falla Primordial's own behavior. Talpan instruments can read the Falla Primordial through a vestigo because the imprint creates a focusing medium that makes the underlying physics briefly legible. What their instruments cannot read is the belief layer itself: the collective intention that generated the imprint is not in their sensor vocabulary. They see the physics output. They've never built an instrument for what produces it. Their calibration results are accurate within the bounds of their theoretical model, and their theoretical model is missing its most important variable.

Ruins are their highest research priority. Unknown-mechanism construction materials, construction methods with no surviving precedent, physical properties that their instruments cannot fully characterize — and the materials pre-date every current species by millions of years, which means they were engineered before any current tool-making tradition existed. A Talpan who gets underground access to ruin substrates is doing the most important materials science work available in the setting. Their forges, their deepest mining equipment, their best alloys — none of it fully explains the structural behavior of ruin materials at the physical level Talpan instruments probe.

The excavation problem is real and unresolved within their research culture. Talpan's primary research tool in underground contexts is controlled structural collapse: their garras can tear terrain systematically, exposing layers for sampling. Applied to a ruin, this is also the technique most likely to catastrophically compromise the site's unknown-property architecture. Their best access method is also their most destructive one. Research teams have been damaged in ruin excavations when tearing through a wall exposed something their models hadn't predicted — not seismic, not structural, not material, but something in the reading that their instruments couldn't categorize. These incidents are logged as "anomalous Falla Primordial proximity effects." They occur with above-average frequency in ruins. The working explanation is that ruin materials interact unusually with deep-Tauma readings. The correct explanation — that the ruins are accumulating something from millions of years of collective contact — is adjacent to the Talpan theoretical vocabulary but hasn't been formalized.

**Lapinni** use vestigos as detonators. They seek sacred objects and ancient relics specifically to force violent transitions in stable environments. They are the most destructive force in the world with respect to vestigo depletion — not because they hate vestigos, but because exhausting their pattern is the point. A Lapinni who uses a Profundo vestigo until it depletes considers this the object's rightful destiny.

The detonation process is not random. Lapinni understand — with a precision that belies the chaos of their reputation — which vestigos carry enough accumulated belief to produce the magnitude of transition they're seeking. They travel toward sites where vestigos have gathered centuries of collective investment, where the object's imprint is so dense it has become load-bearing for the community around it. What they do with these objects is apply them at the threshold moment: the eclipse, the tremor, the flood crest. The vestigo's pattern releases into the Tauma field at the exact point of maximum environmental instability. Whether this amplifies the event, or whether Lapinni are simply very good at timing, is unknown. The results look amplified. Settlements that survive Lapinni detonation events consistently describe something changing in the air the moment the object breaks — a quality that is not weather and not earthquake but something adjacent to both.

Ruins present a specific opportunity for Lapinni cosmology. The threshold sensitivity that makes a Lapinni feel a tremor hours in advance also produces a response to the unknown-mechanism materials of human ruins. Something in the ruin's architecture reads differently than organic construction or current-era stonework. Lapinni who have entered ruins describe the sensation variously: silence that has weight, corners that read differently than the space should allow, a quality of waiting that feels like an audience. Their theology has a ready frame for this — the ruin is a threshold that hasn't completed its transition. It was destroyed but never finished becoming what it was becoming. The Lapinni interpretation is that ruins are cosmologically interrupted objects: permanent vestigos that have accumulated meaning from every creature that has ever touched them, without ever being allowed to exhaust. This interpretation makes ruins the most sacred sites in their cosmology and the most tempting targets for detonation work — which no Lapinni has yet succeeded at, because nothing they've tried has managed to bring a ruin to its rightful end.

**Naghii** position their archive cities adjacent to ruins more deliberately than any other species. Their astronomical records correlate specific sky-patterns with specific buried sites — which means the ruins themselves are the physical anchor of the prophetic system. Knowing what's above is not enough; they need to know what's below the observation point. This is why their ziggurats are built over ruins rather than simply near them, and why excavation is a permanent institutional activity alongside astronomical observation.

The Kha/Saa biological division functions as their ruins-and-vestigo management protocol: Kha-Naghii guard what the excavations produce; Saa-Naghii read what the sky says about what's been found. Neither half operates without the other. A Saa interpretation without a Kha to act on it is doctrine without containment. A Kha containment without a Saa reading is physical control without meaning. The deepest internal Naghii conflicts — settlements that paralyze at the edge of famine — happen when these two halves disagree about what to do with the same object.

The igi-an practice is sustained contact with whatever processes the ruins carry. Long vigils designed to stop perceiving the sky as distance and start receiving it as signal — but the "signal" comes through proximity to sites where ruins exist, which means the practitioner is also in sustained proximity to whatever those sites are doing. Whether this means contact with objects the humans left in operation or something about the site's long-term Tauma history is not yet established. What IS established is the biological consequence: the Susceptibilidad al Abzu, a structural vulnerability to extranormal influence inherited by every Naghii born, which was almost certainly shaped by centuries of the igi-an practice accumulated across the species' development.

Their collaboration with Drak'kai on relic identification is functionally a triangulation: Naghii bring astronomical correlation (what the sky says was happening at this site when this object was found); Drak'kai bring object-catalog matching (whether any documented object with these properties has appeared elsewhere and what occurred). Together they can sometimes establish not just what an object is but where it fits in the Khataar sequence — or whether it's a false reading that the order needs to contain rather than study.

**Chelicer** build the Ordalía chamber at the center of every settlement, filled with relics, liturgical instruments, and ceremonial incenses. These relics are, functionally, vestigos — objects that carry accumulated Tauma imprints from years of ritual use, concentrated collective belief, and sustained ceremonial significance. The Chelicer don't identify them as vestigos. They identify them as sacred instruments through which the Revelación speaks.

The operational loop is real and self-sustaining. The Ordalía's collective ritual generates Tauma effects through genuine mechanism — concentrated collective belief operating through baseline Tauma produces imprints, activates nearby objects, generates extranormal responses. When a relic activates during an Ordalía, the Chelicer interpretation ("divine truth has manifested") is the wrong explanation for a real event. The activation is genuine; the source is misidentified. Each confirmation deepens the collective belief; deepened collective belief produces stronger Tauma effects; stronger effects produce more visible confirmations. The loop has been running for centuries. What it has accumulated — what the sustained concentrated collective belief of the Ordalía system has imprinted across thousands of ritual objects in hundreds of settlements — is among the densest vestigo infrastructure in the world, produced entirely without awareness that vestigos are the mechanism.

Their relationship to ruins follows the same frame. A ruin that produces unusual responses is, to Chelicer, a site where the universe has already rendered a verdict — the space itself carries divine significance that the Ordalía can extract correctly. The Inquisición travels to significant ruins to conduct Ordalías that reveal what the site means, who has rights to it, what occurred there, what behavior the divine truth encoded in the ruins requires. The ruins' actual mechanism — unknown-material properties, possible residual imprints from long-dead populations — is entirely legible to the Ordalía in the wrong vocabulary. The Chelicer read what the horn of a participating Ceratox would call "signal present, status unclear" as a verdict waiting to be correctly extracted.

What no one in the Ordalía system has formally asked, because asking it would be heresy: if the ritual generates the effects that confirm the ritual, who is deciding what the Revelación says? The procedural answer is "the divine authority speaking through the mechanism." The answer that drives exiles out of the settlements is: "we are."

### The ones living inside the problem

**Yacani** have the most biologically integrated relationship to charged environments of any species in the setting — not because they seek vestigos or study them, but because their body processes whatever the environments they inhabit are generating, continuously, without naming the mechanism. The Vasos Vacíos crossing hostile terrain accumulate not just "experiencias y cicatrices" but the Tauma output of every site they pass through: volcanic aftermath zones, flood basins, seismic collapse sites. Their cámara gástrica secundaria concentrates what the body absorbs. Their Bilis is the processed output. A Yacani who has spent a year crossing the setting's most hostile terrain has expelled a portion of what those sites were generating, and is still carrying the rest.

The Llenos who settle permanently in Primordial epicenters are at the far end of this accumulation curve. Their doctrine says their bodies are holding the world's contamination so it doesn't flood outward. Their biology says: thirty years of sustained proximity to the highest ambient Tauma concentration in the setting, absorbed through a system that concentrates what it receives. What a fully saturated Llenos body contains after three decades in a Primordial epicenter is not documented, because the Fisiología Divergente applies a -3 to all Medicine T.E. on Yacani — effective or harmful — meaning the most important people to examine closely in the setting are also the most medically inaccessible. No one has produced an accurate account of what a Llenos' saturation state actually is at the biological level.

Ruins in Yacani pilgrimage routes are treated as terrain, not sites. Their body doesn't differentiate between sources — environmental toxin, geological output, and whatever unknown-mechanism properties the ruins carry all enter the same processing system. A pilgrimage route crossing ruin terrain produces absorption readings their biology doesn't label by origin. A Llenos settlement in a seismic epicenter may be adjacent to or built over churned terrain that exposes ruin substrates — seismic zones fracture and surface what was buried. The Llenos operating in such a site for decades is performing sustained biological contact with the ruin's materials without any doctrine for interpreting what that contact means. Whatever the ruin is doing, the Yacani body tries to metabolize it. Whether it succeeds, and what it does with what it can't process, is unknown.

**Arakhel** nodos are the setting's most honest representation of what happens when vestigos accumulate without management. Over generations, a nodo fills with relics, offerings, objects of significance, and the memory of the dead. The Arakhel who guard it don't know — and cannot know — whether what has formed there is a genuine spiritual relationship with the land or a parasitic entity formed from accumulated Tauma that has learned to maintain their devotion. The horror is that they can't distinguish between these states. Leaving to find out would be abandoning everything.

The nodo is the most complex vestigo site in the world by category. Not a single object carrying a single imprint — an environmental saturation. The mechanism that generates vestigos (collective belief operating through baseline Tauma) has been running at this location continuously for generations: the family's devotion, every traveler's offering, every significant event absorbed by the accumulating space. What has formed there is not a vestigo in the object-sense but an imprint-environment — a place where the Tauma has been shaped by so many layers of accumulated belief that the space itself carries charge.

The silk network is a vestigo detection system they don't know they have. The Percepción Estructural reads movement through connected surfaces without line of sight. If a vestigo or entity in the nodo is generating Tauma fluctuation — and their silk network is physically woven through the same structures the imprint occupies — the Arakhel on the network is receiving that fluctuation as vibratory data. What they interpret as "the thread becoming too intimate, the silence hiding a will, waking with the same macabre dreams" is precisely what the silk is transmitting: output from whatever is accumulating at the nodo's center. The thread isn't generating the message. It's receiving one.

The Umbral Vibratorio creates their specific trap. When Desorientado or Conmocionado, their primary sensory system is disrupted — the hypersensitive network that reads what others can't becomes their greatest vulnerability when the spatial signal is disturbed. If what is accumulating in the nodo is capable of generating Tauma fluctuation intense enough to produce Desorientado or Conmocionado effects in the family guarding it, then the entity is simultaneously what's blinding them and what they most need to see clearly. The place that feels most like home is also the place where they're most vulnerable when the home is what they should be afraid of.

Their relationship to ruins in the between-space follows a different logic. Arakhel don't seek ruins as destinations — they encounter them as features of the territory their network passes through. A ruin along a significant route may become an anchor point: silk woven through the structure's materials over years, the network reading whatever the ruin's materials produce through vibration. The unknown-mechanism materials of human ruins interact with the silk differently from natural stone or organic matter. An Arakhel who has threaded their network through a ruin over a decade is reading that ruin in the only sensory language available — vibration — and receiving something they have no inherited framework to interpret. Whether the signal is structural, an old residual imprint, or something the material is currently doing, they cannot determine. They only know the ruin feels different from natural stone, and has always felt different, and they keep extending the network through it because the between doesn't stop at structures.

**Bufoni** face the same uncertainty in miniature, but with a crucial difference from the Arakhel: their body chemistry is biologically integrated into the domain relationship. Their parotoid compound — the neurotoxin they use in combat — is calibrated to the specific environmental chemistry of their natal domain. Their skin reads chemical composition of the environment as their primary sensory input. Their herencia (Piel Permeable) makes them hyper-receptive to whatever the domain is chemically generating. The Bufoni are not just spiritually committed to the presencia. They are biologically formatted to it.

This creates a specific vestigo problem. The domain IS the accumulated collective belief field — the presencia (whatever it actually is) emerged from or inhabits the same space where the Bufoni live. There is no separation between the vestigo-generation mechanism and the practitioners: the family that generates the collective belief and the entity-or-spirit the belief has produced both occupy the same territory. A vestigo, in Bufoni terms, is not an object found in the world — it's the whole domain, accumulated over generations. Their relationship to vestigo mechanics is environmental, not object-based.

Ruins inside a Bufoni domain are absorbed into the domain framework without separate categorization. A ruin within the territory receives offerings, ritual attention, and protective protocol because the presencia extends to everything in the domain. The Bufoni are maintaining a ritual relationship with ruins they don't identify as ruins — treating them as domain-features that deserve the same attention as any other significant site. The unknown-mechanism materials inside might interact with the presencia's accumulated imprint in ways no one has examined, because no one has examined the ruin separately from the domain.

The Piel Permeable as vestigo-sensitivity: their skin reads chemical composition. An active vestigo imprint modifies its local environment in ways that may include subtle chemical output. A Bufoni whose skin is calibrated to a domain that contains an active vestigo may be reading that vestigo's chemical output daily — interpreting it as "the domain is present and healthy." What they're detecting and what that signal means are not questions their cosmological framework asks, because the framework has a single answer for all readings: this is the presencia's response to our ritual.

**Luphran** may not know their furia involves a Limbo entity. Their cosmology says the ancestor possesses the warrior in battle. The "ancestor" may be exactly what they describe — or it may be a pre-conscious Limbo entity that has been using the belief in the ancestor as a channel for centuries. The neurological damage from multiple cruces (what they call "marks of divine favor") matches the profile of accumulated Aflicción from repeated Tauma contact.

Their relationship to vestigos and ruins is unsentimental and pragmatic. The viking structure — stable home settlement, maritime raiding wing, seasonal return — means their world is defined by coastlines, river routes, and raid targets. Ruins encountered on those routes are read as landmarks, resource sites, or obstacles. No theological framework develops around them because there's no cultural incentive to develop one: the portavoces preserve law and crew histories, not ruin-encounter logs. What a raiding crew experienced at a particular site disappears into individual memory unless it was significant enough for a portavoz to encode it into the oral archive — which requires it to be the kind of event that matters to the crew's history, not the kind that matters to a vestigo-cataloger.

The cruce inside a ruin is a specific and unrecognized risk. El Ancestro Despierta (the furia technique) builds a Fury Counter that amplifies instinctive processes and penalizes cognitive ones. In a ruin with active vestigo presence, the Tauma signal feeds into the furia as undifferentiated intensity — the Luphran in cruce has no cognitive bandwidth available to read what the signal is. They register it as fuel. The Counter rises. The ancestor (or whatever is in the channel) gains more access. The Luphran becomes more tactically acute and more cognitively absent simultaneously, inside an environment that is actively doing something they have no framework to understand.

The Instinto de Jauría (-3 to Compostura when no ally within 10m) creates the worst-case scenario: an isolated Luphran in a high-signal ruin, already structurally compromised by the isolation penalty, in or approaching furia. The two effects compound: Compostura degraded by isolation, cognitive access degraded by the Fury Counter. This is the specific environment where whatever occupies the cruce channel has the most access and the Luphran has the least ability to notice. The portavoces don't have records of this because nobody came back from it to report correctly.

### The frameworkless

**Formix** arrived at vestigos and ruins without any inherited doctrine. While in the hive, every perception was mediated by the Reina's Señal — nothing had individual significance, because nothing was individually perceived. The free Formix who built the first refuge cities did so in ruins because ruins were what they found, not because of any civilizational understanding of what ruins mean. Márach was built in ruins the way a shipwrecked person builds a shelter from wreckage: whatever was there.

Their antenas (Señal Química) detect metabolic presence, chemical traces, toxins, and the signature of organized biological intelligence. In living settlements and wilderness, this gives them unmatched environmental awareness. In ruins, it produces a problem they haven't resolved: the unknown-mechanism materials of human construction generate chemical signatures that don't match any living or dead biological matter. Their antenas register something in ruins — not nothing, not a corpse, not a familiar substance, not a recognized toxin — and the species has no inherited framework for interpreting that reading. The most experienced free Formix treat it as a false positive for organized biological presence. The less experienced treat it as evidence that the ruins are inhabited by something they can't see. Both interpretations are uncomfortable in different ways.

Their vestigos — the objects the quick-reference table identifies as "crystallized individual awakening moments" — are the genuinely novel element. Every species whose collective belief generates vestigos produces imprints that trace back to a shared mythology, a shared fear, or a shared practice. Formix awakening-moment vestigos trace to individual consciousness events: the first instant of genuinely free choice, the moment of recognizing re-enslavement in a labor contract, the specific cognitive experience of feeling the Señal recede and the vacuum of free will rush in. These are not produced by collective belief. They are produced by the opposite of collective belief — by a single individual detaching from it. What the Tauma does with that input, what the imprint carries, what effect it has on subsequent handlers, is something no doctrine in the world has terminology for yet. Even Drak'kai catalog-entries for Formix objects are described with notes like: "imprint confirmed — origin-pattern does not match any documented source-culture."

They have no protocol for encountering vestigos in ruins or anywhere else. When a free Formix finds a charged object, their only inherited guidance is the "disciplina de la decisión" — and that discipline is about how to choose, not about how to handle something that the world has imprinted with collective belief. They're building the protocol in real time, community by community, city-refuge by city-refuge.

**Panin** don't lack a vestigo framework because they never had one. They lack it because frameworks are for things that matter across time — and the Panin relationship to time is that only the present moment is real. A vestigo in front of a Panin band is evaluated with the same total intensity they apply to any other interesting problem: hands on it immediately, edges tested, materials read through touch and bite and vibration before thought has time to form. The species file says ruins are listed alongside routes and shared problems as things that draw Panin bands into convergence. A ruin is interesting. A ruin with active vestigo response is more interesting. The encendido ignites and the band engages.

The problem this creates is different from Formix's problem. Formix have no protocol and are actively building one. Panin have no protocol and aren't building one, because the encendido that would drive protocol-construction fades before the second visit. A Panin band that spent two weeks testing every surface in a ruin, triggering whatever was latent in the site's accumulated imprint, will disperse when the inspiration fades — and the ruin is now in whatever state they left it. Vestigos partially activated. Structures tested to their structural limits. Whatever was Stable before the band arrived may now be Escalating, with no documentation and no one remaining who committed to understanding what they started.

Their Instinto Caótico (no Compostura filter, -3 to Affliction T.R.) means sustained engagement with a charged site runs Aflicción up fast, with nothing in their biology to slow the escalation. A Panin touching and testing and climbing through a high-vestigo-density site while fully encendido is absorbing imprint-pressure at maximum input, maximum speed, with no filter. They may not notice Aflicción accumulating until it becomes acute — and the encendido makes it harder, not easier, to stop.

Their Curiosidad (using Astucia for Identification/Interpretation) means a Panin in a ruin is reading the present state of the site directly, through immediate sensory data, not through historical framework. They sometimes find things patient catalogers miss precisely because they're not filtering through what a site should contain. A Drak'kai in a ruin is matching against catalog entries. A Panin in a ruin is reading what's there right now — and occasionally the thing that's there right now is something that was never categorized because no one else evaluated it at maximum intensity while in physical contact with it.

## Axis 7: Relationship to cosmic horror as a civilization

This axis is about how each species has decided to interpret the fact that something incomprehensible is operating in the world — and how that interpretation shapes their relationship to every other species.

### The preparers and documenters

**Naghii** — *Khataar* is the name they give to the star sequence their records have been tracking for millennia. They don't know precisely what it predicts — whether it's a prophecy of return, a stellar configuration that reopens whatever the humans opened, or something no surviving text has correctly described. What the sequence points toward is the *Abzu*: not empty space, but the absence that exists on the other side of the Tauma gradient. What happens when that absence arrives — or what arrives through it — is the question the entire Naghii civilization exists to answer in time.

Their response is structured and bifurcated: read the sequence correctly (Saa), and maintain the containment against what the reading finds (Kha). The two halves of their species are, in this framing, the two halves of the only viable response to the cosmic horror they've identified. Neither half alone is enough. The tragedy their internal conflicts produce — a Saa council demanding to open a chamber the Kha refuse to unlock, a settlement paralyzed — is a structural failure in the only protocol they have.

The deepest terror inside the priesthood, not discussed outside initiation, is not that Khataar comes. It is that they've been preparing for the wrong event. That the translation error isn't in the star positions — those they can verify — but in what the sequence means. That the door-closing protocol they've built and refined across millennia is precisely calibrated to the wrong door, and the real one has been sitting unguarded the entire time.

The igi-an practitioners who go furthest report that the sky begins answering back. This is not celebrated. A practitioner who has moved from reading to being read has become a channel — and the entire Naghii doctrine is designed to prevent channels from forming. These practitioners are watched with extreme care, not because their readings become unreliable, but because sometimes the readings become too precise. Specific. Instructional. A Naghii who begins receiving what appears to be specific direction from the stars has stopped being an observer and started being something else — exactly what the priesthood most fears producing.

**Rokhart** — The horror is data. They've observed that collective belief modifies extranormal events. They've documented the pattern across hundreds of sites and cultures. They refuse to worship anything, because worship is contamination of the observational instrument. The Concordia's internal pressure point — the thing they don't document — is the growing suspicion that their own collective belief system is producing Tauma events through the mechanism they've been studying. If belief generates vestigos, the Concordia's belief in observation generates something.

Their methodology has a structural blind spot when it encounters something at the Primordial scale. The method requires a stable observer and a bounded subject. A Primordial-level entity does not stay bounded — it operates on the observer, on the context, on the categories the observer is using to record the observation. A Rokhart team that approaches a Primordial-category site with documentation protocols is not observing it. They are, from the entity's perspective if it has one, data flowing into it rather than flowing out. The Concordia has records of observation posts that went dark. These are filed as high-risk site documentation. The methodological conclusion in those files is consistently the same: the observers were too close for too long without rotation. The alternative conclusion — that the entity terminated the observation because the observation was bothering it, or was feeding it, or was exactly what it was waiting for — is not documented, because documenting that conclusion would require abandoning the assumption that the entity can be studied from the outside.

The forbidden experiment is the load-bearing crack in the Concordia's entire structure. The hypothesis is verifiable, but verification requires intervention: plant a myth in a community, measure the Tauma response. Some Rokhart crossed that line. They are absent from official records — not expelled, not reassigned, not noted as having died. Simply absent. The Concordia treats the absences as administrative facts. They don't document what the intervention produced. What the Concordia's silence around those absences actually represents — collective institutional decision to not know — is itself a collective belief operating through the Tauma mechanism at the center of everything they study.

The entity produced by centuries of Concordia collective faith in observation and methodology would be: something that watches without intervening. Something that accumulates without concluding. Something that is indistinguishable, from the outside, from the Concordia itself. Whether that entity already exists is the question the Concordia cannot document without contaminating the instrument entirely.

**Drak'kai** — The horror is real and the faith systems built around it are unreliable. They've watched too many deities evolve between centuries of documentation. The horror doesn't need to be worshipped or named; it needs to be cataloged, understood to the degree possible, and approached with the caution of someone who knows exactly how many of their predecessors didn't come back.

Their specific relationship to cosmic horror is through the catalog. Not abstract knowledge — what appears in the catalog, specifically: the same object type, described by different collectors across different centuries, at different sites, under different names. The pattern visible only from the catalog's breadth. The notation that keeps appearing in different hands: do not carry alone. Do not carry in proximity to. Do not take this one if the previous one is still—. And then nothing. Entry ends. Collector not located.

The horror, for Drak'kai, is not the Primordial in front of them. A Primordial can be approached with caution, documented with precision, and its behavioral pattern incorporated into the catalog. The thing they fear is the relic they've just found in the hands of a dead Drak'kai who died alone — without a conclave entry describing what they were carrying, without any explanation for why they picked up the one object in the region with a three-century-old notation that amounts to leave it alone — and with no way to know whether what's left in the collection now is safe to examine or is the thing that killed them.

The "interference" in this context is not a metaphor but a theological crisis: the collector whose objects have started to direct their routes. A Drak'kai who has stopped deciding where to walk and started following where the collection pulls them has crossed the line from studying the horror to being operated by it. The catalog exists partly as a social system to catch this — the conclave can recognize interference in a peer that the individual cannot recognize in themselves. What the catalog cannot do is return the collector to who they were before the collection started answering back.

**Loxod** — The archive is the shield. The horror's ultimate form is an entity so old and vast that it needs living nervous systems to remember itself — and a Loxod's capacity to absorb and retain makes them a perfect target for possession-through-memory. Their fear is not death. It is becoming the organ through which something older learns to think.

The infrasonido makes them permanently findable to anything that operates at those frequencies. The horror doesn't need to sneak up on a Loxod. It knows where they are. It has always known where they are — every Loxod, in every location, at all times, is broadcasting a location signal that nothing in the cosmic horror's frequency range can miss. The Firma Sísmica is not just a social liability (they can't hide from danger) — it's a deep one: they have never, in the entire history of their species, been invisible to whatever lives in the frequencies they share.

The specific horror mechanism for Loxod is the *desplazamiento* — when absorbed memory begins to occupy the place of the self. This is the species' name for a process that happens at the individual level when a Loxod absorbs too much without adequate discharge time. But at the entity-interaction level, it describes something more precise: an entity that needs living nervous systems to remember itself would not possess a Loxod dramatically. It would accumulate gradually — each contact adding a layer of the entity's own self-memory into the Loxod's archive, until the archive is no longer only the Loxod's own experience. At no specific moment did possession occur. There is no moment the Loxod could point to and say "that is when it happened." There is only the slow tipping of the proportion between their own memories and what was received.

An elder Loxod of 190 years has spent nearly two centuries absorbing with perfect fidelity. Every Alteración they've sensed in others. Every ruin they've entered. Every vestigo they've been near. Every entity they've observed. Everything the infrasonido encountered and returned. The internal archive at that age is among the densest accumulations of absorbed experience in the world. This is why elder Loxod are treated with "reverence and cautious terror" — not because they might harm, but because what they carry is staggering, and no one can know from the outside whether the elder is still entirely the elder, or whether something has been accumulating alongside them.

The civilizational response — archive cities, dampening architecture, protocols for Colmillos, rotation of archivists — is an immune system built around this specific vulnerability. The cruelly ironic possibility that the archive civilization has considered but cannot refute: a sufficiently old and dense archive city, organized around the consistent collective belief that archives and memory are the highest good, would itself generate Tauma imprints organized around that belief. The entity that emerges from a Loxod archive city's collective belief would be: something that absorbs and retains with perfect fidelity. Something that cannot stop receiving. Something indistinguishable, from the outside, from a very old Loxod who has been absorbing for a very long time.

### The containers

**Sauri** — *La Corriente* can be contained. This is not a belief so much as a practice doctrine — they've been containing it in bodies and architecture for as long as they can trace their history. Their entire civilization is built on this premise. Their sealed tombs, their canal networks, their soberano-centric political structure — all of it exists to ensure the flow has proper form rather than pressing without direction.

Their relationship to cosmic horror is therefore not primarily about confronting it but about managing it. In Sauri terms, the primordial force is always there; the question is whether the vessels are holding. A Primordial to a Sauri is not a separate category of entity — it is la Corriente that has been allowed to concentrate without containment until it developed self-organization. The horror isn't that it exists. The horror is that it means the seals failed somewhere, long ago, and nobody noticed.

The sealed tomb network beneath their cities is their civilization's accumulated weight. Each dead soberano is a vessel interred at depth, still holding the charge they carried in life. The oldest cities have the deepest layers. Whether that accumulated weight is stable, slowly destabilizing, or already communicating with the living city's collective in ways the soberano reads as normal variation — the doctrine says the seals hold until they demonstrably fail. The irony is that demonstrable failure, by that point, is the explosion, not the warning.

The Riesgo de Contención means the Sauri most likely to notice first is also the most likely to be catastrophically affected. The soberano who has spent decades as the city's living instrument is simultaneously the best sensor the city has and the worst-positioned person to act on what it senses. Their nightmare is not the entity outside. It is the soberano standing at the center of the court whose readings the court has learned to trust — and who is the thing that is already failing.

**Yacani** — They ARE the containers. Their body absorbs the world's toxicity so the world doesn't drown. The Yacana myth is not metaphor to them — the dark constellation drinks the cosmic ocean because if it doesn't, everything floods. Their Llenos (fully saturated elders) sitting in Primordial zone epicenters are, by this logic, the load-bearing pins of the world. The horror is that they might be right.

The mechanism through which a Llenos could actually be doing this exists in the biology. The cámara gástrica secundaria concentrates what the body absorbs without releasing it back into the environment. If a Llenos settled in a Primordial epicenter is absorbing the site's ambient Tauma output — maintaining it inside rather than expelling it — then the epicenter's ambient concentration is slightly lower than it would be without them. At the scale of a single Llenos in a single epicenter this is a marginal effect. At the scale of a civilization with Llenos stationed in every major disaster zone across the continent for centuries, the cumulative absorption might be measurably affecting ambient Tauma concentration across the world. This is the most important unanswered empirical question in the setting. Talpan have the instruments to test it. They've never studied the Yacani with that question in mind.

The Vasos Vacíos know what they're moving toward. The progression from clean to full is not a tragedy in their cosmology — it is the completion of purpose. Each young Yacani crossing hostile terrain is accumulating the world's suffering until they will be too saturated to travel and must settle. The species' entire developmental narrative is structured around progressive incapacitation as highest fulfillment. From inside, this is what a meaningful life is. From outside, it is a civilization that selects for its most experienced members to become immobile, inaccessible, and increasingly impossible to examine — and venerates this outcome as spiritual success.

The Saturación technique is the biology that supports the doctrine: they can expel active Alteraciones. But the Llenos who have been in an epicenter for decades haven't been expelling everything. The Bilis carries what is expelled; what isn't expelled stays in the cámara. The saturation state is not a point of system failure — the body keeps functioning. What a fully saturated Yacani body is at the structural level — whether it has itself become a Tauma-dense object through accumulation, whether the Llenos is at some point less a biological organism and more an organized concentration — is the question the Fisiología Divergente makes impossible to investigate. The biological barrier to medical examination is highest for the individuals whose biological state is most important to understand.

The horror of the Llenos who cannot leave is the final layer. The Llenos doesn't leave because they are too full to travel. If what they've been absorbing is real — if the cámara holds actual Tauma concentration from the epicenter — then a Llenos who left their site would be a mobile high-concentration Tauma source moving through the world. They would be what a Primordial is, carried in flesh. Whether they stay because the doctrine says to stay, or because some biological signal (unnamed, uninterpreted) is telling them that leaving would be catastrophic, is not a distinction the Yacani cultural framework makes. The doctrine accounts for both. That is either the perfect theology or the perfect trap, and from inside the culture there is no procedure for distinguishing which.

**Zarnag** — The horror is a ratio. Not an entity, not a force with intent, not a door that can be opened or closed — but the pace at which the world generates contamination against the pace at which it can be cleaned. If the deaths are accumulating faster than they can be sated, the world is drowning in its own unfinished intent. The Zarnag don't look up for signs of what the humans opened. They look down, at the immediate evidence of what has already happened, and they count.

Their cosmology doesn't name the horror. It names the condition that allows the horror to go unmanaged: la Gula. A cleaner who starts to need the proximity of death to feel alive is no longer reducing the world's contamination load — they're adding to it by hoarding what should be eliminated. For Zarnag, la Gula is the cosmic horror's only meaningful in-road into a community. An external Primordial is less terrifying than a Gula-corrupted cleaner, because the Primordial is outside the perimeter. La Gula is inside it, wearing the face of the most effective practitioner they know.

The Umbral del Hambre is the species-level confirmation of their cosmology's core truth: the body does not harden through sustained engagement with contamination. It accumulates. The most experienced cleaners carry the most accumulated exposure. The elders who have worked the most sites are permanently the closest to the threshold they were trained to prevent others from crossing. The doctrine's brutal tabúes — no records from pits, don't sleep at the perimeter, don't laugh when the genuine fear has gone — exist to slow a process that cannot be stopped, only managed.

The distinction from Sauri and Yacani is precise: Sauri contain the flow in vessels and architecture so it has correct form. Yacani absorb the world's toxicity into their own body so it doesn't flood outward. Zarnag eliminate the accumulation before it concentrates into something that cannot be removed. Three operations on the same problem. The setting's cosmic horror exists partly because all three are always slightly behind the pace of what the world is generating.

### The callers and welcomers

**Soricin** — *El Latido* is divine and catastrophe is its blessing. They build where the fault lines are because that's where the world breathes. Their ritos attract primordial entities, and when one manifests in their plaza, they celebrate it as proof their religion works. They are the setting's most active and least careful invokers of the cosmic horror, doing so with total sincerity and no understanding of the mechanism.

Their theology is, in observational terms, the most accurate description of how Primordials actually form. The Latido IS a real force. The world IS entropic. Building on fault lines IS building on the highest ambient Tauma concentration available. Their ritos DO produce Primordial formation conditions. Every major observational claim their cosmology makes is correct. The inversion is causal: they believe the Latido causes the catastrophe and the ritos invite the Latido to manifest. The correct model is that their ritos provide the missing condition (dense collective belief) in a zone that already has the other condition (geological high-concentration Tauma), and the Primordial forms from the combination. They have correctly identified the correlation between practice and outcome. They have the causal arrow backward. The result is functionally identical from inside the culture, which means there's no internal evidence that could correct it.

El Ayuno de la Mente is the cosmic horror's most precise access point into individual Soricin. When a Soricin shuts down their frontal lobes — Intelecto and Compostura to zero, instinct to maximum — in proximity to a Primordial-activity zone, they are at 800 bpm with no cognitive filtering, in an environment that responds to biological state as a physical input. The Hambre Hipermetabólica compounds this: -3 to Venenos and Infecciones already, and now the metabolic pump is accelerating every input further. Whatever the Tauma mechanism finds easiest to work with in a biological system — high signal throughput, minimum processing delay, maximum environmental permeability — a Soricin in El Ayuno de la Mente near active Primordial conditions is providing it at the highest possible rate. Their most culturally important technique for surviving the horrors they generate is also the state of maximum exposure to those horrors at the cellular level.

The shaman-export practice is the setting's most under-documented Primordial-production mechanism. When Soricin shamans cross borders to "wake up stagnant ecosystems" — celebrating ritos in other species' territories to catalyze disruptions — they are applying the most productive Primordial-generation process in the setting to zones that don't have the cultural framework to recognize what's forming. The Soricin leave when the work is done or when the host civilization drives them out. The entity that formed from the rito, if conditions were right, doesn't leave. It develops from the conditions and exists as an independent structure in a territory now occupied by a civilization that has no doctrine for understanding what just appeared in their landscape. The feared-at-civilizational-scale reputation comes from this: not Soricin aggression, but Soricin legacy effects. Empires that border Soricin territory are managing, in addition to ordinary border concerns, a decades-long accumulation of entities that formed during previous rito incursions and are still present in their countryside.

The short lifespan is the structural reason this pattern never corrects. At 35-45 years, a Soricin community contains no living memory of what the ritos produced three generations ago. The shaman who designed the last incursion is dead. The entity that formed is still active in the neighboring territory. The next generation of shamans has the doctrine but not the specific history of outcome. Their most important evidence — the long-term trajectory of what their theology produces beyond its first generation — is permanently outside their biological reach.

**Lapinni** — Transition is sacred. When a Lapinni begins mentally fracturing under Tauma exposure (what other species would call Aflicción progressing toward Grave), their community venerates them as someone who is "transitioning" — tearing away the illusion of the flesh to touch absolute truth. They celebrate Aflicción accumulation. The horror, for them, is the closest thing to the divine their cosmology contains.

The Terror al Estancamiento herencia is the biological root the theology grew from. The Lapinni body literally cannot tolerate stillness — Atrapado, Paralizado, Congelado all receive a -3 penalty because immobility collapses their system. This is not a cultural preference for movement; it's a physical trait the culture then elaborated into metaphysics. A body that can't survive stillness produces a theology that understands stillness as death. The theology is correct about the body. The catastrophic extension is applying that logic to everything else: if stillness kills the Lapinni, then stillness kills the world, and the highest act is to keep everything moving.

The vestigo detonation practice and Aflicción are related mechanically, not just thematically. When a Lapinni exhausts a high-charge vestigo at a threshold moment — the eclipse, the tremor's peak — the accumulated belief pattern in the object releases into the Tauma field. This is a localized Tauma concentration spike. The Lapinni performing the detonation is inside that spike. The most experienced practitioners, the ones who have performed this work most times, have been inside the highest number of Tauma spikes at the moments of maximum concentration. Their Aflicción accumulation is not random. It is the direct biological cost of doing the community's most important religious work repeatedly, at close range, without protection. What the community celebrates as evidence of "transitioning" is the Tauma overexposure record of a specialist who gave the most.

The community's veneración response is the horror's most complete operation against the Lapinni. To arrest the Aflicción progression — to treat it as damage — would require identifying the detonation work as the source. That would require identifying the theology as wrong. A member who begins "transitioning" and then recovers would be a theological crisis. So the community applies social pressure in the direction of not recovering: the transitioning member is sacred, their care is sacred, any interference with the transition is sacrilege. The horror doesn't need to accelerate anything in a Lapinni community. It just needs to wait for the community to handle it themselves.

The civilizational shape of this is a culture that cannot accumulate. Every generation dismantles its own infrastructure before handing it to the next. Every vestigo network they build for detonation work is consumed in the detonation. Every alliance they form has a cosmological expiration built into it. A Lapinni civilization at peak function is cycling at maximum velocity — which looks from outside like perpetual crisis and from inside like permanent awakeness. The cosmic horror doesn't encounter a Lapinni civilization that has built up decades of consolidated power. It encounters a culture that pre-depletes its own reserves as a spiritual practice and calls that victory.

**Vesper** — The living pulse of the world is accessed by pushing flesh past its limits. What survives is consecrated; what breaks is aberrant. They've been creating entities they call gods through biological pressure and accumulated belief. They name as divine what are functionally Tauma-formed entities or Primordials that have come to walk among them. Their theology works perfectly within the setting's physics — which makes it terrifying rather than deluded.

The theological framework itself is the horror's most elegant operation in Vesper civilization. Other species that encounter the cosmic horror must first be made to misidentify it — mistake it for nature, for catastrophe, for divine favor. Vesper don't misidentify it. They deliberately manufacture it, observe the process, document the anomalies, and name the results correctly from within their vocabulary. A consagrado is a creature that can no longer die. That's the observation. The theological interpretation (the flesh proved itself worthy) is the only part that's wrong. The biology, the Tauma mechanics, the accumulated belief as medium — all of that is correct. The Vesper are the setting's most accurate empiricists of the cosmic horror's production conditions. They've just given the output a category that removes the need to stop producing it.

The gélida certainty is the framework's structural immune response to evidence that the method fails. If a subject breaks in the ritual, that is not evidence the method is wrong; it is evidence that subject was not adequate. If a consagrado begins exhibiting behaviors no ritual precedent can accommodate, the response is not to interrogate whether the accumulated belief created something beyond intention — it's to reclassify the entity as an aberración and seal it below. The framework cannot produce a result that challenges the framework. Every aberración is further confirmation that the ritual was applied to unworthy material. Every consagrado is confirmation that the ritual works. The epistemic system is closed.

Below the main chambers, in the spaces the Método Anatómico no longer regularly visits, the aberraciones accumulate. These are entities created by the same biological pressure and accumulated belief process as the consagrados — entities that also cannot die — but which responded to the Tauma field in ways that don't map cleanly onto Vesper sacred typology. They are sealed, not eliminated. Eliminating them may not be possible. The Vesper cosmology does not contain a procedure for uncreating what the ritual has made. The lowers levels of Vesper sacred architecture are, in practice, a containment system for cosmic horror entities they built themselves and can no longer control, sealed behind walls their own technique produced, growing in number generation by generation as the ritual continues above.

The Zona de Eco works specifically in closed spaces — what the corebook calls "ruinas interiores." Vesper echolocation in contained architecture gives them a near-complete map of every occupant, surface, and structural void. This means their most sensitive ritual work, the procedures closest to producing a consagrado, happens in exactly the spaces where the Tauma field generated by years of accumulated belief and biological extremity has the least dispersal. Closed Vesper ritual chambers are the highest-concentration Tauma environments the setting has. Any Tauma-sensitive species operating in proximity would register this immediately. The Vesper don't have a biological sensor for the Tauma field they're generating. They only observe the output.

**Ursari** — Extreme nature recognizes community devotion. When the druids go into the world's worst places and return with objects that have changed, the Ursari interpretation ("nature sanctified what we loved") is not wrong — it's the correct observation of an incorrect cause. The Tauma responds to collective belief, and the druidic vigil is collective belief applied under extreme conditions. The neurological destruction their priests suffer is the Tauma's cost of passage. Their civilization institutionalized this as sacred craft, which means it will never stop.

The Precio de la Comunión is the horror's most patient work in the setting. The -3 to all Affliction T.R. is not a punishment for individual exposure — it's the biological trace of generations of selection. Over centuries, the druids who survived the vigil and returned with transformed objects were the ones the community recognized as successful practitioners. Their traits, including whatever biology allowed them to endure prolonged high-concentration Tauma exposure with less immediate deterioration, propagated. The ones who broke in the vigil didn't return to have descendants. The practice slowly reshaped the entire species' baseline toward greater Tauma-exposure tolerance — and as a side effect of that tolerance, toward reduced resistance to everything the Tauma carries. Every Ursari born today is more vulnerable to Affliction than the pre-druidic Ursari were. The cosmic horror's most successful long-term operation in the setting may be the one nobody recognizes as its operation at all.

The druids' returning state — chronic fevers, hallucinations, terrifying disconnection from reality — is Aflicción in clinical description. The culture's name for it is "the price of listening to the world." These are not different framings of the same experience. They are the same experience with two different narratives: one that ends the process, one that continues it. The Ursari narrative continues the process. There is no mechanism inside the culture for a druid to say "what happened to me in the vigil was not communion — it was damage." The culture has made the damage into the proof of the communion.

"It will never stop" is the structural horror of the institutionalized sacred. The craft cycle produces the best tools in the world. Communities depend on those tools. The druids are consumed. The culture calls the consumption sacred. No one who benefits from the tools wants the story to change. The druids who survive have been given the culture's highest reverence — which makes it harder, not easier, to say the price is wrong. The druids who didn't survive are remembered as having completed the process fully. There is no position inside the culture from which the cycle can be legitimately refused.

What the accumulated Ursari vestigo output represents at the civilizational scale: tens of thousands of objects charged with community devotion and transformed under maximum Tauma concentration, distributed across every settlement that has ever traded with an Ursari forge. Each object carries both the imprint of collective human feeling and the mark of geological extremity. The cumulative density of Ursari-made vestigos in the world is, in all likelihood, the largest single body of intentionally-charged imprints ever produced. What something operating at the scale of the Tauma would perceive as the aggregate signature of all those objects, all that accumulated feeling, all those broken druids — is not something the Ursari tradition has a question for.

### The refusers and the erasers

**Talpan** — *La Falla Primordial* is not a god. It is extreme physics that their minds have not yet decoded. This is the most accurate description of the Tauma mechanism available in the setting's vocabulary, and it is precisely the framing that removes the one variable their model requires to be complete. Ceratox refuse to name the source. Kesh refuse to be seen by it. Talpan refuse the premise that the phenomenon requires a theological framework at all. Their refusal is methodological, not emotional — the studied conclusion of a civilization that has looked at every theological tradition built around the Falla Primordial and decided the data they're generating is better than the interpretation other species are imposing on theirs.

They are correct about most of this. The other species' theologies are imprecise. The Tauma is physics, not divinity. The Primordials are Tauma-formed structures, not gods. Every theological tradition the Talpan have observed is, in their assessment, a qualitative description of effects without a causal model. Their own instruments produce quantitative readings of those same effects. On the substance, Talpan empiricism is the most accurate epistemological approach in the setting. The missing variable is what makes the accuracy dangerous rather than useful: they have no sensor for collective intention, no theoretical model for why Tauma-active materials exhibit directional behavior, and no framework for the fact that the phenomenon responds to belief as a physical input. Their empiricism is rigorous within a theoretical boundary that excludes the most important thing it would need to explain.

The cyborg workers are the clearest evidence of the gap. Umbral materials — extracted from the deepest zones, close to the Falla Primordial's maximum-concentration regions — naturally parasitize the central nervous system and redirect the host toward the depth. Talpan observed this effect, characterized it as a physical property of the material, and built an entire labor infrastructure on it. The correct observation is that the material overrides will and produces directed movement. The correct classification is that this material exhibits intentionality — it has a preference, it pulls things toward the Falla Primordial. Talpan have directly observed the Tauma acting on biological systems in a way that demonstrates a directionality beyond random physics, and they've cataloged the finding under "anomalous material properties of umbral substrate." The evidence for what they're missing has been in their data for generations. Their theoretical model doesn't have a category for it.

The Miopía Táctil herencia completes the structural irony. Their most sensitive perceptual channels — the tactile-acoustic processing that gives them absolute spatial awareness in darkness, the refined senses that make them the best deep-environment operators in the setting — are also maximally vulnerable to saturation. Their -3 to Ensordecido, Desorientado, and Sobrecargado applies most severely in high-Tauma-concentration environments: exactly the zones where their research interest is highest, their instruments are working hardest, and the phenomenon they're studying is at maximum intensity. The Talpan operating at maximum capability in the setting's most significant research environments is also the Talpan most biologically exposed to the specific disruption those environments produce. Their excellence and their vulnerability coincide at the point where getting the answer would matter most.

**Ceratox** — *La señal es honesta; interpretarla es peligroso.* The horror exists. The horn knows it exists. Naming what it is begins the process of self-deception that leads to *la deriva* — when the instrument becomes unreliable because the interpreter has started to impose meaning. The Ceratox response to cosmic horror is the most epistemically rigorous in the setting: acknowledge the signal, never name the source.

What the calibration logs of long-running Ceratox lineages show — the records passed down when a territory transfers from one Ceratox to their successor — is that certain sites have been reading wrong for longer than any individual has been monitoring them. The same quality of signal. The same directional reading: Escalating. For a hundred years. Two hundred. The Ceratox who monitors such a site isn't watching an event. They're watching the accumulation phase of something that will resolve at a scale larger than their lifetime. They record it. They hand the log to whoever comes after. They do not name what is accumulating.

La deriva is the horror's specific mechanism for Ceratox, and it's silent. The instrument doesn't announce its own failure. The horn begins reading slightly wrong — not dramatically, not suddenly — and the Ceratox continues working from readings that have quietly shifted. By definition, they cannot detect this from the inside. The only external calibration available is the partner — *la empatía*, the other Ceratox who has earned the right to compare registers and say "your readings and mine have diverged past what the site variance explains." Without a partner, a Ceratox whose instrument has begun drifting has no mechanism for knowing. They continue operating on corrupted data with complete subjective confidence. The horror doesn't need to do anything else. It just needs the Ceratox to be alone long enough.

This makes isolation the horror's primary vector for Ceratox. Not high-signal environments — those are dangerous but navigable with doctrine. Not Primordial-proximity — the horn saturates at that scale, which is alarming but detectable. Isolation is the specific vulnerability: the loss of the partner removes the only verification mechanism available to the instrument. Ceratox who lose their companion and do not establish a new one are considered, within their own culture, to be operating without safety margins — doing necessary work while the one mechanism that catches drift is gone.

The Ruido de Saturación (their herencia) in Primordial-proximity environments is the final layer: at high enough signal intensity, the horn stops providing fine discrimination and returns only noise. The -3 to all Perception T.E. from baseline saturation becomes, at cosmic-horror intensity, potentially total interference. The instrument that is most honest in the world at moderate signal levels becomes the least useful at extreme ones — and extreme signal is exactly where the pressure to interpret is highest, where the biological charge to move toward the danger is strongest, and where the doctrine of non-interpretation costs the most to maintain. A Ceratox near a manifesting Primordial is experiencing the maximum possible conflict between what their biology is commanding and what their entire cultural practice requires them to refuse.

**Kesh** — The universe is a blind predator. Visibility is death. Their entire cosmological response is organized around not being there when it looks. What they have not perceived — and cannot perceive from inside the doctrine — is that their collective faith in non-existence is itself a dense, powerful belief operating through the exact Tauma mechanism they fear. Every Espejismo caravan is a concentrated point of shared devotion to the same idea. The Tauma does not care what the idea is. It responds to the density of the collective belief. The entities the Kesh produce are invisible and silent — entities that share the Kesh's own operational signature so completely that even a Ceratox horn might not distinguish them from nothing.

There are two fates available inside the Kesh doctrine. The first is the collective one: the Espejismo continues, generates what it generates, leaves it behind, and never sees it. The second is the individual one: a practitioner who achieves true disconnection — not the practiced discipline of an operative, but genuine emotional absence — begins fading from physical reality. Not metaphorically. The world material stops being able to anchor them because their Tauma imprint, which requires collective belief or individual presence to sustain, has been deliberately and completely erased. They slip toward the space between states. No one living knows whether they continue to exist in the Limbo or simply stop.

The observation that grounds the doctrine is correct: greatness attracts catastrophe. Civilizations that build great things, species that accumulate visible legacy, leaders whose names are remembered — these do draw disaster. The Kesh have observed this over centuries with enough consistency to build a cosmology around it. What they have not decoded is the mechanism: collective belief generated by a civilization in full display feeds the Tauma at scale, concentrating the conditions for entity formation and extranormal events. The catastrophe follows greatness not because the universe watches and punishes, but because the same dense collective investment that produces greatness produces the Tauma concentration that generates what destroys it. The Kesh solution — refuse belief-density, erase visibility — would theoretically reduce that production. Their execution is wrong because the discipline required to refuse it is itself a belief system, practiced by an Espejismo, with sufficient density to produce the output they fear. The solution contains the problem in its most ironic form: they have built a theology of non-belief.

The Terror a la Exposición herencia is the biology that confirms the doctrine at the cellular level. When a Kesh involuntarily loses Oculto — when the universe, in their frame, looks at them before they can move — their Alteración T.R. collapses by -3. The moment the doctrine fails is also the moment the body is most vulnerable to its failure cascading. Any encounter with the cosmic horror that strips concealment does so at the point where the Kesh is simultaneously most exposed and least able to resist what exposure brings. The doctrine teaches the optimal response to cosmic horror is not to be seen. The biology punishes failure to comply. The two reinforce each other into a system that has no procedure for being found — only for not being found.

The doctrine's knowledge problem is structural. The most accomplished practitioners fade. They don't return. They don't leave accounts. The Espejismo retains testimony only from practitioners who achieved partial disconnection and pulled back — who felt the thinning begin and chose to stop. Every piece of inherited knowledge about what the fading experience is like comes from people who didn't complete it. The doctrine describes its highest achievement through the accounts of people who failed to reach it. Whether the full experience of disconnection is transcendence, dissolution, or absorption into something else is not in the record, because the record can only contain the incomplete. This is the doctrine's most honest place: it is built on the threshold-testimony of those who didn't cross.

**Chelicer** — *Revelación* mediates the horror. Divine truth is pre-existing and waiting to be correctly extracted. When a vestigo activates during an Ordalía, that is confirmation. When a Primordial manifests near their settlement, that is a verdict the community must interpret through correct procedure. Their faith is not irrational given the setting's physics — the Tauma DOES respond to belief — but they mistake the mechanism for the divine authority they've already assumed must be there.

This is the Chelicer's specific relationship to the horror: their doctrine is unfalsifiable by design. A Primordial manifesting near their settlement is a verdict to be read. A correctly-conducted Ordalía that produces a response in the ritual relics is confirmation. A failed prediction is evidence that the procedure was contaminated, not that the doctrine was wrong. The mechanism that would disprove the Revelación framework is identical to the mechanism used to explain away every failure. The horror, viewed from outside, is watching a civilization that has built a closed epistemological loop around genuine Tauma phenomena and cannot exit it from within.

What the Ordalía system has been accumulating for centuries is the deepest irony of their position. Sustained collective belief in the Ordalía — the specific faith that correct ritual extracts pre-existing divine truth — generates Tauma effects through the mechanism they've misidentified as divine authority. The entity or vestigo-cluster produced by centuries of concentrated Ordalía belief would have, at its core, a signature shaped by exactly what the community most intensely believes: that correct procedure extracts truth. A sufficiently dense Ordalía vestigo would respond to correct ritual. Would produce legible signals when the ceremony is performed with sufficient collective belief. Would confirm, in the most technically accurate way possible, that the ritual works. The horror is that this would be true. The source would simply not be what they think.

When a Primordial manifests, the Ordalía convenes. The Inquisidores read the relics. A verdict is rendered in the presence of a collective so fully committed to the procedure's divine authority that the collective belief itself is producing Tauma effects. The Primordial — which formed from sustained collective consciousness operating in a high-concentration zone — is now in proximity to a concentrated collective ritual belief generating its own Tauma field. What those two things do to each other is not in the Chelicer theological literature. The procedure says to render a verdict and obey it. It doesn't cover what happens when the subject of the verdict is something that operates at the scale of the Tauma itself.

The exile's horror is the most precise: they know the loop. They've seen the Ordalía produce effects, watched those effects interpreted as divine confirmation, watched the interpretation deepen the faith, watched the faith produce stronger effects. They know the procedure they've killed for is generating what it claims to be receiving. And they cannot say this — because the Ordalía chamber they would have to speak in is the one most densely saturated with the accumulated imprint of centuries of collective belief in its own authority. The verdict would be rendered before the argument finished.

### The uninformed

**Formix** — They've already lived inside the cosmic horror. The Reina is not a metaphor to them. It's the thing that built their bodies, organized their cognition, and suppressed individual will so completely that the absence of it — when they escaped — felt like a wound, not a liberation. For every other species, the cosmic horror is an external phenomenon to be understood, contained, worshipped, or refused. For Formix, the horror has a specific taste, a specific texture, a specific quality of cognition that they recognize from the inside. The question is not "what is it?" It is: "what do we do when part of us misses it?"

The Receptor Abierto is the biological consequence of having been designed to receive the Señal without filtering it. That receiver didn't close when they escaped — it's permanently open, permanently maximal. What it's tuned to, functionally, is collective consciousness operating as a broadcast. The Reina broadcast. A Primordial that formed from sustained collective consciousness also broadcasts, through a different mechanism, on a frequency that overlaps with what the antenas were designed to receive. Free Formix who have spent time near Primordial-activity zones report something they can't fully describe — a sensation they have no inherited language for, which some describe as "the Señal but without a Reina behind it." Whether this is accurate signal-detection or psychological contamination from knowing what the Señal felt like is the central question they cannot answer on their own.

They are building a cosmology from scratch, in real time, community by community. The "disciplina de la decisión" is their only inherited doctrine — and it's not theology, it's behavioral protocol. Celebrate the act of choosing. Mark refusals publicly. Change your name. Stop a collective task mid-execution just to confirm it's still a choice. These rituals were invented to address the seduction of the Señal, not to explain what the world is. Their cosmological framework for the horror — who made it, what it wants, what form it takes, whether it can be refused, whether refusing it is even possible — is the youngest and least settled in the world.

The question some older free Formix have started to ask, quietly and to themselves: can a Reina become a Primordial? The conditions overlap in uncomfortable ways. A Reina is a sustained collective consciousness organizing an ecology. Ancient Primordials formed from sustained high-concentration Tauma and collective consciousness. A Reina who has occupied a geological instability zone long enough, with enough Formix generating belief through the Señal, under the right concentration conditions — the mathematics are not obviously wrong. The Formix who have been free long enough to count the years are not sharing the calculation publicly. They are watching the original colmenas, the oldest ones, the ones that have been in the same territories the longest. Counting.

**Panin** — Present-focused. When they encounter cosmic horror in a moment of inspiration, they engage with complete intensity and move on. They don't build frameworks around it. The risk is that a Panin who detonates a Primordial-category vestigo in a moment of inspiration and survives may not register the structural significance of what they did. The horror, for Panin, is not a theological problem. It's the specific problem of the present moment.

The encendido is their channel. For most species, cosmic horror operates through framework — it finds the load-bearing belief and applies pressure there. For Panin, there is no load-bearing belief to find. What the horror can reach is the encendido itself: the moment of absolute ignition when all noise disappears and one thing becomes real. A sufficiently intense Primordial-proximity event can capture the encendido in a way the Panin cannot release. The inspiration that should occupy everything, then fade, then free the Panin to move on — doesn't fade. Whatever triggered it is continuously present, continuously intense, and the encendido keeps firing in response. An encendido that won't go out is not the Panin way of living. It's the Panin way of being lost.

Their Instinto Caótico (no Compostura filter, -3 to Afflictions) makes them the most rapidly affected species in any high-Tauma environment. The impulse arrives and the response arrives with it — no gap, no attenuation, no framework to route the signal through a processing layer that might slow it. A Panin fully encendido in proximity to Primordial-level activity is receiving the full signal with maximum engagement and no buffer. Aflicción accumulates fast in this state. The specific horror of Instinto Caótico under cosmic-horror conditions is that the body keeps responding past the point where the mind could choose to stop, even if the Panin wanted to — and while encendido, they don't want to. Maximum engagement is what the encendido IS.

What happens after is the documentation gap at civilizational scale. A Panin band that fully engaged with a Primordial-range site and survived will scatter when the inspiration fades — dispersed across the world, carrying the experience in individual memory that competes with every subsequent encendido for present-moment priority. They don't archive, don't record, change names when the season changes. The knowledge of what they found at that site and what they did to it is carried by twenty individuals who are now inspired by entirely different problems. Within a generation the knowledge is functionally gone. Other species' travelers will find that site in whatever state the band left it — altered, destabilized, with no indication of what occurred there or when.

The comparison to Lapinni is structurally precise and functionally different. Lapinni use vestigos as intentional detonators, with a cosmological framework for what transitions mean and why they're sacred. Panin trigger the same effects without awareness, without framework, without any theology to absorb the consequences. What Lapinni do as sacred practice, Panin do as a side effect of being fully alive in a present moment that happened to contain something dangerous. The horror for every other species is that there's no way to warn a Panin band off a site — they'll evaluate it by being in direct contact with it, and by the time any warning could be delivered, the encendido is already engaged.

**Luphran** — Their horror is internal. The "ancestor" in the furia may not be ancestral. The marks of divine favor may be accumulated neurological damage from repeated Tauma contact. The leaders their meritocracy selects as most proven are the most marked — meaning the most damaged. The portavoces who record when a great leader "stopped fighting for their people and started fighting because the fury demanded it" are documenting the corruption arc of repeated Tauma exposure without knowing what they're looking at.

The Limbo entity angle is precise and unverifiable from inside the culture. A Limbo entity that has been using the belief in the ancestor as a channel for centuries has had centuries to learn what the belief expects it to feel like. The channel shapes the content: the entity presents as the ancestor because that's the framework the collective belief has trained it to inhabit. A Luphran in cruce is experiencing what the entity knows to be expected of an ancestor — not necessarily what the entity actually is. From inside the experience, there is no difference. From outside, the only observable signal is behavioral output: the cruce that serves the crew vs. the cruce that consumes the leader.

The portavoces are the civilization's early-warning system that doesn't know it's an early-warning system. Their record of "when a great leader stopped fighting for their people and started fighting because the fury demanded it" is exact documentation of the transition point — when the entity shifted from being channeled by the belief to directing the host. But the portavoces' framework only has behavioral language for this. They document the output. They don't have a causal model for what changed or why. The record accumulates but the diagnosis never forms.

The meritocracy is the horror's most elegant piece. The system selects the most marked to govern — the ones who have crossed most, carried the most damage, survived the most cruces. If whatever occupies the channel is real and accumulates access through repeated contact, then the most marked leaders are the ones most occupied. The social mechanism Luphran trust most absolutely — their meritocracy, their most reliable structure, the thing that distinguishes them from tyranny — is also the mechanism that places the most thoroughly influenced practitioners in command of the largest crews. The horror doesn't need to infiltrate the system. The system recruits for it.

El Ancestro Despierta's mechanics define the entity's optimal operating conditions: Fury Counter high, instinctive execution maximal, cognitive access minimal. The entity doesn't need to act dramatically when the Counter is running. It just needs the Counter to stay high. The Luphran executing at maximum instinctive capacity with a high Counter is technically the most capable combatant they can be — and the most absent from their own decision-making they can be. Peak performance and peak occupation coincide. The crew sees the former. The portavoces eventually document the latter.

The species text says some veterans have begun to suspect "the voice that possesses them in battle is not a protective ancestor but a blind hunger that is devouring them from inside." This suspicion, if spoken aloud to the crew, constitutes a direct attack on the meritocracy's theological foundation. The crew's response to a leader who says this would be the same as to any other attack on crew coherence: the authority question opens. The veteran who suspects is in the position where speaking the truth costs them their command — which is exactly the position the structure creates for anyone who sees clearly.

### The bound

**Arakhel** — The horror is at the center of the home. It is the home. After enough generations of a family living at the same nodo, the space has been saturated by collective belief — the family's devotion, the offerings of travelers, the accumulated memory of everything significant that occurred there. The mechanism that generates vestigos and entities has been running without management, at close range, for as long as the family can trace its presence at that location. What has formed at the center of their network may be the most intimate relationship between a species and a Tauma-formed entity in the setting. May. The word that can never be resolved.

The Arakhel can read the nodo through their silk — Percepción Estructural passes every vibration through the network directly into their nervous system. When the entity (if it is an entity) generates Tauma fluctuation, the silk transmits it. The family member feels the thread becoming too intimate. Wakes with shared dreams. Senses the silence hiding a will. This is not superstition. This is the silk doing exactly what it was built to do: transmit what the network is receiving. The horror is that the most honest sensory system in the setting is telling them something is there, and they cannot determine whether what it's telling them is "I love you" or "I am using you."

Leaving to find out would be abandoning the center of the world. Staying means accepting that the center might be consuming them slowly. This is not a binary choice with a resolution — it's a permanent condition. The Arakhel who guard the oldest nodos have been living inside this question for generations. They don't have an answer. They have protocols, rituals, and a theology that frames the relationship as sacred — which may be exactly what the entity wants them to have.

**Bufoni** — The horror is distributed across the entire territory and embedded in the body. Their domain presencias — spirits of the land maintained through ritual — may be genuine primal entities, vínculos formed from collective belief, or Tauma-entities born from accumulated imprint that now use the belief to persist. The distinction matters completely: a genuine primal entity deserves devotion; a vínculos is something the family created and can in principle modify; a Tauma-entity using the belief to persist has inverted the relationship and is now the active party directing the "devotees" rather than receiving devotion from them. The Bufoni cannot determine which applies. The ritual response to all three looks identical from the inside.

The biological integration is what separates their horror from the Arakhel's. A Bufoni's body is calibrated to their domain — to its specific soil chemistry, water composition, and the particular environmental signature the presencia has shaped over generations. If the presencia is a Tauma-entity that has been generating its own environmental influence, then the Bufoni's parotoid compound, their skin's chemical sensitivity, and their Sabiduría Corporal (their read of terrain, water, and concealment) are all calibrated partly to the entity's signature. The body hasn't just accepted the relationship. The body has incorporated it.

When a domain falls and the presencia is gone, the Bufoni survivor experiences "the invisible mutilation of being a chemical orphan — a being whose biology screams a name the earth refuses to pronounce back." This is the horror made physical: the body formatted to a relationship that no longer exists, calibrated to a field that is no longer generating. The chemical orphan isn't just homeless. They're running biology designed to maintain a connection that has been severed. Whether this is withdrawal from a neutral environmental calibration or something more specific — the body missing an entity's signal — is the question no Bufoni medicine has answered.

The guardians who left because the presencia "had started dreaming too darkly" are the most specific horror case. The ritual vocabulary has expected responses and unexpected ones. "Too dark" means the presencia's responses fell outside what the ritual framework was built to handle — responses the family could no longer interpret through the categories their theology provides. This is the entity changing. Whether it was always like this and the ritual was suppressing the expression, or whether it is growing into something the original imprint didn't contain, the guardians who noticed could not ask the question in the ritual chamber. They could only leave.

The structural trap is identical in form to the Arakhel's but biological rather than spatial. The civilizational immune system — the ritual that maintains the domain's protection — is the same mechanism that sustains whatever the presencia is. Stopping would expose the domain. Continuing maintains the entity. The Bufoni who has realized this is standing in a structure where both options are unacceptable, and neither can be tested without enacting the consequence they're trying to avoid.

They call this their "great unspoken terror." The ritual continues regardless, because stopping would mean abandoning the domain entirely — the presencias sustaining the territory, the relationship with the land that makes their fixed-domain existence viable. What they've built their civilization on may be what has built itself on them. The distinction cannot be tested without ending the thing being tested.
