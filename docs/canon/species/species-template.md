# Species Template

**Status:** Template
**Purpose:** Canonical authoring scaffold for species-level narrative and system design
**Primary reference:** `docs/canon/species/index.md`
**Related systems:** `docs/system/natural-attack-forms.md`, `docs/system/technique-origins.md`, `docs/system/backgrounds.md`, `docs/system/competencies.md`

---

## How To Use This Template

This template is meant to define a species as a full narrative and systemic
body, not only as a source of combat identity or a list of Techniques.

The goal is to answer three overlapping needs:

- what the species is in the world
- how members of that species live, fear, rule, interpret, and differ
- how the species connects to the game system, especially Techniques, natural
  attacks, resistances, equipment, and player-character creation

When writing a species, prefer:

- thematic plausibility first
- mechanical usefulness second
- the best stable midpoint between both

### World-tone rule

Transcendence is not a safe or comfortably habitable world. Species should be
authored under the assumption that life is persistently pressured by:

- hostile climates and unstable terrain
- scarcity, spoilage, and logistical fragility
- predatory creatures and monstrous ecologies
- ruins that are valuable but dangerous
- Tauma pressure, vestiges, links, and other extranatural fallout
- other species who may compete, raid, exploit, mistrust, or dominate

A species should therefore not read as culturally complete in a vacuum. Its
institutions, rituals, domestic habits, mobility patterns, hierarchy, and
Technique traditions should all reveal how it survives a world where ordinary
continuity is difficult.

Do not reduce a species to:

- animal costume
- combat style only
- one religion with no internal tension
- one social class standing in for the whole civilization
- flat bonuses without worldview
- a stable modern society wearing a mythic skin

### Horror pressure test

Before considering a species layer complete, ask:

- What is trying to kill, corrupt, starve, isolate, or mislead them?
- What daily habits exist because safety cannot be assumed?
- What does this species do because the world is cruel, not because it is elegant?
- What part of their belief system may be true enough to help them and wrong enough to destroy them?

## 1. Identity

Define:

- species name
- animal base
- cultural analog or inspiration field
- cosmicist hook
- first impression

Questions:

- What does this species immediately feel like?
- What makes them distinct from neighboring species before mechanics enter?
- What is the one sentence that explains their deepest civilizational anxiety?
- What part of their identity already sounds like a survival answer to a hostile world?

---

## 2. Body

Define:

- humanoid anatomy and silhouette
- locomotion and physical bearing
- natural weapons
- senses and biological perception
- body-linked symbolism
- relevant biological variants
- size, weight, lifespan, maturity profile

Questions:

- What does this body make easy, hard, sacred, or dangerous?
- Which anatomical features are central to species identity?
- Which anatomical features exist but should not dominate authoring?
- What environmental or monstrous pressures does this body seem built to survive, and which ones still threaten it badly?

---

## 3. Culture

Define:

- core social structure
- authority model
- worldview
- relationship to ruins and catastrophe
- central civilizational metaphor or organizing idea
- how they structure duty, conflict, law, inheritance, labor, and prestige

Questions:

- What keeps this species' society coherent?
- What kinds of people hold real power?
- What is admired, feared, or seen as disgraceful?
- Which customs exist because collapse, predation, contamination, or raid pressure are normal possibilities?

---

## 4. Theology And Cosmology

Define:

- relationship with Tauma
- relationship with El Limbo, Abzu, or equivalent void logic
- sacred history
- major theological claims
- what they think happened to the ancient world
- what they think still threatens the present

Questions:

- What do they believe the catastrophe was?
- What do they think power is?
- What do they treat as sacred, forbidden, or survivable?
- Which theological beliefs help them survive, and which might be catastrophically wrong?

---

## 5. Organization And Internal Variation

Define:

- major factions inside the species
- castes, orders, schools, lineages, city-states, cults, or doctrinal branches
- what is universal across communities
- what varies by region or institution
- internal tensions and stable fault lines

Questions:

- Is this really one civilization, or many related civilizations?
- What disagreements are normal inside the species?
- Which variations matter most to worldbuilding or Techniques?
- Which internal divisions become dangerous because the world does not give them room for error?

---

## 6. Daily Life And Life Cycle

Define:

- food and subsistence
- domestic life
- kinship and family
- childhood and social training
- rites of passage
- adulthood and elderhood
- mourning, preservation, legacy, or afterlife customs
- what a normal life looks like outside elites

Questions:

- How does an ordinary person of this species live?
- What does a successful life look like to them?
- What kinds of losses are culturally unbearable?
- What ordinary behaviors are shaped by fear, scarcity, predation, contamination, or the need to remain hidden, mobile, or armed?

---

## 7. External Relations

Define:

- habitats and geographic spread
- regional placement
- neighbors
- enemies, rivals, and allies
- trade patterns
- border habits
- how other species usually perceive them
- what this species misunderstands about others

Questions:

- Where are they found?
- Who needs them, fears them, or trades with them?
- Which relationships are structurally unstable?
- Which outside pressure is most likely to break them first: environment, monsters, rivals, empire, or false salvation?

---

## 8. Material Expression And Language

Define:

- clothing
- architecture
- tools
- weapons as cultural objects
- ornament
- ritual objects
- burial or preservation objects
- naming patterns
- titles and forms of address
- writing and recordkeeping logic

Questions:

- What does their world look like when touched by their hands?
- What objects instantly feel like they belong to this species?
- How do they mark rank, memory, or sacred use?
- Which tools, garments, or structures exist specifically because the world is dangerous to inhabit?

---

## 9. System Connections

Define:

- competency affinities
- natural attack forms
- combat arsenal fit
- armor identity
- resistance or vulnerability logic
- species-specific mechanical placeholders
- Technique implications
- likely focus areas for species-origin Techniques

Questions:

- Which competencies does this species naturally justify?
- Which weapon or natural attack surfaces are core?
- Which mechanical identities should be avoided even if individuals could learn them?
- Which mechanics best express their struggle to survive pressure rather than just their combat style?

---

## 10. Player Character Hooks And Open Questions

Define:

- what a typical player character from this species knows
- what they likely do not know
- reasons one might leave the home society
- what they lose or keep when doing so
- tensions between diaspora and homeland
- unresolved design questions

Questions:

- What kinds of stories does this species naturally support for PCs?
- What kind of outsider pressure changes them?
- What still needs to be decided before the species is considered closed?
- What does a player character from this species fear losing first: body, kin, doctrine, route, memory, authority, or refuge?

---

## Minimum Completion Standard

A species should be considered narratively usable when it can answer, at
minimum:

- who they are
- what their body means
- how they organize life
- what they believe
- where they fit in the world
- how they differ internally
- how an ordinary member lives
- how they survive a world that is actively hostile to ordinary life
- how they connect to the rules
- what kind of player character can emerge from them

If a species only has:

- a cool aesthetic
- a combat identity
- or a bundle of Techniques

then it is not fully defined yet.

## Migration Note

Older species files may still use a shorter 6-section model:

1. Identity
2. Body
3. Culture
4. Theology
5. System Connections
6. Design Notes

That older structure is still serviceable, but future revision should migrate
those documents toward the fuller 10-layer canonical species template.
