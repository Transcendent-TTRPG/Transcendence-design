## 1. Identity

**Animal base:** Bear (brown bear lineage)  
**Cultural analog:** Celtic forge civilization — Goibniu/Gofannon smith tradition, druidic oral law, champion combat  
**Cosmicist hook:** They build for continuity. Their forge hierarchy promotes the most technically accomplished — who are also, systematically, the most exposed to Tauma-saturated forge sites. The most honored individuals in Ursari society are those whose nervous systems the Limbo gradient can most easily cross through. Their culture cannot distinguish "the smith who mastered the craft" from "the smith the craft mastered." Both look identical from the outside. Neither can be cleanly distinguished from the inside.

The hierarchy produces excellent results. The objeción is correct. The hammer keeps striking.

## 2. Body

Ursari are large bipedal humanoids derived from the brown bear lineage. Their silhouette communicates weight before speed — barrel torso, massive forelimbs, plantigrade stance. This impression is incorrect.

An Ursari can cross short distance with acceleration that contradicts their mass entirely. The charge is not a slow preparation: it is an instantaneous transition from stillness to full movement. Those who have not faced them before assume size implies warning.

It does not.

**Physical parameters:**

- Height: 2.20 – 2.60 m
- Weight: 200 – 300 kg
- Lifespan: 70 – 100 years
- Size category: Large

### Natural weapons

**Garras (Auxiliar):** The central function of the claws is not cutting — it is grip. Forearm musculature is built for sustained inward closure. Once two claws find purchase on a body, extracting that body from contact requires more force than most creatures can apply from a position of disadvantage. Releasing is always the Ursari's choice. This biology extends beyond combat: an Ursari works the world by holding it. Hot pieces, beams, injured bodies, too-heavy hammers, debts no one else can carry.

**Mordisco (Principal):** The bite is supplementary, not an opening. Once a target is held, the jaw adds consequence to existing contact. It requires the Ursari's full commitment — no other weapon in the same activation.

### Biological variants

No biological variants. All Ursari share the same anatomy and have access to both natural weapons from creation.

### Additional biological traits

**Thermal calibration:** Generations of forge work produced accumulated heat tolerance in the skin and respiratory system. An Ursari can operate at temperatures that would cause burns in other species without equivalent preparation. This is not invulnerability — it is calibration. The body learned where the threshold is and how to work near it without crossing.

The problem: the threshold is not only thermal.

**Pelage patterns:** Varies by lineage and geography. Dark brown-red in coastal smithing families; silver-gray in high mountain communities; dense black in deep forest settlements; muted browns in valley settlements; near-ash in ancient volcanic forge lineages. These are not decorative. In most communities they indicate climate, forge type, combustion tradition, mineral access, and the kind of exposure a lineage learned to survive.

**Erect posture:** Ursari can rise to full height on hindlimbs. In ordinary bipedal stance they are already large; fully erect they gain half a meter to a meter of height and change the entire geometry of the threat they represent. They do not fight this way habitually — it reduces stability — but as a declaration of physical presence it has few equivalents.

## 3. Culture

An Ursari community is a forge community. The settlement exists because a viable forge site exists, and the forge exists where geography permits it. The most valued quality in a site is what some call *calidad del fuego* — the way metal responds in certain places. The difference between a forge where work completes and a forge where work speaks.

What Ceratox detect with their horns, Ursari feel in the metal.

### Social structure

Hierarchy is organized by **craft mastery and witnessed honor**. The master whose work has been completed under verified difficult conditions — scarcity, time pressure, public peer evaluation, work at an unstable site, repair of an irreplaceable piece — holds more authority than whoever merely declares having reached that level.

Honor is not an award. It is a public verified record. Someone had to see it. Someone had to be able to describe what conditions existed. Someone had to be there when the metal almost failed and the artisan did not step back.

**The transmission chain**, not bloodlines, is the primary social bond. An Ursari belongs to their master's line. The master belongs to their master's line. Transmission lines spanning five, six, seven generations of named masters carry weight equivalent to lineage in other cultures. A blood-adopted outsider in a strong line is more a member of that line than the biological nephew of a minor smith. A member of long tradition who produces mediocre work loses position progressively.

### The druida class

Druidas hold a unique and revered place in the hierarchy. The Ursari cycle of creation begins with an object of deep personal or communal importance, but it is the druida who must take it into the wild. They travel to the most ferocious, untamed natural sites—geographies highly saturated with Tauma, which they simply consider pure nature. 

They spend long periods in isolated rituals, seeking a "communion" with the environment. Because Tauma anchors to the collective belief of a species, exposing these deeply significant objects to high-gradient sites causes the Tauma to anchor to them, granting them strange properties based on what the Ursari collectively believe they should do. 

When the druida returns with the altered object, the master smiths finally intervene, working the material into its final, functional form (weapons, tools, communal forges) and applying the necessary adornments or structural changes.

This makes the druidas necessary. It also makes them dangerous.

The deep communion they seek alters their perception, bringing them dangerously close to the Limbo dimension. The neurological and spiritual damage caused by this exposure is culturally framed as a sacred connection. The culture has no comfortable language for the difference between the druida who successfully communed with nature, and the one whose mind was simply broken by gradient exposure. 

A druida returns with a relic of incredible power. They also return as a symptom. The system sustains this ambiguity because the results are real.

### Champion combat

Serious disputes — authorship of technique, accusations of dishonor, competition for master position at a forge site, claims over missing pieces or unfulfilled work debts — are resolved through champion combat.

Not because force outweighs reason, but because champion combat is a publicly witnessed record of will. The result does not prove who was right. It proves who was willing to back their position with their body. Druidas do not fight in these disputes. Their verification is a different kind.

This produces resentment.

### Weapons and their lifecycle

Weapons are objects with an intended lifecycle: forged, used, returned to the forge site or destroyed. A weapon that leaves and does not return has an unfinished account. The Ursari does not require others to respect this doctrine, but they notice when it is not observed.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Ursari possess a robust, animistic and polytheistic theology modeled heavily on Celtic myth (e.g., the Tuatha Dé Danann and the Fomorians). They believe the world is divided between the forces of *Shape* (the forge) and the forces of *Raw Will* (the wild).

**The Great Maker (Analog of Goibniu):** The patron deity of the forge, structure, and civilization. The Maker represents the hammer, the anvil, and the limits of the physical world. The masters pray to the Maker for the strength to endure the work.

**The Wild Gods:** Formless, ferocious deities that inhabit the deep forests, volcanic vents, and high peaks. They are not evil, but they are completely indifferent to mortal limits. They represent the raw, unshaped power of nature.

The Ursari believe that an object's true power is not born in the forge, but in the wild. When an ordinary object accrues enough collective belief, it is taken by a *druida* into the domains of the Wild Gods. The druida performs long vigils, offering the object to the raw will of nature. 

Because these wild sites are actually geographies highly saturated with Tauma, the environmental Tauma anchors to the object, changing reality to match the species' belief. However, this high-gradient Tauma exposure also shatters the druida's perception, inflicting severe psychological and sensory *aflicciones* (hallucinations, paranoia, sensory overload). The tragedy of their theology is their interpretation of this neurological damage: the Ursari see the druida's shattered perception as a "sacred fever," proof that the Wild Gods have spoken to them. They cannot distinguish between a successful spiritual connection and a mind broken by Tauma-induced afflictions.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

**Transmission lines are the primary organizational unit.** A craftsperson belongs to their master's line. Lines with documented depth spanning centuries carry social weight equivalent to noble lineage elsewhere. The practical implication: identity, technique, style, material preferences, commercial routes, inter-community alliances, and silences that each school learned not to touch — all travel through the transmission chain.

An outsider — including members of other species — can enter a line by demonstrating work of sufficient quality and being accepted by the master. Biological adoption matters less than artisanal adoption.

### Internal tensions

The most common fractures are not between bloodlines but between transmission lines and between competing schools of exposure doctrine:

- **Early exposure:** Some lines believe promising apprentices should approach gradient forges early to develop deep hearing. 
- **Protective doctrine:** Others consider this sacrificing young people to school ambition.
- **Consumption of druidas:** Some communities protect their druidas from excessive work. Others consume them slowly in the name of pieces "only they" can complete.

When a deep forge produces an extraordinary work and leaves the druida ill for months, the community registers both things. It does not always know which weighs more.

**Disputes between lines** can persist across generations without formal resolution, informing which communities trade with which, which transmission schools will train candidates from which lineages, and which forge sites are considered contested.

## 6. Daily Life and Life Cycle

A settlement is organized around sound. The hammer on the anvil is the clock, the compass, the signal that the site is still active. Silence at the forge is a bad omen. A quiet forge may mean material scarcity, master injury, unresolved dispute, mourning, combustible shortage, ritual closure, or that something in the site changed.

The first diagnostic of an Ursari community's health is done at a distance, before seeing it. By listening.

### Daily rhythm

At dawn, apprentices have already transported water, checked coal, cleaned tools, prepared leather, sorted mineral by expected response, and opened ventilation. Masters do not always speak. Sometimes they only change the position of a piece and everyone with sufficient time in the forge understands what it means.

Ursari teaching uses minimal verbal explanation. The master works; the apprentice watches; the apprentice attempts; the master corrects through demonstration, not description. This is why knowledge cannot be transmitted in text: it is not propositional. It is bodily. The apprentice who has spent years watching how a difficult temper is resolved carries that knowledge in their hands and eyes. Also in the back. Also in the fatigue. Also in how they breathe when heat rises.

### Diet

Dense caloric requirements from forge work at their body mass necessitate quantities of food that other cultures would struggle to sustain. Established communities maintain food reserve systems designed for periods when work intensifies and time for hunting or cultivation shrinks.

### Winter contraction

In cold climates, many communities reduce activity in winter: less work, more rest, consumption of accumulated reserves, tool repair, review of old pieces, oral transmission of disputes and apprentice evaluation. Not true hibernation but a cultural contraction. The forge does not die. It breathes more slowly.

### Material handling

Strange metal — arriving from outside, from sites Ursari cannot classify, metal touched by active Tauma or Limbo entities — is treated with attention before being worked. A Ceratox working near an Ursari master may react before the master does. Metal that produces an anomalous horn response is set aside before entering the main forge. This collaboration is not formalized as alliance. It is simply what works.

### Affection and relationship

Affective relationships are expressed through work. Repairing a tool without being asked. Saving good material for the right apprentice. Being present as witness when a difficult piece is completed. Not interrupting someone listening to metal. Bringing food to someone who won't leave the forge until they finish.

### Mourning

When a master dies, their tools are not distributed immediately. Each hammer, tong, wedge, mold, and incomplete piece is studied to determine what part of the transmission remains inside it. Some tools pass to the lead apprentice. Others return to the fire. Others are kept unused because they still teach, but should no longer touch metal.

### Aging

Aging well among the Ursari is not preserving raw strength. It is preserving judgment about the threshold. Knowing when a piece needs more heat and when the desire to finish it is speaking louder than the metal. Knowing when an apprentice should approach the deep forge and when they only want to prove they can. Knowing when the body says "still can" but the hand already started lying.

A respected old Ursari is not one who never yielded. It is one who knows what must not break for something to last.

## 7. External Relations

Ursari products are valued almost everywhere. Weapons, armor, tools, bridge fittings, locks, chains, machinery components, door reinforcements, precision molds: what comes from an Ursari forge tends to last longer than those who commission it expect to live.

This utility does not imply understanding. Most species want the result. Few want to understand the cost of the site, the body, and the transmission that produced it.

**To the Ceratox:** The most directly useful and most silently tense relationship. The horn responds to Tauma-saturated materials before any Ursari instrument can detect them — invaluable for evaluating metal from unknown sites. The tension: the horn signal does not only indicate danger. It also indicates the best available forge materials. Working alongside a Ceratox at an active site means the Ceratox knows exactly what dangerous gradients the Ursari are exposing themselves to.

**To the Drak'kai:** Weapons of highest Ursari quality circulate outward, pass between hands, are lost, recovered, and appear decades later in collections nobody consulted with the originating forge. Drak'kai who accumulate high-value objects understand that a piece of Ursari origin holds something replicas do not. Some masters believe work should return: the metal of an active forge, at the end of its use, deserves to be returned to fire. What Drak'kai do with their collections — preserving indefinitely, not returning — violates no explicit agreement. That does not prevent it from feeling like an unpayable debt.

**To the Rokhart:** They observe druidas with the systematic interest they apply to any phenomenon generating Tauma pattern data. The process by which a druida survives years of deep-site exposure and remains functional is unusual even for them. The Rokhart do not hide this interest. The Ursari do not appreciate it: no Ursari wants to be treated as a case file while they are holding the hammer and being consumed by the site.

**To the Talpi:** They use forge sites as extraction zones. The mineral that Ursari value for its behavior during work, the Talpi value for composition and density. There is competition for resources that both cultures need, with completely different valuation criteria. For a Talpi, the correct mineral must leave the earth. For an Ursari, not all mineral should leave the site that makes it capable of responding.

**To the Sauri:** The Sauri respect Ursari durability and commission pieces for chambers, gates, channels, and containment structures. The relationship can be solid when both peoples agree form must endure. But Ursari distrust any culture that preserves too much without returning anything to the fire. A sealed chamber for centuries may look like a masterwork or like a refusal to finish the job.

**To the Naghii:** They value Ursari pieces found in ruins, especially when forge marks allow tracing ancient routes or lost workshops. Ursari often become impatient with communities that want to interpret too much before touching the material. Still, both understand something common: a tradition can survive in fragments, and a misreading can cost generations.

**To the Zarnag:** They buy and repair hard tools: hooks, shovels, blades, chains, masks, quarantine closures. Ursari respect those who do necessary work without seeking beauty. Zarnag respect those who manufacture things that don't fail when the site is already failing. The relationship tends to be direct, rough, and functional.

**To the Loxod:** A Loxod can receive the chemical and vibratory history of an ancient forge in ways an Ursari would find disturbing. Together they can reconstruct more than either could alone. The difficulty is that the Loxod ask what burden the work left on the artisan, while the Ursari usually ask if the work was done well.

**To the Formix:** They produce a particular discomfort. A body made for function reminds the Ursari of the least comfortable part of their own culture: the possibility that the craft devours the person carrying it. A Formix Warrior who doesn't want to fight and an Ursari apprentice who doesn't want to forge can understand each other without saying much. They can also be used by their communities in similar ways, under very different words.

**To the Panin:** They are difficult in the forge. They touch, test, ask, move too close to hot pieces, and can see a solution in a mechanism before understanding why no one should move it yet. Ursari usually expel them from delicate work zones. Later, sometimes, they discover the Panin noticed a flaw all the masters had normalized. For an Ursari, Panin talent seems wasted. For a Panin, an entire life around one technique seems suffocating.

**To the Chelicer:** They respect the public testimony of Ursari honor, but are uncomfortable that artisanal truth depends so much on the body and the human witness. Ursari find strange a society where procedure can weigh more than visible work. Both peoples understand the value of being present when something is verified. They disagree on who, or what, has the right to say the verification is finished.

**To the Arakhel:** Their nodes and crossings keep transit, debt, and relationship; Ursari lines keep technique, body, and work. The Arakhel tend to ask what bonds sustain the crossing, while the Ursari ask who taught the hand that made the piece. They can collaborate well when a forge depends on an ancient crossing, but can also deeply irritate each other: to an Ursari, too much Arakhel truth seems dispersed in relationships; to an Arakhel, too much Ursari truth seems trapped in the hammer.

**To the Bufoni:** A Bufoni domain recognizes, demands protocol, and can punish the lack of correct relationship. A deep Ursari forge also responds, although Ursari prefer to speak of fire, matter, and work rather than will. The Bufoni can see druida artisans entering too deep into a domain without admitting they are negotiating with something. Both know the place matters. They disagree on whether the place should be treated as an interlocutor, a condition, or a debt.

**To the Luphran:** Both meritocracies independently select for Tauma-permeable individuals as their most honored — the Ursari through craft proximity, the Luphran through battle-fury exposure. Both produce individuals who survive because they endured closer to something that others could not touch. This can create admiration. It can also create competition over which form of exposure deserves authority.

**To the Myo:** They make Ursari uncomfortable because sometimes they name things before the workshop wants to name them. A Myo omen about a druida or a line can sound like unbearable meddling. However, some communities have learned to listen when a Myo warns that a communion called a "gift" has already started to look too much like an affliction.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

**Language:** Bherac (native language of Ursari communities). Común for external commerce and inter-species exchange.

**Architecture:** Built around acoustic logic. Forge chamber orientation matters — for ventilation, for echo, for the way sound indicates the site is alive. An Ursari settlement heard before it is seen. Construction prioritizes structural permanence and heat management over aesthetic display.

**Weapons as cultural objects:** A piece of Ursari make is not an object with a fixed value — it is an object with a lifecycle. Forged for a purpose, used for that purpose, returned to fire or buried at the forge site when the lifecycle is complete. The marking of a piece (lineage marks, forge-site marks, master marks) is not decoration. It is provenance record. A piece that has been properly marked can be traced back to its origin forge, the line that produced it, the conditions of its creation.

**Rank and honor marking:** Honor is not declared — it is witnessed. No elaborate title system. Rank is established through the record of publicly observed work, accumulated over years. An artisan's position in the hierarchy is legible to those who know which conditions they worked under and who was present.

**Tools of the dead:** The tools of a deceased master are reviewed before distribution. What passes, what returns to fire, and what is kept unused without touching metal — this is one of the most significant decisions a line makes at a master's death. The wrong choice distributes a master's damage alongside their knowledge.

**Recordkeeping:** Oral tradition dominates. Craft knowledge is transmitted through demonstration, not text. The most critical knowledge — which sites carry which quality, how a specific grade of material responds, the history of particular disputed pieces — lives in the transmission chain, not in written archive. When a line loses its oldest member, something irretrievable is lost.

## 9. System Connections

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Precio de la Comunión (Herencia)** — Generations of druidic isolation and incessant exposure to extreme nature have left a biological imprint on a species trained to endure tension that breaks others. Mechanically: Permanent -3 penalty to all T.R. against Aflicciones.

**Constitución de Forja (Legado)** — Mechanically: +1 per 4 NR to T.E. of Aclimatación, Tolerancia, and Supervivencia.

**La Presa del Oso (Legado)** — Mechanically: Active technique (Ritmo 4, Desgaste 1). Attack with Garras. If it hits, deal damage and target rolls T.R. Alteraciones. If they fail, they are Atrapado. While Atrapado, you can spend 1 Desgaste each turn to deal Garras damage without rolling to hit. You cannot move while maintaining the grapple. Target can escape with opposed Fuerza check.

**El Oso No Cede (Legado)** — Passive. While in Agonía, the Ursari can perform actions with Ritmo ≤ 3. All other Agonía effects (stabilization required, risk of death on Crítico to Head/Torso) remain active.

**Armas Naturales:**
- *Garras (Todos los Ursari)*: Auxiliar. Fuerza/Fuerza. Forces T.R. (Alteraciones) on strong hit. Failure permanently reduces target's T.D. by 1 per rank until combat ends (armor tearing).
- *Mordisco (Todos los Ursari)*: Principal. Fuerza/Fuerza. Restriction: No other weapon can be used in the same activation. On strong hit, adds +1d6 to +3d6 raw Impacto damage based on rank.

### Combat identity

The Ursari combat identity is built on unstoppable mass and suicidal endurance. They do not know how to let go. *La Presa del Oso* exemplifies this: they grab the enemy and crush them slowly, refusing to move or release them. *El Oso No Cede* means they will continue fighting and crushing their enemy even as they bleed to death in Agonía. They are terrifying because they interpret pain not as a signal to stop, but as a necessary friction to finish the job.

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

### Why a PC leaves

An Ursari outside their community has lost something. The forge is not work — it is identity, hierarchy, bond, knowledge, and purpose. A master without a forge has not taken a vacation; they have been stripped of the system that gave them form. An apprentice without a master is carrying an incomplete transmission. A druida away from their deep site is discovering whether what they know came from them, from the fire, or from something that still follows them.

Ursari who appear in the open world have usually:
- Lost the site (forge became inaccessible, community dispersed, master died with untransmitted knowledge)
- Left on explicit mandate from the chain: find specific material, locate a new site, resolve an honor debt, recover a piece that left the forge and should return, escort an irreplaceable work, verify a rumor about fire that speaks in another region

### What a PC carries

**Outward:** Ability to evaluate materials by touch and work; bodily-transmitted craft knowledge; instinct to organize relationships through transmission-chain logic; strength to hold what others cannot; the physical certainty that heat is work before threat.

**Unstated:** The question of where the threshold is between knowledge and exposure — and whether the master they learned from crossed it before they could know.

### Character types

- Apprentice who inherited an incomplete technique and is looking for someone who can finish teaching it
- Master who lost the forge and does not know who they are without the sound of the anvil
- Champion who won a dispute of honor and found the result repaired nothing
- Young druida who hears metal too early and fears everyone will call it a gift
- Artisan who wants to prove mastery should not be measured by proximity to the gradient
- Exile who worked a metal that should not have been touched
- Emissary sent to recover a piece their line believes no true Ursari work should die far from fire

### What they fear losing first

The transmission chain. The forge identity. The knowledge that "what I know came from me and not from the crossing."

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **El Lenguaje Bherac:** Su lenguaje tiene raíces orales profundas (gaélico/bretón). No existe lenguaje Bherac escrito para la ley, la teología o la poesía; todo se transmite de maestro a aprendiz. La única escritura Ursari son diagramas técnicos y matemáticos para la forja.
- **Comunidades Proteccionistas:** Hubo forjas "modernas" que intentaron prohibir las inmersiones druídicas para salvar la salud mental de su gente. Sus armas salieron frágiles, ordinarias, sin "peso". Perdieron su estatus y fueron absorbidas.
- **Tensión con Luphran:** Los Ursari llaman a los Luphran *Hermanos de la Quema*. Ambas culturas arrojan a sus mejores individuos al fuego (los Luphran al combate, los Ursari a la forja/naturaleza) hasta que se rompen, generando un respeto mutuo y tenso.

## 11. Cultura Material 

La cultura material Ursari es pesada, duradera y profundamente atada al linaje del artesano.

- **Torc de Autoridad:** Anillos de metal pesado para el cuello (inspiración celta). Un maestro forja un Torc para su aprendiz cuando este está listo para tomar su propia forja. Es un documento de identidad físico; el metal y la técnica del Torc cuentan la historia de todo el linaje de transmisión del herrero.
- **Garras del *Karhunpeijaiset*:** Collares rituales hechos con las garras extraídas de maestros fallecidos. Los druidas los usan como anclajes táctiles de voluntad para intentar no perder la cordura durante sus terribles inmersiones en las zonas taumáticas.
- **Hoces de Oro Taumático:** Herramientas ceremoniales (referencia druídica) usadas exclusivamente para "cortar" materiales del entorno sagrado. Su densidad previene que las propiedades anómalas del material se dispersen antes de llegar al yunque.
- **Fragmentos de Yunque:** Literalmente, trozos de yunques que se partieron durante la forja de una obra maestra. Son conservados por las familias como prueba física de que la voluntad del herrero fue más dura que el hierro.
