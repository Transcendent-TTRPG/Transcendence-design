# Species Development Status

**Status:** Working assessment
**Purpose:** Track how close each species is to the canonical species template
**Primary reference:** `docs/canon/species/species-template.md`

---

## Reading rule

This document does not judge whether a species is "good." It tracks whether a
species is sufficiently developed across the narrative and systemic layers
required by the canonical template.

Assessment bands:

- `Strong` — clearly present and already usable as a reference layer
- `Partial` — present in meaningful form, but still missing depth or breadth
- `Thin` — touched only lightly or implied rather than defined
- `Missing` — not meaningfully established yet

### Sauri summary

The Sauri are already a robust species for tone, cosmology, combat identity,
Technique authoring, and survival pressure. What they most need now is clearer
geographic placement, richer material-social detail, and a more explicit map
of recurring branches, institutions, and community variants.

Current overall state: `Narratively strong, not yet socially complete`

Technique identity, and survival-through-interpretation. Their largest
remaining gaps are now the fully grounded non-elite social world, named
institutional branches, and a deeper mapped relationship to the rest of the
world.

Current overall state: `Narratively strong, not yet socially complete`

## Shared Next Steps

If Sauri and Naghii are the reference-standard species, the next most valuable
expansion layers are:

1. `Material Expression and Language`
   Naming, titles, clothing, writing, ordinary tools, ritual objects, and
   material identity beyond architecture and weapons.
2. `Player Character Hooks`
   What a typical PC knows, what they are cut off from, why they leave, and
   how they remain legible outside their home communities.
3. `Organization and Internal Variation`
   Orders, regional branches, city-state differences, doctrinal fractures,
   and what is truly universal versus local.
4. `Daily Life and Life Cycle`
   Food, kinship, age stages, rites of passage, prestige, mourning, domestic
   structure, and what a non-elite life looks like.
5. `External Relations`
   Where they live, who borders them, who trades with them, who fears them,
   and which relationships define their place in the wider world.
