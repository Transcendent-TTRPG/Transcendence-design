## 1. Identity

**Animal base:** Wolf (gray wolf lineage)  
**Cultural analog:** Norse/Viking — Úlfhéðnar, jarl meritocracy, law-speaker oral tradition, river-route networks, wyrd  
**Cosmicist hook:** Their meritocracy selects as leaders the warriors most proven in combat. The most proven are the ones who have most often entered the battle-fury state — a genuine biological capacity, not supernatural — in which the nervous system becomes highly permeable. Veterans accumulate vestigo exposure from combat environments during their most open neurological moments. The pack cannot know when its leader stopped being entirely its own. The leader cannot know from inside, because each crossing feels more real than the last.

The theology says the wolf-ancestor moves through the marked warrior. The most marked are most favored. The most favored lead.

No one has vocabulary for what leadership means when the leader has been crossed through enough times.

## 2. Body

Luphran are medium-sized bipedal humanoids derived from the gray wolf lineage. Lean, long-limbed, built for distance and social coordination rather than raw mass. Their posture is forward — slightly inclined even at rest, as though the body is always already calculating movement options.

**Physical parameters:**

- Height: 1.70 – 1.95 m (est.)
- Weight: 75 – 110 kg (est.)
- Lifespan: 55 – 75 years (est. — to be confirmed in full design)
- Size category: Medium

### Natural weapons

**Mordisco (Principal):** The wolf's primary weapon. The jaw is built for locking pressure — sustained grip, not single-impact damage. Once established, the bite creates a hold that requires significant force to break from the outside. Profiles oriented around sustained control and bringing down larger prey.

**Garras (Auxiliar):** Wolf claws are relatively blunt compared to bear or cat — not designed for slashing, but for grip, pin, and positional control. Can be used alongside the mordisco in two-weapon combat to fix a target's position while the bite is applied.

*Full weapon statistics to be determined in technique-design phase. Proposed profile directions:*

- Mordisco: Impacto · Restricción · Imparable (locking, sustained, won't release)
- Garras: Impacto · Control de Línea · Intercepción (flanking, positioning, pack-coordination)

*Proposed damage baseline: Mordisco d8–d10 (Principal, full commitment), Garras d6 (Auxiliar, gripping rather than slashing).*

### Biological traits load-bearing for design

**Earned hierarchy instinct:** Wolves read and respond to dominance hierarchy signals continuously and involuntarily. For an intelligent species this means: authority is not declared, it is performed and read. A Luphran who does not continuously demonstrate capacity loses deference naturally, without formal process.

**Vast spatial memory + range:** Pack territory is measured in routes traveled and landmarks memorized, not in borders held. The Luphran carry their territory internally — in the knowledge of routes, approaches, resources, and threats that they have accumulated through traversal.

**Persistence stamina:** Wolves are not ambush predators — they are pursuit predators who outlast prey over distance. The cultural translation: the Luphran endurance ethic is not about maximum force application but about sustained presence under hostile conditions. Not the strongest hit; the last one standing.

**Extreme sensitivity to intra-pack social dynamics:** Betrayal, debt, loyalty, status — these are not abstract concepts for the Luphran. They are continuously sensed, continuously recalibrated, and failures of pack coherence register as immediate threat. A crew that loses social cohesion is in danger before the external threat arrives.

**Battle-fury arousal:** The capacity for extreme arousal states that suppress higher cognitive processing and amplify instinctual response. Genuine neurological shift, not supernatural. The body knows how to do this; the culture built a theology around what it feels like from inside.

## 3. Culture

### Primary social unit: the crew

Not the family. Not the settlement. The crew — 20 to 60 individuals, chosen through demonstrated worth, bound by shared danger and verified loyalty. You join a crew by proving you are worth the space you occupy. The jarl commands because the crew allows it — actively, continuously. A jarl who fails twice stops being a jarl. The title transfers to whoever the crew can follow.

The bio-organic vessel (see §8) is the mobile home. The pack carries its territory with it.

### The holds: refuge, not center

Luphran settlements are winter holds — places where multiple crews converge during hostile seasons. Neutral by convention: a hold is assembly ground, not the territory of any specific jarl. Governed by assembly of present jarls — rotating, never fixed. No Luphran city in the traditional sense. The largest holds have perhaps 200–300 permanent residents plus visiting crews.

Their "empire" is a network of known routes, trusted stops, and tribute relationships. The knowledge of which routes go where, what conditions to expect, and what stops are safe is proprietary to crews. It is the most valuable non-material asset a crew holds.

### Raiding logic

The Luphran do not raid for land — land is not the resource. They raid for supplies, materials, route knowledge, and tribute agreements ("don't raid us and we trade with you"). When a territory no longer yields sufficient return, they move. Not abandonment — expansion. The route known is the territory extended.

Their cosmological frame for this: movement is divine will. The wolf-ancestor moved to survive. The hold that stays fixed becomes prey.

### Oral law and law-speakers

No written records. All law, route knowledge, and ancestral precedent lives in the memory of *law-speakers* — specialists who travel between holds, preserving the accumulated decisions, routes, and history of the species. When a law-speaker dies without transmitting everything, it is a catastrophic loss — not personal tragedy, operational catastrophe. They have rituals for this moment: structured grief, emergency audit of what was carried, and formal declaration of what is now unknown and must be reconstructed.

### Survival habits with biological roots

- **Sentinel rotations:** Always someone awake — mirroring pack sentinel behavior
- **Jarl eats last:** The leader feeds the crew first; they eat after. Pack logic. A jarl who eats before their crew has stopped being a jarl in the ways that matter
- **Territory-clearing before rest:** Entering new space requires evaluation before sleep. Their theology frames this as respecting local entities. The actual function: accumulated threat-assessment protocol encoded in ritual
- **Structured departure of the dying:** When movement is survival, the dying are left with ceremony and care — but left. Every crew member knows this rule before joining. The crew continues; the individual knew the terms

### The civilizational trap

The selection mechanism is self-defeating at civilizational scale:

Crews that survive the hardest battles produce the most experienced jarls → the most experienced jarls have entered the battle-fury state most often → the most honored jarls have accumulated the most vestigo exposure in their most-open neurological state → the holds with the most battle-proven leadership also have the most neurologically compromised leadership.

This is not an individual failure. It is the structure of how the civilization functions.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The battle-fury state is divine contact. The wolf-ancestor moves through the warrior's body during the crossing. Warriors who have been marked most often — who carry the permanent changes of repeated crossings — are considered most favored, most prepared, most worthy of leadership.

This theology is accurate about the experience: something does enter during the state. The mechanism the theology attributes is inverted: it is not the warrior reaching the ancestor. It is the Limbo gradient crossing through the most open channel available at the moment of maximum arousal.

But the crossing is real. The enhancement is real. The mark is real. The theology is wrong only about the direction — and that direction is not visible from inside the experience.

**The wyrd dimension:** Luphran cosmology holds that something of cosmic scale is building. The world is moving toward a confrontation that will determine what survives. The marks their god puts on warriors are not decoration — they are preparation. The most marked are being readied.

They are not wrong that something is building. The cosmological instability the Naghii track is real. What the Luphran theology gets wrong is what "being readied" means: not collected toward a purpose, but opened toward something that uses what it finds.

**Sacred geography:** The law-speakers preserve not just routes but *the character of sites* — which holds carry what presence, which forge zones (foreign — they know Ursari sites by reputation) feel wrong, which river approaches have anomalous behavior. This is framed as theological knowledge. The functional content: accumulated record of Tauma-active environments encountered along their routes.

**The question no one asks:** If the god marks the worthy through the battle-fury crossing, and the most marked lead — who is leading when the marking has gone deep enough that the leader's choices are no longer fully their own? The Luphran theology has no vocabulary for this question. The closest they come is in the law-speaker tradition of recording when a jarl's decisions became "strange after the great battle" — careful, euphemistic, and never a verdict.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

**The crew is the organizational unit.** Political authority above the crew level is assembly-based and temporary. No Luphran king, no permanent central authority. Holdings-level decisions are made by the assembled jarls present; the assembly dissolves when crews depart.

### Variation across crews

Crews develop distinct identities over time: specific route specializations, preferred tribute arrangements, characteristic combat approaches, regional theological variations, and different relationships with the battle-fury tradition.

**Conservative crews** restrict the practice — who can enter the state, under what conditions, how many crossings in a season are permitted. These crews tend to have more stable leadership over longer periods and somewhat less battle effectiveness at the individual level.

**Open crews** celebrate the crossing without restriction. High effectiveness in combat; faster leadership turnover; older jarls with the deepest marks who are also the most changed.

Neither approach is formalized as doctrine. The tension between them is the main internal fault line of Luphran society.

### Law-speakers as cross-crew continuity

The one institution that transcends crew and hold is the law-speaker tradition. Law-speakers travel between holds, hold no military authority, and are protected by a convention of non-interference that even rival crews observe. They are the closest thing to a species-level institution that exists. Their neutrality is structural: they carry everyone's precedent, so they belong to no one.

A law-speaker who dies without completing their transmission threatens not just their own lineage but every crew whose precedents they carried.

## 6. Daily Life and Life Cycle

### Aboard the vessel

The bio-organic vessel is the primary domestic environment for most of the year. It is a living organism — the crew feeds it, maintains it, monitors it for signs of stress, and learns to read its condition the way one reads a crew member's condition. A vessel that is not being maintained is in danger. A vessel that dies mid-voyage means the crew is stranded in hostile terrain with everything they own.

The crew knows the vessel's needs the way they know the route. Both are survival-critical knowledge.

Daily rhythms on the vessel: maintenance watches, route monitoring, material stowage, food preparation for both crew and vessel. The jarl's time is split between navigation decisions, crew cohesion management, and the ongoing assessment of what the current route can yield.

### In holds

Winter holds are semi-communal. Crews inhabit separate sections but share common spaces for assembly, trade, and ritual. The hold's permanent residents manage maintenance, provide seasonal supplies, and serve as law-speaker hosting sites. They have no military authority over visiting crews.

Apprentices (younger crew members, not a formal title) learn by doing — route monitoring, vessel maintenance, material handling, and observation of how the jarl reads situations. Nothing is explained before it is demonstrated. Most learning happens through error and correction in conditions that matter.

### Life stages

**Youth** is spent in the holds or on lower-risk coastal routes with established crews. A young Luphran's first experiences of the battle-fury state are typically induced involuntarily by genuine threat, not by deliberate ritual. The first controlled induction is a cultural milestone.

**Adult life** is crew life. The question "which crew" defines almost everything: route access, material quality, combat opportunity, the quality of the jarl's judgment (which becomes a question with layered implications as the jarl ages).

**The marked:** Veterans who have crossed the threshold many times occupy an honored position in Luphran culture but a complicated one. Their counsel is sought; their decisions are trusted. Their behavior "after the great battles" is recorded carefully by law-speakers, who never attach a verdict. Everyone around a deeply marked veteran notices the changes and describes them as sacred distinction. No one in the crew has language for asking whether they are still entirely the person they were.

**Death at sea:** A crew member who dies on a voyage and cannot be returned to a hold is given overboard with ceremony. The law-speaker carries the account forward. Objects of significant personal history are kept — not indefinitely, but until they can be placed correctly at a hold.

### Food and materials

Primarily carnivorous dietary orientation. Crews hunt, fish, and raid for protein sources alongside traded or grown provisions from holds. The vessel organism itself processes certain organic waste into usable material, which the crew learns to manage as part of maintenance.

## 7. External Relations

**To the Ceratox:** The horn often becomes restless near heavily marked Luphran. To the Ceratox, something in them feels too loaded, too insistent, too similar to a place where the environment has stopped behaving as it should. Interactions with high-ranking jarls end quickly — not always out of hostility, but because instinct keeps signaling what reason cannot confirm or dismiss. A Ceratox may sense the jarl carries a storm inside. The jarl may read that reaction as a challenge, fear, or a sign that the Ceratox knows something they aren't saying.

**To the Ursari:** Both meritocracies end up honoring those who have passed too many times too close to something greater than themselves: the Ursari through prolonged exposure to deep forges; the Luphran through repeated fury crossings. An Ursari master and a veteran Luphran jarl encountering each other usually recognize something familiar in the other without fully identifying it. Each culture's tradition confirms that reading as proof that their own path is the correct one. It brings them closer, but it can also prevent them from warning each other in time.

**To the Zarnag:** Battlefields where Luphran crews operated — especially where several warriors crossed at the same time — do not settle easily. For the Zarnag, it's not just blood, fear, or death working on the terrain. Something harder to close remains, as if the urgency of combat kept searching for a body long after the battle ended. The Zarnag have noticed this and do not consider it an ordinary Residue. Their working relationship tends to be transactional: the crew passes, the Zarnag occasionally need muscle, an agreement is reached. Neither species tries to embellish what the other does.

**To the Naghii:** They find signs difficult to order around high-ranking Luphran. Their records accumulate cases, but no comfortable answer. It unsettles them that something as intimate and fierce as fury leaves recognizable traces across different individuals. The Luphran, in turn, are unsettled by anyone wanting to look at the crossing from outside the battle and treat it as a reading rather than a trial.

**To the Rokhart:** Rokhart presence in any Luphran crew's path usually ends in two possible interpretations: a request for tribute or a veiled threat. The Rokhart tend to stare too long at the most marked members — exactly those a crew protects most zealously. Asking for prolonged access to them, making repeated questions about their crossings, or trying to track a single change for years is received as a direct intrusion into the most sacred and dangerous aspect of the species. The Rokhart know how to observe without being seen. The Luphran know how to notice who is looking too much. That complicates everything.

**To the Drak'kai:** They find unwritten stories and objects that shouldn't have moved so much on Luphran routes. A piece taken in a raid, lost in battle, repaired in another port, and carried for years by a crew can become a valuable accumulation. The Luphran, for their part, distrust anyone who wants to preserve what, for them, belongs to a living route. A crew object is not always a relic. Sometimes it is a debt in transit.

**To the Loxod:** A Loxod can read layers of stress, hunger, death, repair, and fear on a Luphran vessel that the crew has already normalized. This can be useful or unbearable. A Loxod who says too much about what they receive from a ship can seem to be touching memory without permission. But in a shipwreck, a disappearance, or a silenced route, few allies are as valuable as someone capable of receiving what the water, the living wood, and the dead still emit.

**To the Formix:** They produce a strange tension. Their collective discipline, their relationship with orders, and their difficulty in separating function from desire can seem familiar and profoundly alien at the same time. A Luphran can respect Formix efficiency while simultaneously distrusting a group that obeys without the visible friction of a healthy crew. A Formix can see the jarl as a dangerous or enviable figure: someone obeyed not by chemical signal, but by social recognition that can be lost.

**To the Panin:** They irritate and fascinate the Luphran. They are fast, useful in crises, good at detecting immediate changes, and terrible at sustaining long routes. A Panin can save a vessel by noticing a flaw before anyone else, and then forget why it was important to check the same spot tomorrow. Crews learn to use them as a spark, not as a rope.

**To the Chelicer:** They cause discomfort with their relationship to verdict and procedure. For a Luphran, a leader's truth is measured under hunger, water, combat, and retreat. For a Chelicer, a truth can pre-exist the act and reveal itself under correct conditions. The Luphran respect public proof but distrust any system that can tell a crew what is true without having been on the route.

**To the Sauri:** They often see Luphran fury as a force without a sufficient vessel. From their logic, a crossing that pierces body, command, and crew should be contained, channeled, and managed by structure. For the Luphran, that kind of containment can seem like a way of dying before fighting. Even so, both peoples understand something about thresholds, marks, and bodies receiving more than they can explain. The difference is that the Sauri tries to build a channel, while the Luphran asks if they can still return.

**To the Arakhel:** They understand the idea of a route better than many, but do not live it the same way. For an Arakhel, the crossing preserves debt, memory, and relationship; for a Luphran, the route is sustained because a crew travels it, defends it, closes it, or sings it. Both can collaborate in difficult passages, transit refuges, and routes that cross many hands' territories. Tension appears when the Arakhel asks for care of the bond and the Luphran responds with the need for movement. One protects the "between." The other protects the route that still feeds.

**To the Bufoni:** They clash with the Luphran over the concept of domain. A Bufoni expects territorial protocol, recognition of local presence, and correct relationship with the site. A Luphran crew measures territory by route, utility, tribute, danger, and memory of passage. For the Bufoni, that can be mobile arrogance. For the Luphran, a domain that prevents feeding the crew may be asking for conflict even if no one says so. Both understand that places respond. They disagree on whether the response gives the right to stop a route.

**To the Myo:** They uneasily record collective spikes of fury. An entire crew approaching the crossing can produce patterns that the Myo do not confuse with simple violence. Some oracles have warned holds about jarls who had not yet failed, only because in their visions the route behind them appeared too red, too repeated, too hungry. The Luphran listen sometimes. Other times they decide that a people who see before being there have no right to speak of a route they haven't traveled.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

**Language:** Ulfral (native tongue). Común for cross-species exchange.

**The vessel:** The bio-organic vessel is the most significant material object in Luphran culture. Living hull organism, cultivated and shaped for river and coastal travel. Requires feeding, specific water conditions, periodic beaching for rest. If neglected, it dies. A crew whose vessel dies is stranded with everything they own in hostile terrain.

Vessels carry accumulated history in their structure — repaired sections, growth patterns from different routes, the marks of what they've carried. An experienced Luphran can read a vessel's travel history the way an Ursari reads a weapon's work history. Old vessels that have survived many seasons are honored; their loss is treated as crew-member loss, not equipment loss.

**Weapons:** Luphran weapons are working tools, not sacred objects. They accumulate route-marks — crew marks added at holds, marks indicating specific territories traversed, and the personal marks of the bearer. These are practical records (who carried this, where it has been, what conditions it has survived) as much as identity markers.

**Hold architecture:** Holds are built for communal use without implying permanent ownership. Modular, repairable, built to accommodate multiple crew configurations. Nothing in a hold's design suggests a single crew's dominance.

**Clothing and gear:** Designed for rapid transition between vessel conditions and land conditions. Waterproofing, layering for temperature change, equipment that can be stowed compactly. The Úlfhéðnar warrior tradition includes ritual vestments used in battle-fury preparation — specific materials, specific preparations. These are crew-specific, not standardized; each crew has evolved its own version over generations.

**Oral tradition as primary archive:** Nothing critical is written. Route knowledge, law, crew history, the records of jarls and their battles and their changes — all oral, distributed across the crew and especially the law-speakers. The death of the oldest crew member on a long voyage is a catastrophic operational event, not only a personal loss.

## 9. System Connections

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Instinto de Jauría (Herencia)** — The pack animal operating alone is neurologically stressed. Not a failure of character — a structural biological cost. Mechanically: When the Luphran has no ally within 10 m, they suffer a -3 penalty to all T.R. and T.E. of Compostura.

**Memoria de Ruta (Legado)** — Mechanically: +1 per 4 NR to T.E. of Rastreo, Intuición, and Supervivencia. The persistence-predator's essential competencies.

**El Ancestro Despierta (Legado)** — Mechanically: Active technique. Enters the *Furia Instintiva* state and tracks a **Fury Counter** starting at 0. Each time they perform an instinctive T.E. (Saltar, Trepar, Acrobacias, Supervivencia, Intuición, Rastreo), they add +1 to the counter before rolling, and the roll gains the counter as a bonus. Each time they perform a cognitive T.E. (Identificación, Interpretación, Percepción, Saberes), they subtract 1 from the counter, and the roll receives the counter (as a negative) as a penalty. The counter maxes out at their Tenacidad (positive and negative). The state ends at combat end or via a T.C. Enfoque.

**El Lobo No Suelta (Legado)** — Passive. Enemies moving out of physical contact with a Luphran ALWAYS generate an opportunity reaction. No technique or ability that prevents movement opportunity attacks works if the movement starts adjacent to the Luphran.

**Armas Naturales:**
- *Mordisco (Todos los Luphran)*: Principal. Fuerza/Fuerza. Restriction: No other weapon can be used in the same activation. On strong hit (3+ NR), target fails T.R. Alteraciones -> Target cannot perform reactions until their next activation. (Locking down the prey).
- *Garras (Todos los Luphran)*: Auxiliar. Agilidad/Agilidad. Attacks with claws do not generate opportunity reactions for the target, allowing tactical repositioning without reprisal.

### Combat identity

The Luphran combat identity is about relentless pursuit and escalating violence. They are not ambushers; they outlast. *El Lobo No Suelta* makes them impossible to flee from safely. *El Ancestro Despierta* turns combat into a snowballing spiral where instinct takes over, making them terrifyingly effective at savage, physical actions while rapidly losing their cognitive grip on the situation.

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

### Why a PC leaves (or is already away)

A Luphran outside their crew has either lost the crew — catastrophic battle, vessel loss, jarl succession crisis that scattered members — or has been given an explicit mandate: recover a route that went silent, negotiate a tribute agreement in unfamiliar territory, find a specific law-speaker who carries records the home hold needs, verify what happened to a crew that didn't return.

Luphran who travel with mixed-species groups treat those groups as provisional crews. They cannot not organize people this way. The question of who the jarl is — who actually leads when things go wrong — surfaces quickly and is never fully resolved with non-Luphran who don't share the instinct.

### What a PC carries

**Outward:** Stamina and route-reading; tactical spatial awareness from pack-predator instincts; the capacity to read social hierarchy in any group within hours; the persistence to outlast rather than overpower; access to the battle-fury state.

**Unstated:** The record of how many times they've crossed the threshold. Whether they can feel the difference between what they decide and what has started deciding for them. Whether the jarl they followed before they left was the person they thought they were following.

### Character types

- Survivor of a crew dissolution who cannot organize themselves without the social structure of a crew around them
- Young warrior who entered the battle-fury state involuntarily and is trying to understand what happened
- Veteran jarl who notices something off in their own decisions and is traveling to find a law-speaker who might have records that explain it
- Law-speaker apprentice carrying partial records, trying to reach their master before the knowledge degrades further
- Crew member sent to recover a route that went silent — which means finding what happened to the crew that used to run it
- An Úlfhéðnar practitioner who can induce the state deliberately and is being sought for that capacity
- Someone whose crew lost its vessel and is now surviving on land in ways nothing in their background prepared them for

### What they fear losing first

The crew. Not the jarl, not the route, not the vessel — the crew as social coherence. A Luphran who loses the crew loses the structure through which they understand themselves. Everything else (route knowledge, vessel, even the theology) is recoverable. The people are not.

Second: the certainty that their own decisions are their own. This fear is usually unnamed and unexamined — the culture does not have vocabulary for it — but it surfaces as unease around the question of why they do things, and discomfort when people ask them to explain decisions that felt obvious in the moment.

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **Registros de Jarls Corruptos:** Los Portavoces (*Law-speakers*) mantienen un archivo oral encriptado llamado "El Invierno del Comandante". Es la lista de los Jarls cuyas mentes fueron devoradas por repetidos estados de furia. Nunca se discute con las tripulaciones para no quebrar la moral; es un secreto de estado.
- **La Gran Cacería (El Primordial):** La teología Luphran sostiene que el ancestro los está preparando para una confrontación final ("La Gran Cacería"). Trágicamente, los registros de los astrónomos Naghii insinúan que, si llega esa cacería orquestada por el Limbo, los guerreros marcados Luphran no serán los cazadores, sino los perros controlados.
- **Armas Naturales y Furia:** Se consolidaron las Garras como tácticas (para reposicionarse sin sufrir ataques) y el Mordisco como inmovilizador (para anular las reacciones del enemigo).

## 11. Cultura Material 

La cultura material Luphran (inspirada en la era vikinga y la Mongolia nómada) está diseñada para el movimiento constante. No construyen para la eternidad; construyen para la ruta.

- **Pieles de *Ulfhednar*:** Mantos ceremoniales confeccionados a partir del cuero y pelo de depredadores salvajes imbuidos con Tauma. Se utilizan en los rituales de iniciación para inducir la *Furia Instintiva*. Los Jarls veteranos suelen usarlos en batalla, aunque a veces el cuero actúa como un vestigo, dificultando que el guerrero "apague" la furia.
- **Estandartes de Ruta (*Sulde*):** Lanzas ceremoniales rematadas con crines o tiras de cuero que marcan el espíritu de la tripulación. Mientras el Jarl porte el *Sulde* o este esté clavado en el centro del combate, la jauría pelea con cohesión absoluta. Perder el estandarte es un golpe letal a la moral de la tripulación.
- **Bastones de Memoria (*Skáld*):** Los Portavoces no escriben; tallan muescas en largos bastones de madera taumática o hueso. El bastón no es un libro, es una ayuda mnemotécnica. Un Portavoz que pasa el dedo por una muesca específica puede recitar siglos de precedentes legales y linajes de Jarls sin equivocarse jamás.
