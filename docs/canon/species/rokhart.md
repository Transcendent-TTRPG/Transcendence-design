## 1. Identity

The Rokhart observe. That is not a metaphor for their personality — it is their civilizational project, maintained across generations, across distances, across every alliance they have ever built. They go where the data is, establish presence without integrating, and return information to a network that spans more of the world than any political entity has ever controlled without armies.

They are not conquerors. They are not missionaries. They are not archivists in the passive sense. They are researchers who understood early that the world's most important phenomena cannot be studied by standing in the middle of them — and organized an entire civilization around that constraint.

What makes them distinct from every other species is not their intelligence. Multiple species in the world are highly intelligent. What distinguishes the Rokhart is that they are the only species that has systematically observed enough territories, enough cultures, and enough expressions of the same Tauma phenomena to suspect — with something approaching evidence — that the theological frameworks everyone else uses to explain the world are not wrong exactly, but are not the explanation either. They are the description of a pattern that has a cause no one has found yet.

They do not share this understanding. They carry it carefully, the way they carry all their most important knowledge: internally, with the awareness that releasing it to the wrong context destroys more than it illuminates.

The civilizational anxiety the Rokhart carry without ever naming directly: **everything the model still needs to be complete requires doing the one thing the methodology forbids. They have known this for generations. No one has taken that step — which means either no one has wanted to, or everyone who tried could not report back.**

## 2. Body

Rokhart are medium humanoids descended from large corvid-raptor bird stock: upright posture, bipedal, with powerful digitigrade legs built for speed and grip rather than sustained standing. Their arms are adapted wings — broad, feathered, incapable of sustained flight but structurally large, used for balance, display, combat leverage, and thermoregulation. The feathers across body and wings vary from region of origin: some lines carry vivid iridescent plumage, others more subdued tones, all of it functional for communication within their own species.

Their skeleton is hollow and lightweight — the structural legacy of flight ancestry. This makes them lighter and faster than their size would suggest, but structurally vulnerable to blunt force. A Rokhart who absorbs repeated blunt impact will fracture in ways that a denser-boned species would not.

Their eyes are the feature other species notice first: large, forward-facing, set in a skull built to track small movements at extreme distances. A Rokhart can identify the color and approximate size of an object at distances that would render it invisible to most humanoid eyes. Their visual processing is oriented toward pattern detection — small changes in a larger field — rather than color richness. They see the shape of things well before they see the details.

Their beak is not primarily a weapon. It is a precision instrument: capable of delicate manipulation, fine articulation, and targeted force applied to small areas. In combat it functions as a penetrating strike to specific points — not heavy damage, but accurate damage to the right location.

Their talons are the primary natural weapon: the foot structure retains powerful gripping claws capable of anchoring, tearing, and most importantly mobile repositioning. A Rokhart in combat moves constantly, strikes from unexpected positions, and retreats before a counterattack can land. They are not built to absorb hits. They are built to not be where hits are going.

### Physical baseline

- **Height:** 1.60 – 1.80 m
- **Weight:** 50 – 70 kg
- **Lifespan:** 70 – 80 years
- **Size category:** Medium

### Visible variation

Plumage pattern and coloration vary significantly by regional lineage. Within Concordia posts, Rokhart from different origins coexist, making visual variety the normal condition rather than the exception. Plumage is not culturally modified — no dyes, no ritual markings — but natural patterns become associated over time with the posts or lineages that carry them.

## 3. Culture

Rokhart society is organized around a single institution: the **Concordia**. It is not a government. It does not claim territory, issue laws to non-Rokhart populations, or maintain armies for expansion. What it does is maintain the network — the infrastructure of communication, the protocols of observation, the system of access agreements that allow Rokhart researchers to be present in strategic locations across the world without being perceived as a threat.

The Concordia functions because it offers genuine value to the communities it allies with: information, mediation, access to knowledge accumulated across many territories, occasional material resources. What it receives in return — access to sites of interest, freedom of movement, the right to establish and maintain small presences — is not perceived as equivalent to what it gives. That asymmetry is deliberate. Every Concordia post operates at a calculated surplus of perceived generosity.

The internal logic of Concordia post placement has nothing to do with theology, ruins, or cultural familiarity. Posts are established where:
- Two or more distinct belief systems create adjacent territories where the same Tauma element manifests differently — a natural comparative experiment
- Access to zones of known Tauma concentration is possible without direct exposure
- Trade routes or political proximity create access to multiple species without requiring extensive travel
- Long-term observation of a specific phenomenon has been ongoing and is not yet complete

The result is that Concordia posts exist in locations that often seem arbitrary to outsiders but form, seen from above, a research map of the world's most significant extranatural phenomena.

**Internal coherence** across posts is unusually high for a distributed institution. The reason is structural: without theology, there is no doctrinal drift. Without territorial claims, there is no resource competition between posts. The shared methodology — what counts as valid observation, how information is recorded and transmitted, what constitutes a conclusion versus a working hypothesis — creates alignment that persists across distance. A Rokhart from a post on one end of the world and another from the opposite end will disagree on specific findings but not on how to evaluate disagreements. That shared evaluation framework is what makes the Concordia function as a single thing.

**The Concordia does not eliminate internal tension.** The deepest tension is one that is never formally discussed: the methodology has a ceiling. There is a class of information that cannot be obtained through external observation. Every senior researcher knows this. The institution's response has been to accumulate more external data rather than address the ceiling directly. Whether this is wisdom, fear, or the accumulated weight of knowing that those who have tried to cross the line may not have been able to return — no one states openly.

**Rokhart who leave Concordia posts** do so for various reasons: extended field assignment in other communities, voluntary departure, expulsion, or the specific and rare case of researchers who decided to break the methodology. Those who leave on assignment or voluntarily remain connected to the network and eventually return. Those expelled have compromised something the Concordia considers critical — usually information security, occasionally methodology violation. Those who broke the methodology occupy an uncomfortable category: they may know more than anyone in the Concordia, but they are no longer reliable sources by the institution's standards. The Concordia does not pursue or harm them — it simply stops treating their reports as valid data. The isolation this creates is effective and deniable.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Rokhart do not have theology. This is not a gap — it is a position reached through accumulated evidence.

What they have instead is a working model: incomplete, subject to revision, more accurate than any theological framework they have observed, and still not sufficient to answer the questions that matter most.

The model's most solid finding, replicated across generations of comparative observation: the same Tauma element manifests differently in different territories, and the difference correlates with the collective beliefs of the communities that have inhabited those territories over time. Fire as destruction, fire as renewal, fire as transformation — same energy, different faces, different in ways that match what the local culture has believed about fire for generations. The correlation is too consistent across too many territories to be coincidence.

What the model has not resolved: the direction of the causation. Does the collective consciousness shape the Tauma, or does the Tauma — by flowing through the communities that inhabit those zones — shape the collective consciousness? The Rokhart have data suggesting both directions operate. They do not have data sufficient to determine which is primary, or whether the distinction is meaningful. This unresolved question sits at the center of the working model and has not moved in several generations.

What the Rokhart think happened in the ancient catastrophe: they have the most comprehensive comparative records of any species — accounts from multiple traditions, geological observations, the behavior of Tauma phenomena at sites with preserved pre-catastrophic history. Their working interpretation is that the catastrophe was not caused by an external force or entity but by a convergence: communities whose collective beliefs had reached a density of coherence sufficient to produce Primordial expressions of unusual power, combined with conditions that made those expressions unstable rather than stabilizing. They are not certain. The record is incomplete. But this interpretation is the one best supported by what they have.

What they do not have is a belief about what it means. Every other species can reach for theology when the model runs out. The Rokhart reach for more data. That reaching — and the knowledge that it does not end — is the closest thing they have to a cosmological relationship with the world.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

**The Concordia** comprises all recognized Rokhart observation posts and the communication infrastructure connecting them. It has no single geographic center — the closest thing to a central function is the network itself. Major decisions affecting the entire Concordia require communication across multiple posts and are slow for that reason.

**Post Keepers** (working title — see language section) are the senior researchers responsible for each post. They manage access agreements, direct local observation priorities, and serve as the primary points of communication with the network. They are not governors — they do not administer a population — but their authority within the post is near-absolute on methodological questions.

**Field Observers** are Rokhart embedded in other communities for extended periods, sometimes years. They are the most valuable members of the research network and the most methodologically at-risk: long immersion in theological frameworks, relationships with members of other species, and sustained absence from Concordia culture all create conditions where methodological drift is possible. The Concordia monitors field observers through regular required reporting. Gaps in reporting are noted.

**Wanderers** are Rokhart who have left their posts and are not currently assigned elsewhere. Some wander by choice — researchers who found the post structure limiting, individuals pursuing specific questions the Concordia is not interested in, or those who simply wanted to see the world rather than observe a specific part of it. Some wander by expulsion. All wanderers are, technically, still Rokhart — the Concordia does not revoke identity — but they have no access to the network's current data.

**Methodology dissenters** — those who exposed themselves to afflictions — exist in an undocumented category. The Concordia does not formally acknowledge them. What is known, only at senior levels, is that the category exists and that the few individuals who crossed that line have not returned to Concordia life. Whether this is because they could not, or because what they found made return impossible, or because the Concordia made return impossible — this question is never asked openly.

## 6. Daily Life and Life Cycle

The Rokhart lifespan of 70–80 years is short for a species that measures its research in generational timescales. The practical consequence is that knowledge transfer cannot wait — young Rokhart enter observation training early, not as apprentices to a trade but as participants in ongoing research from the first years of functional cognition.

Life in a Concordia post is disciplined without being rigid. The day organizes around observation, documentation, and communication: what was seen, what it means, what the network needs to know. The rhythm is iterative — hypotheses are recorded, tested against new observations, revised. There is less ritual than in any other species' daily life and more deliberate record-keeping.

Social bonds within posts are real but not primary. The Rokhart recognize kin and form attachments, but the institution comes before family in cases of conflict. Post assignments can separate families for years. This is considered a cost of the project, not a flaw in the system.

Death in a Concordia post triggers a specific documentation process: the deceased researcher's complete observation record is reviewed, cross-referenced with current working hypotheses, and archived. The body is disposed of practically. There is no mourning ritual. The loss of a researcher is recorded as a reduction in productive capacity and an archival opportunity — whatever the researcher knew that was never written down is now gone.

Young Rokhart raised outside Concordia structure — in other communities, or by wanderers — develop very differently. They may retain the cognitive patterns that mark the species (pattern recognition, composure under pressure, the instinct to observe before acting) but without the methodological framework. They often find the Concordia disorienting when they encounter it — an institution that treats their most personal experiences as data.

## 7. External Relations

The Rokhart are found on every major landmass where the Concordia has determined there is observational value. No territory is theirs in the political sense — they occupy positions by agreement, not by claim.

Their reputation varies by species and region:

**To most communities:** reliable information brokers, neutral mediators, scholars with useful knowledge of distant places. They are seen as cold but not hostile, transactional but trustworthy in their transactions. Most species have at least one story of Rokhart assistance that cost nothing obvious and wonder occasionally what was actually exchanged.

**To species with strong theological frameworks:** sometimes unsettling. The Rokhart never challenge theology directly — they are too methodologically careful for that — but their questions reveal the edge of doctrinal knowledge in ways that make some theological authorities uncomfortable. A Rokhart researcher who asks the same question three different ways is not being rude. They are testing the internal consistency of the answer. The communities that trust them most are usually those whose institutions are stable enough to tolerate the testing.

**To the Loxod:** a complex, cautious relationship between two knowledge-oriented species with radically different methods. The Loxod preserve oral tradition over geological time; the Rokhart document everything in writing and measure claims against observation. They each have information the other does not, and neither trusts the other's methodology enough to share freely.

**To the Vesper:** unacknowledged parallels and genuine differences. Both species are interested in the mechanism behind the extranatural. The Vesper believe the answer is in biology — in the blood and tissue of creatures with Tauma connection. The Rokhart believe the answer is in the pattern of collective behavior over time. They have never collaborated. If they did, the result would either be the most significant research partnership in the world's history or a conflict over whose methodology is actually valid.

**To the Myo:** the Rokhart have observed Myo territories and found them unusually resistant to pattern analysis. The behavior of Tauma in those zones does not follow the correlations observed elsewhere. They have not identified why. This is recorded as an anomaly, not a priority. If they understood what the Myo were actually doing — that their oracles are intentionally directing collective consciousness to shape local reality — it would be the most significant confirmation of their central hypothesis they have ever encountered. They do not know this yet.

**To the Naghii:** the relationship oscillates between admiration and frustration. Both preserve, compare, and distrust quick conclusions. But Naghii organize knowledge around sacred archives and restricted access; Rokhart organize it around replicable method and distributed observation. To a Naghii, Concordia separates knowledge from reverence; to a Rokhart, Naghii protect interpretations out of fear of not having another.

**To the Sauri:** Sauri are valuable for the model and dangerous as observation neighbors. A Sauri sovereign or sealed chamber is phenomenon, symbol, technology, and government all at once. Sauri do not always tolerate those who take notes on vessels they consider sacred or secret.

**To the Zarnag:** Rokhart value Zarnag memory of death sites and contamination, but often underestimate the cost of Zarnag work. An observation necessary for a Rokhart might require a Zarnag to enter toxic air first. Zarnag quickly detect when a Rokhart wants to preserve a site that should be closed.

**To the Drak'kai:** natural allies and inevitable rivals. Both accumulate knowledge on long scales but oppositely: Drak'kai carry objects and personal experience; Rokhart distribute protocols and archives. Drak'kai accuse Concordia of turning living findings into dead files; Rokhart accuse Drak'kai of keeping truths tied to private collections.

**To the Panin:** noise and revelation. Their presence alters patterns and interrupts sequences. But precisely because they don't respect structure, they can reveal hidden assumptions in the method. Rokhart posts tolerate Panin better in theory than in practice.

**To the Ceratox:** Ceratox pursue through biology what Rokhart pursue through historical patterns (how environment, manifestation, and perception modify each other). There is local collaboration, technical respect, and mutual distrust about who is looking at the wrong part of the phenomenon.

**To the Arakhel:** Arakhel nodes are involuntary observatories of transit. Concordia would love to measure them from the outside, but Arakhel families inhabit the crossing. To Arakhel, the Rokhart gaze is a way of failing to understand that the 'in-between' cannot be conserved as data without changing its nature.

**To the Bufoni:** active Bufoni domains confirm many Rokhart suspicions about conductors (soil, lineage, rite, offering, accumulated response). Bufoni distrust anyone who compares domains as if they were equivalent cases; to them, each domain responds from a concrete relationship.

**To the Ursari:** Rokhart respect Ursari material discipline and distrust their reliance on personal mastery. Ursari see in Concordia a culture that records the risk of exposure but doesn't always know when to stop looking.

**To the Chelicer:** an almost intolerable challenge. Chelicer believe truth is revealed under correct procedure; Rokhart believe every procedure produces conditions of observation that become part of the result. Chelicer see Rokhart doubt as evasion; Rokhart see Chelicer certainty as institutionalized contamination.

**To the Formix:** Concordia observes communities from distributed posts; free Formix know from the body what it means to live in a structure where signal precedes the individual. Rokhart study free Formix cautiously, aware their own network might be reflected in ways they'd prefer not to record.

**To the Luphran:** Luphran preserve route memory and crew oral law in ways Concordia cannot reduce to reports without losing the essence. Rokhart value that memory but suspect its dependence on personal authority. Luphran distrust a species that remembers much but responds little when the crew demands action.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

**Writing and documentation** are the center of Rokhart material culture. Everything is recorded: observations, hypotheses, correspondence, life events. The physical artifact that marks a Rokhart post more than any architecture is the archive — extensive, systematically organized, cross-referenced. The quality and depth of a post's archive is the primary indicator of that post's value to the Concordia.

**Architecture** reflects the priorities of observation: high positions when possible, wide visual fields, communication infrastructure designed for reliability over aesthetics. Rokhart posts are functional without being harsh — there is care in construction, but it serves the work rather than displaying status.

**Art** exists but is rarely purely decorative. Maps, comparative observation records, illustrated documentation of phenomena — the boundary between art and research is not always clear. The Rokhart find beauty in precision and in the moment when a pattern becomes visible that was not visible before.

**Language — Alar:** the Rokhart language is characterized by precision in its vocabulary for states of evidence: there are distinct terms for observed fact, inferred conclusion, working hypothesis, and documented uncertainty. A claim that would require three qualifying phrases in common tongue can be stated in one construction in Alar that encodes all the relevant epistemic weight. This precision is also what makes Rokhart speech feel clinical to other species — not because they lack feeling, but because their language does not treat feelings and observations as equivalent categories.

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Fragilidad Ósea (Herencia)** — Their hollow skeleton makes blunt impact extremely dangerous. Mechanically: While Prone, physical attacks received step up their damage die.

**Imperturbable (Legado)** — Unbreakable methodological composure. Mechanically: Scaling bonuses (+1 per 4 NR) to Enfoque, Contención, Meditación, and Aplomo.

**Visión de Rapaz (Legado)** — Their corvid-raptor eyes are built to track small changes across vast distances. Mechanically: Visual range extended to 70m. Enemies cannot Hide using Light Cover; they require at least Medium Cover.

**La Batida (Legado)** — Strike and reposition without absorbing retaliation. Mechanically: Reactive technique (TC Agility) against melee attacks. Displaces the Rokhart up to half their Speed without provoking reactions. If out of range, the attack fails.

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

**Why a Rokhart leaves a post:**
Assignment to embed in a community long-term. Voluntary departure to pursue a specific question the Concordia is not interested in. Expulsion for methodology violation or information compromise. The rare case of a researcher who decided to cross the line the Concordia forbids — and survived. Or the even rarer case of a young Rokhart raised outside the Concordia who encounters the institution for the first time as an adult and has to decide whether to join it.

**What they lose when they leave:**
Access to the network's accumulated data — generations of comparative observations they can no longer consult. The protection of institutional identity — a Concordia-affiliated Rokhart has access agreements and reputational cover that a wanderer does not. The community of others who think the same way.

**What they keep:**
The methodology — the pattern of observation and evaluation that was trained before they could argue with it. The composure. The instinct to read a situation for what it actually is rather than what it presents as. And, if they were senior enough before leaving, a significant amount of data in their own records.

**Tensions for PC play:**
A Rokhart in a world of theological species is a character who sees the frame around every belief system while still needing to function within those systems diplomatically. They know — or suspect they know — things about how the world works that the people around them would react to badly if stated directly. They have been trained to never state them directly. That training is valuable and constraining in equal measure.

A Rokhart who broke the methodology is a more extreme case: they have subjective experience of something the Concordia has only ever observed from the outside. They are carrying data the institution would desperately want and has officially categorized as invalid. They may not know how to interpret what they experienced. They have no community that fully understands where they are.

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **Los PJs Rokhart y los Vestigos:** Un Personaje Jugador Rokhart es, casi por definición, alguien que ha roto con la Concordia. Han intervenido en el mundo (violación metodológica) o han decidido vagar por su cuenta. Al no operar bajo la doctrina institucional estricta, un PJ Rokhart es completamente libre de usar Vestigos y reliquias; ya no tienen que proteger "la muestra", ahora deben sobrevivir en el mundo.
- **La Doctrina del Secreto:** La Concordia no es una "sociedad secreta" por motivos ocultistas; su secretismo es puramente metodológico. Lo primero que aprende un observador es a ocultar sus verdaderas intenciones, porque cualquier emoción visible contamina a la comunidad estudiada. Su aparente doble intención es, desde adentro, simple rigor de laboratorio.
- **Jerarquía Institucional (Terminología Alar):**
    - *Nidos / Postulantes* (Novicios en formación).
    - *Augures* (Observadores de Campo infiltrados en otras culturas).
    - *Custodios* (Guardianes de Puesto y analistas de datos locales).
    - *El Círculo de Archiveros* (El cuerpo administrativo central que cruza las observaciones globales).

## 11. Cultura Material y Herramientas de Registro

El mapeo cultural de los Rokhart amalgama a los cuervos de Odín (Huginn y Muninn), a Thoth (el dios egipcio de los registros) y a los Augures Romanos. Los Rokhart no buscan Vestigos de guerra; buscan y fabrican herramientas sagradas de registro para asegurar que la historia sea inalterable.

- **Plumas de Muninn:** Objetos rituales escasos (a veces plumas petrificadas o de cristal volcánico). Si un Rokhart encuentra una, puede reproducir pasivamente ante sus ojos la escena exacta que la pluma "grabó", aunque haya ocurrido hace siglos, actuando como la memoria perfecta.
- **Piedras Rúnicas de Archivo:** En lugar de papel, los Custodios de la Concordia usan losas de piedra magnética para inscribir los hallazgos más peligrosos o anómalos. Las tallan de forma que el entorno o la radiación de los monstruos locales no pueda borrar la verdad.
- **Báculos de Augur (*Lituus*):** Bastones curvos, similares a los usados en la antigua Roma. Los utilizan para demarcar físicamente el *templum*: un espacio trazado en la tierra donde toda observación empírica es válida. Es una herramienta de aislamiento psíquico; dentro del círculo trazado, ninguna ilusión extranatural puede distorsionar los sentidos del Rokhart.
- **Plumas de Ma'at:** Pequeñas pesas rituales o estiletes. Usados durante interrogatorios o recolección de datos orales, estas plumas vibran o cambian de peso para revelar si el sujeto observado miente en su intención basal.
