## 1. Identity

The Arakhel are the only species that does not cross the between. They inhabit it.

Every other species treats the hostile wilderness between settlements as a problem: something to survive, transit, map, avoid, or eventually conquer. The Arakhel understand this relationship as a misorientation so fundamental it shapes how every other species thinks, moves, and fails. The between is not an obstacle between worlds. It is the world. Settlements are edges. The crossings are the center.

Their civilization is organized around this inversion: a network of nodes — maintained locations throughout the most dangerous territory in the world — that are navigable only by those who have learned the node's language. Not the language of the Arakhel, which can be studied. The language of the crossing, which must be earned through declaration and offering. Any traveler can attempt to cross the between. Survival is their own problem. Those who want guidance learn that certain points respond to presence and declaration. The path becomes readable. The Arakhel receive what passes through their network involuntarily, through vibration-sensing biology extended into the silk structures they build across terrain.

The information asymmetry is not a policy. It is structural and permanent. The Arakhel know what moves through their network because the network tells them. They did not choose this. They were built this way.

Their civilizational anxiety is quiet and rarely stated: **they cannot know whether what deepens in the oldest nodes is the sacred relationship their cosmology describes, or something that found the accumulated offerings generations ago and has been crossing through their families without their knowledge.** The sensation is identical in both cases. The difference matters completely. They have not found a way to test it.

## 2. Body

Arakhel are medium humanoids descended from large spider stock: lean, low-center frames built for economy of movement and vibration sensitivity across terrain. Their posture inclines slightly forward at rest — a body accustomed to moving across surfaces at variable angles. Their gait is almost silent.

They have eight eyes arranged in two rows across a wide face: the primary pair — large and forward-facing — and secondary rows that provide wide peripheral coverage without head movement. In low-light conditions, their eyes reflect ambient light. In full darkness they navigate primarily through vibration.

Their mouths carry chelicerae — hollow fangs below the jaw that fold flat when closed and extend when biting. These are their primary natural weapon and their primary tool for venom delivery. The bite itself is fast and precise. The effect arrives afterward.

Their hands have one critical modification: retractable spinnerets at the inner wrist and palm that produce raw silk on demand. Production rates are low under ordinary circumstances and increase substantially during rest, when the body allocates energy to silk synthesis. This silk is the material of everything they build, everything they mark, and the medium through which they sense what their environment is doing.

Vibration sensitivity is the sense that most distinguishes them from other species. Their body surface reads vibration through direct contact with any silk they have anchored. This extends the effective range of their perception: an Arakhel standing on a silk line anchored to a structure thirty meters away knows that the structure has been disturbed, from what direction, and approximately what weight produced the disturbance. They cannot fully explain this sensation to most species because there is no equivalent.

### Physical baseline

- **Height:** 1.45–1.70 m
- **Weight:** 50–80 kg
- **Lifespan:** 50–70 years
- **Size category:** Medium

Their lifespan is short by humanoid standards. This is not experienced as tragedy — Arakhel culture does not frame brevity against longevity. It is experienced as the appropriate tempo of a species whose knowledge lives in the network, not in any individual who dies.

### Visible variation

Arakhel vary significantly in coloration, pattern, and eye arrangement. Node-family lineages tend toward recognizable physical patterns — markings that other Arakhel can read as lineage indicators the way Drak'kai read shell markings. No biological caste structure correlates to these patterns; they are identity markers without formal hierarchy.

## 3. Culture

Arakhel society is organized by the node and the family that tends it.

A **node** is a maintained location at a strategic crossing point in the between. Not every crossing has a node. A crossing becomes a node through sustained Arakhel presence: silk structures anchored to terrain, offerings accumulated over generations, and the gradual deepening of whatever the Arakhel cosmology calls the sacred relationship with the crossing. Nodes are inherited. The family that maintains a node does not own it — it is the node's keeper, not its proprietor, and the distinction matters in how they speak about their work.

Node-families are the core social unit. They tend toward small: a mated pair, their offspring, and at most one or two additional family members. Large gatherings at a node are unusual and treated as a sign of either unusual event or structural breakdown. The normal state is a small family managing a crossing point in largely solitary conditions, in contact with adjacent families through silk relay and periodic meeting at established points along shared routes.

What earns respect: depth of node knowledge, age of the family's tenure at its crossing, quality of guidance offered to travelers who come correctly. What is shameful: abandoning a node without cause, misreading the offerings that travelers leave, giving guidance that gets someone killed because the Arakhel did not read their network correctly.

The **offering protocol** is the primary interface between Arakhel and other species. Travelers who learn that certain crossing points respond to declaration and offering approach a node's marker, identify themselves by lineage or generational context — not by current name — and leave an object of genuine personal history. The object stays. The path opens. What "opens" means is not uniform: sometimes the terrain becomes legible in ways the traveler cannot later describe. Sometimes they find shelter they should not have found. Sometimes nothing observable happens and they survive a crossing that kills others. The Arakhel at the node receive the offering through the silk. They did not teach the travelers to do this. The nodes accumulated this capacity over generations, through the vestigos of those who crossed before.

Authority in Arakhel society is informal and experiential. The oldest families at the oldest nodes carry the most weight when communities of nodes coordinate — which happens rarely, at meeting points along their relay networks, when something threatens the network itself.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Arakhel theology centers on a single claim: the between is the real world. Settlements are places where the world's surface has been pushed back long enough for a community to mistake the margin for the center. The crossings are where the world speaks. The node is not a place to pass through — it is where passage is consecrated, acknowledged, and recorded.

In their cosmological framework, what happens at a node when a traveler leaves an offering is a form of declaration to the world itself. The crossing acknowledges those who acknowledge it. This is not metaphorical. The mechanism exists and produces observable results. The Arakhel believe the results occur because the world recognizes declaration in good faith. What they cannot establish is whether the world is doing the recognizing, or whether the accumulated vestigos of generations of such declarations have become something that responds on its own.

The oldest nodes are the ones where the cosmological claim feels most unambiguous. Families that have tended old nodes for many generations describe what they call the crossing maturing: a growing intimacy with the node, a sense that the between recognizes them specifically, that their presence at the crossing is not merely maintained but welcomed. This is the experience their theology names as the sacred relationship deepening.

The horror that runs beneath Arakhel theological life: this experience is indistinguishable from a vínculo that formed quietly in the accumulated offerings and has been using their proximity as a crossing point for generations. A vínculo entity does not announce itself. It does what its nature produces. The Arakhel have read enough about vínculos — from Drak'kai accounts they encounter at crossing points — to know that the sensation they call sacred recognition could be something else entirely. They have not found a way to test this without abandoning the node. Their cosmology calls abandonment of the node abandonment of the center of the world. This is not a position they can move from without the framework collapsing.

What they believe about the catastrophe: the ancient world was not destroyed by something outside the world. It was destroyed by species that confused settlements for centers and the between for void. Civilizations built to face inward failed when what moves through the between kept moving. The Arakhel believe their survival of the catastrophe was not luck — it was structural. The between did not become more dangerous; it was always this dangerous. The species that depended on settled infrastructure collapsed when the infrastructure failed. The Arakhel did not depend on it.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

The Arakhel are not one civilization. They are a network of family lineages that share biology, language, and a cosmology of the crossing, organized around the node system they have maintained since before the catastrophe.

What is universal across all Arakhel communities:
- The Araji language, spoken and silk-transmitted
- The node as the fundamental social institution
- The offering protocol as the primary interface with other species
- The cosmology of the between as center

What varies:
- Node age and prestige — older nodes carry more theological weight and more accumulated vestigos
- Relay connection — some nodes sit at high-traffic crossings with frequent travelers; others sit at remote points with long silences between crossings
- Interpretive lineage — families that have tended nodes long enough develop their own internal traditions for reading offerings, interpreting what moves through the silk, and explaining the deepening relationship with the crossing

Internal fault lines:
- **The testing question:** Whether it is possible to examine what lives in the oldest nodes without abandoning them, and whether any family has the standing to make that decision for their lineage
- **The Drak'kai problem:** Drak'kai who pass through old nodes sometimes identify the accumulated vestigos as a known Tauma pattern and say so. This creates a conflict that has no resolution within either species' framework — the Drak'kai are not wrong by their cosmological standards and the Arakhel cannot accept the implication without the framework collapsing
- **The young nodes:** Families that established new nodes after the catastrophe lack the accumulated history that gives old nodes their character. Whether a new node can develop into what an old one is, or whether something was lost that cannot be rebuilt, is a quiet generational tension

## 6. Daily Life and Survival

Life at a node is a maintenance practice. Silk structures require upkeep — the between damages them through weather, creature contact, and whatever forces concentrate at crossing points with elevated Tauma flow. An Arakhel at a node is always building and repairing.

Their rest periods are the most productive periods biologically: silk synthesis accelerates during full rest, and an Arakhel who has slept well produces substantially more raw silk than one who hasn't. This makes sleep a production activity, not merely a recovery one. The raw silk from a night's rest represents real material value — the family's primary export and the medium of their own construction.

Food at the node is hunted and scavenged from the between. The Arakhel are predators — their venom is effective on prey as well as in combat, and they are patient stalkers. They do not maintain livestock or agriculture in the settled sense. What they have at the node is what the between provides and what travelers leave in exchange for guidance.

Young Arakhel are born at the node. They spend their early years learning the specific character of the family's crossing: what its silk network feels like when an animal of a certain size enters from a certain direction, what its offerings have accumulated, how to read the vibrations of the between without confusing signal with noise. This is not teachable from a text. It is experiential and specific to the node. A young Arakhel who has never left their family's crossing could not fluently read another family's node — the information is there but the vocabulary isn't.

Coming-of-age is the first solo crossing an Arakhel completes: leaving the family node, moving through someone else's territory or un-nodded between, and returning. Many young Arakhel spend years moving between established nodes before returning to tend what they've inherited. This period produces wanderers — Arakhel without nodes who move through the between collecting experience and contact. A wanderer who returns to a node with useful knowledge of what lies between nodes is worth more than one who stayed home.

Death at the node is treated as a transfer, not an ending. The offering objects accumulated over a lifetime don't leave. The knowledge of the crossing stays in the silk. The family continues. What a dying Arakhel fears is dying away from the node — in someone else's territory, where what they knew cannot be read by the silk they built.

## 7. External Relations

The Arakhel are where you find them: at nodes throughout the between. They are not found in cities except as visitors, and visits are typically brief. Settlements make them uncomfortable in ways they do not fully articulate — the density of other people's perception and sound, and the absence of silk they've built themselves, reduces their effective sensory range to biological baseline. They tolerate it when necessary and leave as soon as they can.

**Other species' perception of them:** Most species have heard of the Arakhel before meeting one. The concept of crossing points that respond to offerings is cross-cultural knowledge — how to prepare, what counts as appropriate, what to expect — even if the theology behind it varies by who's telling it. The Arakhel are known as the keepers of the between. They are not known as approachable. They are approached anyway by anyone who needs to cross dangerous territory and wants the odds weighted in their favor.

**To the Drak'kai:** They are the most uncomfortable interlocutors. Not because they are hostile, but because what they accumulate includes patterns and measurements that allow them to make observations the Arakhel cosmology cannot accept without consequence. The family keeps working the node because it's what they do. The Drak'kai keeps saying what they see because it's what they do. A Drak'kai might ask if the family guards the node, or if the node learned to keep the family. Few Arakhel appreciate that question.

**To the Zarnag:** They work death sites in the between: points where something significant died and didn't close. These frequently overlap with crossings the Arakhel consider significant. Both species need what the other knows about the same territories. The exchange, when it happens, is specific and does not necessarily extend to general cooperation. A Zarnag may know that something hasn't finished leaving. An Arakhel may know where its echo is still moving.

**To the Ceratox:** Approaching old nodes, they react to the accumulation as a zone signal. Their horns read enough pressure there to go on alert, and the oldest nodes concentrate it in a way that triggers this response. The Arakhel interpret this as another species' inability to function in a sacred site. The Ceratox would describe it differently. Interactions tend to be brief.

**To the Luphran:** Crossing a node in a state of high nervous activation transmits more signal through the silk than they would choose to transmit. The Arakhel vibratory architecture receives that regardless of whether the Luphran knows they are broadcasting. The Arakhel remember that information. A marked captain can leave a signature that the node recognizes years later, even if the crew never returns. The Luphran do not always take kindly to someone remembering what their fury emitted without permission.

**To the Rokhart:** They want longitudinal access to the nodes for documented research. The offering protocol is incompatible with their methodological requirements: declaring identity and leaving an object of personal history alters what they are trying to measure. The Arakhel understand the incompatibility. They do not see it as their problem to solve. For a Rokhart, contaminating an observation is a methodological error. For an Arakhel, arriving without contaminating the crossing with recognition is arriving lying about what you are.

**To the Formix:** Attempting to use the offering protocol without generational history in the sense the node recognizes does not always trigger the expected response. Not because the Arakhel forbid it: the mechanism has no clear response to what they bring. A free Formix might arrive with a hive object, a function tool, a Queen's mark—something that was theirs before "theirs" meant anything. The node might not know what to do with that. The Arakhel can give verbal guidance. They cannot give what the node did not give.

**To the Loxod:** They can receive a density from an old node that is hard to explain: layers of offering, fear, passage, death, waiting, recognition, hunger, and return. Some Loxod deeply respect the emotional architecture of these crossings. Others find it dangerous for so much to accumulate without formal discharge. For the Arakhel, the node does not need to be discharged. It needs to be sustained. The difference can produce cooperation or rejection.

**To the Ursari:** They understand the link between site, practice, and body better than expected. A forge and a node are not the same, but both teach that the place participates in the work. An Ursari might ask what the thread does to the one who touches it for generations. An Arakhel might ask what the fire does to the one who listens to it too long. Neither answer is comfortable.

**To the Panin:** They are dangerous visitors. They touch without waiting for the crossing to finish presenting itself. They follow immediate inspirations, move offerings, cut threads out of curiosity, or accidentally discover tensions the family had stopped noticing. Some nodes overtly reject them. Others react to them with unsettling clarity. A Panin who listens to a present signal might find something in a node that had been waiting for a disturbance for years.

**To the Chelicer:** They find it difficult to accept a system where recognizing the crossing modifies the legibility of the path without a universal procedure. The Arakhel find it equally strange that anyone believes truth can be revealed in a chamber if conditions are controlled, when every real crossing is changed by who arrives at it. To a Chelicer, that sounds like impurity. To an Arakhel, it sounds like honesty.

**To the Naghii:** They respect the patience of a long reading more than others, but do not always tolerate its lack of a central archive. For a Naghii, the sky and its fragments demand careful translation, comparison, and written memory. For an Arakhel, the crossing cannot be translated outside itself without losing something essential. Both peoples know an incorrect reading can cost generations. They disagree on where that reading should live: in the archive, or in the thread.

**To the Sauri:** They feel affinity and distrust. A deep node can seem to them a poorly defined vessel: something that contains, conducts, or filters a force without sufficient architecture. The Arakhel respond that the node is not a vessel, but a relationship. For the Sauri, a relationship without a channel can break and spill. For the Arakhel, a channel too rigid can kill exactly what makes the crossing recognizable.

**To the Myo:** They pay attention to the shared dreams of old nodes. Where several family members dream the same thing in different chambers, a Myo can hear a pattern before the family admits a problem. Some Arakhel find this useful. Others find it intolerable: an outsider naming the node's dream before the family decides if it was a warning, a memory, or simple thread fatigue.

**To the Bufoni:** They understand that a place can demand protocol, but argue with the Arakhel over what kind of relationship produces authority. For a Bufoni, the domain recognizes, orders, and responds through forms of territorial respect that do not depend on the passage of outsiders. For an Arakhel, the node is made by transit, offering, thread, and custody. Both know crossing without recognition is a grave offense. But the Bufoni tends to ask who rules the place, while the Arakhel asks if the crossing accepted being traversed.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

An Arakhel's material world is largely made of what their body produces. Silk is the primary structural material at any node: anchor lines, structural supports, shelter walls, storage, the relay lines between nodes, and the sensory network that extends their perception across the crossing. Nothing about a node's silk is decorative in origin. It is built to function.

What becomes layered over time is the offering collection. Objects left at a node over generations accumulate anchored into the silk, protected by it — the offerings of many travelers given by many hands at different times. Visiting Arakhel from other lineages read a node's offering accumulation the way they read lineage patterning: as a biography of what has passed through, who left what, what kinds of crossings this node has consecrated.

Clothing is practical and often partially silk — garments that carry weight without slowing movement, that don't snag on the structures they work in. Arakhel working their own nodes tend toward minimalist dress. Arakhel visiting settlements or crossing foreign between territory dress for weather and contact, not display.

**Araji** is the Arakhel language. It has a spoken form and a silk-vibration form — patterns transmitted through anchored silk that can carry language as well as sensory data. The vibration-language is not teachable to other species who lack spinnerets and vibration sensitivity; the spoken form can be learned. Araji has an extensive vocabulary for types of crossing, qualities of offering, vibration signatures, and what happens at nodes over time. Its vocabulary for most social relationships that exist outside the between is borrowed from other languages.

Arakhel name practices involve the node. Full identification includes the crossing a family tends, which node-family line they come from, and a personal name. When an Arakhel introduces themselves to another species with only a personal name, they have chosen to withhold lineage, which is a significant act.

## 9. System Connections

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Umbral Vibratorio (Herencia)** — La arquitectura sensorial Arakhel procesa el mundo a un nivel de grano vibratorio a través de la seda. Reciben una penalización permanente de `-3` a las T.R. de Alteraciones cuando se les aplica el estado **Desorientado** o **Conmocionado**. Esta penalización no es una dolencia; es el costo biológico de un sistema neurológico hipersensible vulnerable a perturbaciones espaciales extremas.

**Percepción Estructural (Legado)** — Mechanically: +1 per 4 NR to T.E. of **Percepción** when detecting movement through solid surfaces or connected structures without line of sight, and to **Rastreo** through surface vibration traces. Also applies to **Trampas** checks regardless of environment.

**Glándulas de Seda (Legado)** — Mechanically: During full rest, Arakhel synthesize raw silk. Yield and quality scale with NR (e.g., NR 1-4 yields 250g grade 1). Used directly for anchoring, improvised traps, or signaling. Requires fabrication processing for durable crafting.

**Urdimbre (Legado)** — Active utility technique (Ritmo 5, Desgaste 2, Alcance 10m). Anchor a web network covering a 6m radius.
- Receives vibration signatures of all movement inside, ignoring line of sight.
- Enemies moving inside must pass T.R. Alteraciones or become Atrapado (scales with rank).
- The Arakhel moves freely without triggering penalties.
- Permanent until cleared manually.

**Armas Naturales:**
- *Mordisco (Todos los Arakhel)*: Principal. Agilidad/Tenacidad. Profiles: Perforación, Precisión, Corrosión, Hostigamiento. Effect: If T.A. exceeds T.D. by 3+, target must pass T.R. Veneno or suffer the Paralizado condition. Severity scales with rank. A slow, deliberate assassination tool.

### Combat identity

The Arakhel do not fight fair. They dominate the geometry of the space before the combat begins using *Urdimbre*, trapping those who do not know how to read the network. Once the prey is restrained, they deliver precise, venomous bites that scale into paralysis. They are patient trappers and executioners, relying on spatial awareness and area denial rather than frontal force.

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

### What a PC knows

- How to read terrain through a silk network — where weight is, from what direction, approximately how large
- The offering protocol and how to receive travelers at a node
- The Araji language, spoken and vibration form
- What the between actually looks like from the inside — which crossings are dangerous for what reasons, which are navigable
- That the oldest nodes hold something whose nature is not fully resolved, and that their family either knows this or has chosen not to examine it

### What they likely do not know

- What lives in their family's oldest silk — whether the deepening recognition is theological or structural
- How other species experience the between (they've heard accounts but never experienced it from outside)
- What their lineage looks like to a Drak'kai who has studied Tauma patterns — and whether the answer would be reassuring or not
- What to do in a settlement for more than a few days without their extended sensory network

### Departure reasons

- The family node was destroyed, and they have nowhere to tend
- Something at the node changed in a way the family cannot explain, and they need outside context — Drak'kai knowledge, Zarnag death-site records, Rokhart analysis — before they can return safely
- They are young and in the wandering period before inheriting the crossing
- They are carrying an offering object left at the node by a traveler who never came back; what they have read from it through the silk has not let them leave it behind

### What they carry with them

- Physical: unprocessed silk; whatever the node's offerings produced that they could bring
- Historical: the character of a specific crossing — what it feels like, what has passed through it, what it has responded to
- Uncomfortable: the possibility that what their family has been tending for generations is not what their cosmology says it is
- Open: who they are when the node isn't there — their extended sensory reach is reduced to biological baseline away from their own silk, which means every other environment they move through is experienced at diminished resolution

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **El Problema Drak'kai:** Cuando un erudito Drak'kai examina un Nodo Antiguo, a menudo reconoce el "despertar de la voluntad" no como una deidad, sino como un patrón parasitario de Tauma gigante que ha crecido gracias a las ofrendas acumuladas. Los Arakhel no debaten este hallazgo: asesinan al erudito en silencio en las redes. Es el único dogma que defienden con la muerte, porque la alternativa es aceptar que han sido el ganado de un parásito durante generaciones.
- **La Seda como Objeto:** La seda Arakhel no es mágica por defecto; es un material orgánico y un objeto físico más. Puede anclar Tauma (convirtiéndose en vestigo), pero requiere el mismo tiempo, impronta y exposición que cualquier otra vasija u objeto en el mundo. No es un "atajo" sobrenatural.
- **Nomenclatura y Distribución:** Guiados por el modelo de migración polinesio de Transcendence, las especies cubren vastas regiones mediante migraciones escalonadas, adaptando su arquitectura pero manteniendo su cosmología. Por tanto, los Nodos toman sus nombres de dos fuentes: la dinastía que los custodia o las características topográficas únicas que dictaron la migración (Ej: *El Nodo Kael*, o *El Cruce de la Piedra Hendida*).

## 11. Cultura Material 

La cultura material Arakhel está profundamente atada al viaje seguro, al cruce sagrado y al almacenamiento de vibración.

- **Marcadores de *Sacbe*:** Monolitos de piedra envueltos en gruesas capas de seda antigua, ubicados en los bordes de sus redes. Es aquí donde los viajeros depositan sus ofrendas para lograr un paso seguro. Con los siglos, actúan como baterías de Tauma pasivo.
- **Vasijas de los *Lares Compitales*:** Pequeños cuencos de cerámica que las familias Arakhel esconden en el centro del nodo. Contienen la ofrenda más antigua jamás depositada allí. Son veneradas como el espíritu fundacional del cruce.
- **Husos de la Abuela Araña:** Herramientas de tejido talladas en madera taumática. Los tejedores los utilizan no solo para fabricar, sino para "leer" la tensión acumulada en hilos kilométricos, interpretando mensajes a enormes distancias.
