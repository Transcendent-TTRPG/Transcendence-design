## Purpose

This document does not list finished Techniques.

Its purpose is to define the Sauri principles that can generate Techniques later. A Sauri Technique should not be a generic heavy attack, a generic elemental power, or a crocodile-flavored bonus. It should carry a recognizable method of solving a problem that emerged from Sauri body, temple culture, Tauma theology, architecture, hierarchy, and survival practice.

The Sauri do not treat force as something to express freely. They treat force as something to contain, channel, judge, and release at the proper moment.

---

## Technique Lessons From The Species

The Sauri have two primary natural attack forms:

- **Mordisco / Bite** - `Impact`, `Bastion`, `Rend`, `Unstoppable`; closure, seizure, sustained pressure, and judgment after contact
- **Cola / Tail Strike** - `Impact`, `Line Control`, `Interception`, `Interruption`; sweeping denial, off-line force, and control of passage around the body

They also have strong cultural combat surfaces:

- shields as mobile gates
- heavy blunt weapons as stone-weighted authority
- armor and scale as vessel preparation
- architecture, water, heat, and sealed passages as tactical environments
- preservation, oath, and sovereign legitimacy as non-weapon Technique roots

Species-origin does not mean natural-weapon-only. A Sauri Technique may use a bite, tail, shield, mace, armor, ritual procession, intimidation, architecture, preservation practice, or Tauma-sensitive reading. What makes it Sauri is the principle underneath: the user turns pressure into a contained decision.

| `transmission_logic` | How the principle is taught or restricted |

## Sauri Seed Principles

### 1. Vessel Under Pressure

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | A prepared body can hold force longer than an unprepared one. |
| `world_root` | Sauri culture treats the body as the first vessel. Ordeals, armor, scale care, heat exposure, hunger discipline, and Tauma-sensitive rites all teach that power is dangerous before it is useful. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that reduce collapse, delay Fatigue consequence, hold position under strain, endure hostile contact, or remain functional while carrying a dangerous burden. |
| `possible_expressions` | Resistance hybrids, armor, shields, Tenacity specializations, ritual endurance, Tauma exposure scenes, carrying relics or preserved bodies. |
| `transmission_logic` | Common in temple service and military training; deeper forms tied to ruler trials, funerary orders, and vessel-preparation rites. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should define what pressure is being contained and what the user risks by holding it. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this universal toughness or flat damage reduction. The vessel holds a specific pressure for a specific purpose. |

### 2. Jaw As Judgment

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | Contact becomes judgment when the Sauri closes the body around it. |
| `world_root` | The Sauri bite is the species' clearest natural symbol: closure, seizure, breath denial, and decision. Their courts and rituals use the same logic. Once judgment closes, the question changes from whether contact happens to what survives it. |
| `technique_space` | Bite Techniques that seize, pin, drag, force Resistance Rolls, break rhythm, punish escape, or convert a successful hit into a sustained contest. |
| `possible_expressions` | Bite, grappling, Intimidation, execution rites, underwater or mud pressure, close-quarters temple defense. |
| `transmission_logic` | Taught through martial rites and predator discipline; formal versions may be restricted to temple guards, executioners, or sovereign champions. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should care about what happens after closure. The bite should change the next decision, not just increase damage. |
| `avoid` | Do not write this as a high-damage bite only. The Sauri jaw is control, sentence, and sustained danger. |

### 3. Tail Keeps The Channel

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | The body controls passage before the enemy reaches the jaws. |
| `world_root` | Sauri temples, canals, corridors, and flooded chambers all teach controlled passage. The tail is the bodily version of a gate or channel wall: it prevents clean approach, clean escape, and clean flanking. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that sweep, interrupt movement, deny a lane, punish circling, force repositioning, or keep a target inside the intended route. |
| `possible_expressions` | Tail, shield, polearm, corridor defense, riverbank combat, formation anchoring, Line Control and Interruption profiles. |
| `transmission_logic` | Common among guards, river wardens, and temple defenders; refined versions are taught in architecture-aware fighting schools. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should define the channel being maintained: doorway, bank, flank, ritual line, corridor, or combat lane. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this generic area control. The tail should shape passage in relation to the Sauri body. |

### 4. Procession Of Force

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | Power becomes safer and stronger when it passes through the proper sequence. |
| `world_root` | Sauri temples are built as processions: open court, narrowed passage, threshold, chamber, vessel. Their rituals, courts, and combat methods often follow ordered stages rather than sudden improvisation. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that reward setup, sequence, prepared stance, step-by-step pressure, forcing an enemy into a later state, or punishing someone who skips the proper order. |
| `possible_expressions` | Shields, maces, armor, Architecture, Theology, command, dueling rites, guarded approach, chamber defense. |
| `transmission_logic` | Taught in temple institutions and formal guard schools; outsiders can learn it through service but rarely receive the inner ritual meaning. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should care about before and after. The effect should emerge because a sequence has been preserved or violated. |
| `avoid` | Do not turn this into empty ritual flavor. The sequence must create a real tactical or scene consequence. |

### 5. Stone Remembers Pressure

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | Surfaces record what has passed through them. |
| `world_root` | Sauri priests read scales, stone, waterlines, mineral stains, preserved flesh, heat marks, and old channels as evidence of pressure. Their culture trusts matter that has endured contact more than speech that changes with fear. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that read traces, identify Tauma pressure, detect past passage, interpret ruins, expose tampering, or distinguish natural pressure from extranatural distortion. |
| `possible_expressions` | Perception, Archaeology, Architecture, Survival, Theology, Tauma-sensitive materials, environmental investigation. |
| `transmission_logic` | Taught to temple readers, builders, embalmers, scouts, and canal wardens; some readings are restricted because they can challenge sovereign legitimacy. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should name the surface being read and what kind of pressure it preserved. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this generic deduction or automatic truth. The user reads residue, deformation, sequence, or absence in matter. |

### 6. Preserved Witness

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | A body, object, or place can continue to testify after the moment has passed. |
| `world_root` | Sauri funerary practice treats preservation as technical memory. The dead, the wounded, the sealed chamber, and the carried relic can all become evidence if they are kept from spilling their meaning. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that preserve evidence, stabilize a body, protect a witness, delay decay, carry a dangerous object, keep a wound readable, or prevent a scene from losing its testimony. |
| `possible_expressions` | Medicine, Craft, Theology, Resistance, Survival, shields, escort scenes, post-combat recovery, investigation under pressure. |
| `transmission_logic` | Strongly tied to embalmers, funerary houses, temple archivists, and royal legitimacy disputes. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should define what is being preserved and why losing it would change the scene. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this generic healing. Preservation protects testimony, function, lineage, or proof. |

### 7. Sovereign Weight

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | Authority is a pressure that makes others move differently. |
| `world_root` | The Sauri sovereign is treated as a living vessel. That idea shapes every level of Sauri command: presence is not persuasion alone, but the social weight of a body believed to carry order. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that force hesitation, compel stillness, mark a forbidden approach, make an oath mechanically relevant, or create pressure through public legitimacy. |
| `possible_expressions` | Intimidation, command, Theology, court ritual, profile-bearing line, execution posture, champion duels, social conflict. |
| `transmission_logic` | Taught through court service, priestly discipline, military command, and sovereign cults; common imitations exist but lack full legitimacy. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should show what authority is resting on: office, oath, witness, ritual mark, visible endurance, or public sequence. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this generic intimidation. The pressure comes from recognized weight, not loudness or aggression. |

### 8. Release At The Correct Gate

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `concept` | Stored force should be released only where the vessel can survive the opening. |
| `world_root` | Sauri canals, tomb doors, reservoir gates, and ritual vessels all teach that release is as dangerous as containment. Power that exits through the wrong opening destroys the structure meant to hold it. |
| `technique_space` | Techniques that convert held pressure into a decisive strike, timed shove, shield break, controlled collapse, or safe discharge of accumulated strain. |
| `possible_expressions` | Maces, shields, bite release, tail sweep, armor stance, Resistance hybrids, environmental hazards, sealed doors, water pressure. |
| `transmission_logic` | Learned after containment discipline; reckless students are kept from release methods until they can prove restraint. |
| `authoring_pressure` | A finished Technique should require prior pressure, posture, hold, or preparation. The release matters because something was contained first. |
| `avoid` | Do not make this a generic power attack. The Technique should spend or redirect stored pressure. |

## Converting A Seed Into A Technique

When writing a finished Technique from one of these seeds, use this order:

1. Choose the seed principle.
2. Choose the expression: bite, tail, shield, mace, armor, specialization, resistance, social rite, or environmental practice.
3. Define the playable situation.
4. Define the primary system surface.
5. Add only one secondary surface if the fiction truly asks for it.
6. Set the cost from timing, commitment, and strain.
7. Write restrictions that preserve the seed's identity.

The test is:

> Could this Technique exist without the Sauri?

If yes, the origin is too thin.

> Could someone learn it from the Sauri and perform it with a different body, tool, or rite?

If no, the origin has become too species-locked.

The target is the middle: a Technique that carries Sauri thought into action.

### Sauri Secondary Specialization Pool

These are not part of the primary 8-Technique specialization spine for the
Sauri Novice pass. They are the short list of **secondary-fit
specializations** that still make strong sense inside the species and can be
used for:

- Resistance hybrids
- deliberate swaps when a local school or community differs
- higher-rank expansion beyond the primary Novice spine

The goal is to avoid treating the full system specialization list as equally
"available" to Sauri authoring. These are the ones that fit the species
strongly enough to matter, but not centrally enough to displace the main 8.

| Specialization | Secondary fit | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Aclimatación` | Strong | Environmental and exposure hybrids. Heat, harsh climate, bad air, long outdoor burden, kiln-court labor, and endurance of hostile ambient conditions. |
| `Contención` | Strong | Internal-pressure and extranatural-pressure hybrids. Holding dangerous influence, Tauma overload, preserved witness burden, fixation, or contamination from spilling into action. |
| `Teología` | Moderate / strong by temple communities | Ritual-contact hybrids. Sacred burden, funerary legitimacy, preserved authority, taboo contact, or doctrinally dangerous exposure where the species' ritual frame matters. |
| `Arquitectura` | Moderate | Built-environment and threshold hybrids. Sealed passage, chamber survival, pressure-routing, and structural containment when the resistance expression depends on the space as much as the body. |

Authoring rule for Sauri secondaries:

- If the hybrid is mainly about enduring climate, heat, exposure, or ambient burden, prefer `Aclimatación`.
- If the hybrid is mainly about not letting dangerous pressure enter, spread, or break form, prefer `Contención`.
- If the hybrid depends on ritual meaning, preserved authority, funerary contact, or sacred legitimacy, prefer `Teología`.
- If the hybrid depends on chambers, thresholds, built containment, or controlled passage, prefer `Arquitectura`.

What we are **not** doing here:

- promoting every fitting Sauri-flavored specialization into equal priority
- letting secondaries compete with the primary 8 by default
- using secondaries with no clear pressure identity just because they are vaguely thematic

### Cerrar la Compuerta

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Cerrar la Compuerta |
| `name_en` | Close the Sluice |
| `origin` | Shield |
| `world_origin` | Species: Sauri; seed: Tail Keeps The Channel / Procession Of Force; transmission: river-warden shield drills and temple gate defense; availability: Restricted |
| `category` | utility |
| `type` | active |
| `trigger` | You set your shield to govern a readable lane, flank, doorway, bridge, corridor, formation gap, or other route before an enemy commits through it. |
| `requirements` | Minimum rank: Novice; weapon profile: Line Control; any weapon competency, natural attack form, or specific item that grants Line Control access can use this Technique unless the Technique or item says otherwise; user must be able to physically occupy or cover the chosen route with the profile-bearing surface |
| `target` | route |
| `range` | adjacent / profile-bearing surface reach |
| `area` | single readable route |
| `duration` | until the next relevant hostile activation resolves, or until you leave the route |
| `cost` | Rhythm 3; Attrition 1 |
| `saving_roll` | none |
| `tags` | defense, control, anti_displacement, setup |

**Fantasy:** The user does not wait for the enemy to choose a target. They set the shield like a stone gate in a canal, making one route costly to ignore. The enemy can still force the issue, but no longer treats the protected side as open water.

**World origin:** Sauri river-wardens and temple guards train with the same logic their architects use in canals and sealed chambers: pressure must meet the correct gate before it becomes flood. In shield practice, that doctrine becomes an active claim over the route.

**Why this is not a base shield defense:** A base shield defense protects against a strike after the strike is declared. `Cerrar la Compuerta` changes the route before the strike is chosen. Its purpose is not to block one attack, but to make attacking through or around the profile-bearing line a worse decision.

**Primary interaction surface:** position and lane ownership.

**Secondary interaction surface:** target pressure, because enemies can still attack past the user, but doing so through the closed route becomes less efficient.

**Cost note:** `Rhythm 3 / Attrition 1` is intentional. The Technique is an active setup, not a guaranteed interception. It costs little because it does not stop the next attack by itself; it changes the next relevant enemy decision through the chosen route.

**Effect:** Choose one readable route within profile-bearing surface reach. The compuerta remains in place until the next hostile activation that could plausibly use or attack through that route resolves, even if your own marker becomes ready first.

During that window, an enemy that uses the chosen route or attacks through that route against a target other than you treats the action as obstructed by your profile-bearing line.

The obstructed action suffers a penalty to its Attack Roll equal to `1 + your the rank bonus of the competency used for this Technique`. Instead of accepting the penalty, the enemy may choose to attack you or your profile-bearing line as the cleaner target.

This does not stop the attack. It makes ignoring the profile-bearing line worse.

**Restrictions:**

- requires a real lane, doorway, flank, bridge, corridor, riverbank edge, formation gap, or other readable route to govern
- cannot be used if the user cannot physically occupy or cover the chosen route with profile-bearing surface reach
- does not create a persistent zone by itself
- does not prevent the enemy from attacking through the route; it only penalizes doing so
- ends after the next hostile activation that could plausibly use or attack through the chosen route resolves, whether or not that enemy accepts the penalty
- ends early if the user leaves the route, loses profile-bearing posture, takes a non-free action that abandons the profile-bearing line, or becomes unable to maintain the profile-bearing line
- should not stack with another copy of the same Technique on the same route

**Authoring note:** This Technique is intentionally written so it can also be learned by non-Sauri shield users. The Sauri origin explains why the method exists: they think of movement as pressure through a channel, and the shield as the gate that decides where that pressure is allowed to go.

### Levantar el Dique

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Levantar el Dique |
| `name_en` | Raise the Dike |
| `origin` | Shield |
| `world_origin` | Species: Sauri; seed: Vessel Under Pressure / Procession Of Force; transmission: temple escort drills and river-warden protection rites; availability: Restricted |
| `category` | attack |
| `type` | reactive |
| `trigger` | An enemy attack or movement-linked strike would reach an ally, protected creature, carried witness, relic, or designated charge within your profile-bearing surface reach. |
| `requirements` | Minimum rank: Novice; weapon profile: Interception; any weapon competency, natural attack form, or specific item that grants Interception access can use this Technique unless the Technique or item says otherwise; user must have a clear physical path to place the profile-bearing surface between the threat and the protected target |
| `target` | ally / creature / object |
| `range` | profile-bearing surface reach |
| `area` | single protected target |
| `duration` | instant |
| `cost` | Rhythm 3; Attrition 1 |
| `saving_roll` | contextual - resolved through the hostile attack or movement-linked strike that the Technique intercepts |
| `tags` | defense, interception, support, mitigation |

**Fantasy:** The user raises the shield before the blow finishes its course, not as a wall for themselves but as a dike for someone or something that must not be reached. The attack still has force, but the force meets the prepared surface instead of the intended target.

**World origin:** Sauri protection rites treat vulnerable bodies, preserved witnesses, and sacred objects as vessels under public responsibility. River-wardens and temple escorts train to read the moment when pressure is about to break through and place the shield where the current would otherwise spill.

**Why this is not a base shield defense:** A base shield defense protects the user. `Levantar el Dique` protects something else by changing the line of contact. Its identity is not personal survival, but taking responsibility for the route between threat and charge.

**Primary interaction surface:** interception and target substitution.

**Secondary interaction surface:** mitigation, because the shield receives a force that was meant for a less prepared target.

**Cost note:** `Rhythm 4 / Attrition 1` is intentional for the Novice version. The Technique is narrower than broad bodyguard coverage because it protects one target against one incoming threat and requires a clear shield path. More durable or repeated protection should belong to higher-rank Bastion or formation Techniques.

**Effect:** Contest the triggering attack or movement-linked strike with a shield response. If the Technique resolves successfully, the attack does not affect its intended target. The shield-bearing user becomes the point of contact for the hostile force; the attack is blocked, redirected, or resolved against the user's shield defense as the scene requires.

If the intercepted attack carries a non-damage rider, such as forced movement, a condition, or a grab, that rider does not transfer automatically to the protected target. The Narrator resolves whether the rider can affect the shield-bearing user based on the fiction of the contact.

**Restrictions:**

- requires a clear physical path for the shield to enter the attack line
- protects one target from one triggering threat only
- cannot intercept effects that do not travel through a blockable line, surface, body, projectile, or contact path
- cannot protect a target already separated from the user by sealed terrain, a closed barrier, or an occupied route the shield cannot cross
- should not become persistent bodyguard coverage without a higher-rank Technique

**Authoring note:** This Technique is Sauri because it treats protection as containment under responsibility. Non-Sauri shield users can learn it, but the Sauri origin frames the protected target as a vessel whose contents must not be allowed to spill.

### Romper el Caudal

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Romper el Caudal |
| `name_en` | Break the Flow |
| `origin` | Shield |
| `world_origin` | Species: Sauri; seed: Release At The Correct Gate / Tail Keeps The Channel; transmission: river-warden breach drills and temple guard interruption forms; availability: Restricted |
| `category` | attack |
| `type` | reactive |
| `trigger` | An enemy within profile-bearing surface reach begins a Technique, heavy action, telegraphed action, or movement-linked execution that requires visible commitment before it resolves. |
| `requirements` | Minimum rank: Novice; weapon profile: Interruption; any weapon competency, natural attack form, or specific item that grants Interruption access can use this Technique unless the Technique or item says otherwise; user must have a ready weapon, natural weapon, or item with explicit attack stats to deliver the counter; the enemy's action must have a readable physical line, windup, focus point, or route of execution the profile-bearing surface can strike or jam |
| `target` | enemy |
| `range` | profile-bearing surface reach |
| `area` | single enemy |
| `duration` | instant |
| `cost` | Rhythm 5; Attrition 1 |
| `saving_roll` | D.R. contest; counter requires clean defensive margin |
| `tags` | attack, defense, disruption, counter_positioning |

**Fantasy:** The user does not wait behind the shield. They drive the shield into the moment where the enemy's action has begun but has not yet become inevitable. If the current breaks completely against the shield, the user's answering strike lands before the enemy can rebuild the flow.

**World origin:** Sauri engineers and wardens know that pressure is most dangerous when it has already found a channel. Their interruption drills teach the same lesson in combat: do not meet the full flood if you can break the flow while it is still forming.

**Why this is not a base shield bash:** A base shield bash is a direct hostile contact. `Romper el Caudal` first breaks the action's structure: the lifted weapon, the planted foot, the turning shoulder, the breath before release, the route that makes the execution possible. The damage comes only if the defense is clean enough to create an immediate opening.

**Primary interaction surface:** defensive counterattack.

**Secondary interaction surface:** execution interruption, because the Technique targets a committed action before it resolves cleanly.

**Cost note:** `Rhythm 5 / Attrition 1` is intentional. The Technique can create damage, so it costs more than a simple reactive spoil. It remains at Attrition 1 because the counter only happens on a clean defensive margin and requires the user to already have a credible striking surface ready.

**Effect:** Contest the triggering execution with a shield-based `D.R.`. If the enemy's attack or execution still beats your `D.R.`, the Technique fails and the enemy resolves normally.

If your `D.R.` equals or exceeds the enemy's relevant attack or execution roll, the shield breaks the execution cleanly. The enemy's action still consumes its declared Rhythm and any declared cost, but it does not damage you through that contact.

If your `D.R.` exceeds the enemy's roll by **3 or more**, you may immediately make one counterattack against that enemy with one ready manufactured weapon, one natural weapon, or a shield item that has explicit attack stats. Resolve the counterattack normally with its own `A.R.` and `I.R.`. This counterattack does not add another Rhythm cost, but it cannot include movement, a second Technique, or a follow-up window.

If the triggering action was a telegraphed action rather than a direct attack, the Narrator uses the same margin logic against the action's relevant execution roll, fixed threat value, or declared interruption threshold. If no such value exists, this Technique can spoil the setup but cannot produce the counterattack.

**Restrictions:**

- requires a readable committed execution; cannot be used against trivial, instant, hidden, or purely mental actions with no blockable line
- requires profile-bearing surface reach and a credible contact point to jam, strike, or break
- counterattack requires a ready weapon, natural weapon, or shield item with explicit attack stats
- affects one enemy action only
- should not cancel an entire boss or champion subsystem unless that subsystem explicitly exposes a vulnerable interruption window
- shield-only damage should not be assumed unless shields later receive explicit offensive stats or the specific shield item says it can attack

**Authoring note:** This Technique shares logic with the Sauri tail because both break clean passage. The shield creates the opening; the counterattack may come from a mace, spear, bite, tail, or other ready surface. The Sauri origin is not the anatomy, but the doctrine of breaking pressure while it is still becoming an action.

**World origin:** Sauri who work exposed terraces, kiln courts, reservoir stone, and desert-edge crossings are taught that heat kills the unprepared twice: once through the body, and once through panic. Proper acclimation teaches the body to accept the outer burden without letting the inner function spill first.

**Why this is not raw Aclimatación:** Raw `Aclimatación` already governs whether the body is generally adapted to hostile climate or exposure. `Bajar el Núcleo` makes one class of those rolls better in a specific way: when the pressure is thermal, the user's trained heat tolerance becomes an extra bonus rather than just background fiction.

**Why this is not raw Heat Resistance:** Raw `Heat Resistance` is the body's native or conditioned tolerance to heat. `Bajar el Núcleo` hybridizes that tolerance with practiced `Aclimatación`, letting the resistance contribute directly to the exposure roll instead of existing only as background susceptibility logic.

**Primary interaction surface:** improved `Aclimatación` rolls against heat-origin environmental hazards.

**Secondary interaction surface:** Fatigue and exposure stability, because better thermal survival rolls make heat-driven degradation less likely to settle.

**Cost note:** `Rhythm 0 / Attrition 0` is correct and deliberate. This is a passive hybrid like the Naghii resistance models. The cost was paid in exposure, acclimation, and training long before the scene began; no declared action is needed when the body is already trained to answer heat better.

**Fantasy:** The contact still opens the world. The difference is that the Sauri does not meet it as raw intrusion. The doctrine gives the phenomenon a place, a lineage, a boundary, a name. What enters is still dangerous, but it no longer arrives as shapeless flood.

**World origin:** Sauri vestige doctrine teaches that a dangerous contact becomes worse when it is taken as undifferentiated force. Temple readers, funerary handlers, and vessel-keepers are trained to classify what kind of intrusion is happening and what boundary it is trying to cross. The doctrine does not seal the opening. **Why this is not raw Teología:** Raw `Teología` interprets the doctrine, symbols, lineage, or doctrinal frame of the contact. `Nombrar el Umbral` turns that doctrinal literacy into a defensive effect: when the user already carries the sensory fracture, naming the kind of intrusion helps keep the next contact from driving it deeper.

**Why this is not raw Affliction Resistance:** Raw `Affliction Resistance` is the being's capacity to withstand the destabilizing pressure that would deepen an extranatural sensory Affliction. `Nombrar el Umbral` hybridizes that resistance with doctrine: the body resists better because the mind and ritual frame have correctly identified what is crossing into it.

**Secondary interaction surface:** stability, because the Technique makes repeated controlled contact less likely to push the user into worse sensory distortion.

**Cost note:** `Rhythm 0 / Attrition 0` is correct and deliberate. This is a passive hybrid like the other resistance models. The cost was paid in doctrine, repeated contact, and learning to survive naming what should not be touched.
