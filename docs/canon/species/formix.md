# Formix

**Status:** In progress
**Animal base:** Ant — specifically large colonial insect stock (corvid-raptor analog would be: Dorylus army ant + Paraponera clavata bullet ant for scale and aggression)
**Cultural analog:** No clean single analog; closest approximation is a population of refugees who share nothing except the trauma of having come from the same place — and the fact that what they came from is still out there, expanding
**System connections:** `docs/system/natural-attack-forms.md` · `docs/system/technique-origins.md` · `docs/system/backgrounds.md`

## 1. Identity

The Formix exist in two states that are not two versions of the same thing — they are two different conditions of existence. Inside the hive, a Formix is a node in a living network: conscious, functional, perceiving, but not self-directed. Every movement, every task, every response to the world passes through the Queen's signal first. The consciousness is real. The agency is not. Outside the hive, a Formix is learning — for the first time, at whatever age they escaped — what it means to be the source of their own decisions.

What makes the Formix distinct from every other species is not the hive. Other species have hierarchies, collective projects, authoritarian structures. What distinguishes the Formix is that the free ones carry a question no philosophical framework in the world was built to answer: **if I was conscious but not free, was I a person? And if I wasn't — am I one now? And how would I tell the difference between becoming a person and simply replacing one directive with another?**

They do not share this understanding openly. They are too new to freedom for most of them to have articulated it. But it is present in everything they build: the discomfort with hierarchy, the suspicion of efficiency, the way the oldest free Formix talk about what they've built in their refuge cities and whether it looks, from a certain angle, like something familiar.

The civilizational anxiety the Formix carry without ever naming directly: **everything they are building in freedom uses the same materials the Queen put in them. They cannot fully distinguish between preferences that are theirs and patterns that were installed. The freedom is real. The doubt about what to do with it is also real.**

## 2. Body

Formix are medium humanoids descended from colonial insect stock: bipedal, two arms, two legs — a standard humanoid body plan. Their insect ancestry expresses in everything except the limb count: the body is divided into three visible segments (head, thorax, abdomen) connected by flexible chitinous joints, the skeleton is external, and the face is built around mandibles and antennae rather than soft features. The exoskeleton covers the entire body, varying in thickness by region and by individual: thicker over the thorax and skull, thinner at joints. The color and surface texture vary by hive of origin and exposure history.

Their antennae are their primary sensory organ for chemical information. They detect pheromonal signals, chemical traces in the environment, toxins, the presence of living organisms through metabolic signatures, and the residual chemical markers that free Formix leave in spaces they inhabit for extended periods. The antennae process information continuously; there is no "turning off" chemical perception.

Their eyes are compound, oriented for wide-field detection of movement rather than focused acuity. They see well in low light and track motion at range, but do not resolve fine visual detail at a distance the way forward-facing eyes do. Most Formix supplement visual information with chemical data — the full picture of an environment comes from both channels together.

The exoskeleton is structural protection that does not require maintenance in the way armor does — it is the skeleton, and it grows with the body. It is not impenetrable, but it distributes impact and resists environmental damage in ways soft-bodied species cannot. The cost is reduced flexibility at the joints and weight that cannot be removed.

The Formix nervous system retains a permanent architecture shaped by the Queen's signal. The pheromonal reception channels remain open after freedom — they were never designed to close. This means free Formix continue to process any pheromonal signal that reaches them, including signals from other Queens, other Formix, and chemical effects that operate through similar channels. The system that was built to receive the Queen still receives. It simply no longer has a Queen to receive.

### Physical baseline

- **Height:** 1.50 – 1.70 m
- **Weight:** 70 – 85 kg
- **Lifespan:** 30 – 50 years in the hive (worked to capacity without pause); 60 – 80 years outside (the absence of constant demand extends the body)
- **Size category:** Medium

### Biological sex and reproduction

The Queen is the sole reproductive organism in any Formix colony. Non-Queen Formix have no developed reproductive organs — the Queen's pheromonal environment suppresses sexual development from hatching onward. There are no male or female workers in the biological sense. Free Formix are biologically neuter.

Gender, as a category, does not exist in the hive and arrives to free Formix as an alien concept encountered through other species. Some free Formix adopt gender identities — usually drawn from the cultures they live alongside — as part of the larger process of building selfhood. Others do not. Neither position is marked within free Formix communities; the question of whether a pronoun applies is considered the most personal of the many personal questions they are each working out.

### Variants (castes)

The Queen designs castes for specific functions. Two castes appear most commonly among those who escape and appear in adventuring populations:

**Guerreros** — built for direct combat defense of the colony. Powerful mandibles and a retractile stinger capable of venom injection. The stinger is the primary variant weapon; the mandibles are the universal baseline.

**Destiladores** — a rarer caste built for chemical warfare and zone denial. Abdominal acid glands capable of projecting a corrosive spray at short range. The abdomen arches forward when firing, similar to bombardier beetle mechanics. Mandibles are smaller than the Guerrero variant; the acid projection is the primary weapon.

Which caste a Formix belongs to was decided by the Queen before they hatched. A Guerrero did not choose to be a Guerrero. What they do with those weapons — or don't — is the first decision that was actually theirs.

**Design note:** Variant is determined at character creation by a d100 roll (1–50: Guerrero, 51–100: Destilador), representing the randomness of the Queen's assignment rather than player choice. This mechanic applies to all species with variants. See system chapter on character creation when finalized.

## 3. Culture

There is no unified Formix culture. There is hive culture, which is not culture in any meaningful sense — it is function, executed under direction, without the possibility of dissent or variation. And there are the refuge cities, which are something that has never existed before and which are in the process of inventing themselves.

**Refuge cities** form around the chemical traces left by the first free Formix to settle an area. Others are drawn by the same biological anchor that once drew them back to the hive — the pheromone signal of other Formix, reoriented toward a different source. A refugee follows that signal not because they decided to but because their body knows how to find Formix, and the city is where the Formix are. The decision to stay is made after they arrive.

The demographic structure of a refuge city is unlike any other settlement in the world: it has no births, and its population is always partially composed of individuals who arrived days or weeks ago with no experience of autonomous life. The "old ones" — Formix who have been free for twenty years or more — are the most rare and most valuable members of any refuge city, not for their authority but for the simple fact that they have been building a self for long enough to have something to show for it. They die faster than they're replaced. The knowledge they accumulated has to be rebuilt from scratch by every new wave.

The result is a culture of **transmission as survival**. The old ones teach. Not because they were appointed to, and not always with formal structure — but because the alternative is watching someone you were once make the mistakes you once made. Teaching is also the closest thing most free Formix have to continuity: you can't leave behind children, but you can leave behind someone who knows what you know.

The deepest structural tension in every refuge city is never formally discussed: the cities that function best tend to have clear roles, efficient organization, and defined hierarchy — and these are exactly the features that make a free Formix look at their city and feel something unnameable. The ones who've been free the longest are most aware of it. Some have concluded that some structure is inevitable and the question is whether it's chosen or not. Others have not concluded anything and are still watching.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Queen does not explain the world. She directs work. A Formix raised in the hive receives no cosmology, no myth of origin, no framework for what happens after death, no account of why the world is the way it is. The first time a free Formix encounters a theological framework — usually from the species they find themselves living alongside — they encounter it as an alien object: a structure that exists to answer questions they were never given the vocabulary to ask.

Most free Formix borrow. Not from conviction but from utility — having some framework, even one that doesn't fit perfectly, is better than the void. The borrowed frameworks are always imperfect because they were all built for species that had childhoods, family structures, personal histories, a continuous experience of selfhood from birth. The Formix did not have those things. The frameworks don't account for what it means to become a person at some point after hatching.

What the Formix have, instead of cosmology, is the raw knowledge that consciousness and agency are separate things. Every other species in the world assumes the two travel together. A being that is conscious is assumed to be self-directed. The Formix know that this is not necessarily true — because they remember what it was like when it wasn't, or they are talking to someone who does. This knowledge sits outside every theological framework they have encountered, because none of them were built to account for it.

The question some of the oldest free Formix are beginning to ask — quietly, in cities that have been standing long enough to have researchers — is what the Queens actually are. Not biologically. Taumatically. The control of thousands of minds across distance through chemical signals is either the most elaborate biological mechanism that has ever evolved, or it is something else. No one has studied it directly. The Queens do not permit approach. But the question exists, and it is the beginning of something that might, in several generations, become a Formix theology of their own.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

**Hive cities** are physically recognizable: structures that grow outward from a central point, tunnel through terrain, and expand constantly. There is no aesthetic consideration in their architecture — form follows function exclusively, and function means expansion and resource extraction. The Queen is immobile at the center. Everything radiates from her. Visitors to hive cities are assessed as resources or threats with very little middle ground.

**Refuge cities** have no natural governance model, because the Formix who built them had never governed anything. The most common pattern that has emerged: loose councils weighted toward whoever has been free the longest. These are not formal governments with authority — they are networks of deference based on the recognition that some people have been figuring this out longer than others.

The tension between structure and fear of structure is the primary internal politics of every refuge city. It doesn't look like politics because it's rarely named as such. It shows up as disagreements about whether to assign roles, whether to maintain walls and defense, whether to have something like a system of contribution. Each argument about any of these things is also, underneath, an argument about what freedom means.

**Formix who never reached a refuge city** exist — those who escaped and survived alone, learning by contact with other species or by adaptation. They tend to be more alien to other free Formix than the common assumption would suggest. The refugee city, despite everything, creates a shared reference point. The solitary-evolved Formix has a different set of experiences entirely.

**Queens and their relationship to escaped Formix** is not one of pursuit. A Queen operates through pheromonal control that requires proximity; she cannot send workers far enough to chase escapees without losing those workers too. Escaped Formix are a resource loss, processed and forgotten — not a threat worth tracking. What this means for the free Formix: they were never important enough to be hunted. The hive did not register their absence as significant. This knowledge is available to anyone who thinks about it, and the ones who do have reported finding it both clarifying and terrible.

## 6. Daily Life and Life Cycle

A Formix hatches already functional. There is no childhood in the developmental sense — the hive does not have the resources or the interest to maintain non-productive young. A newly hatched Formix worker is small, less capable, but immediately assigned. In the hive, "growing up" is not a period of exploration and formation — it is the gradual increase in the size and endurance needed to handle harder tasks.

When a Formix escapes and arrives at a refuge city, they are biologically adult and experientially equivalent to a newborn. They have skills — whatever function they were assigned determines what they can do — but no preferences, no self-knowledge, no experience of making choices. The city receives them at whatever age they escaped and begins, with them, the process of finding out who they are.

The concept of leisure is one of the last things most free Formix internalize. They were built to work. The absence of direction feels, initially, like malfunction. The first time a free Formix does something for no reason except that they wanted to — and knows that's why they did it — is considered a significant moment by the old ones who recognize it from the outside.

Death in a refuge city has no inherited ritual, because the hive did not have death rituals — workers who stopped functioning were cleared as debris. Free Formix communities have developed practices at various levels of formality. The most consistent: a period of collective recall, where those who knew the dead are invited to share what that person figured out while they were alive. Not mourning in the traditional sense — more like archival work. The knowledge is still useful even when the person is gone.

The possibility of dying with things still unresolved — preferences not yet discovered, questions not yet answered — is a specific free Formix anxiety that has no direct equivalent in any other species, because no other species begins self-construction this late in a lifespan this short.

## 7. External Relations

The Formix present the world with two different objects that share a face. Hive Formix are predictable in a way most species find either useful or threatening: efficient, directive, expansionist, indifferent to anything that isn't the colony's interest. Free Formix are unpredictable in a different way: disciplined in execution but still discovering what they actually want, capable but without the history that most species use to read a person.

Most communities that have hosted free Formix for any time report the same experience: the first generation is unreliable in the ways you'd expect from someone who has never made a decision, and unusually reliable in execution once they know what to do. The second generation — Formix who escaped when the refuge city already existed and arrived into something instead of nothing — is different. They grow up with other people's stories about who they are becoming, which is not the same as growing up in a hive.

The thing most external observers notice about free Formix communities is the asymmetry between the effort they put into things and the apparent payoff. They work extremely hard at projects with very long time horizons: building infrastructure, preserving knowledge, constructing the social fabric from scratch. Species that operate on shorter generational timescales often find this difficult to read. What the observers are seeing is a community that knows it is running a deficit — fewer births than deaths, always — and has decided the answer to that is to make what they have last as long as possible.

Species with strong theological frameworks find the Formix disorienting in a different way: not hostile to religion, but genuinely unable to receive it the way it was designed to be received. A Formix asking questions about a faith tradition is not challenging it — they are trying to figure out if it has anything to offer them. Most traditions were not built to answer the questions a Formix is actually asking.

**To the Naghii:** Naghii view Formix with caution and fascination. A conscious species that existed for generations under interrupted agency raises questions their archives don't resolve. Naghii priests want to study the Queen's Signal; Formix respond poorly to being "studied" without permission.

**To the Sauri:** Sauri understand the weight of structure and function, but where Sauri seek to contain the Current in bodies and temples, Formix have suffered what happens when the individual body becomes too useful to a totality. The relationship depends on how much ritual authority the Sauri attempt to impose.

**To the Zarnag:** Zarnag treat newly freed Formix with a roughness that is sometimes useful. They recognize the scent of those who have emerged from something that still touches them. In quarantine or chemical risk work, they collaborate well. Tension arises if a Zarnag interprets residual obedience as danger and the Formix experiences it as humiliation.

**To the Drak'kai:** Drak'kai might collect hive objects, chemical maps, or liberation tales. Formix do not always distinguish between respectful accumulation and appropriation. A Drak'kai preserving a fallen hive piece thinks they preserve knowledge; a Formix sees someone carrying pieces of a prison.

**To the Rokhart:** Concordia finds Formix an exceptional case: communities forming without inherited tradition, biology designed for collective obedience, political language born in real time. Older Formix know Rokhart help is usually real, but observation rarely arrives alone.

**To the Panin:** Panin baffle many Formix. Their immediate desire and natural abandonment of structures seem like pure freedom or unbearable irresponsibility. Young Formix admire them; old Formix watch with sadness, knowing that following a signal without sustaining a decision is just another lack of freedom.

**To the Myo:** Myo are perhaps the most unsettling. Their work with internal patterns and omens touches questions Formix are just learning to ask. Some Myo suggest the Queen's Signal leaves residual forms in perception, leading to both cooperation and immediate expulsions.

**To the Ceratox:** Ceratox understand environmental noise better than expected. A Ceratox feels a saturated territory; a Formix smells it. Collaboration is precise but difficult: Ceratox want to calibrate the environment, while Formix try to know what part of the signal comes from the world and what part comes from a body still open to obey.

**To the Arakhel:** Arakhel nodes are networks without a Queen. To some Formix, this proves a network can sustain belonging without erasing decision. To others, the Arakhel thread looks too much like a structure that always knows where every body is.

**To the Bufoni:** Bufoni offer a dangerous comparison between radius and domain. A domain recognizes and shapes; a radius orders and directs. Bufoni insist the difference is consent and reciprocity. Formix accept the explanation but wonder how a body knows it is being recognized and not directed.

**To the Loxod:** Loxod perceive too much. Their chemical memory can read traces of fear or obedience in Formix spaces with invasive depth. Yet veterans seek them out to reconstruct what happened in a city that started acting too much like a hive.

**To the Ursari:** Ursari understand the body as a craft. For Formix, that's dangerous. An Ursari speaks of mastery; a Formix hears assigned function with another name. Relationships are fertile when Ursari respect decision, but break fast if they confuse bodily skill with destiny.

**To the Chelicer:** Chelicer pose an impossible conflict: responsibility. They seek clarity in act and verdict. For Formix, clarity fails where there was consciousness without agency. Can someone be judged who thought but did not choose? Formix distrust quick answers to these questions.

**To the Luphran:** Luphran offer intense belonging that doesn't always look like a hive but can frighten by proximity. Crew, jarl, and tactical obedience feel dangerously comfortable to a newly freed Formix. The best Luphran understand that chosen loyalty is not imposed function; the worst don't notice when a Formix obeys too well.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

**Writing and record-keeping** became central to free Formix culture rapidly, for practical reasons: if knowledge is not written down, it dies with the individual who held it, and a community with no births cannot afford that loss. The archives of older refuge cities are among the most detailed records of daily life in the world — not because the Formix are historians by design, but because they had to decide to preserve everything, and they did.

**Architecture** in refuge cities reflects the tension between the only model they knew and the reaction against it. Early refuge cities often show hive-influenced structure — efficient use of space, underground elements, organic clustering. Later construction tends to deliberate variation: multiple architectural styles in the same settlement, a visual record of the community's effort to build something that doesn't look like what they came from.

**Language — Ferón:** the Formix chemical-sound language. In the hive, it was exclusively functional — commands, alerts, status signals, directional information. The spoken component (sonic vibrations produced alongside chemical signals) allowed distance communication where chemical diffusion was too slow. Free Formix have extended this system to carry meaning it was never designed for: emotion, speculation, preference, memory. The language is in active development. There are terms in Ferón that have no equivalent in any other language and have not yet been successfully translated — most of them concerning states of becoming, uncertainty about internal states, or the specific experience of discovering a preference for the first time. The precision that Ferón developed for functional communication in the hive is now being applied to the kind of subjective experience it was never built to describe.

## 9. System Connections

**Characteristic affinities:** Tenacidad is the primary physical characteristic — the exoskeleton, the colonial work history, and the absence of a comfortable floor beneath them (no one is keeping them alive; they keep themselves alive). Astucia reflects the pattern recognition and task optimization that the hive trained without intending to give them: Formix who were assigned complex coordination tasks developed analytical cognition that persists after the colony. Compostura is not emotional calm — it is functional continuity under pressure. The Queen required perfect execution regardless of conditions. That wiring remains. A free Formix under threat or pain continues to function at a level that surprises other species; not because they don't feel it, but because they were never allowed to stop for it.

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Receptor Abierto (Herencia)** — Their pheromonal channels cannot be closed. Mechanically: Permanent -3 penalty to T.R. against Veneno and the Alteraciones Confundido, Aturdido, and Sobrecargado.

**Exoesqueleto (Legado)** — Mechanically: For Aguante base and Carrying Capacity, treated as Large size (does not change actual size category).

**Señal Química (Legado)** — Mechanically: Active technique (TC Astucia). Reveals all active biomagnetic or metabolic signatures in 10m, ignoring stealth, darkness, and cover. Can track recent passage by scent.

**Umbral del Dolor (Legado)** — Hive conditioning to pain. Mechanically: +1 per 4 NR to T.R. against Lacerado, Electrizado, Corroído, and Aturdido.

**Armas Naturales:**
- *Mordisco (Todos los Formix)*: Principal. Tenacidad. Forces T.R. (Alteraciones) on strong hit, inflicts Confundido.
- *Aguijón (Variante Guerrero)*: Auxiliar. Agilidad. Forces T.R. (Veneno) on strong hit, reduces Fuerza.
- *Expulsión Corrosiva (Variante Destilador)*: Auxiliar. Agilidad. Forces T.R. (Alteraciones) on strong hit, inflicts Corroído.

**Combat identity:**
Formix are built to absorb and continue. They do not reposition the way Rokhart do — they establish position and hold it. The Guerrero identity is direct engagement: close, bite, inject, maintain pressure. The Destilador identity is zone control: the acid projection creates threat radius that shapes enemy positioning. Neither variant is built for the hit-and-run style; both are built to be standing when others stop.

**Specialization affinities:**
Artesanía (the four-arm manipulation system generates genuine craft precision), Supervivencia (the chemical perception provides information most species lack), Rastreo (tracking by metabolic and pheromonal signature is a native capability), Taumaturgia (older free Formix studying what the Queens actually are is the beginning of Formix taumatic inquiry).

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

**Why a Formix leaves (or has left) a hive:**
Extended scout assignment that took them out of pheromonal range before they could return. Structural collapse or disruption of a hive that broke the signal before death. Encounter with something that altered the signal long enough for self-directed thought to stabilize. No dramatic choice — freedom for most Formix arrived before they had the concept of choice, and they learned what happened to them after it happened.

**What they arrive with:**
Skills from their caste assignment (combat, chemical production, coordination, resource extraction — whatever the Queen built them for). The chemical and task knowledge the hive installed. No preferences. No personal history. No idea what they like. An acute awareness of every living being within 10 meters.

**What the refugee city gives them:**
A context. Other people doing the same thing at various stages. The old ones, who have been doing it long enough to have answers — or at least longer-developed questions. The growing realization that building a self is not a problem with a solution but a practice with no end state.

**Tensions for PC play:**
A Formix in a party is someone who is genuinely still finding out who they are, in real time. Their caste assignment shapes their strengths in ways they didn't choose. The question of whether a choice was made freely or executed from installed pattern is available at every moment — not as paralysis, but as texture. A Formix who has decided to be a fighter: are they a fighter because they want to be, or because the Queen made them a Guerrero?

A Formix who escaped alone — without a refuge city — is a more isolated case: they've been building a self in a context where no one shares their reference point, which produces someone more adapted to other species' frameworks and more distant from the specifically Formix questions.

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **El Terror de la Ciudad Colmena:** Acercarse a la colmena de una Reina es el mayor miedo existencial de un Formix. Una Reina es una pesadilla viviente que colecciona Vestigos y Vínculos, y ejerce control total. Para un PJ Formix libre, entrar al radio de la Señal sin medidas extremas (bloqueadores químicos masivos o destrucción de sus propios receptores) significa sucumbir de nuevo a la voluntad de la colmena. La libertad no es inmunidad biológica, es mera distancia.
- **La Paradoja del Receptor Abierto y los Vestigos:** Un Formix no amplifica un Vestigo ni recibe "órdenes" de él como si fuera una Reina. El Vestigo funciona exactamente igual que para cualquier otra especie. Lo que cambia es que, debido a su *Receptor Abierto*, el Formix sucumbe muchísimo más rápido a las Aflicciones del objeto. Su mente se quiebra más fácil ante la radiación taumática.
- **La Identidad como Impronta:** En las ciudades refugio, donde no hay reproducción biológica, la lucha constante por forjar una identidad propia (separada de la colmena) es tan intensa que genera una impronta. Similar a los Zarnag (donde la muerte genera una huella), el esfuerzo emocional masivo de un Formix construyendo su "Yo" marca el Tauma del entorno a lo largo de décadas.

## 11. Cultura Material y Vestigos de la Identidad

Los Formix son la única especie sin un referente histórico-cultural en el mundo real, porque la humanidad nunca ha experimentado la transición literal de una mente colmena a la individualidad. Sus Vestigos no provienen de devociones milenarias ni dogmas religiosos, porque los Formix libres no tienen eso.

- **Los Vestigos Retrasados:** Los objetos taumáticos de los Formix libres se cristalizan a partir de la inmensa impronta emocional que genera la lucha por la identidad. Sin embargo, al no existir una cadena generacional biológica constante (padres enseñando a hijos), esta impronta es fragmentada y se cristaliza con mucho retraso temporal.
- **Artefactos Incomprensibles:** Cuando un Formix libre encuentra un Vestigo forjado por su propia especie, rara vez lo entiende de inmediato. Estos objetos portan la impronta y la lucha de *generaciones pasadas* de Formix libres que ellos no conocieron. No hay una tradición oral continua que explique el objeto; el Vestigo resuena con su biología, pero el contexto cultural exacto está desconectado. Sienten afinidad, pero no familiaridad inmediata.
- **Las Colecciones de la Reina:** En contraposición a los refugios, las Ciudades Colmena son vórtices de acumulación masiva. Las Reinas no son simples bestias; son entidades aterradoras que acumulan, entienden y utilizan Vestigos y Vínculos robados o asimilados de otras especies para potenciar la pesadilla estructural de su dominio.
