# Drak'kai

**Status:** In progress
**Animal base:** Terrestrial turtle (large species — snapping turtle, giant tortoise)
**Cultural analog:** No clean single analog; closest approximations are wandering hermit-scholars and polymaths who travel accumulating knowledge rather than building institutions
**Primary reference:** [index.md](index.md)

## 1. Identity

The Drak'kai accumulate. They wander, and what they collect along the way — objects, records, languages, encounters, fragments of the ancient world — is the material of their existence. They are not acquiring power. They are not building empires. They are building completeness. A Drak'kai who has traveled for a century and accumulated nothing is, by their own standards, impoverished. A Drak'kai who has traveled for a century and cannot stop accumulating is simply alive.

Their oldest civilizational understanding is simple: the world contains more than any one existence can hold, and that fact is not a problem to solve but a condition to inhabit.

What makes them distinct from archivists or scholars is the body of what they carry. Not all of it is text or memory. Some of it is objects whose nature they have not fully determined. Some of it is encounters with presences they cannot explain. And some of it, they suspect, is knowledge that has already changed them in ways they cannot fully audit.

The civilizational anxiety that most Drak'kai carry quietly: **they know that nothing anyone believes about the cosmos has ever been stable — and they have been watching it change.**

## 2. Body

Drak'kai are heavy medium humanoids descended from large terrestrial turtle stock: dense torsos, strong limbs, a thick integument that becomes a structured shell on the back, and a jaw built for closure rather than speed. Their gait is deliberate. Their posture carries weight without strain. Nothing about their body suggests urgency.

Their shell is integrated — part of their skeletal structure, not armor they wear. As a biped, the shell covers the back, giving the torso natural structural reinforcement from behind. In combat, they have developed ways to position the body so the shell's mass absorbs contact, but this is technique, not reflex.

Their jaw is not their fastest weapon. It is their most committed one. A Drak'kai bite does not strike — it closes. Once it closes, reopening requires more than the target's resistance.

Their claws are not predatory. They are functional: good for grip, handling, digging, climbing, and the careful manipulation of objects that may require delicate treatment. In combat they work as supplementary pressure and control rather than primary damage.

Their magnetoreception is a biological sense most other species lack entirely. They orient through the landscape differently — reading gradient shifts, route history, and environmental signals as a secondary layer beneath ordinary sight and hearing. This sense is not infallible, but it is deep and accumulative: a Drak'kai who has walked a route before reads it differently the second time.

Their perception is calibrated toward environmental and extranatural signals at the expense of social and creature-reading signals. This is evolutionary, not chosen — they read the world well and other creatures poorly.

### Physical baseline

- **Height:** `1.60 - 1.80 m`
- **Weight:** `100 - 200 kg`
- **Lifespan:** `150 - 200 years`
- **Size category:** `Medium`

### Visible variation

Different Drak'kai lineages vary in shell pattern, coloration, jaw prominence, and limb proportion. Some carry heavier shells, some carry thicker necks, some have more pronounced magnetoreceptive sensitivity. These differences are significant socially — shell patterns accumulate marking over a lifetime and become legible to other Drak'kai as rough biographies — but they do not imply rigid biological castes.

## 3. Culture

Drak'kai society is organized around a single motivation that produces a recognizable institution: accumulation as completeness, and the conclave as the place where completeness is tested.

The **conclave** is the central social fact of Drak'kai life. These are periodic gatherings — irregular in timing, held at established sites, attended by Drak'kai who may not have seen each other in decades — where knowledge and discoveries are shared, compared, challenged, and sometimes traded. The conclave is not a government. It has no authority. But it is where a Drak'kai's accumulation is measured against others', and where new accumulations become possible.

Outside the conclave, Drak'kai travel alone. This is not antisocial by their standards — solitary wandering is how accumulation happens. Traveling with others introduces noise, obligation, and the risk of having one's collection become collectively known.

What earns respect at a conclave: depth of collection, rarity of what you've found, quality of your judgment about what is worth keeping, and willingness to share something genuinely valuable. What is shameful: hoarding without understanding, using knowledge to control rather than to complete, or bringing something to a conclave that you cannot account for.

The internal tension that most Drak'kai recognize but few resolve openly: the deeper your collection, the harder it becomes to share freely, because the deeper your collection, the more dangerous it is for others to know what you have. Some Drak'kai withdraw from conclave life as their collections grow. Others continue attending but share strategically. Others have concluded that certain things should not be shared at all — not for power, but for safety.

Their laughter is rare. Their silences are comfortable. Their interest in other species is genuine but often reads as clinical.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Drak'kai have had more encounters with Primordial entities than any other species — not because they seek these encounters out, but because their methods of study and the nature of what they collect make inadvertent contact more likely and more sustained.

These encounters are not interpretable through any single religion's framework. The entities they have encountered behave consistently with the underlying logic of Tauma: they respond to collective consciousness, they change as the beliefs of surrounding communities change, and they cannot be categorized as good or evil in any stable sense.

Over generations, this has produced a Drak'kai cosmological understanding that most other species would find corrosive: **the elemental pantheon is not fixed.** What communities call gods are not external to those communities. They are expressions of what those communities collectively believe, shaped by the Tauma that concentrates in response to that belief. The Drak'kai have watched this happen too many times to mistake it for coincidence.

This does not mean they disbelieve in the elemental forces. They believe in them deeply — they have encountered expressions of those forces directly. What they do not believe is that any particular community's interpretation of those forces is accurate in a lasting sense.

Most Drak'kai do not share this understanding openly. It causes conflict, disbelief, or fear. They carry it the same way they carry their other accumulated knowledge: carefully, privately, aware of its weight.

Their horror is not eschatological. They do not believe something terrible is coming. Their horror is epistemological: **nothing anyone believes about the cosmos has ever been stable, and they have centuries of evidence for this.** The question that follows them is not whether disaster will arrive, but whether any understanding — including their own — will survive contact with what they don't yet know.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

Drak'kai do not organize around territory, genealogy, or doctrine. They organize around what they've found and where they're going next.

The conclave is the only institution that functions consistently across all Drak'kai communities. Beyond that, variation is significant:

- **Route-based wanderers:** The most common form — a Drak'kai who has established long-route patterns over decades, returning to conclave sites periodically, accumulating through sustained travel.
- **Ruin-specialists:** Drak'kai who focus on specific sites — ancient ruins, catastrophic locations, deep caves — and return to them repeatedly rather than ranging widely.
- **Conclave keepers:** A small number of Drak'kai who take on the responsibility of maintaining conclave sites between gatherings. They accumulate less through travel but more through what others bring to them.
- **Withdrawal cases:** Drak'kai who have accumulated something they cannot share safely and have stopped attending conclaves. The community has no formal way to compel their return.

What is universal across all communities: the Drakar language, the conclave institution, the understanding that accumulation is the purpose, and the shared discomfort around the question of what should be destroyed versus preserved.

Internal fault lines:
- What counts as worthy accumulation varies — information, objects, skills, and encounters all have advocates
- The question of dangerous knowledge: some Drak'kai argue that certain things should be brought to conclaves precisely because no one should hold them alone; others argue the opposite
- What happens to a collection when a Drak'kai dies is an unresolved structural problem; currently it is informal, and this creates recurring conflict

## 6. Daily Life and Survival

A Drak'kai's daily life is defined by movement and carrying.

They walk. Not always fast, not always far, but consistently. Their body is built for sustained travel rather than bursts of speed, and they are capable of covering long distances under conditions that would stop other species — extreme temperatures, water scarcity, rough terrain. Their endurance is not romantic; it is biological.

They carry their collections with them. This creates practical constraints that shape all other decisions: where they sleep, what routes they take, how long they stay anywhere, who they allow to travel near them. A collection is not portable by delegation — no one else can be trusted with it.

Food is opportunistic and often preserved. They can go extended periods without eating or drinking without the consequences other species face. Meals at conclaves are often the most deliberate eating they do; between gatherings, food is solved rather than savored.

Young Drak'kai travel with a single mentor during a formative period — not a parent necessarily, but an older Drak'kai who agrees to bring a younger one along. This period ends at the first conclave attendance, after which independent wandering begins. There is no formal graduation; there is simply a point at which the mentor determines the younger one is ready to accumulate alone.

Old age for a Drak'kai is not about physical decline — it is about the weight of what they carry and what they know. The most respected elders are those who have continued to accumulate without losing the quality of their judgment: who can still distinguish something worth keeping from something that only appears worth keeping.

What a Drak'kai fears most is not dying. It is dying with an unaccounted collection — objects whose nature remains unknown, knowledge that no other Drak'kai has, encounters that haven't been brought to a conclave. The idea that centuries of careful accumulation could simply disperse unexamined is, by their standards, a kind of failure.

## 7. External Relations

Other species find Drak'kai hard to read and harder to negotiate with. This is not because they are hostile. It is because they operate on a timescale and with a priority structure that most other species cannot calibrate to. Urgency does not register the same way. Social ceremony reads as overhead. Emotional pressure does not land cleanly.

Their knowledge makes them valuable to anyone who needs historical context, geographical information, or access to information that has otherwise been lost. This also makes them targets. Communities, factions, and individuals who have learned what a specific Drak'kai is carrying — or is rumored to be carrying — have historically attempted to extract it through negotiation, deception, or force. Drak'kai isolation is partly a defense against this.

What external pressures they face:
- Raids and theft targeting their collections
- Coercive "employment" by powerful factions who want access to specific knowledge
- Theological hostility from communities whose beliefs they cannot sincerely affirm
- Suspicion from communities that misread their silence as arrogance or concealment
- Genuine danger from collections that contain objects with active Tauma connections — when what they carry begins affecting their environment, nearby communities notice

They are not widely welcomed anywhere. They are widely useful when specific things are needed. This gap — between the utility others want to extract and the conditions under which Drak'kai are willing to provide it — defines most of their external relationships.

**To the Naghii:** The Naghii respect the antiquity of Drak'kai knowledge, but distrust their informality. For a Naghii, an object must be situated within archive, translation, sequence, and authorization. For a Drak'kai, the object itself may have survived too many mistaken authorizations to wait for permission. Both understand the danger of knowing. Neither entirely trusts how the other administers it.

**To the Sauri:** The Sauri relate to the Drak'kai from material suspicion. An object carried for generations can be a vessel, contamination, relic, or crack. The Drak'kai see in the Sauri a dangerous willingness to preserve bodies, lineages, and chambers beyond what is prudent. The Sauri see in the Drak'kai hoarders who carry things without subjecting them to a sufficiently strong ritual order. Both know some things must be contained. They disagree on who has the right to decide how.

**To the Zarnag:** The Zarnag produce a specific discomfort in the Drak'kai. Where a Drak'kai might want to keep a Remnant for its historical value, a Zarnag might recognize that Remnant is still active and must be satiated, burned, or buried. In ancient death sites, this tension can become immediate. The Drak'kai asks what will be lost if it is destroyed. The Zarnag asks who will pay the price if it is kept.

**To the Panin:** The Panin fascinate and despair the Drak'kai. A Panin can find relationships between objects, routes, or signals that the Drak'kai hadn't noticed, but they can also move pieces, touch without context, or lose interest before finishing explaining what they saw. Some Drak'kai avoid traveling with Panin entirely. Others seek them out for specific problems and then spend years trying to reconstruct the logic of an observation made in three sentences and a gesture.

**To the Rokhart:** The Rokhart are natural allies and inevitable rivals. They document, compare, classify, and preserve patterns, but they do so from institutions and public methods that the Drak'kai usually consider vulnerable. A Rokhart might accuse a Drak'kai of hiding necessary data. A Drak'kai might accuse a Rokhart of publishing paths to things that shouldn't be found by just anyone.

**To the Myo:** The Myo unsettle many Drak'kai because they reach conclusions without carrying the full route of evidence. An oracular vision might coincide with decades of accumulation or contradict it in a way impossible to dismiss. Some Drak'kai consider the Myo a valuable source of non-linear patterns. Others treat them as dangerous shortcuts: knowledge without enough weight to make the receiver responsible.

**To the Ceratox:** The Ceratox are usually useful interlocutors in environments where the environmental signal matters. Their continuous reading of the environment can confirm gradients that the Drak'kai feel en route. However, the Drak'kai tend to distinguish between recording a signal and understanding its history. For them, perception without temporal accumulation can be precise and still incomplete.

**To the Arakhel:** The Arakhel interest the Drak'kai for reasons the Arakhel don't always find comfortable. An ancient node can behave as a route, archive, border, and restless piece all at once. Arakhel families know things about crossings that no object records. The Drak'kai would like to compare those readings across generations; the Arakhel usually remind them that the crossing does not exist to become anyone's burden.

**To the Bufoni:** The Bufoni offer another kind of problem. An active domain cannot be cleanly separated from its soil, lineage, protocol, and kunitsukami. To a Drak'kai, that makes it hard to transport, but not impossible to compare. They can recognize patterns among fallen domains, ancient offerings, and territorial responses that the Bufoni themselves would consider dangerously decontextualized. The Bufoni asks if the domain recognizes. The Drak'kai asks what changed after a hundred years of recognition.

**To the Loxod:** The Loxod are perhaps the interlocutors who most question Drak'kai accumulation. Where the Drak'kai carries pieces, notes, and routes, the Loxod receives chemical layers, vibrations, and environmental memory without needing to possess the object. To a Drak'kai, that reception can seem incomplete without a record and too exposed without a filter. To a Loxod, a Drak'kai collection can seem like a fragmented memory that someone insists on carrying on their back because they don't know how to release it to the place that produced it.

**To the Ursari:** The Ursari understand the mark left by a prolonged craft. A forge and a collection are not the same, but both teach that working for years with charged materials changes the body and judgment. The Drak'kai respect the Ursari who recognize when a work must be abandoned. They distrust those who confuse endurance with wisdom. The Ursari, for their part, might see in the Drak'kai a culture that accumulates too much without transforming enough.

**To the Chelicer:** The Chelicer are difficult for a precise reason: they trust procedure too much as a path to a truth that reveals itself. For a Drak'kai, a truth might have been correctly revealed three generations ago and be responding differently now. A Chelicer might consider that dangerous relativism. A Drak'kai considers it a case history. Both respect the trial, but disagree on how long it remains valid after it occurs.

**To the Formix:** The Formix make the Drak'kai uncomfortable because they force the question of what is accumulation and what is inherited programming. A free Formix might look at a Drak'kai collection and ask how much of that burden was really chosen and how much became identity because the carrier doesn't know how to exist without it. The Drak'kai might answer with irritation. Or they might spend years unable to forget the question.

**To the Luphran:** The Luphran experience the route in the opposite way. Where the Drak'kai walks alone to accumulate without noise, the Luphran navigates with a crew, oral memory, jarl, and shared mandate. Both understand that a route changes whoever follows it for years. But the Luphran measures the path by loyalty and group survival; the Drak'kai, by what the route allowed them to carry, compare, or discover. They can respect each other greatly and still not stand each other's rhythm.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

A Drak'kai's most legible material expression is what they carry. The objects, containers, wrapped items, and visible accumulations on their person communicate more about who they are than any other marker.

Shell markings accumulate over a lifetime. Many Drak'kai mark their shells with carvings, dyes, or inlaid materials that represent significant encounters, important routes, or accumulated distinctions. These are not purely decorative — other Drak'kai can read them as rough biographies, though the specific content remains private until disclosed.

**Drakar** is an old language with a large and precise vocabulary for types of knowledge, categories of objects, qualities of encounter, and degrees of certainty. It has a notably poor vocabulary for urgency, for ceremony, and for social relationships that involve emotional dependency. Drak'kai who learn Common often find it frustratingly imprecise about knowledge and surprisingly elaborate about feelings.

Their tools are built for careful handling and examination: instruments for turning objects without full contact, protective covering for sensitive items, carrying systems designed to distribute weight across long distances. These tools are often themselves old and accumulated — a Drak'kai rarely discards a useful tool.

Conclave sites are established through informal agreement over generations. Drak'kai arriving at a conclave site temporarily elaborate it — storage systems, display areas, exchange protocols — then dismantle everything when the gathering ends. No permanent infrastructure is left behind deliberately.

## 9. System Connections

### Competency affinities

- **Sabiduría:** Percepción, Supervivencia — environmental reading, magnetoreception, long-route endurance
- **Intelecto:** Identificación, Historia, Geografía, Arqueología, Lingüística, Astronomía — accumulated knowledge surfaces
- **Compostura:** Enfoque, Contención, Meditación — patient practice, endurance under pressure, internal discipline
- **Astucia:** Orientación, Rastreo — route-reading, environmental tracking

### Natural attack logic

- **Mordisco (Principal):** Heavy jaw closure and crushing sustained pressure. Contact logic: commit, close, hold. Not a harassment tool — a final word. Profiles: Impact, Unstoppable, Interception, Torsion. Restricted: Shadow Pressure, Precision, Flow.
- **Garras (Auxiliar):** Functional grip and control. Not raking or tearing — handling, gripping, and supplementary close pressure. Profiles: Line Control, Interception, Torsion, Interruption. Restricted: Rend, Shadow Pressure, Charge.
- **Caparazón (defensive, not attack):** Already registered in `natural-attack-forms.yaml`. Profiles: Bastion, Interception, Impact, Line Control. Functions as natural block reinforcement for Torso zone.

### Arsenal fit

Likely weapon affinities:
- Maces, hammers, and blunt crushing weapons — committed strikes match the species identity
- Heavy shields — anchor and barrier role
- Polearms for patient, prepared fighters who want to control approach distance

Less natural:
- Light or fast weapons — their identity is not speed
- Dual-wield — not the natural expression of a species built around commitment over volume

### Species-specific mechanics (Updated from Corebook)

**Percepción Interferida (Herencia)** — Their evolutionary adaptation to read the world leaves them blind to social subtlety. Mechanically: -3 penalty to Instinct and Intuition rolls when reading the intent of living creatures.

**Caparazón (Legado)** — Their bone structure grants immense passive defense. Mechanically: +4 to Torso Block per 4 NR.

**Magnetorrecepción (Legado)** — Their ability to read the magnetic lines of the world. Mechanically: Active technique (TC Wisdom). Reveals all active biomagnetic or metallic signatures in 15m, ignoring stealth, darkness, and cover.

**Reliquias (Legado)** — Their deep accumulation translates to practical knowledge. Mechanically: They can use Wisdom instead of Intellect for knowledge and identification rolls, gaining scaling bonuses (+1 per 4 NR).

### Technique implications

Drak'kai Techniques should emerge from:
- Patient commitment — waiting, reading, then acting decisively rather than repeatedly
- Body weight and structure as advantage in close contact
- Accumulated knowledge applied in specific moments rather than as ambient expertise
- Environmental reading under conditions where others have less information
- Endurance under conditions that degrade other species

They should avoid:
- Fast harassment and chaining style (Zarnag covers this)
- Psychological pressure and deceptive tactics (Shadow Pressure as core identity)
- Ranged or throwing-focused offense
- Techniques whose identity collapses into "smart fighter" generically — their knowledge needs to translate into specific mechanical contexts

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

### What a PC knows

- How to read landscapes, routes, and environmental history through magnetoreception and accumulated travel
- How to handle unfamiliar objects with appropriate caution — without necessarily knowing what they are
- That the elemental pantheon is not fixed, and that collective belief shapes what it becomes
- What long patience feels like from the inside — including when it is the wrong choice

### What they likely do not know

- How to read living creatures' emotions and intentions with precision (Herencia)
- What many of their accumulated objects actually are — identification requires specific interaction, and they may have been deliberately avoiding it
- What to do with grief at the speed other species experience it
- Whether the conclave they last attended would still recognize what they've become

### Departure reasons

- A conclave revealed something that requires investigation in a region they haven't traveled
- Something in their collection has begun behaving unexpectedly, and they need context they don't have
- Another Drak'kai whose collection overlapped with theirs has died, leaving the accumulation unaccounted for
- Something they are carrying has made staying in any one place too dangerous

### What they carry with them

- Physical: a collection of objects whose full nature they have not entirely determined
- Historical: context for events and places that most other species have no record of
- Uncomfortable: the knowledge that no one's religious framework is accurate in a stable sense
- Open: the question of whether anything they've accumulated is a vínculo or vestigio — and what that means for them if it is. Whether decades of carrying an object have added their own imprint to it. Whether their collection is as much a record of who they are as of what they found.

### Canon Resolutivo: Base para el Módulo de Campaña

- **La Paradoja del Registro:** Si un texto antiguo afirma que un objeto curaba, pero el Drak'kai comprueba que ahora envenena, el Drak'kai no cree que el historiador se equivocó. Su cosmología afirma que la *Interferencia* (lo que otras especies llaman Tauma) muta por la creencia acumulada. La historia está viva y es radiactiva.
- **¿Qué sucede en un Cónclave con un objeto incontrolable?** Si un Drak'kai lleva un objeto extremadamente peligroso, el Cónclave no se dispersa ciegamente: debaten la mejor forma de **sellarlo**. Los objetos no se "usan", se aíslan en vasijas de bronce rituales para anular su frecuencia. Si el objeto no puede ser sellado, el Cónclave emite un mandato de **"Ruta de Contención"**: el portador asume el deber de vagar perpetuamente por tierras despobladas, alejando el objeto del mundo hasta morir de viejo.
- **Las Facciones Drak'kai:** Existen dos posturas fundamentales sobre el conocimiento:
    - *Los Hermenéuticos:* Prefieren entender sin tocar. Sellan las reliquias en cofres de jade y las aíslan, catalogando sus efectos desde lejos para proteger su propia biología.
    - *Buscadores de Interferencia:* Creen que la razón es un filtro que oculta la verdad del mundo. Se exponen deliberadamente a las aflicciones y "radiaciones" de los objetos, alterando sus propios cuerpos para entender la frecuencia del objeto en primera persona.
- **La Colección más Antigua:** El *Primer Oráculo* no es un mito muerto. Es un Drak'kai tan anciano que se fundió con su propia colección. Su inmenso caparazón alberga registros geológicos del cataclismo de hace millones de años. Se le busca no por poder, sino porque es la única biblioteca caminante que sabe cómo era el mundo antes del gran desvío.

## 11. Cultura Material y Herramientas de Acumulación

La estética Drak'kai remite a los eruditos itinerantes, los adivinos y los navegantes de la Dinastía Shang y Zhou de la antigua China. No acumulan espadas brillantes; acumulan herramientas de lectura y cajas fuertes portátiles para la realidad.

- **Huesos Oraculares (*Jia Gu*):** Placas de caparazón (a veces de sus propios ancestros) que calientan al fuego para revelar fisuras. No ven el futuro, sino que las grietas resuenan con la *Interferencia* del lugar, adivinando qué evento catastrófico ocurrió allí hace milenios.
- **Cuchara Sinan (Primer Compás):** Instrumentos de magnetita pura tallados en forma de cuchara. Las usan para navegar las "Rutas Magnéticas" (líneas ley) y para detectar anomalías taumáticas invisibles al ojo.
- **Discos de Jade (*Bi*):** Anillos de jade pulido que funcionan como lentes o filtros. Al mirar a través de un Disco Bi, un Drak'kai Hermenéutico sintoniza patrones acústicos o visuales del entorno, logrando "leer" el eco de una reliquia sin tocarla.
- **Vasijas de Bronce Ritual (*Ding*):** Contenedores extremadamente pesados, forjados con aleaciones muertas, diseñados específicamente para **sellar**. Un objeto depositado en un *Ding* queda acústicamente y taumáticamente mudo. Los Drak'kai las cargan en sus espaldas para transportar reliquias inestables.
- **Caparazones Biográficos:** La máxima prueba de erudición de un Drak'kai no está en un pergamino, sino en su propia espalda. Tallan la historia de las reliquias que han sellado y las rutas de contención que han sobrevivido directamente en sus escudos, convirtiendo a los más ancianos en libros vivos de historia prohibida.
