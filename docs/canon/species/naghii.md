## 1. Identity

**Animal base:** Serpent
**Cultural analog:** Sumerian / Mesopotamian
**Cosmicist hook:** The Naghii hold the oldest astronomical records in the world — records that predate every other living civilization. In those records, the period immediately preceding the catastrophe is marked by an event called **Khataar**. They cannot date it. They cannot know how many cycles have passed since it occurred, because the species that could remember it died, and the species that came after had to start from nothing. What they have is evidence in the ruins and in the sky — and the belief that the state of the world, the shape of the ruins, the behavior of Tauma, all point to something that happened up there and has not fully finished.

They do not watch the sky because it is beautiful. They watch it because they are not certain it is done.

## 2. Body

The Naghii are bipedal humanoids with a distinctly serpentine frame — lean, elongated, and built for precision of movement rather than raw mass. Their limbs are strong but fluid, suggesting control over each degree of motion. Their prehensile tail, roughly as long as their torso, functions as a natural weapon, a balance mechanism, and an instrument of restraint.

Their scales form dense, overlapping patterns across most of the body, shifting in tone between deep blacks, dark greens, and muted golds depending on the individual. Under direct light — particularly starlight — the scales of some Naghii produce a faint iridescence, as though something beneath the surface reflects frequencies the eye barely registers. The Naghii themselves associate this quality with "carrying the sky inside."

Their eyes are notably still. Where most creatures' eyes shift constantly, a Naghii's gaze settles. This stillness is not calm — it is the quality of something that has learned not to reveal what it sees.

Their forked tongue is a primary sensory instrument, capable of detecting heat signatures and chemical trails at distances and in conditions where visual tracking fails. They perceive their environment in layers that other species do not — smell, heat, vibration through the ground — and this compounds in combat and tracking situations to a degree that can feel almost anticipatory to observers.

**Physical parameters:**

- Height: 1.68 – 1.83 m
- Weight: 59 – 77 kg
- Lifespan: 90 – 100 years (significantly longer than most species)
- Size category: Medium

### Biological variants

All Naghii share the same basic anatomy, culture, and theology. Two distinct biological adaptations appear within the species, present from birth and considered theologically significant. They appear in all known Naghii communities regardless of region, though their cultural interpretation varies.

**Kha-Naghii** possess retractable fangs connected to venom glands seated behind the jawline. Their venom is injected on direct contact and acts systemically, degrading physical resistance over time. They are the majority. In the theological vocabulary of most Naghii communities, they are *those who hold* — those who can sustain direct contact with what is dangerous without being destroyed by it.

**Saa-Naghii** possess specialized ducts that allow them to project venom outward at short range. Their venom is often faintly phosphorescent — a quality most Naghii communities call the *aliento astral* / *astral breath*. They are a minority. In theological terms, they are *those who speak outward* — those who can transmit what they perceive to a distance, who project rather than hold.

Neither variant is chosen. Both appear in all lineages without predictable pattern. This is central to the theological and political tension within Naghii society.

## 3. Culture

Naghii civilization is organized around the **archive** — not a library in the passive sense, but a living record maintained within ziggurat-temples that function simultaneously as observatories, administrative centers, and sacred sites. The archive is the reason the civilization exists. Every structure, hierarchy, and custom serves to protect, extend, or interpret it.

### Multiple communities

The Naghii are not a single unified civilization. Multiple Naghii communities exist across different regions, each having developed around the ruins they encountered when they first settled. Because no two ruin sites are identical, and because the Naghii's understanding of their own history is filtered through interpretations of an incomprehensible language, communities that developed in different places arrived at different conclusions about what the ruins mean, what Khataar was, and what the Kha and Saa variants represent theologically.

The broad cultural patterns — the ziggurat architecture, the priest-astronomer hierarchy, the veneration of the Firstborn — are consistent enough to be recognizable across communities. The specifics of belief, ritual, and political organization vary considerably.

### Social structure

Most Naghii communities are governed by a **theocratic hierarchy of priest-astronomers** (sacerdotes-astrónomos). Within that hierarchy, the two biological variants occupy structurally distinct positions that have been formalized over generations into a dual-power arrangement:

**Kha-Naghii** hold **physical authority** — they guard the archive's most restricted sections, control access to initiation, and serve as the hands of the priestly order when decisions must be enforced. Their position rests on the theological logic that they are constitutionally capable of guarding dangerous knowledge without being undone by it. In practice, they control who enters, who advances, and what actions are taken.

**Saa-Naghii** hold **interpretive authority** — they read the astronomical records, interpret celestial events, and advise the order on what the sky is saying and what must be done. In practice, they tell the order what is coming — but they require the Kha-Naghii to act on it.

The tension is structural and persistent: the Saa-Naghii perceive and the Kha-Naghii decide. When they agree, the order moves with unusual coherence. When they disagree — particularly about the significance of a celestial event — the result is internal paralysis or, historically, schism.

### The interpretation problem

The Naghii's oldest records are not original texts. They are Naghii interpretations of inscriptions, tablets, and markings found in the ruins of the ancient civilization — written in a language that no living species can read. The Naghii assembled their foundational knowledge by comparing visual patterns, cross-referencing astronomical observations with symbols they found in ruin sites, and building a body of meaning over generations.

What they know is what they believe they translated. The original texts — if they could be read — might say something entirely different. The Naghii priesthood knows this at some level. Most of them choose not to examine it directly. The archive is sacred not because it is certainly true, but because abandoning it would mean having nothing.

### Knowledge transmission

Knowledge within Naghii society is stratified into layers of initiation. The outer layers are broadly shared: basic astronomical literacy, the mythological history of the Firstborn (*Primigenios*), the ritual calendar. The inner layers narrow progressively and are not entered freely — they require demonstration of readiness, formal rite of passage, and in the deepest cases, the agreement of both a senior Kha-Naghii and a senior Saa-Naghii.

### Architecture

Naghii structures are built on principles borrowed from the form of ziggurats — stepped, ascending, oriented to specific celestial alignments. Many of their major sites are built on, around, or incorporating ruins of the ancient civilization, which the Naghii interpret as sacred ground where the Firstborn worked closest to completing their great design.

### Naghii outside their communities

Individual Naghii exist outside priestly communities for many reasons — taken in childhood, separated by circumstance, fled from political tension, raised among other species. These individuals are not automatically pursued or reclaimed; the relationship between diaspora Naghii and their communities of origin depends heavily on the specific community and the specific situation. Some communities maintain contact with known diaspora members. Others do not.

## 4. Theology

### The Firstborn (Los Primigenios)

The Naghii call the ancient pre-catastrophe civilization the **Primigenios** (ES) / **the Firstborn** (EN). In Naghii theology, the Firstborn were not mortal in the way living species are mortal. They were the architects of the ordered cosmos: beings who knew the true names of stars, who built structures that touched the boundary of sky and void, and who at the end of their age did not simply die but *dissolved into the void* — completed their work and became part of what they had always studied.

The catastrophe, in this reading, was not a failure. It was a culmination.

This interpretation is the cosmological armor built over a truth the Naghii cannot see from within it: the Firstborn were mortal, technological, and they destroyed themselves and everything else. What the Naghii have interpreted as sacred design was a catastrophic failure. Their most sacred texts are partial translations of documents written by people who did not know what they were doing with what they had found.

### Khataar

Khataar is the central unresolved question of Naghii theology. Their astronomical records identify it as a specific celestial configuration observed in the period immediately before the catastrophe. What they cannot determine is how long ago this occurred. The extinction and re-emergence of species spans centuries beyond the reach of any continuous record. The Naghii did not exist when it happened. What they have are ruins with astronomical markings, sky observations that align with patterns described in those markings, and the theological conviction that the state of the world — the behavior of Tauma, the shape of the ruins, the specific configuration of the sky — is the aftermath of Khataar.

They do not know if it has happened once or many times. They do not know if what they are watching for is an event that recurs or a single threshold that has not yet fully closed.

There are three schools of thought within the priesthood:

**Khataar as warning** — the configuration signals that something is approaching or that the boundary between the physical world and the Abzu is thinning. Those who hold this interpretation believe Khataar must be watched for, and when it comes, the Naghii must be ready. Ready to do what is where this school fractures internally.

**Khataar as threshold** — the configuration does not warn of something coming from outside but marks a moment when the world becomes permeable. The catastrophe was not caused by what arrived during Khataar but by what was done *at* the threshold — the Firstborn attempted to pass through it, or to hold it open. This school believes the Naghii's role is to ensure no one attempts that again.

**Khataar as completion** — the most restricted and most contested interpretation, held by a small minority in the deepest archive: Khataar was not a warning or a threshold. It was the event the Firstborn were building toward. It will come again. The question is not how to prevent it but whether the Naghii will be prepared to receive what it brings.

No school has enough evidence to fully discredit the others. The innermost records have passages that support each interpretation, depending on what is translated and what is left aside — and those translations were made from a language no one truly reads.

### The Abzu

The Naghii do not use the same name for the void dimension that other species call El Limbo. Their theological vocabulary calls it the **Abzu** — a term inherited from the ancient symbolic vocabulary they encountered in ruins, which they reinterpreted to name the dimensionless space that exists beyond what can be observed. Where the Sumerian-derived original referred to a primordial deep beneath the earth, the Naghii's Abzu is the deep behind the sky: the void between stars from which Tauma flows and into which the Firstborn disappeared.

The Abzu is not worshipped. It is studied, watched, and feared in the specific way you fear something that has already demonstrated what it is capable of.

### Igi-an — the sky-seeing practice

The Naghii term **igi-an** (from *igi*, to see, and *an*, the sky/heaven) describes the contemplative observational practice through which experienced priests extend their perception into Tauma-sensitive states. In this practice, a priest does not simply observe the sky — they allow the signals of the sky to enter, reading Tauma flux in celestial terms: certain configurations correspond to intensified Abzu activity, certain alignments to quiet.

The cost of this practice is taught explicitly within the order: opening the mind to celestial signal also opens it to influence from the Abzu. The most experienced practitioners of *igi-an* are simultaneously the most capable and the most vulnerable members of the priesthood. The biological susceptibility to enchantment and mental influence that the Naghii carry as a species is the hereditary trace of generations of this practice.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

Naghii society does not fracture only because people disagree. It fractures
because disagreement under ruined skies, dangerous records, unstable sites,
and incomplete truth can kill communities before certainty arrives.

### Communities, archives, and regional divergence

The Naghii are united by archive logic, not by a single state. Different
communities formed around different ruin fields, different sky visibility,
different survival routes, and different interpretations of what the Firstborn
left behind. Some are inward archive-cities with deep initiation layers and
minimal outside contact. Others are route-facing communities that survive only
by trading warning, maps, escorts, and interpretation outward. Some are more
Kha-dominant and enforcement-heavy. Others give more practical space to Saa
projection, scouting, and signal authority.

What remains universal is not uniform theology. It is the conviction that
danger must be filtered through controlled access.

### Bodies of authority

Most Naghii communities contain some shifting combination of:

- **priest-astronomer hierarchies** who preserve doctrine, calendars, and celestial interpretation
- **Kha enforcement bodies** who guard thresholds, restricted records, and dangerous access points
- **Saa interpretive bodies** who project warning, pattern, and consequence outward
- **archive custodians and record chains** who preserve continuity across damage, fire, theft, or mistranslation
- **surveyors, scouts, and ruin readers** who keep the outer world legible enough for the archives to remain relevant

In theory these functions support one another. In practice they often compete
over which kind of certainty deserves authority first.

### Internal fault lines

The three schools of Khataar interpretation are only one axis of tension.
Other recurring fractures include:

- Kha enforcement urgency versus Saa interpretive delay
- inner archive secrecy versus field necessity
- local ruin knowledge versus central doctrinal control
- preservation of dangerous records versus destruction of what may be too costly to keep
- communities that see the Firstborn as sacred completion versus communities that increasingly suspect civilizational failure
- priestly authority versus surveyors, couriers, and guards who pay for delay with their bodies

These tensions matter because Naghii life often depends on timing. A wrong
sequence, a late warning, a blocked courier line, or a priesthood frozen by
internal dispute can turn doctrinal disagreement into famine, ruin loss, mass
exposure, or a preventable Abzu event.

## 6. Daily Life and Survival

For the Naghii, survival is not only a matter of food and shelter. It is also
the survival of procedure, record, warning, and correct reading. A Naghii
community that eats but misreads the sky, mishandles a ruin, opens the wrong
archive layer, or trusts a false translation may still be living inside the
shape of its own extinction.

### Food, labor, and the cost of caution

Most Naghii communities live by carefully managed grain, fish, reptiles, small
herd stock where terrain allows it, preserved meat, bitter oils, fermented
stores, and trade goods that can survive long keeping. Their settlements often
depend on routes that are vulnerable to interruption, contamination, predation,
or political seizure.

This makes caution material, not philosophical. A wrong reading may send a
labor caravan into unstable ruins. A missed signal may cost a watch post its
water. A badly timed opening of a sealed chamber may kill scribes, scouts, and
guards before the priesthood finishes arguing about what happened.

### Childhood, initiation, and fear

Naghii children are raised under layered restriction. They learn early that
not all doors are for opening, not all records are for touching, not all ruins
are entered simply because they have been found, and not all truths become
safer because they are spoken aloud.

Initiation therefore has a survival function. It teaches not just reverence
but sequencing:

- when to wait
- when to read
- when to close distance
- when to refuse access
- when to leave a thing unnamed because naming it badly is worse than silence

### Ordinary anxiety

The common Naghii fear is not constant dramatic revelation. It is layered
misreading:

- a sign taken too early
- a warning dismissed because the pattern was incomplete
- a chamber opened in the wrong sequence
- a projected reading turned into force before it was ready
- a priest who trusted doctrine more than the ruin in front of them
- a Kha and Saa authority split that leaves everyone else exposed while they debate

This makes daily life narrower than it appears from outside. The archive exists
because forgetting is fatal, but so is certainty built on the wrong fragment.

### Aging, diaspora, and unbearable loss

To age well among the Naghii is to remain useful as a keeper of sequence, a
reader of pattern, a witness of precedent, or a body disciplined enough not to
break under dangerous knowledge. Elders are valued not merely for memory but
for surviving long enough to have seen which interpretations cost lives.

What the Naghii find hardest to lose is often not territory alone but chain of
meaning:

- an archive segment burned, flooded, stolen, or mistranslated
- a lineage that can no longer verify an old rite
- a watch tradition broken for one season too many
- a diaspora child who never learns which dangers their people already named
- a signal that arrives and is recognized too late

## 7. External Relations

Naghii communities tend to anchor themselves around ruins, stepped temple
sites, dry uplands with sky visibility, archive-cities, guarded corridors, and
trade routes where information can be controlled as tightly as goods. These are
not comfortable holdings. They are exposed, contested, and often dependent on
fragile infrastructure and dangerous neighboring ground.

### Neighbors, rivals, and dependency

Other species seek the Naghii for records, routes, warnings, astronomical
interpretation, sealed knowledge, ruin maps, and controlled access to sites no
one else understands. That makes the Naghii useful, but also resented. A
people who control interpretation can be accused of wisdom, fraud, cowardice,
or hoarding depending on how desperate everyone else is.

Their archives create asymmetrical relationships:

- some species need Naghii records to move safely
- some fear Naghii gatekeeping more than the ruin itself
- some trade for venom, signal-reading, or route logic
- some would rather burn a record than let one priesthood decide what it means

### The shape of danger

The outside pressures most likely to damage Naghii continuity are:

- ruin collapse and false archaeological confidence
- tainted chambers, vestiges, and Abzu-sensitive sites
- caravan interruption and route-loss
- predators or raiders that strike scribes, couriers, and surveyors instead of fortresses
- other species forcing access during famine, panic, or celestial fear
- internal doctrinal splits that paralyze action until the danger has already crossed the threshold

### How they are read by others

To outsiders, the Naghii often look like a species that would rather classify a
disaster than prevent it. Their patience can read as cowardice, their ritual
gates as arrogance, and their caution as a form of cruelty that lets danger
spread while permission is debated.

Sometimes that judgment is unfair. Sometimes it is exactly what happened.

## 8. System Connections

### Competency affinities

The Naghii's history and body produce strong natural tendencies toward:

- **Lore competencies** — astronomical knowledge, archival reading, interpretation of ancient records
- **Evasion competencies** — fluid body control, instinctive threat-reading through layered sensory input
- **Social competencies** — specifically coercion, reading of intent, and intimidation; the Naghii's stillness and sensory precision translate directly into social pressure

### Natural attack forms

All Naghii:

- **Cola / Tail Strike** — prehensile tail; contundente / blunt damage; reach 1–2m; oriented toward control, knockdown, and field disruption

Kha-Naghii only:

- **Mordisco / Fang Bite** — retractable fangs; perforante / piercing damage; reach 1m; venom injection on threshold success

Saa-Naghii only:

- **Expulsión de Veneno / Venom Spit** — projected venom; veneno / venom damage; reach ~10m; phosphorescent; blindness effect on threshold success

### Species-specific mechanics

**Lengua Bifurcada / Forked Tongue** — The Naghii's forked tongue grants enhanced detection of heat and chemical signatures. They can track living creatures by heat or scent in conditions where visual tracking fails, and this extends to detecting movement and proximity outside their direct line of sight. Provides a bonus to tracking and perception rolls in relevant situations.

**Susceptibilidad al Abzu / Abzu Susceptibility** — Generations of *igi-an* practice have left a biological trace. The Naghii carry a structural susceptibility to enchantment, mental influence, and Abzu-origin effects. Manifests as a penalty to resistance rolls against effects that seek to control, deceive, or influence behavior.

**Kha-Naghii — Resistencia al Veneno / Venom Resistance** — Extended biological proximity to their own venom glands has produced systemic tolerance. Kha-Naghii have enhanced resistance to venom effects, including those from other sources.

**Saa-Naghii — Alcance Perceptivo / Extended Perception** — The Saa-Naghii's configuration for projecting outward extends to perception. They read signs, patterns, and signals at ranges where others see noise. Provides a bonus to lore-based interpretation rolls and to perception rolls that involve reading patterns rather than direct sensory input.

### Technique implications

The Naghii's history produces identifiable technique traditions. Because techniques are world-grounded knowledge rather than species-locked abilities, these techniques can be learned by members of other species through legitimate transmission — what marks them as Naghii-origin is that they emerged from this specific history, body, and practice, not that only Naghii can use them.

**Species-origin techniques** that would emerge naturally from Naghii physicality and practice:

- **Body control** — techniques based on fluid, precision movement; repositioning; using the whole body as a weapon surface rather than only the arms. The Naghii body made these natural to develop, but the underlying logic is applicable to any species.
- **Reach and control** — techniques that use the tail's range in conjunction with overall body geometry; leverage-based disruption and restraint. The tail reach is specific to the Naghii body, but the control and field-disruption principles translate.
- **Sensory anticipation** — techniques whose triggers and conditions are built around reading the opponent's heat, movement, and intent rather than visual tracking. Effective in low-visibility conditions.

**Doctrine-origin techniques** associated with the priestly order:

- **Observational disciplines** — endurance and contemplation practices developed for the *igi-an* state; training the mind to receive without being overwhelmed
- **Archival combat methods** — formalized techniques preserved within the priestly order, some of which originated as practical skills and were later codified with ritual framing
- **Venom-ritual practices** — controlled venom use in ceremony and structured combat with theological significance

**On technique conditions:** Naghii-origin techniques should use conditions that the player can actively engineer — an enemy approaching, a target in range, a prior action having been taken — rather than conditions tied to specific times, celestial events, or environmental circumstances outside the player's influence. The astronomical and ritual identity of the Naghii shapes the *fiction* of the techniques, not the *trigger conditions* that make them available in play.

## 9. Design Notes

### What makes them distinct

The Naghii are not mysterious because they are cold or arcane. They are mysterious because they are **careful**. Every layer of secrecy, every test, every restriction on archive access exists because at some point a Naghii priest decided that a piece of knowledge was too dangerous to share freely. Some of those decisions were right. Some were wrong. The Naghii do not always know which is which — especially given that their foundational texts are interpretations of a language nobody can read.

Their specific horror is the very concrete anxiety of holding the oldest record of the worst thing that ever happened, being unable to fully read it, and knowing that the configuration it describes could appear in the sky again — without knowing if they have already missed it happening once, twice, or more.

### How other species perceive them

To most other species, the Naghii read as too absorbed in something that probably does not exist. They are the civilization that is perpetually waiting for an apocalypse that, as far as anyone else can tell, already happened — and that worrying about it more does nothing to prevent. Their caution, their layers of initiation, their refusal to simply live in the present reads from the outside as paranoia institutionalized into religion. They are respected for their knowledge but found exhausting as neighbors. The common perception is not hostility; it is a kind of bewildered pity for people who cannot let go of a disaster that is over.

The Naghii, of course, do not believe it is over.

### What to avoid

- **The cold rational archetype** — Mesopotamian astronomical culture was deeply religious and acutely afraid of omens. The Naghii should carry that fear, not suppress it behind composure.
- **Light vs. darkness as central identity** — this was a placeholder in earlier versions and should not return. Their duality is Kha and Saa, holding and projecting, not abstract cosmic balance.
- **Generic arcane mystery** — every specific detail (the archive, Khataar, the three schools, the interpretation problem, the Kha/Saa conflict) should do work that "mysterious snake people" cannot.
- **Locking technique identity to biology** — Naghii-origin techniques should describe methods that emerged from Naghii history and physicality without requiring a Naghii body to execute.
- **Celestial triggers in technique conditions** — the Naghii's astronomical identity is fiction-level, not trigger-condition level. Techniques should not require specific celestial events or time-of-day conditions that players cannot influence.

### Open design questions

- What do the pre-Khataar ruins actually contain? What patterns did the first Naghii interpret, and how wrong were they?
- Are there Naghii communities whose interpretation of Khataar led them to a fundamentally different theology — one that does not see the Firstborn as divine at all?
- What does a Naghii player character who has left the priestly community know, and what are they cut off from?
- How do the three schools of Khataar interpretation map onto the Kha/Saa political divide — do they align, cut across it, or vary by community?
