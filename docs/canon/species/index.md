## Purpose

This document is the master index for all species in Transcendence.

Each species is a distinct evolutionary line of bipedal humanoid descended from an animal lineage that survived the catastrophe and re-emerged over generations. Species are not cosmetic ancestry — they are the primary lens through which cultures, beliefs, bodies, and interpretations of the ancient world are expressed.

Every species carries:

- an animal inheritance (body, instinct, symbolic weight)
- a cultural identity (their interpretation of the ruins and the ancient world)
- a cosmological reading (how they understand Tauma, El Limbo, and what happened)
- a technique body (the practices they developed from their history)

## Design Principles

### Species are not races

Species in Transcendence are not interchangeable fantasy ancestry with minor stat differences. Each one is a distinct civilization with a distinct body, worldview, mythology, and set of fears. Playing a different species should feel like inhabiting a fundamentally different relationship with the world.

### Animal heritage is not a costume

The animal base is not decoration. It shapes the body, the instincts, the social structure, the fears, and the symbolic logic of the culture. A hyena species will have a fundamentally different relationship with death and hierarchy than an elephant species — not just visually, but in how they think, organize, and interpret the ruins around them.

### The ancient world is not neutral

Every species encountered the ruins of the ancient civilization and drew different conclusions. These conclusions are not academic — they are religious, existential, and political. Two species looking at the same ruin will see completely different things, and both will be partially right.

### Cosmicism is not uniform

The horror of the setting is not equally visible to all species. Some look at the sky and see danger. Others see order. Others have deliberately stopped looking. The cosmicist dimension of each species should emerge from their specific mythological inheritance, not from a generic "scary universe" overlay.

## Cross-Species Notes

- **Naghii and Sauri contrast:** Naghii look upward and read the catastrophe as a celestial problem of incomplete interpretation. Sauri look to water, stone, body, and ritual containment; their horror is not that the sign cannot be dated, but that the vessel may never have been strong enough.

## Structural Template

Each species document follows this structure:

1. **Identity** — name, animal base, cultural analog, cosmicist hook
2. **Body** — physical description as humanoid, anatomical traits, natural attack forms
3. **Culture** — social structure, worldview, interpretation of the ruins and the catastrophe
4. **Theology** — relationship with Tauma and El Limbo, deities or belief entities, what they fear
5. **System connections** — competency affinities, technique implications, species-specific mechanics
6. **Design notes** — distinctive tone, what to avoid
