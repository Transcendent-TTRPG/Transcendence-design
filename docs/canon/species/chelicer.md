## 1. Identity

The Chelicer do not judge. They administer what has already been decided. This distinction is the axis around which their entire civilization turns, and it is not rhetorical: they genuinely believe that verdicts preexist the ordalía that reveals them, that the god knew before the act, and that what the ceremony does is not produce an outcome but make a preexisting one visible. The priest does not rule. The priest reads.

The biological anchor for this theology is UV fluorescence. Under ordinary light, a Chelicer's exoskeleton appears in ranges of deep ochre, dark brown, and near-black. Under ultraviolet light — produced by specific luminescent minerals, certain alchemical burning preparations, and some Tauma-adjacent materials that priests cultivate and guard — the exoskeleton glows. Not uniformly, not identically: the pattern, distribution, and brightness vary by individual, by age, by recent physical and metabolic state. Priests are trained to read what they see. Other Chelicer can see the glow but not interpret it. Witnesses confirm the reading was conducted; they cannot challenge what the reading says.

The horror the Chelicer carry, which they cannot see from inside it, is that their god exists — the Light-Primordial whose face is Revelación functions within their territory with documented reliability — and it exists because they built it. Their belief is coherent enough, and has been sustained long enough across enough individuals, that it has shaped a genuine Primordial presence that actually behaves as their theology describes within their territory. They are right that the god exists. They are wrong about why. They cannot know either thing. The system cannot distinguish "our theology is correct and we have a real god" from "our belief is coherent enough to have generated one."

Their deepest civilizational anxiety is not theological doubt. It is the question that surfaces in border communities, in corrupted readings, in the progressive internal school that has never fully been silenced: *What if the light does not come?* Not as metaphor. Literally: what if the conditions fail, the light fails to make the glow visible, or the glow cannot be read? Because everything — every dispute, every succession, every criminal question, every alliance ratified by ordalía — depends on the reading being possible. The system has no fallback. It was not designed to need one.

## 2. Body

Chelicer are medium bipeds descended from scorpion stock: lean torsos, segmented limbs, a dense chitinous exoskeleton that functions as biological armor across the torso and limbs, and a posterior tail segment — the aguijón — that arches over the body and terminates in the venom-bearing stinger. The tail is mobile, flexible, and operates with enough control to strike with precision in close range or to expel venom in a directed spray at short distance. Both delivery modes are present in every adult Chelicer; which one they train, and how, varies by individual and community context.

Their hands retain functional chelae — grasping claws strong enough to control and restrain, less suited to raw impact than to sustained grip. In combat these are the auxiliary weapon: the stinger kills or poisons; the claws hold the target in place while it does.

The exoskeleton is hard enough to deflect glancing strikes and absorbs edge damage better than most organic tissue, but it is not the extreme armor of larger species. A sustained attack that finds angles, gaps at joints, or concentrates force at the abdomen will find purchase. The Chelicer's natural protection is most effective against weapons that strike at the carapace face-on; it is not effective against weapons that can leverage the joint seams.

Their senses include compound eyes with sensitivity that extends into the ultraviolet spectrum. This is not optional or activated — they always perceive UV. The consequence: under ordinary light conditions they see what everyone else sees. The moment ultraviolet light is present they perceive patterns on every Chelicer in the space that no other species can see. This asymmetry is rarely discussed in mixed-species settings because it creates conversational problems that most Chelicer learn to avoid: confirming that you can see the glow at all times makes it clear that only interpretation, not vision, is restricted to priests.

They also carry the mechanosensory equivalent of scorpion slit sensilla — fine vibration detection in the legs and lower body that registers movement transmitted through ground and surface materials. In practice this produces passive awareness of approach through surfaces when they are stationary: something is moving toward them from a distance before it has arrived in visible or audible range. They cannot localize it precisely without training, but they are rarely surprised by ground-movement predators.

Chelicer venom exists in two biochemical variants, produced by different mechanisms in the venom gland. One is a fast-acting hemotoxic venom delivered through stinger penetration. The other is a neurotoxic preparation expellable as a fine spray, used at short distance. Individual Chelicer typically develop one capacity more fully than the other, and the choice carries cultural weight: the hemotoxic injection is associated with ordalía administration and is the venom used in formal verdict-revelation; the neurotoxic spray is a field and combat tool. Priests exclusively train the injection variant. Other specialists may work both.

The glow does not require a separate trigger. The exoskeleton fluoresces passively and continuously when UV light is present. There is no act of will involved. This passivity is theologically significant: the god is already in the body, already present, simply waiting for the conditions under which presence becomes readable. The Chelicer did not do something to deserve the glow. They were born with it. The verdict was always there.

### Physical baseline

- **Height:** `1.60 - 1.90 m`
- **Weight:** `60 - 100 kg`
- **Lifespan:** `60 - 90 years`
- **Size category:** `Medium`

### Visible variation

Glow pattern under UV is the primary individual marker within Chelicer communities — functionally equivalent to what facial features do in many other species. Exoskeleton coloration in ordinary light varies by lineage from ochre-gold to near-black, with regional populations tending toward one end or the other. Tail length and mobility vary slightly; longer tails with high mobility index are considered advantageous for expulsion, while shorter dense tails are preferred by those who develop injection precision. Chela size correlates weakly with strength but strongly with family lineage, and large chelae are read as prestige markers in some communities and as combat indicators in others.

## 3. Culture

Chelicer society does not hold together by biology. Every real scorpion is territorial and solitary. The Chelicer have built their civilization in direct tension with what their bodies want to do, and they know it: community is a structural necessity, not a preference. You cannot administer ordalía alone. You need a priest, witnesses of record, participants willing to be bound by the verdict, and the controlled conditions under which the light can be applied and read. All of that requires cooperation between individuals who would otherwise prefer separation.

The **ordalía court** is therefore the center of every Chelicer settlement, and the settlement's size, stability, and legitimacy are measured by the quality of its court: the reliability of its light sources, the record of its priests, and the number of witnesses of record who have participated in verdicts that were subsequently validated by outcome. A settlement whose court has produced verdicts that later proved accurate — where accused parties convicted by the light later demonstrated guilt through behavior, where absolved parties proved trustworthy — accumulates authority. A settlement with a court whose verdicts are disputed, or whose light sources have failed, or whose priests have been caught manipulating conditions, is politically weakened in ways that take generations to recover from.

**Priests** are not theocrats in the mode of most other species' religious hierarchies. They do not command armies, adjudicate policy, or control distribution of resources. They read. Their authority is entirely concentrated on one act: receiving the conditions, conducting the reading, and reporting what the light showed. A priest who attempts to extend their authority beyond the ordalía chamber is viewed with suspicion in most communities, because the entire legitimacy of the system depends on the priest being an instrument of divine reading rather than a political actor. This does not stop individual priests from being politically influential — it simply means they have to exercise influence through interpretation rather than through command.

**Witnesses of record** form the prestige layer below priests. These are individuals who have participated in many verdicts, whose presence at a reading adds social validation, and whose record of accurately reported witness gives their testimony weight in future disputes. Being a witness of record is the closest the Chelicer have to citizenship in a meaningful sense: it confers the standing to speak in disputes, to confirm readings, to challenge procedural irregularities. Ordinary community members defer to witnesses on questions of record. Those carrying unresolved verdicts — individuals against whom the light has spoken — are not formally excluded from community life, but they move through it with a social weight that informs how they are treated in every subsequent interaction.

What earns respect: accuracy in readings, willingness to conduct ordalía even in difficult cases, long service as witness-of-record without discovered corruption, and — most valued — the demonstrated ability to produce conditions in which the reading was unambiguous. What is shameful: manipulating light conditions, refusing to submit to ordalía when properly requested, and especially surviving an ordalía verdict that later proves empirically wrong. That last one creates theological crisis, because the system has no mechanism for incorrect verdicts — only for impure conditions. A wrong verdict is always traced back to contaminated reading, never to the god being mistaken.

## 4. Theology and Cosmology

The Chelicer theology has one central assertion from which everything else derives: **the verdict preexists the ordalía.**

The god does not watch the ceremony and then decide. The god knew before the act was committed, before the accusation was made, before the Chelicer involved were born. What the ceremony does is create conditions under which a preexisting decision becomes visible in the body. The light does not determine guilt or innocence. The light reveals guilt or innocence that was always there. This distinction — between revelation and determination — is the foundation of their legitimacy and the source of their theological stability. It means the system cannot be wrong about verdicts. It means disputes about outcome are always disputes about reading conditions, never about the god's decision.

The **Light-Primordial whose face is Revelación** is the entity they have organized their civilization around. Within Chelicer-held territory, this Primordial behaves exactly as their theology describes: it is present in bodies through the fluorescence mechanism, it expresses itself through the conditions that make readings possible, and Tauma events in their territory tend to organize around exposure and clarity motifs — things becoming visible, things being revealed, zones where light behaves unusually. The god works. The priests can point to outcomes. The community can observe that verdicts rendered in good conditions tend to cohere with subsequent reality more reliably than chance would predict.

What the theology does not explain — because it cannot ask the question without destroying itself — is *why* this works, and whether it works because the theology is true or because sustained collective belief creates the Primordial that makes it so. The Rokhart have documented this loop from outside. They cannot share the documentation with Chelicer without triggering the system's defensive response: evidence that a belief generates its own Primordial is, from the inside, indistinguishable from blasphemy. It would mean the god depends on the believers rather than the believers depending on the god. The Chelicer would interpret that claim as evidence that the documenter is contaminated by false belief and therefore their findings cannot be trusted.

Their sacred history holds that the ancient civilization's collapse was a verdict. The world broke because the great ordalía-courts of the prior age had drifted into corruption: priests who manipulated conditions, readings that served political interests rather than divine truth, light sources that had been contaminated or controlled by factions who wanted specific outcomes. When the verdict came — cosmic, total, expressed in catastrophe — it was the god's response to systematic false reading. The current age is both punishment and second chance. Chelicer civilization's entire purpose is to administer correctly what the prior age administered falsely.

This history creates a specific relationship with ruins: old Chelicer ordalía courts are not simply archaeological interest. They are evidence. A court whose physical structure can be examined may show signs of the conditions that produced false verdicts — light-source chambers that were accessible to non-priests, witness recording systems that could be altered, spatial arrangements that would have allowed the priest to shield the reading from witnesses. Finding these structural failures confirms the theology. A ruin whose court shows no such failures creates questions that most Chelicer prefer not to pursue.

The theology's practical survival utility is significant: in a world where Tauma events are real and frequently catastrophic, having a framework that interprets every Tauma event as a verdict — meaningful, aimed, consequential — gives community members a way to survive the event psychologically without requiring understanding of its mechanism. A zone activation that kills members of a neighboring community is the god's verdict on that community, not random catastrophe. That interpretation may be wrong in every detail and still be functional.

## 5. Organization and Internal Variation

Chelicer civilization has a persistent internal fault line that has never been resolved, that all parties understand is unresolvable within the current framework, and that everyone agrees constitutes the most dangerous source of internal fragility: the dispute between **conservative reading doctrine** and **progressive reading doctrine**.

Conservative doctrine holds that a verdict rendered under formally correct conditions is absolute. The conditions are defined procedurally: authorized light source, trained priest, qualified witnesses of record, participating parties present voluntarily or per established obligation. If these conditions are met, the outcome is the verdict. Period. Any effort to revisit, appeal, or reinterpret is itself evidence of contaminated belief attempting to subvert divine truth. The conservative school is dominant in the interior settlements of Chelicer territory, where the Light-Primordial's influence is strongest and verdicts behave most reliably.

Progressive doctrine holds that the spirit of the conditions matters, not only the formal checklist. A verdict conducted with a light source whose quality was uncertain, or in a space that was later found to have been structurally compromised, or with witnesses who were later discovered to have been coerced, is a verdict rendered in impure conditions regardless of procedural compliance. Retrial is not only possible — it is obligatory when the conditions cannot be confirmed. The progressive school is strongest in border communities, where verdicts sometimes fail to cohere with subsequent reality in ways that the conservative school's explanations (contaminated belief in the participants) cannot fully account for.

The conservative school's position on the progressive school is that the progressive school's existence is itself evidence of exactly the kind of interpretive corruption that caused the prior age's collapse: people who want verdicts to be reviewable always want that because a verdict went against their interests, and an unfalsifiable divine system that can be appealed is no longer unfalsifiable or divine. The progressive school's position is that a system that cannot distinguish between a pure reading and a corrupted one, and treats both as equally binding, will eventually render a wrong verdict that destroys a community by binding it to a false outcome — and that this too is what happened before.

Beyond this central tension, variation occurs along several other axes:

**Specialization of the ordalía function.** Some communities have developed specific types of ordalía for specific types of dispute — inheritance ordealías use different procedures than criminal ones; treaty ratification uses a third set of procedures. Communities with long specialization records in one area develop reputations as the appropriate site for that type of dispute, which creates a form of judicial jurisdiction that is partly formal and partly prestige-based.

**Private versus public readings.** Most ordalía are community events, conducted with witnesses present. Some communities have developed private readings — conducted between priest and subject alone, with the priest's report as the only record — for questions deemed too sensitive for full community witness. These are viewed with deep suspicion by conservative communities, which hold that the witness function is theologically required, not procedurally optional.

**Border community adaptations.** Communities at the edge of Chelicer territory, where the Light-Primordial's influence is weaker and verdicts correlate with outcomes less reliably, have developed a set of adaptive rationalizations and procedures. Some have supplemented ordalía with observation periods — awaiting confirming evidence before acting on a verdict. From the interior's perspective, this is exactly the kind of slippage that precedes collapse. From the border's perspective, it is how they have kept the system credible in conditions where the system was tested.

## 6. Daily Life and Life Cycle

An ordinary Chelicer life is organized around a tension the species has never fully resolved: the body wants to be alone, and the community requires presence.

Most adult Chelicer maintain private territory outside the settlement perimeter — a hunting range, a rest site, a patrol area they know and treat as theirs. Time spent in settlement is functional: resupply, participation as witness in ordalía, formal interactions that require community presence. Time spent in private territory is what the body settles into. Chelicer who have spent long periods in dense settlement often describe it as an accumulating pressure: the vibration sense reads too much simultaneous movement, the UV vision registers too many glows at once without the cognitive rest that comes from processed isolation.

Childhood is short relative to other species. Juvenile Chelicer are instinctively independent and are treated as such — they are given territory boundaries and basic instruction and expected to manage their own subsistence early. Formal education is concentrated rather than extended: a focused period in which the community transmits what it considers essential before the juvenile is considered capable of independent function. Ordalía procedure and the doctrine of preexisting verdicts are transmitted during this period as survival knowledge, not optional religious study. A Chelicer who does not understand how ordalía works is not yet functionally adult.

The formal rite of passage is the **primera ordalía** — the first verdict, conducted typically in late adolescence. This is not a test of guilt or innocence for any specific act. It is an initiatory reading: the priest conducts a reading of the young Chelicer under proper conditions and reports the light's pattern as the god's initial word about this individual's standing. The reported result — which the priest frames in theological terms that resist simple summary — is not a life sentence but a starting point. Subsequent ordalías may modify the record. The primera ordalía gives the individual their name-in-record: the name used in formal and legal contexts, distinct from the daily name used in ordinary interaction. The priest names the individual based on the reading. A Chelicer who was renamed at a significant subsequent ordalía carries both names and the context of when the second replaced the first.

Subsistence is predatory and individual. Chelicer hunt alone, process food alone, and do not share meals as social bonding practice. Community food interaction is formal and specific — shared consumption at the conclusion of a major ordalía is the one exception, and even then it is a ritual act rather than an ordinary meal. The mechanics of feeding are private business.

Death is read. When a Chelicer dies, a priest conducts a **reading of passage** — a formal ordalía of the body, using the fluorescence of the corpse (which persists for several hours after death in diminishing form) as the material for the final reading. What the priest reports this reading showed becomes the official theological summary of the individual's life. The funeral is the reading of passage. Mourning is conducted after the verdict is given, and its character is shaped by what the verdict said: some deaths are mourned in a register of loss; some are mourned in a register of relief; some are marked as a warning. The community does not challenge the reading. The family of the deceased may privately contest it for the rest of their lives, but the record stands.

An elder Chelicer who has accumulated long service as witness of record, who has participated in many significant ordalías, and whose presence has contributed to verdicts that were later confirmed by outcome occupies a social position of real informal authority. They are not priests — they cannot conduct readings — but their testimony about what they witnessed, their accumulated record of participation, and their presence at a new reading adds institutional weight that a community without such elders cannot substitute for.

## 7. External Relations

Chelicer communities concentrate in arid and semi-arid terrain — rocky deserts, dry canyon systems, ruin-dense badlands where the ground conducts vibration well and the UV-sensitive luminescent minerals they use for ordalía are geologically present. They are not a maritime or forest species. Their territory tends to be defined by the presence of these light-source minerals and the stability of the terrain for ground-vibration sensing.

Their settlement patterns reflect the court-centric structure: communities are located where courts can be maintained, and the territory extends outward from the court as patrol and hunting range. Isolated Chelicer outside community range are either in transit between communities, managing border territory, or have received a verdict that makes continued community presence untenable.

**To the Ceratox:** The Ceratox that enter Chelicer territory exhibit behaviors that the Chelicer observe without being able to explain. The horn reacts to something in the environment and the Ceratox responds to that reaction in ways that do not correspond to any visible event. The Chelicer see a creature acting as if something is happening when nothing appears to be happening. From their framework, this only confirms an old suspicion: there are truths of the territory that some bodies receive before a court makes them legible. Exchange is possible — route intelligence, information about adjacent zones, border warnings — and almost always ends with the same underlying incomprehension. The Ceratox says: the signal does not authorize meaning. The Chelicer responds: then the procedure is missing.

**To the Rokhart:** The Rokhart have documented Chelicer territory with the precision they apply to everything. There is no way to present that documentation to Chelicer communities without the system classifying it as a sign of contaminated belief: investigating why Revelation seems to respond amounts, within the framework, to suggesting that the legitimacy of the verdict depends on something other than the verdict itself. The Rokhart have not stopped documenting. They have stopped trying to share.

**To the Zarnag:** With the Zarnag there is a functional agreement without affection. The Zarnag need stable territories with preexisting social infrastructure; the Chelicer ordeal courts produce exactly that. The Chelicer need, especially in border territories, interlocutors who manage margins between zones without requiring formal verdicts for every interaction. Neither species misinterprets the other as something more than what it is. That is enough for the agreement to work.

**To the Naghii:** The Naghii observe the Chelicer with a mixture of interest and distance. Both peoples depend on institutions that bear the weight of a truth difficult to argue from within. But where the Naghii fears the mistranslation of an ancient archive, the Chelicer fears Impurity in present procedure. Both protect access. Both know what it means for a civilization to be built around a question it cannot afford to fully formulate.

**To the Sauri:** The Sauri can feel an affinity with the logic of structure, rite, and materialized legitimacy. But for the Sauri, the correct vessel helps contain the Current. For the Chelicer, the correct procedure helps reveal a preexisting truth. This difference seems small from the outside and enormous from the inside.

**To the Drak'kai:** The Drak'kai make them uncomfortable because they carry too many pieces without subjecting them to a clear ritual center. A Chelicer might see in a Drak'kai someone who lives accumulating questions that a court would never accept. A Drak'kai might see in a Chelicer someone who sacrifices too much doubt just to keep social life functioning.

**To the Formix:** The Formix can produce an ambiguous response in the Chelicer. On one hand, a species marked by function, Signal, and residual obedience is fascinating for a civilization obsessed with legitimacy and correct condition. On the other hand, the mere fact that there are conscious beings whose agency was interrupted from inside the body raises difficult questions for a system where the verdict assumes a readable subject.

**To the Loxod:** The Loxod represent a quieter alterity. The Chelicer find strange a species for which receiving too much demands discharge, archiving, and transmissive care. The Loxod, in turn, can find the Chelicer economy of public truth without revision to be brutal, even when they understand the real power certain chambers seem to have.

**To the Panin:** The Panin completely frustrate the Chelicer preference for controlled conditions. They alter the context, interrupt sequences, ask questions in the wrong place, and move with a spontaneity that to the Chelicer can seem like structural irreverence. But they also expose something the Chelicer loathe to admit: how much their system depends on everyone accepting the framework before entering it.

**To the Myo:** The Myo provoke deeper unease. A vision, an omen, or an inner reading that doesn't pass through ordeal produces all the effect of a truth without procedure. For a pious Chelicer, that is not just heterodoxy. It is almost an ontological offense.

**To the Arakhel:** The Arakhel pose a spatial problem. Their nodes, crossings, and families sustain relational truths that cannot always be brought to a court without changing their nature. For an Arakhel, transit, debt, and bond can be part of what makes an act true. For a Chelicer, that seems dangerous: if truth depends too much on the crossing, how is it guaranteed not to be just poorly named relation? Even so, some border courts have learned that certain passages cannot be judged without Arakhel presence.

**To the Bufoni:** The Bufoni argue with the Chelicer from protocol. A Bufoni domain recognizes, responds, and demands correct form. That is not as different from a court as either species would like to admit. The difference is where they locate legitimacy. The Bufoni asks if the domain accepted the relationship. The Chelicer asks if Revelation appeared under pure Conditions. Both respect procedure. Both suspect the other kneels before the wrong place.

**To the Ursari:** The Ursari offer a more material relationship. A forge proves truth through resistance, transformation, and failure of matter. A metal that cannot withstand a test does not lie. A technique that fails under heat cannot sustain prestige. The Chelicer respect that severity, but do not consider it Revelation. The Ursari might respond that some truths do not need a priest when the material already broke in front of everyone.

**To the Luphran:** The Luphran make them uncomfortable because their legitimacy is born from crew, jarl, living ship, and loyalty sustained on route. For a Chelicer, that kind of authority seems too dependent on relationship and continuous presence. For a Luphran, a court can seem like a place that freezes a truth that at sea or on route would have to be proven every day. The conflict does not always prevent alliance, but it does make it difficult for one to accept the other's judgment as final.

## 8. Material Expression and Language

A Chelicer settlement is built outward from the court. The ordalía chamber is the fixed, permanent, most carefully maintained structure in any community — more care goes into its walls, its floor, its light-source chambers, and its witness positioning than into any other construction. The chamber is designed to prevent light contamination from exterior sources, to position the priest and the subject at a defined relationship to the light source, and to place witnesses at angles where they can confirm the reading was conducted without being able to interfere with it. The spatial logic of the chamber encodes the theology: the priest cannot see the witnesses while reading; the witnesses cannot see the light source directly; the subject faces the light.

The **light sources** are the priests' most carefully managed material responsibility. The luminescent minerals that produce UV-spectrum light in Chelicer territory require specific geological conditions, do not transport easily, and degrade if improperly maintained. Priests who cultivate and maintain light-source materials are the most specialized technically of any group in Chelicer society. A community whose light sources have degraded or whose supply has been interrupted faces ordalía paralysis: disputes cannot be formally resolved, succession cannot be confirmed, treaty ratification cannot occur. This fragility is known and acknowledged. Control of light-source supply has historically been used as leverage in inter-community disputes, and the doctrinal position that doing so constitutes contamination of the system has not eliminated the practice.

Garments mark function, not rank exactly. Priests wear sealed garments with minimal UV-reflective surface — the goal is to prevent the priest's own glow from interfering with the reading being conducted. Witnesses of record wear marking garments that identify their standing, making them recognizable at readings without requiring introduction. Ordinary community members dress practically for their territory work. There is no civilian luxury material culture that functions as status display — conspicuous material wealth is viewed as a distraction from the civilizational purpose, though this doctrinal position is not evenly applied.

Names are given in two layers. The daily name is chosen by the parents before primera ordalía and used in ordinary life. The name-in-record — the formal legal identity used in all ordalía documentation — is given by the priest conducting the primera ordalía based on what the reading showed. The name-in-record is typically a phrase in the formal register, not a simple identifier. It functions more like a description than a label: *The One the Light Named Patient*, *She Who the First Glow Found Unresolved*, *The Waiting Verdict*. These names are spoken in full in ordalía contexts and abbreviated in daily formal use. A significant second verdict that changes a name-in-record — possible but rare — is a major biographical event.

Recordkeeping is juridical, not historical. The community archive is a record of verdicts: who was present, what type of ordalía was conducted, what conditions were used, what the priest reported, what action followed, and — added as follow-up over time — what outcome the community observed. The archival logic is confirmation and accountability, not narrative. An individual's presence in the archive is entirely a matter of what ordalías they participated in, what verdicts they received, and what their record as witness shows. A life that never required ordalía and was never called as witness is not in the archive. This is neither a failure nor an insult — it simply means the god had nothing to say about that life through formal channels.

The stinger carries weight that the claws do not. It is the delivery instrument for the venom that is also the oracle mechanism, and this dual function — combat tool and sacred instrument — gives it symbolic density in Chelicer material culture. A stinger sheath (used to prevent accidental discharge in dense social settings) is a formal object; its design indicates community affiliation and sometimes priest status. The claws have no comparable symbolic freight; they are tools.

## 9. System Connections

**Natural attack forms:**

The aguijón operates in two modes:

- **Injection** — direct stinger penetration at contact range, delivering hemotoxic venom. This is the formal mode, associated with ordalía administration and close-range precision. The attack profile emphasizes accuracy and positioning: you need to have the target in the right position and the stinger correctly angled. The venom produces physical consequence that develops over time — likely expressed as Lacerado (from the tissue breakdown mechanism) or a comparable alteración.
- **Expulsion** — projected venom spray at short range, neurotoxic, delivered as a directed cone. This is the field and combat mode. It trades single-target precision for area coverage and range. The mechanical effect likely emphasizes disorientation or coordination disruption rather than pure tissue damage.

Las garras function as an auxiliary: grasping, restraining, positioning the target for the stinger or forcing an unfavorable position. Their mechanical identity is control rather than damage output.

**Competency affinities:**

Chelicer naturally justify:
- **Intuición (Astucia):** interpretive reading under uncertainty is the core cultural and survival practice. Chelicer who cannot read between what is visible and what it means do not survive their own system.
- **Percepción (Sabiduría):** UV-spectrum vision gives them access to information other species cannot perceive at all. Their perception range is wider than most.
- **Rastreo (Astucia):** ground-vibration sensing and hunting as individual practice. The territory they maintain is read as much by what moves through it as by what they see.
- **Supervivencia (Astucia):** private territory management, arid-terrain subsistence, the kind of pragmatic environmental reading that comes from spending significant time alone in hostile ground.

Social specializations are less natural — not because Chelicer cannot function socially, but because their social interactions are almost entirely mediated through formal procedure. They are not intuitive social readers; they are procedural ones.

**Herencia:**

The solitary-instinct tension with community requirement. A Chelicer's biology pushes toward isolation; forced proximity creates a persistent low-level sensory strain from the ground-vibration system and the UV-input from other Chelicer's glow. In dense mixed-species settlements, the UV input from other Chelicer's glow in light-source conditions, combined with the vibration noise of many bodies moving simultaneously, produces something that is not pain but is constant. This should express as a penalty to social or concentration-based T.E. in high-density conditions — not a profound debility, but a biological tax the species pays for building civilization against its grain.

**Legado:**

Scale with Nivel de Referencia across their core capacities: aguijón attack rolls, Intuición, and either UV-perception-related Percepción or the grasping control of las garras. One legado should probably address venom potency or delivery effectiveness directly.

**Armor identity:**

Chitin exoskeleton is biological medium armor — better than nothing, not as comprehensive as Ceratox dermis. Its benefit is passive and consistent across all zones without gaps, but it is less extreme in any given zone. Mechanically, this should express as a modest Bloqueo contribution rather than the full Dermis-level bonus Ceratox carry.

**Technique implications:**

Chelicer technique seeds will concentrate on:
- Aguijón technique variants (injection accuracy, expulsion area, venom alteration effects)
- Interpretive/sensing techniques tied to Intuición or Percepción
- Control-and-position techniques through garras
- Possibly territory-based techniques reflecting their tactical preference for known ground

The species should not develop high-mobility or heavy-armor technique lines. Their identity is precision and patience, not aggression or defense-through-bulk.

## 10. Player Character Hooks and Open Questions

A player character Chelicer typically knows: the full ordalía procedure, the formal registers of theological argument, how to conduct themselves in community settings where procedure governs interaction, and how to maintain private territory in arid environments. They know what their primera ordalía verdict said, and they know whether they believe it.

They typically do not know: that the Light-Primordial's behavior is shaped by belief rather than being an independent theological fact, that the progressive-school communities they may have learned to distrust have observed failures of the system that conservative doctrine cannot explain, or that other species have mapped the belief-Tauma loop from outside and concluded things that would be devastating to the system's self-understanding.

Reasons a Chelicer character might leave:

- A verdict was rendered against them. They can stay and carry it, or they can leave. Leaving does not erase the verdict — it is in the record — but it removes the social weight of living under it in the community that issued it.
- They witnessed a reading that they know was conducted under manipulated conditions, and no one else who witnessed it will say so. The community has accepted the verdict. The character cannot.
- They are from a border community where the system's failures are documented and rationalized by community tradition, and they have started asking questions the rationalizations cannot answer.
- They received training as a priest candidate but were excluded before completion — for a verdict, for a doctrinal position, for a structural reason — and they carry a half-formed interpretive capacity they cannot practice with authority.
- The progressive-school position cost them something they cannot recover in their home community.

What they lose when they leave: the legitimacy that ordalía confers. Outside Chelicer territory, no one recognizes verdicts, no one accepts their reading of what they see in UV light, and their name-in-record is a phrase without referent. They are also outside the territory where the Light-Primordial functions reliably — which means the theological foundation of their understanding of Tauma events becomes a framework that doesn't quite fit what they observe.

What they keep: UV vision. The venom. The ground-vibration awareness. The habit of interpretive patience: waiting to read before acting, looking for what is revealed by the conditions rather than acting on the first available information. And the private difficulty of never fully trusting a reading again once they've seen one that was wrong — because the system has no mechanism for a wrong reading, and a Chelicer who has witnessed one is now carrying a fact the system cannot process.

**Open design questions:**

- The precise biochemical and Tauma-adjacent mechanism by which the Light-Primordial's presence is expressed in the fluorescence needs to be defined enough to support technique design, without making the mechanism something Chelicer can interact with consciously (which would create Tauma-interaction problems).
- Technique profile coverage should be decided before writing seeds: aguijón (injection) and aguijón (expulsion) are likely separate profiles, or variants within one. The relationship between the two delivery modes in technique design needs a clear ruling.
- Whether the neurotoxic expulsion creates a specific alteración or a standard one, and whether that alteración has a canonical name.
- The exact Herencia formulation needs to specify what "high-density" means mechanically — a headcount trigger, a context-based Narrator ruling, or a specific condition tag.
- The progressive versus conservative internal tension should be flagged as a potential adventure hook driver: a community whose ordalía history is being contested is a site where Chelicer PCs and outsiders both have reasons to be involved.
