## Purpose

This document does not list finished Techniques.

Its purpose is to establish the structural foundation for Drak'kai technique authoring: which natural attack forms and weapon surfaces are available, which specializations are primary and secondary, and how the novice pass should be distributed.

Seed principles — the abstract conceptual seeds with `world_root`, `technique_space`, `authoring_pressure`, and `avoid` fields — are added iteratively with designer review after this skeleton is approved.

### Origin vs. access

**Drak'kai-origin techniques are not species-locked.** The species origin explains why a technique exists — what problem it was developed to solve, and what body of knowledge or practice produced it. The mechanical surface (weapon profile or specialization) determines who can execute it. Any character wielding a weapon with the required profile, or holding the required specialization, can use a Drak'kai-origin technique regardless of their species.

What the Drak'kai origin does: gives the technique its identity, constraints, and thematic coherence.
What it does not do: prevent non-Drak'kai characters from learning or executing it.

The test when authoring: *Could this technique exist without the Drak'kai?* If yes, the origin is too thin. *Could someone execute it with a different body, tool, or practice?* If no, the origin has become too species-locked. The target is the middle.

### What a Drak'kai Technique should be

A Drak'kai Technique should carry a recognizable method of solving a problem that emerged from Drak'kai body (weight, shell, crushing jaw, patient endurance), Drak'kai practice (long-route wandering, object handling, conclave exchange), and Drak'kai knowledge (accumulated encounters with the world and with things in the world that cannot be fully explained).

A Drak'kai Technique should not be a generic "wise elder" bonus, a generic heavy-armor fighter move, or a knowledge dump that bypasses the mechanical system.

The Drak'kai do not treat knowledge as decoration. They treat it as load-bearing — and the weight shows.

## Technique Lessons From The Species

The Drak'kai have three primary natural attack forms:

- **Mordisco / Bite — Principal:** `Impact`, `Unstoppable`, `Interception`, `Torsion`; committed jaw closure, hold sustained beyond initial contact, body weight behind the close — the bite is not a repeated tool, it is a final decision
- **Garras / Claws — Auxiliar:** `Deflection`, `Interception`, `Torsion`, `Interruption`; grip, angle redirection, and supplementary close pressure — not raking aggression but catching, holding, and redirecting contact
- **Caparazón / Shell — Defensive:** `Bastion`, `Interception`, `Impact`, `Line Control`; structural reinforcement of the torso, body-anchor positioning, contact absorption — the shell is a defense surface, not an attack vector

They also have strong cultural and biological technique surfaces:

- maces, hammers, and blunt crushing weapons for committed heavy strikes
- heavy shields for anchor, barrier, and controlled passage denial
- polearms for approach management from a patient position
- Percepción and Rastreo for magnetoreceptive environmental reading and route history
- Supervivencia and Orientación for long-route endurance and wayfinding
- Intelecto specializations (Identificación, Historia, Arqueología, Geografía) for accumulated knowledge applied in specific moments
- Compostura specializations (Enfoque, Contención, Meditación) for patience, internal discipline, and sustained function under pressure
- object handling and vínculo/vestigio encounter practice as non-weapon Technique roots

Species-origin does not mean natural-weapon-only. A Drak'kai Technique may use the bite, shell, claws, mace, hammer, shield, polearm, accumulated knowledge, environmental reading, or patient preparation. What makes it Drak'kai is the principle underneath: the user solves a problem through commitment, structure, weight, and accumulated understanding — not through speed, deception, or volume of strikes.

### Novice pass profile pressure

Profiles that Drak'kai naturally help cover well:

- `Bastion`
- `Interception`
- `Impact`
- `Unstoppable`
- `Deflection`
- `Torsion`

Profiles Drak'kai might touch in smaller numbers:

- `Rend`
- `Interruption`

Profiles the novice pass should not force unless a concept genuinely demands them:

- `Flow`
- `Shadow Pressure`
- `Skirmish`
- `Unpredictability`
- `Charge`
- Ranged profiles

### Novice pass — initial authoring order

1. Establish the 12 attack/weapon profile assignments first
2. Start with bite and shell — they are the most structurally distinctive surfaces
3. Expand into maces/hammers and shields — committed blunt and anchor
4. Add polearm techniques only where the profile genuinely needs it
5. Write two defenses from shell positioning and patience-read
6. Write two resistance hybrids from `Contención` and `Enfoque` secondary surfaces
7. Close with the eight primary specialization Techniques

## Seed Principles

### Cierre en el Umbral

Mordisco · Intercepción · Reactivo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai who has traveled long routes through many kinds of terrain and encounter has learned to read commitment before it lands. The bite that responds is not a panic reflex — it is a decision that was already made before the attacker finished the motion. The jaw closes because the moment was recognized, not because the impact surprised the body.

**technique_space:** Reactive bite that catches the attacking limb or body at the moment of approach commitment. The Drak'kai intercepts not the impact but the movement — closing the jaw as the attack enters reach. The result is not a block: the bite is now on the attacking surface. The trigger is a physical attack against the user; the Drak'kai declares before their own T.D., makes a reactive T.A. with the bite, and if the T.A. meets or exceeds the attacker's T.A., the bite closes on the attacking surface before the attack resolves — the incoming attack is disrupted, and the attacker is left Impedido on the limb that entered range. If the T.A. falls short, the interception fails and the attack resolves normally. Impedido severity scales with rank.

**authoring_pressure:** Self-oriented — not ally protection, which is already covered from two angles by existing Interception techniques (Levantar el Dique, Cerrar el Flanco). The hold effect should be Impedido (jaw on the weapon arm disrupts technique execution), not Atrapado (Sauri territory). The technique must cost the user something — it is a calculated risk, not a free nullification. The Drak'kai is betting their read was correct.

**avoid:** Universal attack negation. Atrapado or any hold that fully immobilizes (that is Sauri jaw logic). Ally protection. Any framing that makes this a reflex or a surprise response — the Drak'kai is not startled, they are executing a decision. Should not require the attacker to fail an additional roll for the bite to connect; the question is only whether the timing was precise enough to disrupt the attack.

### La Masa Continúa

Mordisco · Impacto · Activo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai does not stop at the contact point. The jaw closes and the body continues moving forward — the bite is not the end of the action, it is where the force enters the target. Weight does not ask permission to keep going. The technique is not about breaking structure or disrupting balance from the side; it is about a mass that decided to be somewhere and does not stop until it is there.

**technique_space:** Active attack in which the bite closes and the Drak'kai drives their full body weight forward through the contact point. The jaw is the anchor that connects the mass to the target; the mass is what produces the effect. If the attack succeeds, the target is knocked down (Derribado) — not displaced to the side, not pushed back to a neutral position, but driven to the ground by the forward commitment. This is not a swing, not a lateral shock, and not a break attempt; it is the result of a body that did not stop. The technique does not require the target to be actively guarding or resisting — it works against any target within bite reach that can be driven through.

**authoring_pressure:** The effect must be Derribado, not Desequilibrado (lateral shock, covered by Barrer la Orilla) and not forced displacement alone (covered by La Corriente No Retrocede against defenders). The distinction from La Corriente No Retrocede must be clear in trigger and requirement: that technique requires the target to be actively defending; this one does not — the mass drives through regardless of the target's defensive posture. Cost should reflect that this is a committed forward drive: higher rhythm than a basic attack, some attrition from the body commitment.

**avoid:** Desequilibrado as the primary output. Structural break (Sauri territory). Any requirement that the target be actively defending or resisting — the mass continues whether or not there is resistance. Framing this as a charge or rush — the Drak'kai does not sprint into it; the body was already close and the commitment was already made.

### El Cierre Que No Se Negocia

Mordisco · Imparable · Activo

**world_root:** The Drak'kai does not reconsider once the closing motion has begun. The decision was made before the jaw moved — weight, angle, and direction were already set. A block placed in the path of a committed close does not stop the close; it absorbs less than it should. The force does not negotiate with what is in the way.

**technique_space:** Active bite attack in which the committed closing force bypasses or reduces the target's passive Block in the contact zone. The technique does not produce displacement and does not require the target to be actively guarding — it works against any target within bite reach. The Block bonus on the contact zone is reduced by the user's bite competency rank, or at higher ranks negated entirely. The attack still resolves its normal damage through whatever Block remains. This is not about breaking structure and not about driving someone back — it is about the jaw closing through whatever passive protection stands between the Drak'kai's decision and the target.

**authoring_pressure:** The output must be Block reduction or negation, not Derribado (covered by La Masa Continúa), not forced displacement (covered by La Corriente No Retrocede), not structural break (Sauri territory). The distinction from La Corriente No Retrocede must remain clear: that technique requires active defensive commitment from the target and produces displacement; this technique requires neither and produces no movement. Rank scaling should affect the degree of Block reduction — partial at low ranks, complete at high ranks.

**avoid:** Forced movement or displacement of any kind. Desequilibrado or Derribado as a rider. Any requirement that the target be bracing or actively defending. Framing this as armor penetration in a general sense — it is not bypassing armor material, it is reducing the effective Block value of the zone the jaw is closing on.

### El Peso Que Gira

Mordisco · Torsión · Activo

**world_root:** The Drak'kai jaw is not a striking instrument — it is an anchor. Once it closes on a surface, the bite holds while the body moves around it. The rotation is not a separate technique layered onto a grip; it is the completion of the same commitment. The jaw sets the point; the mass follows through.

**technique_space:** Active technique that establishes jaw contact and body rotation as a single committed action. The bite closes and the Drak'kai rotates their full body weight around the contact point, forcing the target into a repositioning they did not choose. The target cannot escape the rotation by retreating — the jaw holds and the turn follows. The result is the target ending at a different angle than they intended, with flank or back exposed. This is a control technique, not a damage technique: the primary output is positional displacement and angle disruption, not raw harm. The degree of repositioning scales with rank.

**authoring_pressure:** Must require real jaw contact — this is not a ranged or reach-based technique. The rotation is what makes it Torsion rather than Impact; the effect should be repositioning or angle disruption, not knockdown or raw damage amplification. Distinguish clearly from Barrer la Orilla (Sauri, Impact): that technique uses lateral swing shock against posture; this one uses an established grip and body-mass rotation through the contact point — the fictional mechanism and the requirement are different.

**avoid:** Desequilibrado as the primary output — that is already covered from the lateral-shock angle. Atrapado or full immobilization. Any two-step framing where the bite is one action and the rotation is a separate follow-up — the commitment is simultaneous. Should not require a prior hold state to trigger; the bite and the turn are the entry condition together.

## Garras

### El Ángulo Que Falla

Garras · Desvío · Reactivo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai who has spent decades reading the lines of terrain, routes, and approach understands that redirecting is not the same as stopping. The claw does not absorb the incoming force — it guides it past. The attack does not land because it no longer knows where it is going. What the claw takes from the blow is not its energy but its direction. What follows belongs to the jaw.

**technique_space:** Reactive technique that triggers when the user is targeted by a physical attack within claw reach. Rather than blocking or absorbing, the Drak'kai guides the incoming attacking surface — limb, weapon arm, or body — past the intended contact point through precise claw placement at the moment of approach. The attack fails to land cleanly or loses significant force through redirection. The deflection opens a follow-up window specifically for the bite: the attacker's arm or body is now past the Drak'kai at the redirected angle, and the jaw has a clear, uncontested line. The technique does not deal damage directly — its output is redirection and the follow-up opening it creates.

**authoring_pressure:** The primary output must be attack redirection, not block or absorption (that is Bastion/shield territory) and not process spoil (that is Interruption). The follow-up window for the bite should be explicit — this technique exists to set up the principal natural weapon, not to be a standalone defensive resolution. The Drak'kai does not move during the deflection; the attack is what moves. Distinguish clearly from Cierre en el Umbral (Interception): that seed catches the attacking surface and holds it — this one guides it past and lets it go, opening a different consequence. No existing Deflection technique to avoid.

**avoid:** Any framing as blocking, absorbing, or tanking the hit. Damage output on the deflection itself. The follow-up window becoming a free guaranteed bite — it should open the opportunity, not execute it. Forced movement applied to the attacker as a result of the deflection — the redirect changes their angle, not their position.

### La Vuelta Que Suelta

Garras · Torsión · Activo

**world_root:** The Drak'kai does not pull the weapon away — they rotate the arm that holds it until the grip becomes untenable. They have handled enough objects, specimens, and unknown things over long routes to understand that a grip fails not when it is overpowered but when it is taken past the angle it can sustain. The hand opens because the joint left it no choice.

**technique_space:** Active technique in which the claw wraps around the weapon arm, wrist, or gripping hand of a target in contact range and applies rotational force against the natural axis of the joint. The target's grip on the weapon fails — not through raw strength but through angle: the arm is taken past what it can hold through. The weapon drops. This is disarm through structural rotation, not a pull or a strike. The technique does not reposition the target's body (that is the bite's role) and does not damage the limb directly — the joint is stressed to the point of grip failure, nothing more.

**authoring_pressure:** The primary output must be disarm, not Desequilibrado (that is covered from the lateral-shock angle by Barrer la Orilla and from whole-body repositioning by El Peso Que Gira), not damage, not immobilization. Distinguish from El Peso Que Gira (bite Torsion): that technique uses the jaw as the anchor and rotates the whole body — this technique uses the claw to rotate a specific limb, producing grip failure rather than positional change. The technique requires real claw contact on the weapon-bearing surface — it cannot work at reach, through armor that covers the hand, or against a target not holding a weapon.

**avoid:** Raw damage to the joint. Whole-body repositioning — this is limb-specific, not a throw or sweep. Atrapado or hold effects — the claw releases once the grip fails; it does not hold afterward. Framing as a yank or pull rather than a controlled angular rotation through the joint axis.

### La Base Que Falta

Garras · Interrupción · Reactivo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai who has spent decades reading encounters knows that a technique does not begin when the weapon moves — it begins when the body shifts to support the weapon. The claw does not wait for the attack. It acts at the adjustment: the weight transfer, the grip change, the repositioning step that every technique requires before it can enter execution. What the opponent planned is still in their mind when their body no longer has the foundation to execute it.

**technique_space:** Reactive technique that triggers when an enemy within claw reach enters the preparatory transition for a Technique or heavy committed action — visible as a weight shift, stance adjustment, grip change, or repositioning step before the execution window opens. The Drak'kai acts at that transition moment, disrupting the physical foundation the intended action required. The technique does not execute. The opponent loses the rhythm invested in the preparation without gaining the execution; the cost is spent, the action is not. The claw does not hold, damage, or reposition — it collapses the setup.

**authoring_pressure:** The trigger must be the preparatory transition, not the weapon arc or the execution itself — distinguish from Naghii claw Interruption (spoils the weapon arm during commitment arc) and Zarnag claw Interruption (spoils an action crossing a contested space). The output is preparation collapse: the opponent's action is aborted before its execution window and the rhythm they spent does not return. The Drak'kai's read is what makes this possible — the technique should require that the transition is perceptible, not that the Drak'kai guesses blindly.

**avoid:** Damage as a primary output. Hold or grip effects after the interruption — the claw disrupts and withdraws; it does not close. Acting during the weapon arc or mid-execution (that is Naghii territory). Applying Impedido, Desequilibrado, or other conditions — the output is rhythm loss and action collapse, not a physical state on the target. Triggering on attacks that have no visible setup or that are already in motion.

### El Tránsito Que No Llega

Garras · Intercepción · Reactivo

**world_root:** The Drak'kai does not move to intercept — they are already where the interception happens. Someone trying to circle past, reach something behind the Drak'kai, or cross through the space the Drak'kai occupies finds the claw there before the movement completes. The Drak'kai did not react. They were positioned. What the mover committed to no longer exists as a clear route.

**technique_space:** Reactive technique that triggers when an enemy within claw reach begins a movement to reposition, flank, reach past the user, or transit through the user's occupied space toward another target or objective. The claw catches the body in motion — a grabbing arm, a shoulder, a passing limb — before the movement completes. The transit stops. The enemy's committed movement resolves into contact with the claw rather than into its intended destination. The grip does not hold — the claw catches and releases — but the enemy is now stopped inside the Drak'kai's space at a vulnerable angle, with the jaw in uncontested range. The technique creates the opening; the bite is what follows.

**authoring_pressure:** The trigger must be a movement in transit — repositioning, flanking, or crossing through the user's space — not an attack. Distinguish from Cierre en el Umbral (bite Interception): that seed catches the attacking limb of an attack directed at the user; this one catches a body in transit toward a different destination. Distinguish from El Ángulo Que Falla (claw Deflection): that seed redirects an attack and releases — this one stops a movement and positions the target for the bite. The claw does not hold; the follow-up window for the bite is the primary output.

**avoid:** Triggering on attacks directed at the user — that is the bite's role. Ally protection framing — this technique is self-oriented and does not redirect force away from another target. Damage on the claw catch itself. Atrapado or any hold effect — the claw releases; what follows belongs to the jaw. Triggering on movements the user could not physically intercept given their position and the enemy's transit line.

## Armas Culturales

### La Costura Que Cede

Maza · Ruptura · Activo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai who has handled enough objects — bones, shells, relics, ancient tools — learns what makes joined things fail. Armor is a joined thing. No assembly holds everywhere equally. There is always the place where one piece seats against another, where the riveting runs thin, where the material meets its grain. The mace does not have to break the armor in full — only find that place and make it remember the contact.

**technique_space:** Active mace strike that targets the weak point in a worn armor assembly rather than the body beneath it. The declared target is a specific armor zone on the opponent — the user must be able to reach it and have identified the fault through prior contact, observation, or accumulated knowledge. On a successful strike to that zone, the armor assembly at that location is structurally faulted: its Block contribution at that zone is permanently reduced for the remainder of the encounter. The damage from the strike is secondary; the lasting fault is the output. This is not breaking a held shield or a barrier — it is degrading the protection that is part of the target's body. Severity of Block reduction scales with rank.

**authoring_pressure:** The primary output is a lasting armor fault in a declared zone — not immediate damage, not Derribado, not displacement. Distinguish from the existing Zarnag Sunder technique (Hacer Ceder el Resguardo): that technique breaks a held or placed protective structure that is currently screening the target; this technique targets worn armor on the body itself, creating a fault that persists through the encounter rather than removing a piece entirely. The identification requirement is important: the Drak'kai does not strike blindly — they have read the assembly and know where it fails. This should require some fiction of prior observation, identification, or contact.

**avoid:** Breaking the armor entirely or removing it from play. Derribado or displacement as a rider. Targeting body damage beneath the armor rather than the armor structure itself. Triggering against unarmored targets or targets whose protection is not a worn assembly. Framing as a lucky hit rather than a read-informed strike.

### El Peso Que Planta

Maza · Bastión · Activo

**world_root:** The mace is not only a striking tool. A Drak'kai who has spent decades walking long routes under load knows that a shaft planted at the right angle holds more than it weighs. The same mass that swings can brace. The same grip that strikes can lock. When the mace is used as an anchor rather than a weapon, it becomes the reason the opponent cannot close the angle they wanted or advance through the contact they committed to.

**technique_space:** Active technique in which the Drak'kai plants the mace shaft or head as a brace point — against the ground, against the opponent's body, or across the axis of their approach — rather than completing a full swing. The planted mace holds: it resists displacement of the user, controls the opponent's approach axis, and creates a held contact that the opponent must either break or work around. The held position does not deal damage directly. Its output is control of the contact axis and resistance to being dislodged or flanked from that position. The difficulty of dislodging the held position scales with rank.

**authoring_pressure:** The output is positional control and anti-displacement through held mace contact — not a swing, not damage, not a stance posture. Distinguish from the Sauri Bastion shield stance: that technique anchors a point with a raised shield and is about holding defensive space while acting; this one plants the mace as a physical contact anchor that controls an opponent's approach axis, not just a space. The mace must be in contact or threateningly placed — this is not a ranged control effect.

**avoid:** Damage as a primary output — the mace is planted, not swung. Stance identity that mirrors the Sauri shield Bastion. Any effect that controls a broad space rather than a specific contact axis or approach line. Framing this as a generic defensive posture — the planted mace is a specific physical anchor, not an abstract guard.

### La Posición Que Se Hunde

Escudo · Bastión · Activo

**world_root:** A Drak'kai does not simply hold a position — they settle into it. The longer they remain, the more the weight has distributed, the more the ground has become part of the structure, the more the body has calibrated to what is coming. Other species can hold a line for a moment. The Drak'kai who holds it for several becomes progressively harder to move, as if their mass has increased with each resolved exchange.

**technique_space:** Active stance technique in which the user anchors with a shield, committing to a held position. Unlike a simple defensive brace, this Bastion deepens over time: the user's resistance to displacement, knockback, and posture loss accumulates the longer the position is maintained. Each exchange resolved from the held position adds to the structural resistance — the position does not weaken under pressure, it strengthens. At Novice rank this is modest; the scaling is the identity. The user cannot freely reposition while the deepening is active — movement resets the accumulation.

**authoring_pressure:** The identity is temporal deepening — resistance that grows while held, not a flat defensive bonus. Distinguish from the Sauri Bastion shield stance (which holds a point while preserving action output): that technique is about acting from a defended position; this one is about the position itself becoming progressively stronger the longer the Drak'kai refuses to move. The trade-off is explicit: movement resets accumulation, so the Drak'kai must decide between mobility and depth.

**avoid:** Flat static defense with no temporal dimension — the deepening is the technique. Offensive output while deepening — this is a pure hold, not a counter-attack platform. Becoming an immovable object that trivializes combat — the deepening should have meaningful scale limits at Novice rank.

### El Margen Que Se Mueve

Escudo · Control de Línea · Activo

**world_root:** The Drak'kai do not build walls. They move them. The shield is not raised to say "you cannot pass here" — it is pressed forward to say "you will be here instead." The Drak'kai's mass behind the shield is not decoration; it is the reason the direction means something. What the shield controls is not the space it occupies but where the opponent ends up when the push resolves.

**technique_space:** Active technique in which the Drak'kai commits their full body mass behind the shield and drives it forward as a steering surface rather than a blocking wall. The shield contacts the opponent or their approach line and redirects them — not backward in a straight push, but to the angle the Drak'kai chooses. The output is forced repositioning of the target to a specific position the user selects within the scene geometry. This is not Desequilibrado and not Derribado: the target ends up where the Drak'kai steered them, still upright, still able to act — but now in the wrong position. The precision of the steering and the distance of the redirect scale with rank.

**authoring_pressure:** The primary output is chosen repositioning, not knockdown, not Desequilibrado, not displacement in a single fixed direction. Distinguish from the Sauri Line Control shield stance (which blocks a lane and stays stationary) and from the Sauri anti-displacement self-brace (which prevents the user's own movement): this technique actively steers the opponent to a chosen position using committed mass. The Drak'kai must move with the shield during the drive — this is not a passive gate but a committed push with direction. The target must be redirectable: this cannot work against targets that are already anchored, in Bastion stance, or have more mass committed than the user can overcome.

**avoid:** Knockdown (Derribado) as the primary output. Desequilibrado as the primary output — the target is repositioned, not destabilized. Stationary use — the Drak'kai moves with the push. Broad multi-target sweeping — this steers one body to one chosen position. Infinite mass override — the target's Bastion or anchored state should resist this.
