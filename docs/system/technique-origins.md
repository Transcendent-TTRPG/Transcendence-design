# Technique Origins Framework

**Status:** Adopted (initial framework)
**Primary references:** `docs/system/techniques.md`, `docs/system/technique-interaction-framework.md`
**Related docs:** `docs/system/competencies.md`, `docs/system/specializations.md`, `docs/system/backgrounds.md`
**Related systems (pending):** Species, Doctrine, Region

---

## Purpose

This document defines how techniques are grounded in the world of Transcendence.

Its purpose is to prevent techniques from existing as purely mechanical unlocks or abstract rewards detached from the setting. In this system, techniques are not granted automatically by raising ranks or levels of competency. Instead, they exist as real forms of knowledge within the world: developed, preserved, transmitted, restricted, forgotten, adapted, or rediscovered by peoples, doctrines, and environments.

The role of this framework is to answer:

- why a technique exists
- where it comes from
- who developed it
- how it is transmitted
- what makes it feel like part of this world rather than a floating mechanical option

---

## Core Principle

> **Techniques are not granted automatically by progression.**
> Competency determines whether a character is capable of learning, understanding, refining, and executing a technique — but the technique itself must exist in the world and be acquired through a real source of transmission.

This means:

- ranks do not unlock techniques for free
- progression gives capability, not spontaneous knowledge
- techniques must have an origin
- techniques must be learned through contact with the world

A character may become highly skilled in a competency and still not know any advanced technique if they have never been taught, exposed, initiated, or guided into one.

---

## Competency vs Technique

A technique is **not** the same thing as a competency.

### Competency

A competency is the mechanical and practical foundation that makes a technique possible.

It answers:
- can the character understand this type of action?
- can they execute it reliably?
- can they refine it?
- can they improve it?

### Technique

A technique is a specific codified method of doing something.

It answers:
- what exact method exists?
- who created it?
- what cultural or doctrinal body does it belong to?
- how is it performed?
- why does it exist in the world?

### Relationship

> **Competency enables learning.
> The world origin (Species, Doctrine, or Region) creates the technique.
> The character acquires it through transmission.**

---

## Why Techniques Need World Origins

Transcendence is not a class-based progression game where abilities emerge automatically from abstract leveling.

The world itself is the source of knowledge.
Its peoples, beliefs, histories, bodies, and environments create the practices that later become techniques.

Without this, techniques risk becoming:
- disconnected from species and cultures
- interchangeable across the setting
- purely mechanical upgrades
- detached from the tone of survival, interpretation, and inherited ruin

This framework exists so that a technique always feels like something that was:
- developed by someone
- for a reason
- in a place
- under certain conditions
- and preserved through a body of practice

---

## Current Fronts of Origin

For the current stage of design, techniques may originate from three major fronts:

1. **Species**
2. **Doctrine**
3. **Region**

These fronts are not mutually exclusive, but every technique should have **one primary origin front**.

A technique may also have a **secondary front** if necessary.

---

## 1. Species-Origin Techniques

Species-origin techniques are techniques developed by a species through the interaction of:

- anatomy
- instinct
- culture
- worldview
- survival habits
- inherited symbolic interpretation of the world
- long-term collective practice

In Transcendence, species are not lightly separated from culture. Since each species is deeply distinct in body, mentality, social organization, mythology, and interpretation of the world, species-origin techniques often carry both:
- a practical function
- and a civilizational identity

This means species-origin techniques may arise from:
- body structure
- weapon habits
- traditional warfare
- forms of movement
- ritualized conduct
- social hierarchy
- inherited fears or taboos
- species-specific interpretations of the ancient world

### Important principle

Species do **not** own all techniques.
But species may be the origin point of large, coherent bodies of technique.

Species-origin does **not** mean species-locked execution.

If a Technique was invented from a species' natural weapon, anatomy, sensory
habit, venom, shell, horn, tail, or other biological surface, the Technique
should still be written so that non-members can learn an analogous method when
the fiction supports it. The non-member must provide a credible substitute:

- manufactured weapon
- prepared ammunition
- kit-based delivery
- tool or apparatus
- training analogue
- environmental setup

The species origin explains **why the method exists**. The requirements explain
**what the user needs to perform it**.

Example:

- a Naghii venom-projection Technique may be used naturally by a Saa-Naghii
- a non-Naghii user may need prepared residue ammunition, a venom capsule, a
  marking kit, or a projector

The adaptation must remain non-magical unless the Technique belongs to a later
explicit magic subsystem. Do not use species-origin Techniques as hidden
species-only powers unless the Technique is intentionally marked as biologically
exclusive.

### Species-origin techniques may be:
- common within a species
- restricted to a lineage, caste, order, or noble house
- secret
- sacred
- partially shared

### Species-origin techniques are ideal for:
- traditional martial systems
- inherited survival practices
- species-specific symbolic behaviors
- culturally rooted technique sets across multiple competency families

### Species Rank Coverage

When a species receives a full Technique pass for a rank band, the set should use a uniform coverage target. This keeps species comparable by quantity without forcing them to have identical effects, weapons, or tactical personalities.

For the current **Novice / rank 1** pass, the target is **20 species-origin Techniques**:

| Section | Count | Origin surface | Notes |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Attack / weapons | 12 | three core attack surfaces, four Techniques each | These Techniques express the species' culturally valued arsenal. Each species should define three core attack surfaces that relate to its natural weapons, cultural weapons, or compensating combat traditions. |
| Defense | 2 | armor or Evasion | These Techniques express how the species avoids, absorbs, redirects, or survives pressure. They should follow the species' actual armor and movement identity. |
| Resistance hybrids | 2 | Resistance plus another competency, specialization, equipment competency, or bounded state | Resistances never stand alone. These Techniques show how the species handles poison, fear, pain, corruption, mental influence, exposure, or similar pressure through a practiced method. |
| Specializations | 4 | one specific specialization or a tightly paired specialization expression | These Techniques cover non-weapon identity: perception, tracking, intimidation, interpretation, medicine, architecture, theology, command, and similar domains. |

This quota is an authoring target, not a statement that every member of the species knows all twenty Techniques.

The attack quota is built from **three core attack surfaces**:

- each core attack surface should receive four Techniques at the rank band
- those four Techniques should ideally explore different profiles, triggers, or tactical problems inside that surface
- a core attack surface may be a manufactured weapon competency, a natural weapon form, or a manufactured weapon competency that deliberately mirrors a natural weapon form

For example, a serpentine species might choose:

- `Spear`, because point-first reach mirrors fang, horn, or tusk entry
- `Flexible Weapons`, because flexible weapon logic mirrors tail or tongue control
- `Ranged Weapons`, because ranged delivery mirrors venom spit, spines, or other natural projection

The quota answers:

- does this species have comparable Technique volume to other species?
- does the species have more than weapon expression?
- did we include defense, resistance pressure, and non-combat or utility identity?

The quota does **not** answer:

- whether the Techniques are common or restricted
- whether they are equally useful in every scene
- whether every species uses the same equipment
- whether every species has the same number of natural weapons

Species with fewer natural weapons should not be padded with fake anatomy. They should express their attack quota through culturally valued manufactured weapons, doctrine-like training, or the stronger development of the natural weapon they actually possess.

Species-origin quota should be checked after the species has:

1. natural weapon profile access
2. culturally valued manufactured weapons
3. core armor / Evasion identity
4. likely Resistance pressures
5. core specialization surfaces

If those maps are not defined first, the quota will produce generic Techniques.

## 2. Doctrine-Origin Techniques

Doctrine-origin techniques are techniques developed through:

- religion
- cult
- ritual practice
- theological interpretation
- spiritual discipline
- metaphysical worldview
- devotion to a deity, force, or sacred principle

Doctrine is a major independent front because it can cross species boundaries.

A doctrine may create shared technique systems between characters of different species, not because they share anatomy or culture, but because they share:
- belief
- sacred method
- ritual discipline
- cosmological interpretation
- devotion to the same power or truth

This makes doctrine a powerful design tool for creating:
- cross-species identities
- ritual or ascetic practices
- codified spiritual methods
- sectarian knowledge
- forbidden rites
- sacred bodily or mental disciplines
- martial systems rooted in theology rather than species alone

### Doctrine-origin techniques may be:
- open to all initiates
- restricted by rank of initiation
- guarded by priesthood or inner circles
- fragmented across sects
- corrupted, heretical, or disputed

### Doctrine-origin techniques are ideal for:
- meditation systems
- ritual combat methods
- tolerance and suffering disciplines
- tauma practices
- sacred social techniques
- techniques that depend on worldview as much as on skill

---

## 3. Region-Origin Techniques

Region-origin techniques are techniques developed because of:

- climate
- geography
- terrain
- local hazards
- endemic fauna or flora
- environmental pressure
- recurring local conditions
- survival needs tied to place

These are techniques born not from what a people **is**, but from where they live and what the land forces them to become.

A region may generate:
- movement techniques
- survival techniques
- weapon adaptations
- local combat habits
- travel disciplines
- environmental responses
- craft methods tied to local material conditions

This front is especially important in Transcendence because the world is harsh, altered, and deeply shaped by the remnants of catastrophe. Even without fully formalizing the vestige/ruin layer yet, region can already account for a major part of how different places force different practices into existence.

### Region-origin techniques may be:
- widely known within the region
- misunderstood outside it
- difficult to practice without local conditions
- useful only under certain terrain or climate realities
- integrated into multiple species that inhabit the same area

### Region-origin techniques are ideal for:
- traversal methods
- local survival practices
- climate-specific adaptations
- terrain-specific movement
- combat methods built around local hazards
- practical skill sets shared by multiple peoples in the same land

---

## Front Priority Rule

Every technique should have:

- **one primary front of origin**
- optionally **one secondary front**

This is important for keeping the system readable.

### Examples
- a hyena funerary hunting technique → primary: **Species**
- a ritual pain-endurance method taught by an ascetic order → primary: **Doctrine**
- a glacier traversal technique shared by multiple peoples in the same frozen region → primary: **Region**
- a sacred march preserved by one species and transformed by a doctrine → primary: **Species**, secondary: **Doctrine**
- a regional martial form later sanctified by a cult → primary: **Region**, secondary: **Doctrine**

No technique should require too many front tags to exist. If a design needs three or four fronts just to make sense, it is probably too diffuse and should be simplified.

## Transmission

Because techniques are not unlocked automatically, each technique must be connected to a real mode of transmission within the world.

A character does not invent advanced technique merely by becoming numerically better. They may become more capable of learning or adapting technique, but the technique itself must come from somewhere.

> The mechanical implementation of learning — study hours, practice paths, mentor requirements, validation — is defined in `docs/system/techniques.md` (Learning section). This section defines the **world-side framing**: what kind of source in the world holds the knowledge and makes it available.

### Valid transmission sources include:
- direct teacher
- martial or ritual school
- elder or mentor
- oral tradition
- liturgical instruction
- scroll, manual, tablet, codex, or inscription
- community rite
- observation followed by practice
- guided adaptation under someone who already knows the method

### Structural rule

> A technique should always imply a believable path by which it can enter a character's life.

This does not mean the path must be easy.
It may be:
- restricted
- dangerous
- politically controlled
- sacred
- incomplete
- mistranslated
- or accessible only under certain social conditions

---

## Availability

Not all techniques of a front should be equally available within the world.

To give the world texture, each technique should have an **availability profile** — distinct from the mechanical access rules in `docs/system/techniques.md`, which govern whether a character meets the competency, rank, and equipment requirements to use a technique. Availability governs who in the world is allowed or able to encounter it.

### Availability levels
- **Common** — broadly taught or widely practiced within its originating front
- **Restricted** — limited to members of a group, caste, rank, or social layer
- **Secret** — deliberately hidden or guarded by those who hold it
- **Sacred** — tied to ritual permission, initiation, or divine sanction
- **Lost** — no stable living transmission remains; recoverable only through ruins, fragments, or rediscovery

These are not required mechanical categories yet, but they are highly useful for design and worldbuilding.

---

## Adaptation Across Fronts

A technique may originate in one front and later be practiced outside that front.

This is especially important because:

- species may learn from other species
- doctrine may cross species boundaries
- a region may shape common practices shared by many peoples
- some methods may be translated, repurposed, or distorted

However, a technique learned outside its original front should not necessarily feel identical.

### Rule

> A technique may preserve its function while changing its expression.

This means:
- the method may look different in another body
- the ritual framing may change across doctrines
- the terrain assumptions may shift outside the original region
- a foreign learner may preserve only part of the original body of practice

This allows techniques to travel without making the world feel mechanically flat.

---

## Species, Doctrine, and Region Are Not Equal in Function

These fronts do not all do the same kind of work.

### Species
Gives techniques:
- civilizational identity
- embodied continuity
- inherited worldview
- species-rooted style and symbolic meaning

### Doctrine
Gives techniques:
- shared spiritual discipline
- initiation structure
- sacred meaning
- cross-species continuity

### Region
Gives techniques:
- environmental necessity
- local realism
- practical adaptation
- place-based logic

This distinction should be preserved when designing technique sets.

---

## What This Means for Technique Sets

When building groups of techniques, the designer should not ask only:

> "What would be mechanically useful here?"

They should also ask:

> "What would this people, doctrine, or place have actually developed?"

This means technique sets should emerge from:
- recurrent need
- body reality
- belief
- danger
- material condition
- memory
- interpretation of the world

A technique set should feel like the natural result of a people's or doctrine's lived history.

---

## Relationship to Species Design

Species are especially important in this framework because species in Transcendence are not merely cosmetic ancestry. They are:

- distinct evolutionary lines
- distinct societies
- distinct mythic inheritances
- distinct symbolic readings of the old world
- distinct bodies with distinct practical constraints

This makes species-origin technique sets one of the strongest sources of identity in the setting.

However, the framework avoids turning species into hard classes by allowing:
- doctrine-origin techniques
- region-origin techniques
- adaptation
- shared transmission across boundaries

---

## Relationship to Doctrine Design

Doctrine allows the world to produce technique systems that are not reducible to body or homeland alone.

This is especially important in a world where:
- theology matters
- tauma matters
- sacred interpretation shapes practice
- ritual systems can unify or divide peoples beyond species identity

Doctrine-origin techniques should feel like something a believer, initiate, or order preserves because of what they think reality means.

---

## Relationship to Region Design

Region-origin techniques are where the world's geography and environmental cruelty become mechanically meaningful.

A frozen region, a poisoned valley, a flooded ruin belt, or a wind-scoured plateau should not only change scenery. It should create methods of:
- travel
- survival
- fighting
- building
- enduring
- interpreting danger

This makes region a strong but grounded source of technique bodies.

## Technique Schema

The `techniques.yaml` schema has two distinct origin fields:

### `origin`

The `origin` field refers to the **competency origin**.

Example:

- `main: Spear`
- `secondary: [Evasion]`

This field answers what the character must be able to do mechanically.

### `world_origin`

The `world_origin` block refers to the **lore origin**.

Required fields:

- `world_origin.primary_front` — Species, Doctrine, or Region
- `world_origin.secondary_front` — optional
- `world_origin.source` — the specific species, doctrine, region, order, school, or tradition
- `world_origin.seed` — the conceptual seed, pressure, doctrine, or regional need that generated the Technique
- `world_origin.transmission` — the world-side source through which the Technique is passed on
- `world_origin.availability` — Common, Restricted, Secret, Sacred, or Lost
- `world_origin.adaptation_notes` — optional notes for how the Technique travels outside its origin

This field answers why the Technique exists and where a character could encounter it.

## Technique Design Checklist

When creating a technique, answer these questions:

### 1. What competency supports this technique?
What must a character already know how to do?

### 2. What is the primary front of origin?
- Species
- Doctrine
- Region

### 3. Does it need a secondary front?
Only if necessary.

### 4. Why did this technique emerge?
What real need or worldview created it?

### 5. How is it transmitted?
Teacher? rite? text? communal practice?

### 6. Who can encounter it?
Common? restricted? sacred? secret?

If these questions do not have clear answers, the technique is probably under-grounded.

---

## Structural Conclusions Adopted

1. Techniques are not granted automatically by rank or progression.
2. Competency enables learning and execution, but does not create techniques by itself.
3. Every technique must have a real origin inside the world.
4. For the current phase of design, the valid fronts of origin are: Species, Doctrine, Region.
5. Every technique should have one primary front and at most one secondary front.
6. Species-origin techniques anchor technique sets in body, culture, and inherited worldview.
7. Doctrine-origin techniques allow cross-species technique systems rooted in belief and ritual.
8. Region-origin techniques ground techniques in local environmental necessity.
9. Every technique must imply a believable path of transmission.
10. Technique identity should emerge from history, need, belief, and survival — not only from mechanical usefulness.
